# 04 — Reappropriating Traditions

The contemporary scene's characteristic move: **not correlation with modernity but retrieval of tradition** — after the critique of foundations has made naive retrieval impossible.

---

## A. Postliberal theology (James Fodor) — the Yale school

A movement for the **faithful retrieval of Christian tradition and the ecumenical renewal of the church**.

### George Lindbeck — *The Nature of Doctrine* (1984)
Three models of religion and doctrine:

| Model | Doctrine is… | Weakness |
|---|---|---|
| **Cognitive-propositional** | Objective truth-claims about reality | Cannot explain doctrinal change without contradiction |
| **Experiential-expressive** (liberal, post-Schleiermacher) | Expression of a prior inner feeling or experience | Makes the community's language secondary and interchangeable |
| **Cultural-linguistic** (Lindbeck) | **Communally authoritative rules of discourse, attitude and action** | Charged with relativism and sectarianism |

- **Rule theory of doctrine** — "Jesus is Lord" functions as a **second-order directive** governing Christian speech and life, not primarily a first-order metaphysical report. This is what lets doctrine be both *constant* and *historically changing*: the rule holds while its formulations shift.
- **Intratextuality** — theology **redescribes reality inside the scriptural framework** instead of translating scripture into alien extra-scriptural categories. Slogan: **"the text absorbs the world."**

### Hans Frei
- **The eclipse of biblical narrative** — modern historical consciousness made theologians fit the biblical stories into external explanatory frameworks (either transcendent truths or historical facts), **robbing scripture of its reality-constituting power**.
- **Identity before presence** — Christians must first read the gospel narratives to learn Jesus's *specific identity* before asking what his presence means for believers now. Reverse the order and you make Christ a function of religious experience.

### Criticisms of postliberalism
1. The focus on narrative **obscures the cognitive and metaphysical work** of systematic discourse.
2. If the text "absorbs" the world, can the Church still be **judged and corrected by** the cultures it meets — or does it merely assimilate them?
3. Is the authoritative reading grounded in **the texts themselves or in a community's practices**? Postliberals are pressed to say.
4. They need a **more robust account of truth** than "modest cognitivism."
5. The standing charge: **sectarianism and political quietism**. Their reply — the "separated life" is a reflection of God's elective love and an appropriated sense of **Israelhood**, undertaken *for the service of the world*.

> [!tip] Why this matters in India
> Lindbeck's cultural-linguistic turn is the most powerful available argument that a local church's grammar of faith is not a defective version of a universal essence. It is also the sharpest argument *against* the inculturationist assumption that the gospel is a translatable kernel inside a cultural husk. Set it against Pieris and the Dalit critique in [[09 - Particularizing Theology II - African, South Asian, East Asian, Postcolonial]].

## B. Systematic theology after Barth (John Webster)

Building on Barth while emerging from his shadow.

### Eberhard Jüngel (1934–2021)
Barth plus existential Lutheranism (Bultmann, Fuchs, Ebeling). The revelatory word is an **eschatological "speech-event" that interrupts human self-identity**. The doctrine of God is derived **from the cross**: *God's being is in becoming*, and the "death of God" at Calvary expresses God's **sovereign freedom to identify with the crucified Jesus**. His anthropology rests on justification — passive reception of grace over human self-realization.

### Robert Jenson (1930–2017)
Radicalizes Barth to free theology from residual **Greek metaphysics**. In *The Triune Identity* and *Systematic Theology*: **God's identity is located in his dramatic, eschatological movement with creatures toward the future**, not in a timeless pretemporal eternity. He extends the Lutheran *communicatio idiomatum* to ecclesiology — the sacramental church as **"the risen Christ's Ego."**

### Colin Gunton (1941–2003)
Critiques Barth's **Augustinian** doctrine of the Trinity for subordinating the distinct persons to a monistic unity and producing a restricted pneumatology. He proposes the Trinity as a **communion of persons** and stresses the **mediation** of the triune God through creaturely means by the Spirit. *The One, the Three and the Many* offers a trinitarian metaphysic of culture affirming **creaturely particularity** against modern homogenization.

## C. Roman Catholic theology after Vatican II (Paul D. Murray)

| Figure | Move | Signature |
|---|---|---|
| **Bernard Lonergan** (1904–84) | Opens the neoscholastic system **from the inside** using transcendental philosophy, cognitional theory and philosophy of science | *Insight*, *Method in Theology*; dynamism and historicity injected into the manual tradition |
| **Edward Schillebeeckx** (1914–2009) | Phenomenology plus acute awareness of social reality; **critical-constructive** studies of doctrines in close dialogue with contemporary Christian practice and experience | Sacrament as encounter; the Jesus books |
| **Hans Küng** (1928–2021) | Symbol of the revisionist, progressivist spirit; questioned papal authority in *Infallible? An Enquiry*, and was stripped of his missio canonica | Later: interfaith dialogue and the **Global Ethic** |
| **Karol Wojtyła / John Paul II** (1920–2005) | Formed under Nazism and Soviet communism; the modern **"cultures of death"** must be confronted by the transforming power of the gospel; calls for a disciplined, militant church | *Redemptor Hominis*; personalism |
| **Nicholas Lash** (1934–2020) | Lay theologian; critiques modern **reductionist rationality**; shares postliberal suspicion of liberalism while keeping the liberal value of rigorous self-critique | Theology as **"the uneasy conscience of faith"** |

> [!info] The internal argument of post-conciliar Catholicism
> Lonergan and Schillebeeckx push method and experience; Küng pushes institutional reform; Wojtyła answers with a christocentric personalism that confronts the culture rather than correlating with it; Lash refuses both triumphalism and accommodation. All five are inside the same council's reception — which is why "the spirit of Vatican II" arguments never resolve. Cross-reference [[000 - Vatican II MOC]] and [[05 - Theologians Influence Map]].
