# 10b — Interlocutors, Opponents and Heresies

[[10 - Major Theologians and Their Methods]] gives the protagonists. **No theologian generates a method in a vacuum**: each one is answering somebody. This note is the supporting cast — the heretics, patrons, sparring partners and friends who forced the questions. Read a method backwards from its opponent and it stops looking arbitrary.

> [!tip] Exam use
> When asked *why* a theologian adopted a method, the safest answer names the opponent. Irenaeus is canonical-holistic **because Gnostics cherry-pick**; Athanasius is soteriological **because Arius makes salvation impossible**; Luther is anti-Aristotelian **because the schools made grace a subsidiary of ethics**.

## I. The sub-apostolic world

| Figure / text | Date | What they held or did | Consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Papias** | c. 60–135 | Bishop of Hierapolis, disciple of John, prizes **early oral tradition** | Later dismissed by Eusebius over his literal millennialism — a warning about how transmission gets filtered |
| **Walter Bauer** | 1934 | *Orthodoxy and Heresy in Earliest Christianity*: the earliest Christianity was plural, and **Rome imposed its version by force** | The thesis is heavily flawed, but modern critics still use it to recast orthodoxy as mere authoritarianism |
| **Marcion** | c. 100–160 | Docetic; the good New Testament Saviour-God is a **different God** from the evil Old Testament Creator | Ignatius, *Barnabas* and above all Irenaeus reply that **the Creator is the Redeemer** and the Old Testament is Christian Scripture |
| ***The Shepherd of Hermas*** | c. 110–140 | Hugely popular quasi-apocalyptic visions | Evidence of the second-century slide into **moral legalism and works-salvation**, cut loose from Paul |
| ***The Didache*** | c. 100 | Church manual, "teaching of the apostles"; rediscovered 1873 | Same diagnosis: ethics without the gospel of grace |
| ***Letter of Barnabas*** | 70–135 | Anonymous, defends Christianity against Judaism by **Old Testament typology** | Argues the faithful were always Christian and that a carnal reading misses the Jews' own scriptures |
| ***Letter to Diognetus*** | 150–190 | Apologetic open letter to a pagan, possibly the emperor's tutor | The bright exception — articulates **grace and substitutionary atonement** against the surrounding legalism |
| **Minucius Felix** | 2nd c. | *Octavius*, apologia against public slander | Explains that "incest" and "cannibalism" are pagan misreadings of the **kiss of peace** and the **Eucharist** |

## II. Apologists and Gnostics

- **Trypho** — an educated Jew (possibly Rabbi Tarphon) who debated **Justin** for two days, c. 160. Sceptical about a crucified Messiah and about abandoning the law; Justin answers **entirely from the Hebrew Scriptures**, which is why his method is Christological exegesis rather than philosophy at this point.
- **Valentinus** (c. 100–160) and Gnosticism — deny the goodness of creation and the physical incarnation. **Irenaeus** deals the mortal blow in *Against Heresies*: God is the Creator, and salvation is **the redemption of the flesh**, not escape from it.
- **Marcion** again — the pair Valentinus/Marcion is what makes Irenaeus **canonical and holistic**: the whole plan from creation to consummation, against verses lifted out like mosaic tesserae.

## III. The Arian crisis

- **Alexander of Alexandria** — the bishop who talent-spots the young **Athanasius** and pays for a first-rate education; convenes the bishops who condemn Arius in **318**, and attends **Nicea (325)**.
- **Arius** (b. c. 256) — Alexandrian presbyter: **there was a time when the Son was not**. Athanasius's whole method flows from the reply — a created Son destroys the identity of the *Father* and abolishes real communion with God.
- **Serapion** — the friend to whom the exiled Athanasius writes his masterly defence of **the deity of the Holy Spirit**.
- **Antony** (b. c. 251) — desert hermit; Athanasius writes the *Life of Antony* to display **Christ's victory over demons** and to feed the monastic movement, while quietly avoiding Antony's anti-material tendencies.

## IV. Augustine's opponents and teachers

- **Plotinus and Porphyry** — Neoplatonist monism. Augustine uses them to break **Manichaeism**, then finds them empty of incarnation and cross. This is the source of the standard criticism against him in [[10 - Major Theologians and Their Methods#Augustine of Hippo (354–430)]].
- **Ambrose** of Milan — allegorical preaching plus Neoplatonic sympathy makes Christianity **intellectually credible** to the searching Augustine.
- **Pelagius** — austere, brilliant British monk, in North Africa as a refugee: humans are **born innocent** with free will sufficient for moral perfection. Augustine answers that the will is **enslaved** and only God's grace and Spirit change what we love.
- **The Donatists** — an ultra-pure North African schism holding sacraments of lapsed clergy invalid. Augustine's reply founds Western sacramental theology: validity rests on **Christ's authority, not the minister's purity**, and the Church is a mixed body.

## V. The scholastic frame

- **Gaunilo** — obscure monk, "**Reply on Behalf of the Fool**": you cannot conjure a perfect island into existence by conceiving it. The first and still the standard objection to the ontological argument.
- **Boso** — Anselm's interlocutor in *Cur Deus Homo*; the dialogue drives him to concede the **rational necessity of the incarnation** — only a God-man can pay the recompense sin requires.
- **Peter Abelard** (1079–1142) — of the next generation. Bernard of Clairvaux accused him of a mere **moral-influence** theory of the cross, but on the volume's reading Abelard in fact held an objective, penal-substitutionary view. Useful caution against textbook labels.
- **Aristotle** — "the Philosopher"; the newly recovered corpus offers compelling logic together with the troubling doctrine of an **eternal universe**. **Aquinas** integrates him as the natural foundation on which supernatural theology builds. Luther will detonate exactly this move.
- **Dominic Guzman** — founds the Order of Preachers (1215) to combat heresy; its **study and mendicancy** attract the young Aquinas over his family's objections.
- **Albert the Great** — spots the genius of the "dumb ox", mentors him and takes him to Cologne.
- **Pseudo-Dionysius** — fifth/sixth-century apophatic writer long mistaken for the Dionysius of Acts 17. Aquinas leans on him for the claim that **it is easier to say what God is not**. The line runs on to Rahner's incomprehensibility ([[10a - Karl Rahner - A Deep Dive#6. The incomprehensibility of God — and his reading of Aquinas]]).

## VI. Reformation

- **Erasmus** — humanist of doctrine-light unity who first welcomed Luther, then wrote *On the Freedom of the Will* (1524). Luther's *On the Bondage of the Will* (1525) answers with **total inability**: nothing in us improves our standing before God.
- **Zwingli** — Swiss reformer; he and Luther **fail to agree on the Lord's Supper** at the Marburg Colloquy, 1529.
- **Melchior Wolmar** — teaches the young **Calvin** Greek at Orléans and opens humanism to him.
- **Nicholas Cop** — rector of Paris whose Lutheran-sounding address (1533) forces his suspected ghostwriter, Calvin, to flee.
- **Guillaume Farel** — the fiery Genevan preacher who **coerces a reluctant Calvin** into staying by imprecation.
- **Martin Bucer** — Strasbourg; shelters Calvin after his expulsion and matures him pastorally and theologically. The 1539 *Institutes* comes out of this exile.
- **Cardinal Sadoleto** — invites Geneva back to Rome; the city turns to the exiled Calvin, whose *Reply* is the classic short vindication of the Reformation.
- **Michael Servetus** — anti-Trinitarian; arrested and burned in Geneva, **1553**. The permanent stain in the Calvin dossier ([[10 - Major Theologians and Their Methods#John Calvin (1509–1564)]]).

## VII. Puritans and evangelicals

- **John Bunyan** — Congregationalist preacher, *The Pilgrim's Progress* (1679). **Owen** used his influence to find Bunyan a publisher, though not to keep him out of prison.
- **Solomon Stoddard** — "Pope" Stoddard, Edwards's grandfather and predecessor at Northampton, who admitted the **unconverted to communion**. Edwards abolished the practice and was dismissed for it in 1750.
- **George Whitefield** — sparks the **1740** Northampton revival; reading his biography at Oxford was a turning point for **Packer**.
- **David Brainerd** — missionary to Native Americans; visits and **dies in Edwards's house, 1747**. Edwards's *Life of Brainerd* is the reason his name survives.
- **Richard Baxter** — the "seraphic" Puritan; subject of **Packer's Oxford doctoral thesis** and the deep shaping influence on his pastoral vision.
- **Martyn Lloyd-Jones** — criticised Packer for **entangling himself in ecclesiastical politics** instead of producing a major systematic theology in the Warfield line. The Lloyd-Jones/Stott division of 1966 is the background.

## VIII. Modern theology

- **Count Zinzendorf** (d. 1760) — shelters the **Herrnhuter (Moravian) Pietists**, whose living faith over dry orthodoxy forms the young **Schleiermacher**.
- **G. W. F. Hegel** (b. 1770) — Berlin colleague; his **evolutionary model of history** matches Schleiermacher's account of religion evolving toward Christianity.
- **Adolf von Harnack** and **Wilhelm Herrmann** — Barth's liberal teachers. Herrmann's transparent love for Jesus shaped him; then in **1914 the same teachers endorsed the war**, and Barth turned back to the Bible. Liberal theology's *ethical* failure, not its arguments, is what broke it for him.
- **Emil Brunner** — Barth's fellow dialectical theologian, open to a point of contact in natural theology. Barth's answer in **1934** is a tract titled simply ***No!***
- **Hans Urs von Balthasar** — proposed that Barth made a decisive shift from **dialectic to analogy**; the reading governed Barth studies for decades.
- **Bruce McCormack** — overturned it by re-reading the early texts: Barth's **dialectical theology was never a passing immature phase**.
- **Colin Gunton** — calls Barth an **"aesthetic theologian"** whose dogmatics is built like Mozart's music, meant to play on revelation rather than argue. This is the source of the "dogmatics as preaching" line in [[10 - Major Theologians and Their Methods#Karl Barth (1886–1968)]].

## Heresy → orthodox counter-move

| Heresy / position | Core error | Answered by | The methodological consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Docetism / Marcionism** | Christ's flesh unreal; two Gods | Ignatius, Irenaeus | Physical realities carry theological weight; the canon is one |
| **Gnosticism** (Valentinus) | Creation evil; salvation as escape from matter | Irenaeus | Scripture interprets Scripture; the whole plan, not fragments |
| **Arianism** | The Son a creature | Athanasius | Start from soteriology, not from abstract definitions of God |
| **Pneumatomachianism** | The Spirit less than God | Athanasius to Serapion | Extend the Nicene logic to the Spirit |
| **Pelagianism** | Nature suffices for perfection | Augustine | Theology begins from grace, not from moral capacity |
| **Donatism** | Sacraments depend on ministerial purity | Augustine | The Church is a mixed body; validity rests on Christ |
| **Legalism** (*Didache*, *Hermas*) | Ethics without gospel | *Diognetus* | Grace must be stated before duty |
| **Aristotelian synthesis** | Reason as foundation of theology | Luther | *Theologia crucis*: God known only in the crucified |
| **Liberal culture-theology** | Religion as human consciousness | Barth | Revelation, not experience, is the source |
| **Neo-scholastic two-tier nature/grace** | Grace as extrinsic superstructure | Rahner | The supernatural existential ([[10a - Karl Rahner - A Deep Dive#3. The supernatural existential and concupiscence]]) |
