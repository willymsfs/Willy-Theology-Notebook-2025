# 02 — Historical Development of Theological Method

> [!abstract] The plot in one paragraph
> Theology began by **deducing from authority** and reading allegorically; the universities split exegesis from speculation and built the *quaestio*; Aquinas ordered the whole pedagogically; humanists sent theology back *ad fontes*; neo-scholasticism froze the deductive manual; historical consciousness turned the sources into data; Schleiermacher relocated theology in consciousness, Barth blew it back to the Word, Tillich correlated the two, Rahner and Lonergan integrated history with dogma; Vatican II retrieved the Fathers; liberation put praxis first; postmodernity dissolved the meta-narrative into grammar and negotiation.

## 1. Patristic era (2nd–5th c.)

- Dominant method: **authoritarian-deductive**, deeply Platonic (Origen).
- Move: start from authoritative tradition → apprehend general truths by **noetic insight** → deduce detailed truths → confirm from Scripture.
- Central task: keep equilibrium between the **original canonic symbols** and evolving human consciousness — achieved chiefly by **allegorical interpretation**.
- **Irenaeus sets the brake:** using a logic akin to Empiric medicine, he argues believers know *that* God did something (e.g. created) by revelation, without needing to know *how* or *why*. Clear scriptural witness outranks speculative causal explanation.

> [!tip] Use this
> Irenaeus is the patristic warrant for **epistemic humility** — a permanent methodological limit, not a primitive stage.

## 2. Monastic vs. scholastic (12th–14th c.)

| | Monastic | Scholastic |
|---|---|---|
| Setting | Cloister | University |
| Practice | *Lectio* and meditation on the Bible | Dialectical disputation |
| Continuity | Patristic pattern | Aristotelian recovery |
| Treatment of texts | Whole, prayed | Piecemeal: *sententiae* extracted, grouped as *quaestiones* |

The 12th-century turning point: rise of the universities → **bifurcation of biblical exegesis and systematic speculation**. Scholasticism deploys the **empirico-metaphysical method**, aided by the newly recovered Aristotle. Peter Lombard's *Book of Sentences* is the emblematic product.

## 3. Thomas Aquinas (13th c.)

- Perfects scholasticism by ordering theology **pedagogically** (*ordo disciplinae* / *via disciplinae*) — arranged for best learning, not for the order of discovery.
- Distinguishes **way of discovery** (from sense data toward causes; what is first *for us*) from **way of teaching** (from revealed principles to explanation; what is first *in itself*).
- On mysteries: uses **analogy**, not proof. The *psychological analogy* compares the divine procession to the procession of a concept from an act of human understanding — yielding fruitful but limited understanding.

## 4. Reformation and biblical humanism (15th–16th c.)

- **Valla and Erasmus** attack scholasticism's dialectic and abstract metaphysics: it makes theology into philosophy.
- Slogan: ***ad fontes*** — back to the Greek New Testament and the Fathers.
- **Grammatical-historical exegesis** becomes the normative theological method; the Reformers adopt it, making exegesis of Scripture the central theological task.
- Goal shifts: not solving frivolous logical questions but **moving God's people to piety and Christian living**.

## 5. Post-Tridentine manuals and neo-scholasticism (19th–early 20th c.)

- 19th-c. Catholic theology splits: **traditionalists** (revelation alone) vs. those accommodating modern philosophy.
- **Leo XIII (1879)** backs the Aristotelian-Thomist restoration → **neo-scholasticism**.
- Result in practice: rigid, deductive **textbook theology**, presenting itself as timeless truth while ignoring modern philosophical and historical insight.

## 6. Enlightenment and historical consciousness (18th–19th c.)

- The scientific revolution shatters the static worldview.
- **Historical-critical method replaces allegory**: Bible and dogma become evolving historical products rather than eternal dictates.
- Theology is pushed from an *a priori*, deductive method toward an **empirical, inductive** one based on experience and history.

> [!warning] The methodological crisis
> Once sources become *data* rather than *premises*, every subsequent method in this list is an attempt to answer: **how can criticised history still be normative?**

## 7. Schleiermacher (19th c.)

- Pioneers the **experiential / relational method**: theology as the analysis of religious consciousness — the **feeling of absolute dependence**.
- God is known **only in relation to the believer**, not in a hidden internal essence; divine attributes are modes of divine causality experienced in human life.
- Bridges historical criticism and belief by making theology an *indirect expression* of pious experience.

## 8. Barth and dialectical theology (20th c.)

- After WWI, **neo-orthodoxy** rejects Schleiermacher's turn to experience and culture.
- Stresses the **"strange otherness"** of God and the infinite qualitative distinction between divine and human.
- **Pneumatic exegesis**: all human thought stands under divine judgement; revelation breaks in from the transcendent rather than emerging from historical probability.
- Organises the *kerygma* in biblical/classical terms without correlating it to secular philosophy.

## 9. Tillich (20th c.)

- **Apologetic theology** via the **method of correlation**: existential questions of the human situation ↔ answers of the Christian message.
- Interprets religious and eschatological symbols **ontologically**, making them speak to psychological and cultural crisis.

## 10. Rahner and Lonergan (20th c.)

Both integrate modern philosophy and historical consciousness with the Thomistic tradition.

- **Rahner** — critiques the isolation of Trinitarian theology; *"the economic Trinity is the immanent Trinity"*; roots theology in the human experience of God's self-communication.
- **Lonergan** — *Method in Theology* (1972) executes the **turn to the subject**: theology grounded in experiencing, understanding, judging, deciding, worked out as **eight functional specialties** in two phases, pivoting on **conversion**.

## 11. Vatican II, liberation, contextual theology (late 20th c.)

- **Vatican II** shaped by *nouvelle théologie* and ***ressourcement*** — return to biblical, patristic and liturgical sources, plus a theology of history and patristic typology (Daniélou, de Lubac), breaking neo-scholastic rigidity.
- **Liberation theology** (Latin America): theological method must include **socio-economic analysis of systemic oppression**; inductive rhythm of **praxis — see, judge, act**; preferential option for the poor.
- **Theology of the people / contextual theology** (influencing Pope Francis): evangelisation of culture, wisdom of popular piety, lived reality of the people over abstract conceptual elaboration.

## 12. Postmodern and postliberal turns (late 20th–21st c.)

- Postmodernity (Lyotard) rejects universal reason and grand meta-narratives; knowledge is historically contingent and tied to power.
- **Lindbeck's cultural-linguistic model**: religions are distinct language games that *shape* experience; doctrine functions as grammar.
- **J.Z. Smith**: religion is not a static grid but constant **negotiation** — uncoupling and recoupling inherited elements for new contexts.

## Comparative synthesis
| Era | Where authority sits | Chief operation | Chief risk |
|-----|---------------------|-----------------|------------|
| Patristic | Rule of faith + allegory | Deduce from insight | Uncontrolled speculation |
| Scholastic | Authoritative texts | Dialectic on *quaestiones* | Divorce from piety |
| Humanist | Original text | Philology | Anti-speculative thinness |
| Neo-scholastic | Manual + Magisterium | Deduction | Ahistoricism |
| Critical/modern | Historical evidence | Induction | Naturalism |
| Experiential | Consciousness | Analysis of feeling | Subjectivism |
| Dialectical | The Word | Proclamation | Isolation from culture |
| Transcendental | Authentic subject | Functional specialisation | Complexity, elitism |
| Liberationist | The suffering poor | Praxis spiral | Ideological capture |
| Postliberal | Community grammar | Rule-analysis | Relativism, incommensurability |

## Flashcards
Origen's method :: Authoritarian-deductive — authoritative tradition, noetic insight into general truths, deduction of details, scriptural confirmation
Irenaeus's methodological limit :: We know *that* God acted through revelation without needing to know *how* or *why* — clear scriptural witness over speculative causation
12th-century turning point :: Rise of universities; exegesis separates from speculative theology; sententiae organised into quaestiones
Aquinas: via inventionis vs via disciplinae :: Way of discovery (from sense data to causes, first for us) vs way of teaching (from revealed principles, first in itself)
Ad fontes :: Renaissance humanist slogan — return to the original sources (Greek NT, Fathers) rather than secondary digests
1879 significance :: Leo XIII endorses the Aristotelian-Thomist method — neo-scholastic restoration
What historical consciousness did to theology :: Turned Scripture and dogma from absolute premises into historical data needing critical investigation
Schleiermacher's foundation :: The feeling of absolute dependence; God known only in relation to the believer
Barth's core claim :: The strange otherness of God; revelation breaks in from beyond, not from historical probability or culture
Rahner's Rule :: The economic Trinity is the immanent Trinity and vice versa
Ressourcement :: Renewal of Catholic theology by returning to biblical, patristic and liturgical sources rather than neo-scholastic manuals
Lindbeck's model :: Cultural-linguistic — religion as comprehensive framework, doctrine as its grammar
