# 10a — Karl Rahner: A Deep Dive

Rahner gets one entry in [[10 - Major Theologians and Their Methods#Karl Rahner (1904–1984)]] because he stands in the same list as Clement and Packer. But **twelve of the thirteen sources in the notebook are about him alone** — the Reeves volume is only one source among many. This note works that Rahner corpus properly: the machinery under the slogans.

> [!abstract] The one-sentence core
> Rahner asks the *transcendental* question — **what must already be true of the human subject for revelation to be receivable at all?** — and answers: the human being is *spirit-in-world*, an unlimited openness (*Vorgriff*) to being, always already graced (the supernatural existential), and therefore the whole of theology can be re-said as anthropology carried to its radical end.

---

## 1. Formation and context

| | |
|---|---|
| **Born** | Freiburg, 1904; enters the Society of Jesus, 1922 |
| **Formation** | Ignatian spirituality — the *Exercises* as the matrix, not decoration; a pastoral concern for the ordinary believer runs under every speculative page |
| **Philosophical lineage** | Transcendental Thomism via **Pierre Rousselot** and **Joseph Maréchal**; Aquinas read through **Kant's** transcendental method |
| **Heidegger** | Attended his seminars at Freiburg; takes over a phenomenological style of questioning and *Dasein* (historicity, being-in-the-world) |
| **The famous rejection** | His doctoral director **Martin Honecker rejected *Spirit in the World*** as too Heideggerian. The rejected thesis became his most influential philosophical book |
| **What he was breaking out of** | Desiccated **neo-scholasticism** — the manual tradition (Karl Lehmann's assessment) |
| **What he was writing into** | The trauma of twentieth-century Europe and a Church whose believers were becoming a **diaspora** in a secular world (Paul Hypher) |

> [!tip] Read him as a pastor
> Hypher's point is the one students miss: Rahner is a **radical pastor-theologian**. The speculative apparatus exists to serve mystical and pastoral theology — to let a modern person recognise grace in an ordinary day. Cf. the "mysticism of everyday life" in [[10 - Major Theologians and Their Methods]].

## 2. *Vorgriff* and the limits of conceptual thought

> [!info] Definitions
> **Vorgriff** (pre-apprehension) — the dynamic, *unthematic* movement of the human spirit toward the infinite horizon of all possible objects (being as such, *esse*). It is the *a priori* condition that makes any human knowing possible.
> **Conversio ad phantasma** — Aquinas's "turn to the image": the intellect knows only by turning to sense data, so human knowledge is essentially **receptive** and tied to sensibility.
> **Abstraction** — the agent intellect grasps a sensibly intuited object *as limited* (form limited by matter) and so universalises it **against** the infinite horizon opened by the *Vorgriff*.

**The claim.** Knowledge is always the conjunction of a sensible singular with the infinite horizon of being. Therefore **the concept is relativised**: it is only ever a limited objectification, an approximation of the subject's original unified self-presence, and it must keep referring back to the concrete singular to stay intelligible. This is why Rahner can say a dogmatic formula is a *beginning*, not an end.

| Critic | Charge | Reply |
|---|---|---|
| **Patrick J. Burke** (1994) | The stress on the dynamic, existential order **relativises conceptual thought to the point of threatening its validity**; by neglecting the formal (essential) element of the intellect, concepts shrink into subordinate parts of a judgment | — |
| **Karen Kilby** (as reported by Ryan Duns) | The argument for the *Vorgriff* **equivocates on "limitation"**: limitation of *form by matter* is not the same as an object's merely *not being everything* | **Duns** answers that the objection ignores Rahner's **analogical** understanding of being, in which the two senses are related, not identical |

## 3. The supernatural existential and concupiscence

> [!info] Definitions
> **Supernatural existential** — humanity is universally and permanently ordained to a supernatural end. Grace is not an external add-on but an intrinsic, *ontological* "existential" characterising human reality **prior to any free decision**.
> **Concupiscence (Rahner's revision)** — not an evil inclination but a **pre-ethical, spontaneous conative (striving) potency**: the dualism between what a person *is* as **nature** (prior to existential decision) and what they *become* as **person** (through free decision).

**Ferrugia's exposition (2005).** Rahner breaks with the strictly negative Augustinian and Tridentine reading. Concupiscence is an *actus indeliberatus* — an indeliberate act that **precedes and resists** the free decision of the will — and so it can carry a person toward good as easily as toward evil. Because nature and person never coincide in this life, every attempt to make the whole self obey the free decision is **asymptotic**: always approaching, never arriving.

> [!tip] Why this matters for method
> Concupiscence stops the supernatural existential from becoming cheap optimism. Grace is universal *and* integration is never finished — which is exactly the anthropological ground of Rahner's insistence that the Church is a Church of sinners. See [[11 - Fault Lines and Lineages#2. Nature and grace]].

## 4. Christology — two of them

| | **From below (ascent)** | **From above (descent)** |
|---|---|---|
| **Starting point** | The historical Jesus as the culmination of the **evolutionary self-transcendence** of humanity | The Incarnation: only the **Logos** could become man |
| **Central move** | Jesus is the ultimate human **response** — obedience and love — to God's self-communication | The Logos' **self-emptying formally constitutes** his humanity |
| **Consciousness of Jesus** | *Visio immediata* (immediate vision of God) held together with *docta ignorantia* — learned ignorance, real growth, even an identity crisis | God is **immutable in himself but becomes mutable in the finite other** |

**McDermott's critique (1986)** — the standard one, and the one to quote in an exam:
- The two Christologies **do not coalesce**.
- The ascent Christology posits **two independent, free, conscious subjects** in Christ — the Logos and Jesus' human *Aktzentrum* (active centre) — and so drifts toward **Nestorianism or Arianism**.
- The descent Christology, if the Logos expresses *all* of Jesus' human acts, slips the other way into **Monophysitism**.
- Root cause: Rahner **lacks an adequate traditional concept of "person"** capable of unifying the two natures.

## 5. Death and the dying Christ

- Death is **not a biological full stop** but the definitive culmination of freedom, the point at which a person is totally withdrawn from themselves.
- **Life is a lifelong process of dying.**
- In death one final decision is forced: **cling in desperation to a fading autonomy, or let go** in faith and hope into the incomprehensible mystery of God.
- **The cross.** Jesus reaches the high point of his mission by total self-abandonment and perfect obedience — he lets himself **fall into the abyss of death, turning the cry of despair into blessed surrender** into God's incomprehensibility.
- **The payoff.** By dying, Christ redeems death itself; cross and resurrection make grace irreversible, so that our own dying can become a redeeming, life-creating act of self-abandonment.

## 6. The incomprehensibility of God — and his reading of Aquinas

- **God remains absolute, incomprehensible mystery *even in the beatific vision*.**
- Incomprehensibility is **not a defect** in our knowing and not a temporary limitation to be removed in heaven — it **is the beatifying experience itself**.
- Rahner grounds this in Aquinas's ***excessus***: the intellect's ultimate movement out toward infinite being.
- He then translates Aquinas for the modern mind: contemporary humanity is an **"unanswerable question"** facing a pluralism of sciences none of which delivers ultimate meaning.
- Beatitude is therefore not conceptual mastery of God but the **loving, worshipping capitulation of the intellect** into God's incomprehensibility.

Cross-reference: the apophatic strand in [[10 - Major Theologians and Their Methods#Thomas Aquinas (1224/26–1274)]] (Pseudo-Dionysius, see [[10b - Interlocutors Opponents and Heresies]]) and God-and-suffering in [[11 - Fault Lines and Lineages#6. God and suffering]].

## 7. Ecclesiology, Church as sacrament, anonymous Christianity

> [!info] Definitions
> **Church as *Ursakrament* (prime sacrament)** — the Church is the historical, eschatological **perceptibility and bodily presence** of Christ's victorious grace in the world.
> **Anonymous Christianity** — grace is universally offered and can be **implicitly accepted**: whoever loves the neighbour in radical selflessness has already accepted God's self-communication, whatever they call themselves.

The Church exists **not for itself** but as the vanguard and sacramental sign of a salvation God offers the whole world.

**Kilby's assessment (2009)** — nuanced, and worth reproducing exactly:
1. Critics dismiss Rahner as a **"liberal"** because he engages modern thought, but his ecclesiology in fact **undercuts the liberal/conservative divide**: he insists on the *theological necessity* of calling the Church both **worldly and sinful**.
2. "Anonymous Christianity" has become **so mainstream it has lost its novelty** — the scandal has worn off.
3. Her real doubt: Rahner predicted the Christian of the future would be a **solitary "everyday mystic"**. Kilby counters that it is **equally plausible** that a secularised world makes the Church *more* dependent on community and external ritual, not less.

## 8. Vatican II — what he actually did

- **Peritus** (theological expert) of the first rank; served on the **theological commission** preparing the pivotal texts.
- Documented contributions: ***Christus Dominus*** (bishops), ***Gaudium et Spes*** (Church in the modern world), and input into ***Lumen Gentium***.
- His own reading of the Council: a **"caesura"** — a break of the order of the Jerusalem-to-Gentiles transition — in which the Church for the first time **acted as a World Church** and stopped being a European cultural export.
