# 04 — Contextual, Liberation and Non-Western Methods

> [!abstract] The common thesis
> The Gospel is never delivered culture-free. **Incarnation is translation.** Therefore method must begin from a place, a people and their suffering — with a *crucified* rather than a *crusading* mind.

## 1. Kosuke Koyama (Japan / Thailand / Singapore)

- Method centres on **authentic contextualization** driven by a theology of the Incarnation.
- Demands the **"crucified mind"**, not the crusading, imperialistic mind.
- Rejects Western **"pure theology"** as an ideological danger and a form of theological imperialism that pushes aside the living God.
- Requires **epistemic humility and self-emptying**; the theologian moves toward the margins to meet the poor and to engage other traditions (notably Buddhism).

## 2. Kazoh Kitamori (Japan)

- **Theology of the Pain of God**: critiques Western **divine impassibility** — a God who cannot suffer cannot bear the sins of the world.
- Models a method **"from above to below"**: the universal Gospel must be mediated through particular cultural realities.
- With Koyama, deliberately uses **paradox and aestheticism** rather than Western propositional logic — held to be more faithful to Eastern wisdom traditions.

## 3. Shusaku Endō (Japan)

- Does theology **through literature**.
- Translates the Gospel into a Japanese idiom by emphasising a **maternal God** and a universally embracing, suffering Christ who shares human misery — resonant with cultural values of harmony and benevolence.

## 4. Juan Carlos Scannone & Pope Francis (Argentina) — *Teología del pueblo*

- Argentinian branch of liberation theology; the matrix of Francis's thought.
- **Inductive method: see–judge–act**, preferential option for the poor.
- **Distinctive**: unlike liberation theologies leaning on Marxist socio-economic analysis, it prioritises the **evangelisation of culture** and honours the **popular piety** and symbolic, cosmological expressions of the poor.

## 5. Rogate Mshana (Africa)

- **Transformative theology** confronting neo-liberal economic globalisation and eco-injustice.
- Draws on indigenous cosmo-visions: African ***Ubuntu*** ("I am because we are") and Asian ***Sangsaeng*** (a sharing community) to frame economic, social and ecological justice for the poor.

## 6. Critique of Western dominance — and its limit

- Global dominance of Western theology, carried by the **"linguistic juggernaut" of English**, imposes cognitive constraints on how faith can be articulated.
- True inculturation reads the **Incarnation as an act of translation**, requiring local idiom.
- **But**: Koyama and Kitamori do *not* call for wholesale rejection of Western theology. They call for **mutual correction** — Western and non-Western theologies listening to and enriching one another.

> [!tip] Balanced exam line
> "Contextual method is not anti-Western; it is anti-*absolutised*-Western. Its watchword is mutual correction, its posture kenosis, its guard-rails the canon and the catholic community."

## 7. Guard-rails against relativism

Contextual constructions are assessed against **two ultimate criteria**:
1. the **biblical canon**;
2. the **global ecclesial community** (catholicity).
