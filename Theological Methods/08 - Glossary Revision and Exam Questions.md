# 08 — Glossary, Revision and Exam Questions

## A. Glossary

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| ***Fides quaerens intellectum*** | Faith seeking understanding — inquiry grounded in faith, seeking coherent understanding of the mysteries without claiming absolute rational proof |
| ***Ad fontes*** | "To the sources" — Renaissance humanist call to return to original texts (Greek NT, Fathers) rather than secondary digests |
| ***Sic et non*** | "Yes and no" — scholastic laying-out and evaluation of conflicting arguments and doubtful solutions before resolving a problem |
| ***Quaestio*** | A specific issue extracted from texts and organised logically; the basic unit of scholastic disputation |
| ***Sententiae*** | Statements extracted piecemeal from authorities and compiled into anthologies by issue |
| ***Ordo / via disciplinae*** | The way of teaching: presenting matter in the order best for learning (vs. the way of discovery) |
| ***Ressourcement*** | Renewal of Catholic theology by returning to biblical, patristic and liturgical sources instead of neo-scholastic manuals |
| **Hermeneutical circle** | The dynamic by which the final stage of theological communication produces present realities that become the historical data for the next generation's research |
| **Horizon** | The structured boundary or perspective within which meaning, knowing and choosing occur; a radical shift of horizon requires conversion |
| **Conversion** | (Lonergan) A radical vertical shift from one horizon to another, transforming experiencing, understanding, judging and deciding — religious, moral, intellectual, psychic |
| **Functional specialty** | Lonergan's eightfold division of theological tasks based on distinct operations of consciousness |
| ***Epoché*** | Phenomenological suspension of judgement permitting impartial, attentive listening and unbiased description of religious phenomena |
| **Eidetic vision** | The search, after *epoché*, for the underlying essence or structure of a religious phenomenon |
| ***Praxis*** | An action-reflection rhythm aimed at intentional transformative action, especially changing oppressive social structures |
| ***Kenosis*** | Self-emptying; absolute self-sacrifice — central to Christian understanding of divine love and to the contextual theologian's posture |
| ***Lectio difficilior*** | Text-critical rule: prefer the more difficult reading, since scribes smooth texts |
| **Stratigraphic method** | Excavating the chronological layers of texts (Crossan) to reconstruct the web of historical conflict |
| **Cultural-linguistic model** | Lindbeck: religion as a comprehensive cultural framework, doctrine as its grammar |
| **Hermeneutical privilege of the victims** | Liberation theology's principle granting interpretive priority to the marginalised |
| **Ubuntu / Sangsaeng** | African "I am because we are" / Asian "sharing community" — cosmo-visions used in transformative eco-justice theology |
| **Transcendental precepts** | Be attentive, be intelligent, be reasonable, be responsible |

> [!note] Terms *not* defined in these sources
> ***Aggiornamento*** and ***loci theologici*** are not explicitly defined anywhere in the 46 sources — supply definitions from other reading if you use them.

## B. One-page revision sheet

> [!abstract] Definition to memorise
> Method = **a normative pattern of recurrent and related operations yielding cumulative and progressive results** (Lonergan). It examines the **bases, ordering and norms** of theology.

**Method ≠ technique ≠ system.** Technique is mechanical; system is the dated end-product; method is the open, self-correcting process producing systems.

**Four precepts:** attentive → intelligent → reasonable → responsible.

**Eight specialties:** Research · Interpretation · History · Dialectics ‖ *conversion* ‖ Foundations · Doctrines · Systematics · Communications.

**Two starting points:** anthropological ↔ revelatory.

**Three imbalances:** experience→mysticism · history→false naturalism · reason→rationalism.

**Two opposite failures:** methodological imperialism (scientism) ↔ irrationalism (sectarianism).

**Correlation trio:** finitude→God · despair→the Christ · history→Kingdom.

**Praxis trio:** see · judge · act.

**Comparative five:** epoché · agreement · impartial terms · comparison · synthesis.

**Inculturation four:** kenosis · from above to below · dialogue and mutual correction · canon + catholicity.

**Student's four errors:** short-circuit · illusion of neutrality · impatience · lost humility.

**Four functions of a good method:** rigour · cumulative progress · self-correction · fidelity (through conversion).

## C. Exam questions

### Short answer (5 marks)
1. Define theological method and distinguish it from a theological system.
2. State the four transcendental precepts and the operation each governs.
3. What is *epoché*? In which method does it function, and why?
4. Explain the "short-circuit" fallacy with one example.
5. What are the two guard-rails that keep inculturation from relativism?
6. Distinguish *via inventionis* from *via disciplinae* in Aquinas.
7. What did Irenaeus's method refuse to speculate about, and why?

### Essay (15–20 marks)
1. "Discord between methods is graver than opposition between doctrines." Discuss with reference to at least three methods.
2. Compare the scholastic and biblical-humanist methods. Which criticisms of each still hold today?
3. Expound Lonergan's eight functional specialties and evaluate the standard criticism that the distinction between *Doctrines* and *Systematics* is unworkable.
4. "Objectivity is the fruit of authentic subjectivity." Explain and assess this claim as a foundation for theological method.
5. Assess Tillich's method of correlation, including Taubes's charge that it drifts toward a theology of immanence.
6. How did historical consciousness change theological method? Trace the consequences from the Enlightenment to Vatican II.
7. Evaluate the claim that contextual method is anti-Western, using Koyama and Kitamori.
8. On what criteria should a theologian choose a method? Illustrate with two concrete theological tasks.
9. "Methods can and must be combined." Discuss with reference to Lonergan's pipeline and Yoder's *ad hoc* mixing.
10. What would an Indian theological method require that these sources do not supply?
