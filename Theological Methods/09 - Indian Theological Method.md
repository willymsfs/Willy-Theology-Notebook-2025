# 09 — Indian Theological Method

> [!info] Why this note exists
> The 46 sources behind notes 01–08 carry **no Indian material** (their "Asian" is East Asian). This note fills the gap from two other notebooks: *An Introduction to Theology: Indian Theological Series* and *George Soares Prabhu: A Biblical Theology for India*. Cite these, not the note-01–08 bibliography, for anything below.

> [!abstract] The thesis
> Indian theological method is a **hermeneutical process incarnating the Gospel within a millennia-old civilization** — integrating its cultures, philosophies and socio-economic realities. It splits into two great streams that criticise each other: **inculturation** (from the Indian mind) and **Dalit/tribal liberation** (from the Indian wound).

---

## 1. Beyond the translation model

| Model | What it does | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **Translation** | Renders Western doctrine using upper-caste Hindu terms | Rejected as artificial |
| **True inculturation** | Natural, spontaneous — the Church immersing itself in the concrete lives, struggles and languages of the people | The goal |

Indian theology embraces the **cosmotheandric vision**: cosmic, divine and human are inseparable dimensions of one reality.

## 2. *Anubhava* — experience as starting point

- Many Indian theologians prioritise direct spiritual **experience (*anubhava*)** over abstract doctrine.
- Fostered by the **Christian Ashram movement**: centres of inner dialogue integrating *jnana* (wisdom), *bhakti* (devotion) and *nishkamakarma* (selfless action) into Christian spirituality.
- Compare with the anthropological starting point in [[01 - What Is Theological Method and Why It Matters#5. Method and the sources of theology]] — but here experience is *contemplative and non-dual*, not existential-analytic as in Tillich.

## 3. Dialogue with Hindu philosophy

| Path | Use in Christian theology |
|---|---|
| ***Advaita*** (non-duality) | Vedantic categories translate Christian mystery — the Trinity as ***Saccidananda*** (Being–Consciousness–Bliss) |
| ***Bhakti*** (devotion) | South Indian devotional tradition grounds warm personal devotion and absolute surrender to God's love |
| ***Yoga*** | Chenchiah: integral yoga as the way into the New Creation |

## 4. Dalit theological method — the counter-move

> [!warning] The critique that reframes everything
> Christian Dalits **reject classical Indian philosophical categories**, recognising them as the language of the upper castes used to maintain superiority. Inculturation into *Sanskritic* Hinduism is, on this reading, inculturation into the very system that oppresses them.

Dalit method instead:
1. Starts from the **"little traditions"** — Dalit myths, folklore, and the lived experience of oppression.
2. Centres the **Crucified Jesus**, who gives meaning in suffering and power to revolt against injustice.
3. Holds the **Risen Christ** as hope for a new, egalitarian society.

**Tribal / Adivasi theology** runs parallel: marginalised indigenous groups struggling against displacement and exploitation; God's voice resounds specially in the cries of the marginalised.

## 5. Indian liberation method

- Operates on the **epistemological privilege of the poor**: God is uniquely present among the oppressed, who therefore hold a better vantage point for knowing God.
- Uses **social sciences** to analyse root causes of poverty and caste oppression.
- Interprets the Bible from the standpoint of the condemned and destitute.

Compare the **hermeneutical privilege of the victims** in Latin American method — [[03 - Catalogue of Theological Methods#6. Praxis / Liberation]].

## 6. George M. Soares-Prabhu — a worked Indian method

**Critique of the historical-critical method:** it claims universal objectivity while being culturally conditioned, overly academic, and ideologically biased toward capitalist values. **Yet indispensable** — it anchors the text historically and prevents fanciful, subjective readings.

**Read with two eyes:**
- the **Indian situation** — poverty and caste;
- the **Indian mind** — inclusive, cosmocentric, context-sensitive.

**Three-dimensional reading:** religious · social · intertextual.

**Hermeneutical circle (after Juan Luis Segundo), four steps:**

```mermaid
flowchart LR
    A[1 New experience of reality] --> B[2 Ideological suspicion]
    B --> C[3 Exegetical suspicion]
    C --> D[4 New interpretation of the Bible]
    D -.-> A
```

**Indian categories he uses:**
- ***Dharma*** — the complex of religious insight and ethical value in Jesus' teaching, above all the Sermon on the Mount ("the dharma of Jesus").
- ***Dhvani*** (resonating meaning) and ***vyanjana*** (suggested meaning) — classical Indian literary concepts for grasping the inspired sense of scripture.
- ***Sannyasa*** acknowledged — but he **critiques modern Christian ashrams as elitist** and disconnected from the struggles of the poor.

**The defining triad of the Indian context:** poverty · pluriform religiosity · the caste system.

**Inculturation** is not superficial translation or adaptation of customs but a **conversion** that changes fundamental attitudes and worldviews. **Dialogue** must move beyond exclusivism to genuine recognition of other religions as ways of salvation.

**Order of operations:** theology is always a ***second step***, following intense spiritual discipline and committed pursuit of social *and* personal liberation.

> [!tip] Exam-grade line
> For Soares-Prabhu the historical-critical method is *necessary but not sufficient*: it disciplines the text, while the Indian situation and the Indian mind supply the questions that Western exegesis never thought to ask.

## 7. Who's who — Indian theologians and their distinctive move

| Theologian | Distinctive move |
|---|---|
| **Brahmabandhab Upadhyaya** | Nationalist *sannyasi*; uses *Advaita Vedanta* to make Christian faith intelligible to the Indian mind |
| **A.S. Appasamy** | *Bhakti* tradition; personal God; distinguishes the divine persons via *Sat–Chit–Ananda* |
| **P. Chenchiah** | Christocentric; rejects organised Christianity as a threat to Indian heritage; Christ as **New Creation** through integral yoga |
| **Vengal Chakkarai** | Begins from experience of Christ as ***avatara***; links Christology to Pneumatology — the Spirit as the continuing living Christ |
| **Swami Abhishiktananda** | Experiential *advaitic* approach; enter the **cave of the heart**; conceptual definitions limit the mystery |
| **Vandana Mataji & Sara Grant** | Ashram theology; contemplative "theology from the heart," experiential and intuitive |
| **George M. Soares-Prabhu & Samuel Rayan** | Biblical scholars building an Indian liberation theology by reading Scripture through poverty and caste |
| **Sebastian Kappen** | Marxism + activism + dialogue; Jesus' prophetic protest in continuity with Indian dissent (Buddha, *bhakti*) |
| **Raimundo Panikkar** | Deep cultural-philosophical engagement with Indian traditions plus humanisation and secularity |
| **A.P. Nirmal** | Dalit theology (present in the source's bibliography only — supplement from his own writings) |

## 8. Placing Indian method beside the Western methods

| Western method | Indian counterpart / correction |

| Correlation ([[05 - How to Use the Methods - Step by Step#3. Tillich's Method of Correlation]]) | The questions come from poverty, caste and pluralism, not existential anxiety |
| Liberation see-judge-act ([[05 - How to Use the Methods - Step by Step#5. See – Judge – Act (the pastoral spiral)]]) | Retained, with caste analysis added to class analysis |
| Inculturation ([[04 - Contextual, Liberation and Non-Western Methods]]) | Split by the Dalit critique: whose culture is being inculturated into? |
| Comparative theology ([[05 - How to Use the Methods - Step by Step#7. Comparative Theology (Burtt's cooperative procedure)]]) | Dialogue as recognition of other religions as ways of salvation, grounded in *anubhava* |
