> [!abstract] What makes a theologian "major"
> Not output but paradigm. A major theologian **addresses the deepest cultural and existential crisis of the era and crafts a paradigm that shapes what comes after** — either becoming standard orthodoxy or posing so massive a challenge that later generations must define themselves for or against them.

## I. Apostolic Fathers and Apologists (1st–2nd c.)

### Clement of Rome (wrote c. AD 95)
Apostolic Father, third Bishop of Rome · ***1 Clement***
**Central idea** — Church unity, apostolic succession, ecclesiastical order.
**Method** — Pastoral and authoritative, driven by a practical crisis (elders ousted at Corinth). Grounds order in God's commissioning of Christ and the apostles; leverages early scriptural texts to enforce obedience and humility.

### Ignatius of Antioch (†c. 110)
Bishop of Antioch · Seven letters
**Central idea** — Episcopal unity (the bishop represents Christ); the physical reality of the Incarnation against Docetism; martyrdom as the ultimate imitation of Christ.
**Method** — Written hastily under extreme duress on the road to martyrdom. **Intensely experiential and pastoral**, reacting viscerally to Docetism and Judaizing. Links physical realities (flesh, Eucharist, bishop) directly to spiritual truths: if Christ had no real flesh, his own impending martyrdom is meaningless.

### Polycarp of Smyrna (†c. 155)
Bishop of Smyrna · *Letter to the Philippians*
**Central idea** — Orthodoxy as the foundation of moral behaviour.
**Method** — **Pragmatic and disciplinary**: mild with moral failings, severe against doctrinal corruption ("the firstborn of Satan") — right doctrine drives right practice.

### Justin Martyr (c. 100–c. 165)
Apologist, converted Platonist · *First* and *Second Apology*, *Dialogue with Trypho*
**Central idea** — Christianity as the "true philosophy"; Christ pre-existent and foretold; defence of the Trinity.
**Method** — **Philosophical apologetics.** Synthesises Greek education with Christian revelation; starts from common ground. To pagans: the *Logos* they dimly recognise is fully incarnate in Christ. To Jews: Christological exegesis of the Old Testament.
→ ancestor of the **method of correlation** ([[03 - Catalogue of Theological Methods#3. Method of Correlation]]).

### Irenaeus of Lyons (c. 130–c. 202)
Bishop of Lyons · *Against Heresies*, *Demonstration of the Apostolic Preaching*
**Central idea** — **Recapitulation** (Christ the second Adam undoing the fall); unity of the two Testaments; salvation of the flesh.
**Method** — First architect of a coherent systematic theology. **Strictly canonical and holistic**: *Scripture can only be understood by Scripture*. Against Gnostics who cherry-pick verses like tesserae in a mosaic, he traces the **grand plan** from creation to consummation, proving Creator and Saviour are one God. Also sets the limit on speculation — we know *that* God acted, not always *how* ([[02 - Historical Development of Theological Method#1. Patristic era (2nd–5th c.)]]).
**Criticism** — Dismissed for a blundering, confused, "barbarous" style.

## II. Patristic era (4th–5th c.)

### Athanasius (c. 296/298–373)
Bishop of Alexandria · *Against the Heathen*, *On the Incarnation*, *Against the Arians*, *Life of Antony*
**Central idea** — Eternal deity of the Son (*homoousios*); Incarnation as deification, bringing humanity into the communion of the Godhead; defeat of death.
**Method** — **Soteriologically driven polemics.** Starting point is the reality of salvation: if the Son is a creature, he cannot save or bring us to the Father. Refuses to let abstract philosophical definitions of God (e.g. "ungenerate") dictate theology — we know God *through the Son*, and a true Father requires an eternal Son. Pastorally flexible on terminology so long as full deity is protected.

### Augustine of Hippo (354–430)
Bishop of Hippo · *Confessions*, *On the Trinity*, *City of God*, *On Christian Doctrine*, *On the Spirit and the Letter*
**Central idea** — Original sin; the absolute necessity of grace; freedom governed by what we love; the Church as a mixed body; predestination.
**Method** — **Introspective, psychological, polemical.** Begins from his own life and memory to understand identity and grace (*Confessions*); synthesises Neoplatonic motifs (the soul's journey to rest) with revelation; the mature work is hammered out defensively against heresies, sharpening the claim that the will cannot choose God without the prior gift of the Spirit.
**Criticism** — Neoplatonism unduly shapes his exegesis and theology of love.

## III. Medieval scholasticism (11th–13th c.)

### Anselm of Canterbury (1033–1109)
Benedictine, Archbishop of Canterbury · *Monologion*, *Proslogion*, *Cur Deus Homo*
**Central idea** — Ontological argument (*that than which nothing greater can be conceived*); satisfaction theory of atonement.
**Method** — ***Fides quaerens intellectum* by unaided reason**, in a monastic, meditative mode. Characteristic move: prove Christian doctrine by pure logic *without* leaning on Scripture or earlier authorities, on the premise that God is Reason itself and so Christian truth must be defensible to any logical mind.
**Criticism** — Binds God to logical necessity; frames salvation as a harsh, conditional transaction.

### Thomas Aquinas (1224/26–1274)
Dominican · *Summa contra Gentiles*, *Summa Theologiae*, biblical commentaries
**Central idea** — The Five Ways; nature and grace; transubstantiation; Christian Aristotelianism.
**Method** — **Scholastic dialectic and synthesis**, structured on university debate: question → objections → *sed contra* → "I answer" → replies. Reason and natural knowledge form a reliable foundation on which revelation builds; balanced by **apophatic** restraint — easier to say what God is *not*. See the procedure in [[05 - How to Use the Methods - Step by Step#1. The Scholastic Quaestio]].
**Criticism** — Leans too heavily on pagan philosophy; a "theology of glory" (Luther).

## IV. Reformation (16th c.)

### Martin Luther (1483–1546)
Augustinian friar, German Reformer · *95 Theses*, *Heidelberg Disputation*, *Babylonian Captivity*, *Freedom of a Christian*, *Bondage of the Will*
**Central idea** — Justification by faith alone; **theology of the cross**; bondage of the will; priesthood of all believers.
**Method** — **Paradoxical, polemical, fiercely biblical.** Rejects natural theology and scholastic reason ("the Devil's whore"). God is known *only* in the crucified Christ, which shatters human pride. Hermeneutic: canonicity tested by whether a book "teaches Christ."
**Criticism** — Vitriolic polemic, coarseness, extreme paradox.

### John Calvin (1509–1564)
French Reformer, Geneva · *Institutes*, Geneva Catechism, Commentaries
**Central idea** — Sovereignty of God; double predestination; imputed righteousness; marks of the true Church; the *extra Calvinisticum*.
**Method** — **Systematic and accommodative**, working as a *doctor* of the Church equipping pastors and laity to read the Bible rightly. Splits the task in two tracks — **exegesis in the Commentaries, doctrine in the Institutes** — and structures the latter on the Apostles' Creed. Doctrine of **accommodation**: God "lisps," using baby-talk to finite creatures. Heavy debt to Augustine.
**Criticism** — Caricatured as a cantankerous dictator; the chill of reprobation.

## V. Puritanism and early evangelicalism (17th–18th c.)

### John Owen (1616–1683)
English Puritan, Congregationalist · *Communion with God*, *Theologoumena Pantodapa*, *Justification by Faith*, *Christologia*, *Pneumatologia*
**Central idea** — Five-point Calvinism; covenant theology; distinct communion with each divine person; imputed active righteousness of Christ.
**Method** — **Exhaustive, exegetical, strictly Trinitarian.** Divides all theology under Christology and Pneumatology; weaves dense exegesis with historical theology; applies doctrine directly to **the affections** — truth rolled through the mind to steer the soul's desires. Theology is inherently practical and doxological.

### Jonathan Edwards (1703–1758)
American Puritan/Calvinist · *Religious Affections*, *Freedom of the Will*, *Original Sin*, *History of the Work of Redemption*
**Central idea** — True religion consists in holy affections; the will chooses what it loves but cannot naturally love God; continuous creation; the end of creation is God's glory.
**Method** — **Enlightenment tools in service of Calvinist orthodoxy**: natural science and psychological observation deployed to defend Reformed doctrine. Against later liberals, holy affections *arise from* true doctrine (contrast Schleiermacher). Reads all history and Scripture chronologically as the continuous redemptive work of Christ.

## VI. Modern theology (19th–20th c.)

### Friedrich Schleiermacher (1768–1834)
Prussian Reformed; "Father of Liberal Protestantism" · *On Religion: Speeches to Its Cultured Despisers*, *The Christian Faith*, *Brief Outline*
**Central idea** — Religion as the consciousness of being **absolutely dependent**; Christ as the man of perfect God-consciousness; rejection of miracles and the devil; God as impersonal/non-relational.
**Method** — **A Copernican revolution centred on religious experience.** As an apologist to the Enlightenment, he insulates theology from scientific and historical attack by making inner experience the source of truth; doctrines are secondary, cultural expressions of religious affections. See [[03 - Catalogue of Theological Methods#13. Relational / Experiential]].
**Criticism** — Reduces God to an impersonal force (pantheism); evacuates objective historical truth by dissolving doctrine into feeling.

### Karl Barth (1886–1968)
Swiss Reformed; dialectical theology / neo-orthodoxy · *Commentary on Romans*, *Church Dogmatics*
**Central idea** — The infinite qualitative distinction between God and man; the threefold Word of God (revealed, written, preached); Christ as both electing God and elected/rejected man.
**Method** — **Christocentric, assertive, dialectical.** Vehemently rejects natural theology — any human route to God by reason or inner religion. Starting point: the self-revealing Trinity. Does dogmatics **as preaching**: massive, repetitive, "sermonic," aesthetic rather than argumentative — playing on revelation so its truth and beauty shine. Ethics must flow from the doctrine of grace, never be derived abstractly.
**Criticism** — Impenetrable bulk; rejection of natural theology; suspicion of latent universalism.

### Karl Rahner (1904–1984)
Jesuit; transcendental Thomism · *Spirit in the World*, *Hearers of the Word*, *Theological Investigations*, *Foundations of Christian Faith*
**Central idea** — **Anonymous Christianity**; the **supernatural existential** (humanity always already oriented to grace); the economic Trinity *is* the immanent Trinity; Jesus as absolute bringer of salvation in an evolutionary world.
**Method** — **Transcendental, anthropological, mystagogical.** Blends Thomism with Kant and Heidegger. Starting point: the human subject as **spirit-in-world**, always questioning, open to infinite mystery. Asks for the ***a priori* conditions of possibility in the subject** that make reception of revelation possible; begins from the facticity of revelation and works back to the hearer. *"Anthropology in its most radical sense is theology."* Unites speculative dogma with the **mysticism of everyday life**.
**Criticism** — Subjectivises being (Gilson, Maritain); "anonymous Christian" undermines the necessity of Church and mission; an underdeveloped notion of personhood in Christology drew charges of near-Nestorianism or Arianism.

### J.I. Packer (b. 1926)
Anglican evangelical, "modern Puritan" · *Knowing God*, *'Fundamentalism' and the Word of God*, *A Quest for Godliness*
**Central idea** — Penal substitutionary atonement; biblical inerrancy; mortifying sin by changing the heart's desires.
**Method** — **Catechetical and historically rooted.** Calls himself a "theologizer," not an academic pioneer: translates classic theology (Owen, the Puritans, Luther) into accessible doxological instruction for the laity. Theology as **"water engineering"** — occasional polemic simply to clear the sewage so the Church can drink clean.

## VII. Since 1918 — the modern field

This note stops at Packer because its source notebook did. The **whole post-1918 field** — Bonhoeffer, de Lubac, Balthasar, Pannenberg, Moltmann, Torrance, the Niebuhrs, Lindbeck, Frei, Jüngel, Jenson, Gunton, Lonergan, Schillebeeckx, Küng, Marion, Milbank, Teilhard, Weil, Ruether, Cone, Gutiérrez, Sobrino, Bediako, Oduyoye, Pieris, Panikkar, Koyama, Kitamori, Ahn Byung Mu, Sugirtharajah, Bulgakov, Lossky, Florovsky, Zizioulas, Hick, D'Costa and the rest — is now covered in its own unit, built from Ford and Muers, *The Modern Theologians: Christian Theology Since 1918*.

> [!tip] Where to go
> - **The roster** — [[13 - Master Roster of Modern Theologians]]: every figure with dates, tradition, central concept, significance and use today.
> - **The map** — [[Modern Theologians Since 1918 - Course Home]]: Ford's five types, the eight parts, competency checklist.
> - **The three figures shared with this note** (Barth, Tillich, Rahner) are treated at greater length there, in the context of what came after them: [[02 - Classics of the Twentieth Century]].

**Ford's five types** are the modern counterpart of this note's method index: they classify a theologian not by the operations they run but by **how they relate Christian identity to modernity** — Type 2 priority to Christian self-description (Barth, Bonhoeffer, de Lubac, Balthasar), Type 3 correlation (Tillich), Type 4 integration through a modern philosophy or issue (Bultmann, Pannenberg), with Types 1 and 5 as the limit cases. See [[01 - What Makes Theology Modern - Ford's Map]].

## Flashcards
What makes a theologian "major" :: Addressing the deepest crisis of the era with a paradigm that shapes all later thought — as orthodoxy or as the challenge others must answer
Irenaeus's hermeneutical rule :: Scripture can only be understood by Scripture; against Gnostic cherry-picking he traces the grand plan from creation to consummation
Recapitulation :: Christ as the second Adam historically undoing the fall (Irenaeus)
Athanasius's starting point :: Soteriology — if the Son is a creature he cannot save us or bring us into communion with the Father
Anselm's characteristic move :: Prove Christian doctrine by unaided logic, without appeal to Scripture or earlier authorities, since God is Reason itself
Aquinas's Summa structure :: Question, objections, sed contra, "I answer", replies to objections
Luther on reason :: "The Devil's whore" — natural theology breeds pride; God is known only in the crucified Christ
Luther's canon test :: Whether a book "teaches Christ"
Calvin's two-track method :: Exegesis in the Commentaries, systematic doctrine in the Institutes
Accommodation (Calvin) :: God "lisps" — uses baby-talk suited to finite human capacity
Owen's division of theology :: Under two heads, Christology and Pneumatology, applied to the affections
Edwards vs Schleiermacher on affections :: Edwards — holy affections arise from true doctrine; Schleiermacher — doctrine is an expression of affections
Schleiermacher's Copernican revolution :: Making inner religious experience, not external revelation, the source of theological knowledge
Barth's threefold Word of God :: Revealed (Christ), written (Scripture), preached
Barth's method style :: Dogmatics done as preaching — assertive, aesthetic, sermonic rather than argumentative
Rahner's supernatural existential :: Human nature is always already graced and dynamically oriented to God; grace is not an add-on
Rahner's transcendental question :: What a priori conditions in the human subject make the reception of revelation possible?
Rahner's axiom on anthropology :: Anthropology in its most radical sense is theology
Packer's self-description and image :: A "theologizer" doing "water engineering" — clearing sewage so the Church can drink pure truth
