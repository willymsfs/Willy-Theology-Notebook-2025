# 08 — The Other Twelve Documents

## The three that changed everything

### *Unitatis Redintegratio* — Ecumenism (21 Nov 1964)

The Catholic Church formally enters the ecumenical movement it had refused to join for fifty years.

- Other Christians are **"separated brethren"** — genuinely brothers and sisters, incorporated into Christ by baptism (UR 3).
- Those born into division today **are not personally guilty** of the sin of schism (UR 3).
- Their communities are not empty: the Spirit uses them **as means of salvation** (UR 3).
- **The hierarchy of truths** (UR 11) — Catholic doctrines vary in their relation to the foundation of Christian faith. Not all are equally central. This is a crucial ecumenical tool: it allows dialogue to begin with what is central rather than treating every point as equally non-negotiable.
- Renewal of the Catholic Church herself is part of ecumenism; the Church is "always in need of purification" (UR 6).

> [!warning] Do not overread
> UR does not say all Christian communities are equal, nor abandon the claim that the fullness of the means of salvation is found in the Catholic Church. It rests on *subsistit in* → [[05 - Lumen Gentium - The Church]].

### *Nostra Aetate* — Non-Christian Religions (28 Oct 1965)

Five paragraphs. Possibly the most consequential text of the Council for anyone living in Asia.

> [!quote] NA 2
> The Catholic Church **rejects nothing that is true and holy in these religions**. She regards with sincere reverence those ways of conduct and of life, those precepts and teachings which, though differing in many aspects from the ones she holds and sets forth, nonetheless often reflect a ray of that Truth which enlightens all men.

- **Hinduism** and **Buddhism** are named and described with respect — a first for a conciliar text (NA 2).
- **Muslims** are "esteemed"; the Council notes their adoration of the one God, merciful and almighty (NA 3).
- **On the Jews (NA 4)** — the heart of the document:
  - The Church's beginning is found in the patriarchs, Moses and the prophets; the Church draws sustenance from the root of the good olive tree.
  - What was done in Christ's passion "cannot be charged against all the Jews, without distinction, then alive, nor against the Jews of today" — the repudiation of the **deicide** charge that had underwritten centuries of persecution.
  - The Jews remain "most dear to God", for God does not repent of his gifts.
  - The Church "decries hatred, persecutions, displays of anti-Semitism, directed against Jews at any time and by anyone".
- **NA 5** — any discrimination or harassment on grounds of race, colour, condition of life or religion is "foreign to the mind of Christ".

> [!info] Context
> This is 1965 — twenty years after the Holocaust. The document was fiercely resisted, partly on theological grounds and partly on political ones (Arab states objected to any statement seen as favouring Israel). It survived largely through Cardinal Bea's persistence and Paul VI's backing.

### *Dignitatis Humanae* — Religious Freedom (7 Dec 1965)

The clearest case of genuine **development of doctrine**, and therefore the hardest for the "continuity" reading.

**The old position:** "error has no rights". A Catholic state should recognise Catholicism and may restrict public propagation of other religions. Tolerance is a concession granted where Catholics are a minority.

**Dignitatis Humanae:**

> [!quote] DH 2
> The human person has a **right to religious freedom**. This freedom means that all men are to be immune from coercion on the part of individuals or of social groups and of any human power, in such wise that in matters religious no one is to be forced to act in a manner contrary to his own beliefs.

The ground shifts decisively: the right belongs to **the person**, by reason of dignity, **not to the truth**. It is a civil and constitutional right — immunity from coercion — not a claim that all religions are equally true. DH 1 explicitly reaffirms that the one true religion subsists in the Catholic Church.

- Chiefly drafted with **John Courtney Murray** SJ, an American Jesuit previously silenced on exactly this question.
- Rejected by Archbishop **Marcel Lefebvre** and the SSPX as incompatible with prior teaching — this document, more than any other, is the root of that rupture. → [[10 - After the Council]]

> [!tip] How to defend it as development, not contradiction
> The subject changed. The old teaching concerned what is *true* (error has no rights *as error*). DH concerns what is owed to *persons* in civil society (no one may be coerced in religious matters). Both can stand: error still has no rights, but persons do — including persons who are in error. This is the standard argument; know it, and know that critics find it insufficient.

## The other nine, briefly

**Decrees**

- ***Inter Mirifica*** (press, film, radio, TV) — the Council's weakest document by wide agreement, drafted before the media landscape it addressed. Notable for establishing **World Communications Day**.
- ***Orientalium Ecclesiarum*** — the Eastern Catholic Churches' rites, discipline and heritage are to be preserved, not Latinised. They are of equal dignity with the Latin Church.
- ***Christus Dominus*** — bishops as true pastors, not papal branch managers; implements collegiality practically; proposes the Synod of Bishops; calls for episcopal conferences; bishops should offer resignation at 75.
- ***Perfectae Caritatis*** — renewal of religious life by a **double return**: to the Gospel, and to the original charism of the founder — combined with adaptation to present conditions.
- ***Optatam Totius*** — seminary formation. Philosophy and theology to be integrated, not sequential silos; **Scripture the "soul of all theology"** (OT 16); pastoral formation throughout.
- ***Apostolicam Actuositatem*** — the laity's apostolate flows from **baptism itself**, not from a delegation by the hierarchy. Their proper field is the temporal order.
- ***Ad Gentes*** — the Church is "**missionary by her very nature**" (AG 2), because she originates in the mission of the Son and the Spirit. Mission is not one activity among others but the Church's constitution. Calls for local churches genuinely rooted in their own cultures.
- ***Presbyterorum Ordinis*** — priests as co-workers of the bishops; ministry of word, sacrament and pastoral governance; celibacy reaffirmed for the Latin Church while acknowledging it is not demanded by the priesthood itself.

**Declarations**

- ***Gravissimum Educationis*** — a universal right to education; the primary right and duty of parents; the role of Catholic schools and universities.
