# 10 — After the Council

> [!info] Scope
> An orientation to the sixty-year argument about what Vatican II meant. Your Notion guide **Vatican II: The Battle for Meaning** treats this in nine chapters; this note gives you the map so that guide makes sense.

## 1. The problem in one sentence

The Council produced **compromise texts**. To secure near-unanimous votes ([[09 - The Turning Points]]), drafters often placed the majority's new formulation and the minority's traditional one **side by side**, unresolved. Both sides could then vote yes — and both could later quote the document against the other.

> [!quote] The interpretative consequence
> Almost every post-conciliar dispute is a quarrel about which half of a compromise text is the governing half.

## 2. The three interpretations

| | **Rupture / discontinuity** | **Reform in continuity** | **Traditionalist rejection** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Claim | The Council broke with the past and began a new Church | The Council developed the tradition without breaking it | The Council broke with the past — and was therefore wrong |
| The documents are | A starting point; the "spirit" goes further | The sole authoritative measure | Ambiguous or erroneous where novel |
| Key idea | The **event** exceeds the texts | Continuity of subject, reform of expression | *Dignitatis Humanae* contradicts prior teaching |
| Associated with | Bologna School (Alberigo); "spirit of the Council" | Benedict XVI's 2005 address; Marchetto | Lefebvre, SSPX; at the extreme, sedevacantism |

> [!tip] The one text to know
> **Benedict XVI, Address to the Roman Curia, 22 December 2005.** He names two hermeneutics: a "hermeneutic of discontinuity and rupture" and a "hermeneutic of reform" — continuity of the Church as one subject, combined with genuine development. This address is the reference point for the whole debate; cite it precisely.

## 3. "The spirit of the Council"

A phrase doing enormous work, and worth pulling apart.

- **Legitimate sense.** Documents have a direction. *Sacrosanctum Concilium*'s principle of participation implies more than its explicit provisions. Reading intent is normal interpretation.
- **Illegitimate sense.** "The spirit" invoked to justify things the documents never said, or contradict — with the implication that the letter was a compromise the real Council would have exceeded.

The test question: **can you cite a paragraph?** If an appeal to "the spirit" cannot be anchored in a text, it is doing the second thing.

## 4. Event vs documents — the historiographical fight

**Bologna School** (Giuseppe Alberigo, *History of Vatican II*, 5 vols): Vatican II was an **event** — a transition, a caesura, whose significance exceeds the compromise texts it produced. Understanding it requires the diaries, the drafts, the politics.

**The Roman response** (Agostino Marchetto; also John O'Malley in a distinct, more moderate register): the Council **is** its documents. What the bishops actually approved is what the Council taught; the rest is background.

O'Malley's own contribution in *What Happened at Vatican II* (2008) is the argument about **style**: the Council adopted a new **genre** — invitational, dialogical, using words like *dialogue*, *partnership*, *collegiality*, *pilgrim*, rather than the juridical-condemnatory register of prior councils. On his account the change in rhetoric *is* a change in substance, because it changes how the Church relates to everyone it addresses.

## 5. The liturgical dispute

The most visible fight, running from the 1970s to today.

- 1969/70 — **Missal of Paul VI**. The reform as executed goes beyond the letter of SC → [[04 - Sacrosanctum Concilium - The Liturgy]].
- 1970s — Lefebvre refuses the new rite; founds the SSPX.
- 1988 — Lefebvre consecrates four bishops without papal mandate; **excommunications** follow.
- 2007 — Benedict XVI, ***Summorum Pontificum***: wider permission for the older rite, framed as "two forms of the one Roman rite" — ordinary and extraordinary.
- 2021 — Francis, ***Traditionis Custodes***: sharply restricts the older rite, on the judgement that its use had become a vehicle for rejecting the Council itself.

> [!warning] The real question underneath
> Not "Latin or vernacular". It is whether the reformed liturgy represents **organic development** (SC 23) or **fabrication by committee**. State the question that way and you are arguing at the right level.

## 6. What actually changed in ordinary Catholic life

| Before | After |
|--------|-------|
| Latin Mass, priest facing the altar | Vernacular, priest facing the people |
| One-year lectionary cycle | Three-year Sunday cycle |
| Laity attend | Laity read, serve, distribute Communion, lead ministries |
| Bible reading uncommon among laity | Bible study normal; ecumenical translations |
| "Heretics and schismatics" | "Separated brethren"; formal dialogues |
| Other religions outside salvation | Rays of truth acknowledged; interreligious dialogue |
| Confessional state as the ideal | Religious freedom as a human right |
| Holiness for priests and religious | Universal call to holiness |
| Mission = conversion of individuals | Mission includes inculturation and development |

## 7. The honest ledger

**Achievements.** Liturgical participation and scriptural literacy; ecumenical and interreligious relations transformed; religious freedom; the theology of the laity; the Church becoming genuinely global rather than European with dependencies.

**Difficulties.** Sharp decline in priestly and religious vocations in the West from the late 1960s; catechetical confusion in the 1970s; polarisation; liturgical experimentation many found destabilising; the *Humanae Vitae* rupture of 1968.

> [!tip] The causation question — where the marks are
> Were the difficulties **caused by** the Council, **coincidental with** it (1968, the sexual revolution, secularisation, affluence hit every Western institution simultaneously), or caused by **misimplementation**? A strong answer refuses the simple version and notes that the Church declined *most* in precisely the Western societies where every other traditional institution also declined, while growing where the Council's inculturation programme was applied — Africa, Asia. Correlation is not causation, in either direction.

## 8. Pope Francis and synodality

The current phase. Francis has drawn heavily on *Lumen Gentium*'s People of God and *sensus fidei*, and on *Gaudium et Spes* (he cites it more than any other conciliar text). The **Synod on Synodality** (2021–2024) is presented as implementing collegiality's unfinished agenda.

Critics argue synodality goes beyond LG 22, which locates supreme authority in the college of **bishops** with the Pope — not in a consultative body including laity. Supporters reply it develops *sensus fidei* (LG 12) and the People of God chapter. This is the collegiality argument of 1964, still running.
