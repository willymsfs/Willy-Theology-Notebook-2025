# Vatican II — Course Home (BTh)

**What it is:** the 21st ecumenical council of the Catholic Church, held in St Peter's Basilica in four autumn sessions, **11 October 1962 → 8 December 1965**. It produced **16 documents** and reshaped almost every part of Catholic life.

> [!quote] The Council in one line
> Vatican II was the Church's decision to stop defining itself *against* the modern world and start speaking *to* it — without changing a single dogma.

> [!info] Start here if you know nothing
> Read [[01 - What a Council Is and Why Vatican II Happened]] first. It assumes zero background. Do not start with the documents themselves; they will not make sense until you know why they were written.

## The 16 documents at a glance

| Rank | Type | How many | What the rank means |
|------|------|----------|---------------------|
| 1 | **Constitutions** | 4 | Highest authority. Doctrine about the Church itself. |
| 2 | **Decrees** | 9 | Practical direction for a particular group or activity. |
| 3 | **Declarations** | 3 | A statement of position on a question facing the world. |

> [!tip] Exam trap
> All 16 are "documents of Vatican II", but they are **not equal in authority**. A declaration (e.g. *Nostra Aetate*) does not carry the weight of a dogmatic constitution (*Lumen Gentium*). Examiners test this.

## The four Constitutions — quick reference

| Document | English | Promulgated | One-line point |
|----------|---------|-------------|----------------|
| *Sacrosanctum Concilium* | On the Sacred Liturgy | 4 Dec 1963 | Worship is the whole people's action, not the priest's alone |
| *Lumen Gentium* | On the Church | 21 Nov 1964 | The Church is first the People of God, then a hierarchy |
| *Dei Verbum* | On Divine Revelation | 18 Nov 1965 | God reveals *himself*, not just information |
| *Gaudium et Spes* | On the Church in the Modern World | 7 Dec 1965 | The world's joys and griefs are the Church's own |

## Golden thread

The Council's method was **ressourcement in order to achieve aggiornamento** — going *back* to Scripture and the Fathers in order to go *forward* into the modern world. Every major document follows this pattern: recover an older, richer idea that scholastic manuals had flattened, then apply it to the present. If you can spot that move in a document, you understand it.
