# 04 — Selected Models of Theology

> **Chapter 4 of the textbook** · seven Models, each shown through one representative Theologian

> [!quote] Ex 3:2-6
> One Fire. Many who turned aside to see it.

[[03 - Development and Pluralism in Theology]] showed that Theology takes many Forms across History. This Chapter goes closer: **seven worked-out Ways of doing Theology**, so that you see how each actually operates.

---

## A. SITUATING THE EXPOSITION

Every Theology carries two Dimensions: **Universality**, because Revelation has universal Value; and **Contextuality**, because it is expressed in the Categories of a particular Philosophy and addressed to a particular Culture. Any Model is therefore always an **Embodiment of both Faith and Culture**.

### Four Ways of Balancing Faith and Culture

| Model | Focus |
|---|---|
| **Translation Model** | Primarily the inherited Faith-Tradition, at times to the Detriment of the Culture |
| **Anthropological Model** | Primarily the cultural Context underpinning any Expression of Faith |
| **Synthetic Model** | Seeks Balance, giving each its due Weight |
| **Transcendental Model** | Not the **content** but the **subject**: not what is studied, but **how** one theologizes |

> [!info] Why THESE seven Models. The Indian Reason.
> The Catholic Church in India is a Communion of **three Individual Churches**: Latin, Syro-Malabar, Syro-Malankara. Hence:
>
> - **Two** rooted in Indian ecclesial Reality: the **East Syrian** Model (Ephraem) and the **Celebrative Liturgical** Model (Schmemann)
> - **Two** from the Western Church, since India is open to the Universal Church: **Medieval Scholastic** (Aquinas) and **Contemporary Western** (Rahner)
> - **Two** arising from the Indian Context itself: **Critical Reflection on Praxis** and the **Contemplative Approach**
> - **One** ecumenical: the **Kerygmatic** Model (Barth)

## B. A SYRIAC-SEMITIC APPROACH: ST. EPHRAEM

> [!info] Why this Model matters most for India
> Early Christianity was shaped by three Cultures: Semitic, Greek, Latin. The **Semitic-Syriac** Tradition is Asian in Origin and was **the only significant Christian Presence in India** before the sixteenth Century, surviving in the **St. Thomas Christian Communities of the Malabar Coast**.

**Ephraem**: born about **306**, Deacon and catechetical Teacher at **Nisibis**, later **Edessa**, died about **373**. Heir to Mesopotamian, Jewish and partly Greek Culture. He represents Christianity in its as yet **unhellenized** Form, a Counterbalance to an overly cerebral Tradition.

### a) Theology as Poetry

Ephraem **shuns Definitions**. He considers them potentially dangerous: by chalking out Boundaries they **fossilize** Theological Inquiry. Worse, to define God is to try to **tie down the Limitless One**, which he views as close to blasphemous. His Method proceeds by **Paradox and Symbol**, and Poetry is the fitting Medium because it refuses to pigeonhole.

### b) THREE ATTITUDES TO THE THEOLOGICAL ENTERPRISE

| | Attitude | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Domination**: the Theologian controls the Object of Enquiry, God | Basic Error of Method; the Error of many Heretics. **Rejected outright** |
| **2** | **Detached Study**: God studied in an uninvolved Manner | Reduces God to an Object; a one-way Street. **Problematic** |
| **3** | **Love and Wonder** (Ephraem's own) | Holistic; constant Interaction between God and the Theologian. **Only in and through such an Exchange of Love can Knowledge of God grow** |

God has crossed the Chasm in three Ways: through **Types and Symbols** in Nature and Scripture; through **Names and Metaphors** in Scripture; and above all in the **Incarnation**.

### c) A Broad-based Understanding of Revelation

Revelation is not limited to the Pages of Scripture but is **woven on many levels into the World**. Scripture and Nature are **two complementary Realms**: Nature is harder to interpret, Scripture clearer and able to lead further. In both, the Initiative belongs **exclusively to God**. God remains hidden except insofar as he allows himself to be revealed.

Ephraem uses the Syriac term ***galyata***, Instances of divine Self-Manifestation. What lies hidden in the Symbols of Nature and Scripture is revealed **in Christ at the Incarnation**; what lies hidden in the Sacraments will be revealed **on the Last Day**.

### d) and e) Sacred Scripture and its Interpretation

| Sense | Refers to | Character |
|---|---|---|
| **Exterior** | Historical Reality | Finite, can be fixed and conclusive |
| **Interior** | The Reality of the Spirit | Fluid; perceived by the **inner eye of Faith** |

Both are acceptable **provided neither makes exclusive Claims**. Error arises when one claims a spiritual Interpretation is the only one possible, or that the two Meanings are incompatible. **Christ is ultimately the Key** to any authentic Understanding of Scripture.

### f) FAITH, THE LUMINOUS EYE

> [!important] The Signature Image
> **Faith is a Perspective. It is the Eye which can see hidden Things.**
>
> As an Attitude it is holistic: it must be accompanied by **Love, Prayer, Wonder and Awe**. It is the initial Offering to God that must be made before any Progress in Knowledge of him is realized.

> [!tip] Ephraem the proto-ecologist
> Far ahead of his Time, Ephraem draws out the **ecological Consequences of the Abuse of Free Will**. The physical and spiritual Ecospheres are so intimately linked that any Abuse of Nature arising from the Misuse of Free Will has Repercussions anywhere in the Cosmos.

**Evaluation.** Dating to the undivided Church, Ephraem is the common Legacy of **all** Christian Traditions. His Message reaches the unlearned and the intellectual Elite alike because his Imagery is drawn so deeply from the Bible. His Lesson: an integral Approach requires that one both **confront** Scripture and **live out** the Challenges it poses.

## C. ARTICULATION OF THE LITURGICAL EXPERIENCE: ALEXANDER SCHMEMANN

**The Orthodox Eastern Churches**: the Patriarchates of Constantinople, Alexandria, Antioch and Jerusalem, separated from Rome in **1054** and again after **1453**. The major, perhaps only significant, theological Difference is the **Refusal of the jurisdictional Primacy of the Bishop of Rome**.

**Alexander Schmemann (1921-1983)**, Russian Orthodox. Christian Existence is a **lived Experience of Wholeness**, which is why Orthodox Theology refuses to be pigeonholed by West European Categories. The Approach is like **peeling an Onion**, layer by layer inward to the Core.

### The Ecclesial Emphasis: two Dimensions held together

| Dimension | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Preparation** | The Church prepares us; the Value is "at the end", "still to come", the **not yet** |
| **Fulfilment** | The Christ-Event **has already taken place**; the Church is the **Sacrament of the Kingdom** |

### The Centrality of the Liturgy

> [!important] Theology is best expressed as Praise
> Theology is best expressed and communicated in the form of **doxology**. Liturgy is the Revelation, the Embodiment and the Expression of the Faith of the Church.
>
> The Paradox: one must **have Faith to enter** the Liturgy, and yet the Liturgy **fulfils** the Faith, is its existential Expression and also its Gift.

The Liturgy has a **cosmic** Dimension, assuming all Creation into Christ, and an **historical** Dimension, assuming all Time into Christ. Hence *Lex orandi lex credendi*: true Understanding is made possible **by belonging** to the Church; it is the Fruit, not the Condition. → [[01 - What Is Theology]]

> [!example] Food and Blessing: the Cosmic Argument
> All that exists is the **Gift of God**, given as a Symbol of Communion with God. To **name** a Thing is to **bless** God for it and in it. Blessing God is not a cultic Act but a **Way of Life**.
>
> Human Beings alone can bless God in return for the Food and Life they receive. Hence the Christian is the **Priest who stands at the Centre of the Cosmos**, receiving the World from God and offering it back to God. **The World is the Sacrament of God.**

### Tradition and Scripture

> [!quote] Tradition, as the Orthodox understand it
> Tradition is **"the life of the Holy Spirit in the Church."** Not the Remembrance of a beautiful Past but a **Summons and an Inspiration**. Compare it to human Memory: its real Function is to transform and integrate the Past into the Present, and so to assure the Continuity of Life.

Strata in which Tradition is embodied: Holy Scripture, Dogmatic Tradition, Liturgical Tradition, the Fathers, Christian Art, Icons.

Scripture is **not opposed to Tradition**: Scripture is itself **part of** Tradition, and Tradition is the reading and appropriation of Scripture in the Life of the Church. Scripture is envisaged first of all as **a Way of Life**, not as an Authority to be quoted for Propositions.

**Evaluation.** This Model overcomes the Division between **Theology, Liturgy and Piety**, a Separation that has helped none of the three: it deprived Liturgy of its Fullness, Theology of its living Source, and Piety of its Content.

## D. A SCHOLASTIC MODEL: THOMAS AQUINAS

**The Golden Age**: the second half of the **thirteenth Century**, when Theologians confident that Philosophy is allied to Theology and Reason in harmony with Revelation built masterly Syntheses.

**Life**: born at **Roccasecca near Aquino in 1225**; Dominican in **1244**; student at Paris **1245-1248** under **Albert the Great**; Master in Theology **1256**; taught at Paris, Anagni, Orvieto, Rome, Viterbo; died at **Fossanova in 1274** on his way to the **Council of Lyons**.

### The Goal

Aquinas always considered himself a **Theologian**. In the thirteenth Century the Term **"philosopher" carried a derogatory Connotation**, meaning among other things a non-believer. His Goal was **theological Wisdom**: *Fides quaerens intellectum*.

### Theology and Philosophy

> [!important] The Handmaid Formula
> Theology does **not borrow its Principles** from the philosophical Sciences. It borrows **their Method of Exposition** and whatever facilitates Understanding. It uses them **not as Superiors but as Inferiors and, as it were, Handmaids** (*ancilla theologiae*).

Aquinas felt free to **replace** the Philosophy used by his Predecessors, even Augustine, where he judged a better one available. His Reason is simple: **Philosophy is not necessary for Salvation**, and it is not even necessary for Theology to resort to Philosophy; **but if it does, the Philosophy it uses should be an adequate Philosophy.**

The Truth of his Philosophy in no way depends on the Truth of his Faith; it stands on its own rational Evidence.

### Theology as Sacred Science

*Scientia* keeps its Meaning of certain and true Knowledge, and adds the Aristotelian Sense of a Body of **Conclusions deduced from Principles**.

| Kind of Science | Principles come from |
|---|---|
| Arithmetic, Geometry | The natural Light of the Intellect |
| **Theology** | **The Science of God made known by Revelation** |

The Unity of the formal Object of Theology consists in its being **"divinely revealed."** Theology thereby became **teachable like any other Art or Science**, a real Shift from Augustine, for whom Theology was rooted in the personal Experience of the Theologian.

> [!warning] What the Synthesis cost
> **1.** A **Duality in the way we come to know**: what Reason knows unaided, and what only Faith knows through Revelation. **Faith was no longer the exclusive Way to Knowledge; it became one of two.**
>
> **2.** Aquinas replaced the *a posteriori* **Way of Discovery** (*via inventionis*) with the *a priori* **Way of Exposition** (*via doctrinae*). Exposition is valuable **only for those who have already journeyed** the Way of Discovery. It has too often been treated as a **Substitute** for it.

> [!tip] Bernard Lonergan: two Reasons the Development helped
> **Practical**: the variety of patristic Commentaries produced conflicting Interpretations. Aristotelian Dialectic gave Tools of right Reasoning, producing the technique of the ***quaestio***, which promoted Theology from mere Commentary to systematic ***Summae***.
>
> **Epistemological**: the Aristotelian Logic adopted in the patristic Period could not answer the Questions its own Adoption raised. They had to be placed in the broader Context of **Metaphysics**. That is precisely what Aquinas did.

## E. THE KERYGMATIC APPROACH: KARL BARTH

> [!info] Read the Reformation fairly
> What appears from a Roman Catholic Point of View as a Rebellion is from a Protestant Point of View a **genuine Reform**. Luther and the Reformers came to lose their Belief that the existing Church of their Period was really the Church of Christ.

They reacted against moral Corruption, progressive Secularisation, decadent Theology and irresponsible Administration. The **last Straw** was the Preaching of **Indulgences**, done in an ambiguous and exaggerated Way, taken by some Reformers as literally a **Sale**.

Meanwhile the Thomistic Synthesis broke down: the Quest for Understanding was replaced by a **Desire for Certitude**, Theology grew abstract and cut off from the Sources of Faith, reaching its high Point in **Nominalism**. → [[03 - Development and Pluralism in Theology]]

**Karl Barth (1886-1968)** continues the Lutheran Line.

### The Role of Theology

Three Presuppositions: the general Event of **human Existence** confronted by the God who proclaims himself in the Gospel; the **Response of Faith**; and the **Capacity of Reason** for Perception, Judgement and Language.

> [!quote] The God of the Gospel
> God is unique; he can and does show Mercy. He is free. He exists **neither next to humans, nor merely above humans, but with humans, by humans, and most important of all, FOR humans.**

Theology is **humble and without Pretensions** because it is determined by God who is its Subject. It has **neither the Right nor the Competence** either to repeat or to anticipate the Actions of God in History. **All speculative Theology is staunchly rejected.**

### Scripture, Church, Response

**Sola Scriptura** means that God speaks through Scripture **without omitting anything necessary for Salvation**, not merely that Scripture contains a number of revealed Truths. This rejects **not Tradition as such** but a particular Concept of Tradition: it accepts the exegetical Tradition of interpreted Scripture and rejects **extra-biblical oral Tradition**.

The Place of Theology is concretely **in the Community**, the secondary Witnesses. *Credo ut intelligam*. In the Protestant Understanding the Church is the gathered Community trekking through History: **none of the Members is above the other; none is closer to the Head.**

**Theology stands and falls with the Word of God**, for the Word precedes all theological Words, creating, arousing and challenging them.

### FOUR ATTITUDES REQUIRED OF THE THEOLOGIAN

1. **Astonishment.** Stands at the Origin of all theological Inquiry. Indispensable.
2. **Concern.** Wonder involves Concern, because of the Forcefulness of the Object.
3. **Commitment.** The Object desires something not only **for** us but **from** us. Theology involves **Ethics as well**.
4. **Loneliness.** The Theologian will often feel he stands alone in this Calling, because of the special Response demanded of him.

### Faith

> [!important] Two Latin Formulas to distinguish
> Faith is not merely ***credere quod***, believing **that**, assenting to Propositions. It is ***credere in***, believing **in**, a living Relationship and Communion.
>
> **"Faith is a history, new every morning."** One must believe in order to become and be a Theologian.

**Evaluation.** For Barth, Theology is a Response **exclusively** to the Word of God, not to the human Quest. There is an Emphasis on the **Transcendence of God to the Detriment of the human**. One significant Consequence: an **Inability to account theologically for other Religions**, except in very negative Terms. Compare [[06 - Theologizing in the Indian Context]], where that Inability would be fatal.

## F. THEOLOGICAL ANTHROPOLOGY: KARL RAHNER

**Karl Rahner (1904-1984)**, the leading Figure of the Renewal of Catholic Theology. Compared to **"an intellectual spider spinning a splendid web."** Catholic Theologians must situate themselves **within the Faith of the Church**, not by passively accepting a Doctrine but by making that Faith their own; to this extent one may speak of a **"personal theology."**

> [!quote] Rahner's Axiom
> **"For Rahner, non-philosophical theology is bad theology."**

### The Anthropological Presuppositions

One can only grasp that the Totality of Beings is **finite** against a **Horizon** which is **infinite**. Limitedness stands out only against an implicit Grasp of the Unlimited.

The Human Person is a **restless, questing, questioning Being**, continually thrusting forward into what lies beyond his Reach. This Openness is experienced only in an **implicit, preconceptual, unthematic** Way.

| The Human Being is... | Consequence |
|---|---|
| **Spirit** | Perpetual reaching out towards the Absolute |
| **Embodied Spirit** | Intellectual Knowledge only through and with Sense Knowledge |
| **Historical Spirit** | If God is to reveal himself, he must do so **in History** |

Rahner uses the Aristotelian and Thomistic **hylemorphism**, the Theory of Matter and Form, to explain how Things can be radically distinct and yet intimately one: a Person's Spirit is not the Body, yet a Person **is** the Body.

By this **transcendental** Analysis he uncovers the Conditions of Possibility of any Act. This necessary Structure is called an ***a priori***.

> [!warning] Not Modernism
> Rahner explicitly excludes any Reading that would make all this a mere **Projection of the Human Spirit**. Theological Doctrines are **not** merely Statements about human Self-Understanding.

### The Place of Jesus Christ

Since God is present to every finite created Being, Matter is endowed with the Possibility of **transcending itself**. In that Process Matter develops into the human. Humans seek God and are historical. Therefore, if God is to respond meaningfully to this Orientation, **he must do so in History.**

> [!important] Therefore the Incarnation is not a Freak Event
> "It is not totally unexpected; in our very being we are searching for God's free epiphany in history, for an absolute saviour, that is, for the kind of event that took place in Jesus of Nazareth."

### NATURE AND GRACE: THE SUPERNATURAL EXISTENTIAL

**The Problem.** Scholasticism held a radical Distinction between the Order of Nature and the Order of the Supernatural. But then the Supernatural seems to arrive **solely from outside**, superimposed on Nature. The Consequences are appalling: if a Person is completely at home in a self-enclosed World of Nature, **there is no reason to be interested in Revelation at all**, and such Revelation would be unintelligible because unrelated to the human.

> [!important] Rahner's Solution. Memorise this.
> **The Supernatural Existential.** Since God desires the Salvation of all, our **concrete, actual, historical State has already been raised to the supernatural Level from the beginning** by the gracious, gratuitous and supernatural Call of God. This remains a **free Gift**, but it is **never absent in the concrete**.
>
> The Openness of the Human Spirit is therefore not a passive Ability to receive Grace but an **active Seeking and Desiring**, and this Desire is itself a Gift.

Rahner thereby **rejects all Dualism** between Nature and Grace, and situates the working of divine Grace **precisely in the area of human questioning, wondering and exploring**.

### Church, Scripture, Faith

The Church is the **lasting Presence of Christ** and the **real Symbol** of God identifying himself with the World in Christ. Scripture is **the Book of the Church**, displaying the character of an ***anamnesis***, a conscious existential Reference to its Origin. The **Teaching Office is not a Source** of the Content of Faith but is entrusted with sheltering and interpreting the Deposit.

Faith has an **intellectual Moment**: an irrational Conception of Belief would turn Faith into an **Ideology**.

**Evaluation.** The most serious Objection to transcendental Theology is the Risk of **over-emphasising Anthropology**, and the delicate Task of relating human Existence as the transcendental *a priori* Condition of Faith with Christianity as the *a posteriori* historical Condition.

## G. CRITICAL REFLECTION ON CHRISTIAN PRAXIS

**Liberation Theology**, a Model of the **1970s** from Latin America, a Context marked by utter Dependence and Oppression for millions.

### THREE QUESTIONS PUT TO THE THEOLOGIZING SUBJECT

> [!important] The Radical Novelty
> The Point of Departure is not an Experience, nor a Truth, **but a Praxis**: the concrete Involvement of the Theologian or the theologizing Community.

**1. From WHERE do the Theologians speak?** The Theologian is **not an uninvolved Spectator** watching the World go by. Every Understanding of Faith is **situated**. This Approach challenges Theologians to lead a **transformed Life**: Theology should manifest itself in a **changed Life-Style**.

**2. WHY do the Theologians speak?** Theology is one Way of communicating the Faith and of critically evaluating the Forms in which Faith has already been translated. This can be done **only if Theologians have included themselves in a Commitment**.

**3. To WHOM do the Theologians speak?**

> [!warning] The Sentence that defines this Model
> The Target is not primarily the **non-believer** (the Atheist), but the **NON-PERSON** (the poor and oppressed), who challenges not merely our religious World but our economic, social, political and cultural World.
>
> "The great challenge is, therefore, not how to announce God in an adult world, but **how to announce God's love in a non-human world**."

The Option for the Poor derives **from Faith**, because they live in a Condition **God does not intend for them**. This Approach starts from a Praxis and returns to a reformed Praxis: **orthopraxis**.

### Context, Social Analysis, Hermeneutics

Social, economic and political Questions replace Questions of a speculative Nature. The **Social Sciences** are used to find and understand the **Causes** of Oppression and Dependence.

The Hermeneutic is simply **the reading of the Bible from the Standpoint of the Poor and Oppressed**, from the Perspective of the condemned Human Beings of the Earth, because theirs is the Kingdom of Heaven. The Praxis Model adopts a **reader-centred Approach**, placing itself **in front of** the Text: what does the phrase *Kingdom of God* say to a Situation of utter Oppression?

**The Exodus** is the key paradigmatic Old Testament Text, revealing a God who **liberates**, who is concerned with **Justice**, who remains **compassionate**.

### The Three Levels of Liberation

| Level | Liberation as |
|---|---|
| **Social Sciences** | Analysis of the Context; Projects for a more just and human Future |
| **Philosophy and History** | The gradual **Conquest of authentic Freedom**; the Emergence of the new Human Person able to live with Dignity |
| **Theology** | The Place where the **Promise of Salvation** proclaimed by Christ is realized |

**Reciprocal, interpenetrating and inseparable, but yet distinct.**

**The Mission of the Church** is prophetic: **to denounce Sin and to announce the Salvation of God**, concretely through an Option for the Poor touching every Aspect of the Life of the Church.

> [!warning] The two Roman Instructions. Know both.
> Congregation for the Doctrine of the Faith:
> **1.** *Certain Aspects of the Theology of Liberation*, **6 August 1984**
> **2.** *Christian Freedom and Liberation*, **22 March 1986**
>
> While appreciating the Aspiration for Liberation, they critique: the adoption of **Marxist Analysis** with its unacceptable ideological Presuppositions regarding God, Community and the Human Person; and the adoption of **Violence** as an acceptable Means, except as a last Resort in Cases of extreme Urgency. They specify that the Governance of political and economic Society **does not directly pertain** to the Mission of the Church, and that the Mission is **not reducible** to it. The Critique is aimed at a **merely political Reading** of the Bible.

## H. A CONTEMPLATIVE APPROACH: SWAMI ABHISHIKTANANDA

**Henri Le Saux**, born **30 August 1910** at St-Briac, Brittany; Benedictine at **Ste-Anne de Kergonan, 1929**; Priest **1935**; sailed to India **1948**, joining **Fr. Jules Monchanin OSB** at **Shantivanam Ashram**, Kulitalai, Tamil Nadu, taking the Name **Abhishiktananda**; died **7 December 1973** at Indore.

### The Conceptual Approach and Experience

Christianity is footed in Semitic Culture and in the Graeco-Roman World, and understands itself as **Revelation**: the Mystery of a free Encounter **face to face**. The typical Hindu Approach he found was **Advaitic**: it does **not feel the need to objectify outside itself** the Mystery experienced in the depths of one's own Being, and so does not **name** the Absolute, much less define it.

Therefore any Christian wishing to contact the mystical Tradition of India needs an inward Disposition, a ***habitus*** of Recollection and Contemplation, entering **"the cave of the heart"**, the Locus of the Indian Experience of the Mystery.

> [!important] Do not confuse the Vessel with the Treasure
> The West was chosen for a specific Mission: to give the Infant Church the Means of **articulating in conceptual Terms** what had been received. The Church was profoundly enriched by that blending of Biblical Revelation and Greek Wisdom.
>
> **To the Advaitic Experience of India, instead, was entrusted the Task of laying the Foundation for the working of the Spirit of Wisdom.** Abhishiktananda sees the *Advaita* of India as the **final Preparation for the Gospel**, for it disposes human Beings to experience Revelation.
>
> "Christianity cannot claim the universality which it affirms is integral to its reality, as long as it has not recognized and integrated this spiritual experience of the East."

### Faith, Revelation, Scripture, Church

**Faith** is the inner Relation of the Human Being to what transcends, articulated in Beliefs. But **the Certainty of Faith transcends all conceptual Analysis**; the Intellect cannot comprehend the Mystery even when enlightened by Grace. This **Awakening** depends neither on Knowledge nor on Discipline of the Will. **Only God can bring it about.**

**Revelation by Stages**, becoming more inward as Humankind became able to hear:

```mermaid
flowchart LR
    A["COSMIC REVELATION"] --> B["OLD TESTAMENT<br/>Revelation of the FATHER"]
    B --> C["Revelation of the SON"]
    C --> D["PENTECOST<br/>Revelation of the SPIRIT"]
```

**Scripture** must be the Point of Departure **and** the Point of Return, not a Reference Book consulted to confirm Discoveries made elsewhere. It would be **out of place to interpret Scriptural Intuitions as though they were Aristotelian Definitions**; they burst the limited Significance of their Words. This explains the disconcerting **Anthropomorphisms**: Anthropomorphism is false **only when it reduces God** to the Form the Words attribute to him.

**The Church** must make People realize that there is offered them **something deeper than the most profound Interiority discovered by the Sages and Mystics: the Abyss of the Heart of Christ.**

### Theology as Holistic, Experiential and Advaitic

Theology is a **Word (Logos) concerning God**. But the Word **is** God, is in God. Therefore **it is only within God himself that we can hear the Word which God says of himself.**

Abhishiktananda professes a **Christian Advaita**, in which the Godhead is a **Communion of Persons in Love**.

> [!quote] The hard saying
> As long as any Christian looks upon God as "another", in the sense in which their neighbour is other to them; as long as Jesus too is "another", and the divine Persons are "other" both to them and among themselves, **they have not begun to understand anything either of themselves or of God. The ultimate Mystery lies at the very heart of non-duality.**

**The Advaitic Night**: a Point in the spiritual Ascent where all sense of Individuality appears **swallowed up in God**. Yet on awakening into an Awareness of one's **divine Sonship**, Individuality is still held within the Mystery of the Relationship of Christ to the Father as the only Son, expressed in the cry **"Abba."** In time comes a Realization both of one's **Uniqueness** and of one's **Communion**, and with it the **"return of the cosmos"**: Creation rediscovered as within the Fullness of Christ, a ***Christophany***.

### EVALUATION: THREE VOICES

> [!quote] Antony Kalliath, The Word in the Cave (1996), 360
> "To understand Abhishiktananda, his person and the work, what is required, I think, is **more a theological sympathy than an objective analytical reason**. That is to say, the validity of Abhishiktananda's witness exists primarily not in logical truth but in **intuitive truth**."

Kalliath adds at once that this does **not** imply the Testimony is beyond Interpretation and Critique.

> [!warning] Bede Griffiths, his own Disciple, objects
> Abhishiktananda **failed to maintain the required Balance between the rational and intuitive Faculties**. He dives deeply into the intuitive Realm **without the necessary Accompaniment of Reason**.

The Crux: **can any Theology be articulated purely on the plane of Experience?** All Catholic Theology has inset a **Community Dimension**. The Criteria whereby a new Model is tested cannot be purely experiential. → [[05 - The Criteria of Christian Theological Reflection]]

> [!quote] Jacques Dupuis, Jesus Christ at the Encounter of World Religions (1991), 90
> His Experience "seems to pose more than one dilemma: between a unity that abolishes distinctions and an interpersonal communion that deepens in direct proportion to the distinctions themselves."
>
> And yet: "His experience **opens an important avenue toward a Christian theology of religious traditions** that would be based on an existential encounter with these traditions in interreligious dialogue."

## Review Questions (from the textbook)

1. Taking Ephraem as exemplar, what are the Advantages and Disadvantages of Symbolism as a Vehicle of Faith-Experience?
2. Aquinas made Theology a Science. What have been the Advantages and Disadvantages?
3. How significant is the Liturgy in the Orthodox Approach of Schmemann? Has it succeeded in integrating Christian Life and Worship?
4. What is the main Thrust of the Theology of the Reformers, especially Barth? Its Strengths and Weaknesses?
5. All Theology has an anthropological Dimension. Highlight the Contribution of Rahner.
6. In what way is the Praxis Model a Critique of traditional Approaches? Advantages and Disadvantages?
7. Abhishiktananda emphasized Experience. Has he sufficiently taken into account the **Community Dimension** intrinsic to Christianity?
