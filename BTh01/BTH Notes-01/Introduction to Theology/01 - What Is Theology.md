# 01 — What Is Theology?

> **Chapter 1 of the textbook** · the God-Question, the Language Problem, the sevenfold Definition, the Creative Polarities, the Goals

> [!quote] Ex 3:3
> "I will turn aside and see this great sight, why the bush is not burnt."

Theology begins in that Turning Aside. Not in a Library, not in a Syllabus, but in Wonder before a Fire that burns and is not consumed.

---

## A. THE GOD-QUESTION

**Theology = Theos + Logos = Discourse on God = God-Talk.**

But in a World of Secularism, Agnosticism and Atheism, the Existence of God cannot be presumed. Three Positions stand at the Threshold.

### A. The Atheistic View

Rejects the Existence of God. The book names four: **Albert Camus, Karl Marx, Friedrich Nietzsche, Sigmund Freud**.

Three Reasons for the Denial:
1. **The Problem of Evil.** There is so much unmerited Suffering and Injustice that a good and loving God seems impossible.
2. **God as Projection.** God is a Creation of the Human Mind, used by Religions and powerful Groups to subdue the Powerless and the Poor.
3. **God as Psychic Need.** For Freud, the God who controls and provides is created by the Human Psyche out of its Need for Love, Safety and Security.

> [!warning] Note carefully for the exam
> Some **Religious Traditions are themselves non-theistic**: the **Charvaka** school of Hinduism and **Theravada Buddhism**. Atheism is therefore not a purely Western or purely modern Phenomenon.

### B. The Agnostic View

Simply does not know whether God exists. For the Agnostic the Question is not even an important one. With or without God the World goes on as it is.

### C. The Theistic View

God is the **Ultimate Reality**, the **Ultimate Ground of Being**, the **Ultimate Horizon of Meaning**. Conceived personally by Christianity, Judaism and Islam; conceived impersonally by other Systems as Absolute Power, Supreme Mind, *Actus Purus*, the Divine, the Sacred.

This is the Position the Church endorses, and it carries the Magisterial Citation:

> [!quote] Vatican I, Dei Filius 2; Vatican II, Dei Verbum 6
> "Our Holy Mother the Church holds and teaches that God, the first principle and last end of all things, can be known with certainty from the created world by the natural light of human reason."

Man has this Capacity because he is created "in the Image of God" (Gen 1:27) (CCC 36).

> [!example] The Five Ways, and their Limit
> Thomas Aquinas gave **Five Cosmological Proofs**, moving from Effect to Cause to the Prime Mover. But Cardinal **John Henry Newman** observed that such Proofs are abstract and philosophical and yield only a **"convergence of diverse probabilities."**
>
> The Believer finds the Five Ways illuminating. The Unbeliever remains unmoved. **Reason opens a Path to God; it does not compel the Traveller to walk it.**

---

## B. CAN WE SPEAK OF GOD?

Granting that God exists, can finite Language reach an Infinite and Ineffable God? Again three Positions.

| | Position | Claim |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | Classical Hindu Way | *Neti, Neti*: Not This, Not This. We can say only what God is not |
| **B** | Mystical and Negative Theology | Denies the Possibility of speaking positively of God |
| **C** | Biblical and Mainline Christian | A limited but **real positive** Knowledge of God is possible |

> [!quote] CCC 40-43, citing Wis 13:5, Lateran Council IV, Aquinas SCG I, 30
> "We can name God only by taking Creatures as our starting point... for from the greatness and beauty of created things comes a corresponding perception of their Creator... yet between Creator and creature no similitude can be expressed without implying an even greater dissimilitude."

> [!info] The Indian Lens
> The Chapter sets the Biblical Position directly against the Hindu *Neti-Neti* and the Apophatic Way. The Indian Seminarian lives this Tension daily: the Upanishadic Instinct towards Silence before Brahman, and the Biblical Confidence that God has a Name and a Face in Jesus Christ.
>
> Analogy is precisely the Middle Path between the Silence of *Neti-Neti* and a naive Literalism about God.

### THE ANALOGICAL WAY

Gloss every Term on arrival. This is examinable.

| Term | Meaning | Homely Example | Verdict for God-Talk |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Univocal** | Same Word, same Meaning | *Six* Mangoes, *Six* Books | **Impossible.** God is Infinite, Creature is Finite |
| **Equivocal** | Same Word, unrelated Meanings | *Bank* of a River, *Bank* of Money | **Impossible.** Theology would collapse into Nonsense |
| **Analogical** | Same Word by Proportion and Participation | *Father* on Earth, *Father* in Heaven | **The only Way.** Developed by Aristotle, refined by Aquinas |

> [!example] Jesus taught us to say "Our Father"
> This is neither Univocal, since God is not a Father exactly as a Human Father is, nor Equivocal, since the Term is not meaningless. We purify the earthly Notion of Father of all its Limitations and apply it to God in an **Eminent Way**.
>
> **Analogy alone makes the whole Theological Enterprise possible.**

## C. GOD-EXPERIENCE AND SYMBOLIC LANGUAGE

To speak of God at all, Humankind must first experience God.

**Religious Experience** = the intimate and immediate Awareness of being touched or grasped by the Divine; the Encounter with the Tremendous and Fascinating Mystery (**Rudolf Otto**, *The Idea of the Holy*, 1950).

Three further Authorities: **William James**, *The Varieties of Religious Experience* (1902); **Martin Buber**, *I and Thou* (1937); **I. T. Ramsey**, *Religious Language* (1957).

### Three Theophanies, One Experience

| Text | Experience |
|---|---|
| The Burning Bush (Ex 3:2-6) | Something very strange |
| The Transfiguration (Mt 17:1-13) | Glory and Brightness |
| Emmaus (Lk 24:13-35) | Hearts Burning |

> [!quote] I. T. Ramsey
> "Theology uses ordinary language in an extraordinary way."

### RELIGIOUS SYMBOL

**Religious Symbol** = the Meeting-Point between the Human and the Divine. A Symbol both **points beyond itself** and **participates in what it points to**.

> [!example] The two Examples the Chapter gives
> **Jesus Christ**, supreme Symbol of Christianity: in him the Divine and the Human meet and unite. Jesus is not merely a Pointer to God; he is God-Incarnate, the **Sacrament of God**.
>
> **The Eucharist**, Symbol and Sacrament of the Presence of Christ in the Church: it both signifies and *is* the Body of Christ, mediating a Presence that already exists prior to the Celebration.

Since God's Self-Communication reaches us only through Mind, Reason, Body and Sense-Perception, both Natural Theology and Christian Theology have, in the end, a **Symbolic Function**.

## D. DEFINITION IS A SERIES, NOT A SENTENCE

Nothing is defined once. Multiply the Angles, attribute each Angle to an Authority, then synthesize.

### 1. THEOLOGIA (Etymology)

*Theos* (God) + *Logos* (Word, Discourse, Science). **Plato and Socrates** used the Term to criticise the Greek Poets on the gods. **Aristotle** used it for his Metaphysics or First Philosophy. **Clement of Alexandria and Origen** first applied it to Trinitarian and Christological Discussion. With the Scholastics and the rise of the University, Theology became an academic Discipline alongside Arts, Medicine and Law.

### 2. FAITH SEEKING UNDERSTANDING (Anselm, 1033-1109)

***Fides Quaerens Intellectum.*** *Credo ut Intelligam*: I believe in order to understand.

Theology is not Philosophy of God, nor Sociology, Psychology or Phenomenology of Religion, however scientific those are. Its Starting Point is **Faith and the revealed Word of God**, not merely observable Data.

Anselm balances two Errors:

| Error | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Fideism** | Accepts everything revealed by God without any Attempt to scrutinise it by Reason |
| **Rationalism** | Rejects everything not clearly grasped by Reason |

> [!quote] Fides et Ratio 73
> "Theology's source and starting point must always be the word of God revealed in history, while its final goal will be an understanding of that word."

*Fides qua* (Faith as Trust) grounds *Fides quae* (Faith as Content). And Faith is never a finished Product, for a **Pilgrim Character belongs to the very Nature of the Church**. Every Generation must give its own "account of the truth of our hope" (1 Pet 3:15).

### 3. LEX ORANDI, LEX CREDENDI (The Eastern Way)

The Law of Praying is the Law of Believing. Possibly coined by **Prosper of Aquitaine**, fifth century.

Where the West was rational, academic and philosophical, the East was Biblical, Patristic and Liturgical. Liturgy is, for the East, the main ***Locus Theologicus***; God is known not by rational Discourse but by Contemplation (UR 17).

The Dictum runs in reverse also: *Lex Credendi Lex Orandi*. Faith produces Prayer as much as Prayer produces Faith-Formulation.

> [!info] The Indian Lens
> The Eastern Instinct, God known by Contemplation rather than Discourse, resonates with the Indian **Bhakti** and **Jnana** Traditions, where *Darshan* and *Puja* are themselves ways of Knowing. The Indian Seminarian formed in both a Scholastic Classroom and a Devotional Home already lives this Polarity before he opens a Textbook.

### 4. THEOLOGICAL ANTHROPOLOGY (Karl Rahner)

> [!quote] Karl Rahner, Theological Investigations, vol. 9, p. 28
> "Whatever we say about God says something about us, and whatever we say about ourselves says something about God... God and Humanity are correlative Terms."

The Object of Theology is not God as such but Humankind as related to God, in its Transcendental Openness, its *potentia oboedientialis*. This is Theology **from Below**, complementing Theology **from Above**. It is not Reductionism: we speak of the God revealed to us and received in Faith, not of a God who is our own Creation. → [[04 - Selected Models of Theology]]

### 5. CRITICAL REFLECTION ON CHRISTIAN PRAXIS (Gustavo Gutierrez)

> [!quote] Gustavo Gutierrez, A Theology of Liberation (1974), 6-15
> "Theology as a critical reflection on Christian praxis in the light of the Word."

The Shift is from Orthodoxy alone to **Orthopraxis**. The Church finds itself by losing itself, living "the joys and the hopes, the griefs and the anxieties of men of this age" (GS 1). Liberation Theology is not a separate Branch but **a new Way of doing Theology**.

### 6. THEOLOGY AS HERMENEUTICS

Historical Consciousness (GS 5) makes Pluralism and Change inevitable. Four possible Responses to the resulting Uncertainty:

| Response | Claim |
|---|---|
| **Nihilism** | Truth can never be known |
| **Relativism** | Everything is equally true and false |
| **Fundamentalism** | My Position alone is absolutely true, and I impose it |
| **Hermeneutics** | The Past is continually reinterpreted in Dialogue with the Present |

> [!quote] Gaudium et Spes 4
> "The Church has the duty of scrutinizing the signs of the times and of interpreting them in the light of the Gospel."

The Double Movement: the Gospel questions the Situation, and the Situation questions the Gospel so that Faith itself may be reinterpreted.

### 7. INTERPRETATION OF THE CHRISTIAN STORY (Mythos)

Theology begins not with a speculative System but with the **Christian Story**: God entering human History in Jesus Christ. Some prefer *Mythos*, which designates "that set of symbols, rituals, narratives and beliefs which taken together announce and mediate the presence and action of the Divine in the life of a community of persons" (**Theodore W. Jennings Jr.**, *Introduction to Theology*, London: SPCK, 2ff).

### 8. THE WORKING DEFINITION

> [!important] Memorise this for the Oral Examination
> "Christian theology is a **systematic** and **critical interpretation** on the meaning of human life and reality in general **from the perspective of the Revelation in Jesus Christ** on the one hand, and a **reinterpretation of Christian Faith** on the other, in the light of the **new experience and context of the changing realities** of the world, **in and by the believing community**."

Unpack the five weight-bearing Words:

- **Systematic**: a coherent Whole, each Part verified by its Relation to the Whole
- **Critical**: prophetic, challenging Faith and Praxis to become what they ought to be
- **Revelation in Jesus Christ**: the Perspective, enshrined in Bible and Tradition
- **Reinterpretation**: Faith itself is challenged by the Sciences and by new Experience
- **In and by the believing community**: the Theologian is Spokesperson of the Community, even in Prophetic Criticism, never independent of it

## E. CREATIVE POLARITIES IN THEOLOGIZING

The signature Reasoning Form of this course. State the Thesis, state the Counter-pull with **"But"**, and hold both. Drawing on **Gerald O'Collins**, *Fundamental Theology* (London: DLT, 1981), 5-31.

**1. Mystery and Intelligibility.** Faith-Content can be rationally understood. **But** God's Mystery always eludes complete Comprehension; Theological Language remains Symbolic, never a literal Photograph of God.

**2. Identity and Change.** The Identity of Christian Theology rests on the Christ-Event, its Confession and Witness. **But** the Church, being in History, needs continuous Reform of its Formulations, Structures and Praxis.

**3. Committed and Critical.** Theology is a committed Engagement; a Non-Believer may be a Scholar of Religion but not a Theologian. **But** Theology is also a critical Activity, examining Belief in the light of contemporary Experience.

**4. Community and Individual Theologian.** The Christian Community is the real *Locus Theologicus*. **But** Reform is sparked by the prophetic Gift of individual Theologians who challenge the Community from within.

**5. One and Many.** There is a Core, the Christ-Event, and a common Heritage: Scripture, Baptism, Eucharist. **But** Catholic, Orthodox, Protestant and Pentecostal Traditions generate an accepted Pluralism.

> [!quote] RV 1:164:46
> *Ekam sat viprah bahudha vadanti.* Truth is One; the Sages call it by many Names.

**6. Universal and Contextual.** All Theology, even the *theologia perennis* of Aquinas, is conditioned by its own Time. **But** this is no Defect; it is the Root of every Contextual Theology.

> [!example] Aquinas was a Product of his Time
> The new Synthesis Aquinas built with Aristotelian Philosophy was so controversial in his own Day that **his Books were burned by the Bishop of Paris soon after his Death.**
>
> Hence no Theology is perennial. Hence Liberation, Feminist, Black, **Dalit** and **Tribal** Theologies.

**7. Orthodoxy and Heterodoxy.** Fidelity to the Apostolic Faith is a perennial Concern. **But** every Articulation, once fixed in Word or Image, becomes limited; Heterodoxy, though to be resisted, has historically carried new Insight. Example: Luther's *Sola Fide* pushed the Church towards a more integral Understanding of Faith.

**8. Orthodoxy and Orthopraxis.** Correct Doctrine matters. **But** in a Context of real Theological Pluralism no single cultural Formulation can claim exclusive Orthodoxy without imposing one Culture on others.

> All Theories are tested by Praxis; Practice in turn challenges the Theories that become inadequate.

Is the Burning Bush not itself a Polarity held together? Fire that burns and does not consume. Nearness and Otherness. Holy Ground and Sent Mission (Ex 3:5, 10).

## F. THEOLOGY AND OTHER SCIENCES

*Scientia* = a System of clear, precise, valid Knowledge. Theology qualifies: the systematic Study of Christian Faith with its own Method and Principles.

**Three Reasons all Sciences are related:** all explain the same Reality from different Angles; all Truth comes from the one God, so Reality has an underlying Ontological Coherence; and it is the same Human Mind that integrates every Branch of Knowledge. **No Science, including Theology, may claim Absolutism.**

### Theology and Philosophy

Philosophy was long called the ***ancilla theologiae***, the Handmaid of Theology.

| Philosophy used | By whom | To explain |
|---|---|---|
| Platonic and Neo-Platonic, the *Logos* | Origen, Clement of Alexandria, Augustine, Bonaventure | Creation, Incarnation, Redemption |
| Aristotelian, *Nature* and *Person* | Thomas Aquinas, Albert the Great | Christology |
| Existentialist | Rudolf Bultmann, Karl Rahner | Christian Anthropology |
| **Vedanta** | **Brahmabandhav Upadhyaya, Swami Abhishiktananda, Raimundo Panikkar** | **The Trinity as *Saccidananda*** |

> [!info] The Indian *ancilla theologiae*
> This is the Chapter's own Answer to the Question, what would an Indian Handmaid of Theology look like. Not Aristotle or Plato alone, but the **Vedantic Categories already native to this Soil**, placed at the Service of the Christian Mystery exactly as the Greek Fathers once placed the *Logos* at the Service of Creation and Incarnation.
>
> *Sat* + *Chit* + *Ananda* = Being, Consciousness, Bliss = **Saccidananda**. → [[04 - Selected Models of Theology]]

### Theology and the Social Sciences

Classical Western Theology sought mainly to **understand** Reality. The contemporary Contextual Theologies, Political Theology, Theology of Hope and Liberation Theology, use Sociology, Anthropology, Economics and Politics as Tools to **transform** Reality.

### Theology and the Religious Sciences

Sociology, Psychology, Phenomenology and Anthropology of Religion supply empirical Data. **No Area of Human Experience is excluded from Theological Reflection.**

> [!quote] Gaudium et Spes 36
> "Methodological research in all branches of knowledge... can never conflict with the faith, because the things of the world and the things of faith derive from the same God."

## G. THE EIGHT GOALS OF THEOLOGIZING

Complementary, never separable, though a particular Context may emphasise one above the others.

1. To **awaken, strengthen and communicate** the Christ-Experience, the founding Experience of the Disciples
2. To **explain, analyse and systematize** Faith so that it may be assimilated ever more deeply
3. To **sift the human Words** of Scripture and uncover the authentic Word of God for Today
4. To exercise an **interpretative and prophetic Role**, making Faith relevant for the present Age
5. To expose the **Unity of Faith** according to the **Hierarchy of Truths** (UR 11), the Foundation being the Mystery of Christ
6. To fulfil a **pastoral Role**, strengthening the Faith of ordinary Believers
7. To serve the **Mission and Witness** of the Church, for a Theology without Missionary Value "is not worth the name"
8. To work for the **Transformation of the World into the Kingdom of God**, the Community of Communities built on Love, Justice, Peace, Equality and Harmony

## CLOSE: THE BUSH THAT SENDS

We opened with the Bush that burns and is not consumed. The Chapter's own Itinerary, from the God-Question through Symbol through the sevenfold Definition through the Polarities to the Goals, is itself an Enactment of the Turn of Moses: "I will turn aside and see this great sight" (Ex 3:3).

Theology begins in this Turning Aside, in Wonder before Holy Ground on which the Theologian already stands. And it ends, as the Encounter of Moses ended, in a Sending: **"Come, I will send you" (Ex 3:10).**

No Definition given above, from *Fides Quaerens Intellectum* to Critical Reflection on Praxis, is complete without this final Turn towards Mission.
