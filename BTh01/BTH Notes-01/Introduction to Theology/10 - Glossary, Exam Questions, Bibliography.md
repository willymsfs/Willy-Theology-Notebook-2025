# 10 — Glossary, Exam Questions, Bibliography

---

## PART I: GLOSSARY

### Latin and Greek

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| ***Actus Purus*** | Pure Act; a Name for God in impersonal Theistic Systems |
| ***Ancilla theologiae*** | The Handmaid of Theology; the classical Title of Philosophy under Aquinas |
| ***Anamnesis*** | Conscious existential Reference to one's Origin; Rahner's word for Scripture in the Church |
| ***Credere quod / credere in*** | Believing **that** (assent to Propositions) / believing **in** (living Relationship). Barth requires the second |
| ***Credo ut intelligam*** | "I believe in order to understand" — Anselm |
| ***Deus ex machina*** | The Stopgap God, called on where human Effort fails. Rejected by Bonhoeffer |
| ***Diakonia*** | Service; the Church serving Christ in the poor and suffering (Mt 25) |
| ***Ex cathedra*** | "From the Chair" of Peter; the Pope teaching with the Charism of Infallibility |
| ***Fides qua / fides quae*** | Faith as Trust, the act of believing / Faith as Content, what is believed |
| ***Fides quaerens intellectum*** | "Faith seeking Understanding" — Anselm's Definition of Theology |
| ***Galyata*** | Syriac: Instances of divine Self-Manifestation through visible Creation (Ephraem) |
| ***Kerygma*** | The Proclamation; the core Announcement of the Gospel |
| ***Leitourgia*** | Liturgy; the Church professing Faith in Sacraments and Prayer |
| ***Lex orandi, lex credendi*** | "The Law of Praying is the Law of Believing" — the Eastern Approach |
| ***Locus theologicus*** | A "theological Place": a Source from which Theology draws |
| ***Martyria*** | Witness; the Church announcing the Good News |
| ***Norma normans non normata*** | "The Norm that norms and is not normed" — Sacred Scripture |
| ***Paradosis*** | Tradition; that which is handed on |
| ***Potentia oboedientialis*** | The Capacity of the Human Being to receive God's Self-Communication (Rahner) |
| ***Praxis*** | Committed Action; the Starting-Point and Return of Liberation Theology |
| ***Preparatio evangelica*** | Preparation for the Gospel |
| ***Quaestio*** | The Scholastic Method of disputed Question, producing the *Summae* |
| ***Scientia*** | Science: a System of clear, precise and valid Knowledge |
| ***Sensus fidei / sensus fidelium*** | The supernatural Sense of Faith of the whole People of God (LG 12) |
| ***Sola Scriptura / Solus Deus*** | "Scripture Alone" / "God Alone" — the Reformation Principles |
| ***Theologia universalis*** | One universal Theology applicable everywhere; the pre-Vatican II Assumption |
| ***Via inventionis / via doctrinae*** | The Way of Discovery / the Way of Exposition (Aquinas) |
| ***Via antiqua / via moderna*** | The Ancient Way of the Scholastics / the Modern Way of Ockham |

|---|---|
| ***Advaita*** | Non-duality; the Vedantic Teaching of Shankara |
| ***Adivasi*** | "Original Inhabitant"; the Tribal Peoples of India, about 8% |
| ***Ahimsa*** | Non-violence; central to Jainism and Buddhism |
| ***Artha, Kama, Dharma, Moksha*** | The four *Purusharthas*: Wealth, Pleasure, Righteousness, Liberation |
| ***Ashram*** | A Community of Prayer, Study and Manual Labour; the Christian Ashram Movement |
| ***Atman*** | The individual Self or Soul; identical with Brahman in Advaita |
| ***Avatara*** | Descent, Incarnation of the Deity; used by Chakkarai for Christ |
| ***Bhakti*** | Devotion; the Path of Love. Followed by Appasamy |
| ***Brahmachari, Grahastha, Vanaprastha, Sanyasi*** | The four *Ashramas* or Life-Stages |
| ***Brahman*** | The one Absolute Reality of the Vedas |
| ***Charvaka*** | The non-theistic School within Hinduism |
| ***Dal*** | The Root of "Dalit": to break, to cut, to oppress |
| ***Dalit*** | "The oppressed People"; the self-chosen Name of the Scheduled Castes |
| ***Darshana*** | A School or "Viewpoint" of Indian Philosophy; there are six classical |
| ***Guna*** | The three Qualities of Matter in Sankhya: *sattva, rajas, tamas* |
| ***Han*** | Korean: repressed Anger; central to Minjung Theology |
| ***Harijan*** | "People of God"; the Name given to the Dalits by Gandhiji |
| ***Hindutva*** | The Ideology seeking a Hindu Rashtra with one homogeneous Culture |
| ***Jnana*** | Knowledge, Wisdom; a *marga* to Liberation |
| ***Karma*** | Action; as *nishkama karma*, selfless Action, a *marga* |
| ***Kudumi*** | The Tuft of Hair worn by de Nobili as a Brahmin *sanyasi* |
| ***Marga*** | A Path or Way to Liberation: *jnana*, *bhakti*, *karma* |
| ***Maya*** | The World of Appearance from which one must be liberated |
| ***Minjung*** | Korean: "the powerless people" on the Periphery |
| ***Moksha*** | Final Liberation |
| ***Neti, Neti*** | "Not This, Not This"; the apophatic Hindu Way |
| ***Panchama*** | "Fifth"; the Caste outside the four, the Outcaste |
| ***Prakriti / Purusha*** | Matter / Soul; the two ultimate Principles of Sankhya |
| ***Puja*** | Ritual Worship in popular Hinduism |
| ***Sadhana*** | Spiritual Discipline; the Indian Ideal of Education |
| ***Saccidananda*** | *Sat* + *Chit* + *Ananda*: Being, Consciousness, Bliss; used for the Trinity |
| ***Samskara*** | The sacramental Rites of Life, about forty in number |
| ***Sanyasi*** | The Renouncer; the fourth *Ashrama*. Adopted by de Nobili and Upadhyaya |
| ***Sobornost*** | Russian: Communion in an Organism at once one and multi-personal, spiritual not juridical |
| ***Sudra*** | The fourth Caste, comprising innumerable Sub-Castes |
| ***Swaraj*** | Self-Rule; the Nationalist Movement seen as a Movement for an Indian Church |
| ***Vedanta*** | "The End of the Vedas"; the dominant Philosophical School |

### English Technical Terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Analogy** | Predication by Proportion and Participation; neither Univocal nor Equivocal |
| **Apophatic / Cataphatic** | The Way of Negation / the Way of Affirmation in speaking of God |
| **Collegiality** | The Bishops as one College, with the Pope as Head and Member |
| **Cosmotheandric** | Reality as one, with a triple Dimension: Cosmic, Divine, Human |
| **Dogma** | A propositional Formulation divinely revealed **and** proposed as such by the Magisterium |
| **Doctrine** | Broader than Dogma: any authoritative Formulation, revealed or not |
| **Epistemological Privilege of the Poor** | Because the Poor depend wholly on God, they have privileged Knowledge of God |
| **Fideism / Rationalism** | Faith without Reason / Reason without Faith. Both rejected |
| **Hierarchy of Truths** | Truths vary in their Relation to the Foundation, the Mystery of Christ (UR 11) |
| **Hylemorphism** | The Theory of Matter and Form, used by Rahner for Spirit and Body |
| **Inculturation** | The Incarnation of the Gospel within a Culture, naturally and not artificially |
| **Naturism** | The Oppression of Nature, linked by Ecofeminism to the Oppression of Women |
| **Nominalism** | Only individual Singulars are real; Universals exist only in the Mind (Ockham) |
| **Non-Person** | The poor and oppressed denied Dignity: the Interlocutor of Liberation Theology |
| **Orthodoxy / Orthopraxis** | Right Believing / Right Doing |
| **Pluralism / Relativism** | One Truth from many Angles / no one Truth at all |
| **Sacramental Principle** | God's Self-Communication is normally mediated by created Realities |
| **Supernatural Existential** | Our concrete historical State already raised to the supernatural Level by God's free Call (Rahner) |
| **Symbol** | Points beyond itself **and participates** in what it points to |
| **Synodality** | Authority emerging **from** the Communion of Churches, not imposed **over** them |
| **Transcendental Method** | Uncovering the Conditions of Possibility in the Subject (Rahner) |

## PART II: EXAM QUESTIONS

### A. The textbook's own Review Questions, by Chapter

**Chapter 1** → [[01 - What Is Theology]]
1. Is Theology only a Discourse on God, or a Discourse on the Meaning of being Human, or both?
2. Why do we have several Definitions of Theology? Which do you prefer, and why?
3. Why does Theologizing involve a set of Polarities? What underlies them?
4. Does Theology depend on other Sciences? Is there not a Danger of Relativism?
5. What are the Goals of Theologizing? Classify them in order of Priority.

**Chapter 2** → [[02 - The Sources of Theology]]
6. Is the Faith-Experience of the earliest Community the primary Source, and does that tally with Revelation and Faith as Foundations?
7. What were the Issues in the historical Debate on the Sources? One, two, or three Sources?
8. What are the Roles of the Magisterium, the *Sensus Fidelium* and the Theologians, and their Relationship?
9. What is the Basis for distinguishing Sources from Resources? Is the Distinction valid?
10. What are the Attitudes of the Church today towards other Cultures and Religions?

**Chapter 3** → [[03 - Development and Pluralism in Theology]]
11. Although the Bible is one, there is a Plurality of Theologies in it. Explain.
12. What Issues faced the post-apostolic Communities? What were the main Patristic Trends?
13. What did the Scholastic Debate on Faith and Reason contribute?
14. List the Factors that led to the Disintegration of the Scholastic Synthesis.
15. In what Sense is Vatican II a Turning Point for Catholic Theology?
16. In what does the Novelty of Contextual Theologies consist?

**Chapter 4** → [[04 - Selected Models of Theology]]
17. Taking Ephraem as Exemplar, what are the Advantages and Disadvantages of Symbolism?
18. Aquinas made Theology a Science. Advantages and Disadvantages?
19. How significant is the Liturgy for Schmemann? Has it integrated Life and Worship?
20. What is the Thrust of Barth? Strengths and Weaknesses?
21. Highlight the Contribution of Rahner to the anthropological Dimension.
22. How is the Praxis Model a Critique of traditional Approaches?
23. Has Abhishiktananda sufficiently taken account of the Community Dimension?

**Chapter 5** → [[05 - The Criteria of Christian Theological Reflection]]
24. Why does Theology need a Criterion of Authenticity?
25. Compare the Orthodox, Protestant and Roman Catholic Emphases.
26. How does the Church understand the Role of the Magisterium in authenticating a theological Assertion?
27. Describe the Gradations of Doctrine and the Assent due to each.
28. What is the Role of the professional Theologian?
29. Trace the Parameters of the Catholic theological Enterprise.

**Chapter 6** → [[06 - Theologizing in the Indian Context]]
30. What are the salient Features of Indian Society today?
31. Is the Diversity of the Indian Churches a Blessing or a Problem?
32. Compare the Approach of the St. Thomas Christians and of Robert de Nobili.
33. How should we theologize in the socio-economic and political Context of India?
34. Is there one typical Method for theologizing in India, or many?

### B. Synthetic Questions across Chapters

35. **Trace the Burning Bush (Ex 3) through the whole Course.** Show how one Text can carry a Definition, a Doctrine of Sources, a Criterion, and a Missionary Mandate.
36. "Theology is one Enterprise open to many Realizations." Defend this against the Charge of Relativism, using Chapters 3 and 5.
37. Compare **Aquinas** and **Abhishiktananda** as Models. What does each gain, and what does each risk?
38. Trace the Concept of *Locus Theologicus* from Chapter 2 through Schmemann in Chapter 4 to the Cry of the Poor in Chapter 6.
39. "The Gospel has not yet fully encountered the Indian genius." Assess this Verdict using Chapter 6, Sections A and B.
40. Show how the **Sensus Fidelium** (Chapter 2), the **Corporate Reception of Doctrine** (Chapter 5) and **Dalit Theology** (Chapter 6) form one continuous Argument about who does Theology.
41. Compare the **Translation Model** critique in Chapter 6 with the **Universal and Contextual** Polarity in Chapter 1.
42. Why does the Catholic Tradition reject both **Fideism** and **Rationalism**? Illustrate from Anselm, Ockham, Barth and Abhishiktananda.
