# 06 — Theologizing in the Indian Context

> **Chapter 6 of the textbook** · the Indian Churches, our Cultures and Religions, our Poor

> [!quote] Ex 3:7, 10
> "I have seen the affliction of my people... I have heard their cry... Come, I will send you."

Every preceding Chapter has led here. The whole Course now turns to one Question.

> [!important] The governing Question of this Chapter
> When the Gospel met the Greeks, the Romans and the Syrians, it **became** Greek, Roman and Syrian Theology through a real **incarnational Process**. Individual Churches were born of that Encounter.
>
> **Did the same thing happen when the Gospel met India?**

## A. THE DIFFERENT ECCLESIAL TRADITIONS IN INDIA

### 1. THE ST. THOMAS CHRISTIANS

According to Tradition the Apostle **St. Thomas** reached the South Indian Coast with Arabian Traders in **52 AD**, preached the Gospel, and the Church was firmly established in India **from the first Century itself**. These Christians were **highly respected by all, especially by the Rulers of the Country**.

> [!quote] The historians' formula
> **"Hindu in culture, Christian in faith and Syrian in worship."**

They made **no substantial Break with their cultural Traditions**, which were typically Hindu. In social and cultural Practice it was typically an **Indian Church**. But in Worship it became Chaldean or Syrian, the Result of a **Persian Connection**: a Colony of Christian Immigrants arrived in Malabar in the **fourth Century**, bringing their Clergy and Bishops, and Persian Bishops gradually came to rule the St. Thomas Christians.

> [!warning] This ancient Apostolic Church is today divided into EIGHT Churches
> **1. Syro-Malabar Church** — the most ancient, **always maintained Communion with Rome**; about **5 million**; now a **Major Archiepiscopal Church** with its own Synod and legitimate Autonomy
> **2. Syrian Orthodox Jacobite Church** and **3. Malankara Orthodox Syrian Church** — one Church until **1970**, when they divided over Allegiance to the Patriarch of Antioch or an independent Indian Orthodox Church; together about **2 million**
> **4. Mar Thoma Church** — from the CMS-influenced Reform of **Abraham Malpan**; episcopal Ordination of **Mar Athanasius Mathew, 1843**; about 900 Parishes
> **5. Syro-Malankara Catholic Church** — a **Reunion Movement**: in **1930** a group led by **Mar Ivanios** and **Mar Theophilus** entered Communion with Rome, keeping the Antiochene Liturgy; about 5 lakh
> **6. Chaldean Syrian Church (Church of the East)** — about 20,000 around Trichur
> **7. Independent Syrian Church of Malabar (Thozhiyur)** — from **Mar Koorilos**, 1772-1773
> **8. St. Thomas Evangelical Church of India** — separated from the Mar Thoma Church in **1961**

> [!info] Why the first Division happened
> Portuguese and other western Missionaries arriving in the sixteenth Century **interfered in the ecclesiastical Matters** of the St. Thomas Christians and began to **Romanize and Latinize** this ancient Church, which the Native Christians resented.
>
> The Missionaries had their own Understanding: they saw unchristian Practices based on the Caste System, and Nestorian Tendencies from the Persian Link. The tragic Consequence was **Division**: one Group continued Communion with Rome; the other broke Communion and affiliated to the **Patriarch of Antioch in 1665**, known as **Jacobites**, after **Mar Jacob Baradaeus** who reformed the Antiochene Church in the sixth Century.

### 2. THE LATIN RITE CATHOLIC CHURCH IN INDIA

When the Portuguese arrived at the end of the fifteenth Century, **no Traces of Christian Communities remained** in India except the ancient Church of the St. Thomas Christians.

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| c. 1518 | **Franciscans** first arrive, working in Goa, Bassein, Cochin, Quilon |
| **1534** | First **Bishopric established in Goa** |
| **1542** | **St. Francis Xavier** arrives in Goa: **the Apostle of Latin Christianity in India** |
| 1606 | **Robert de Nobili** begins at Madurai |
| **1886** | The **Hierarchy for India** established: 8 Provinces, 19 Dioceses, 3 Vicariates |

Today the **biggest Church in India**: **114 Dioceses**, about **10 million** Members, concentrated in Kerala, Tamil Nadu, Mumbai, Mangalore, Goa and the Tribal areas of central and northeastern India.

> [!important] The Catholic Church in India is a Communion of THREE Individual Churches
> **Syro-Malabar · Latin · Syro-Malankara**
>
> The **CBCI** was established in **1944**. Following the Letter of **John Paul II in 1987**, the CBCI met in **1988** and restructured into **three separate Episcopal Bodies**, while the CBCI itself remains an **overarching Body** for supra-ritual and common Concerns. → [[05 - The Criteria of Christian Theological Reflection]], Parameter 4

### 3. PROTESTANT CHURCHES IN INDIA

During the Colonial Period nearly all Protestant Churches of Europe and America established themselves: Lutherans, Presbyterians, Anglicans, Baptists, Methodists, Congregationalists, Salvation Army, Mennonites, Brethren, Disciples of Christ, Pentecostals.

> [!tip] The first Fruit of the modern Ecumenical Movement in India
> **Church of South India (CSI)**, formed **1947**, uniting **four** Churches: Anglicans, Presbyterians, Congregationalists, Methodists. Today **21 Dioceses**, about **1.5 million**.
>
> **Church of North India (CNI)**, formed **1970**, uniting **seven** Churches, adding Baptists, Disciples and Brethren. Today **23 Dioceses**, about 7-8 lakh.

Outside these Unions: the American-origin **Baptist Churches** of the North East and Andhra Pradesh, about **3 million**; and nine or ten **Lutheran Churches**, federated since **1975** as the **UELCI**, about 1 million.

### 4. CONCLUSION TO SECTION A

> [!warning] The honest Assessment
> Christians in India are **a tiny Minority, only about 2.5%** of the Population: about 25 million out of nearly 1000 million. The Churches are **diverse and remain divided**. The Ecumenical Movement in India **"seems to be very feeble."** The Roman Catholic Church has not joined the **NCCI**.
>
> The biggest Problem: many Indian Churches **still carry the Label of their foreign Origin**, Latin, Syrian, Antiochean, Anglican, Lutheran.
>
> **"The Gospel has not yet fully encountered the Indian genius."** Such a Fecundation, Symbiosis or Incarnation of the Gospel in India is **the greatest Challenge of all the Indian Churches.**

## B. THEOLOGIZING IN OUR CULTURAL, PHILOSOPHICAL AND RELIGIOUS REALITIES

In the Indian Tradition, Culture, Philosophy and Religion form **one integrated Reality**. We may distinguish them theoretically; we cannot separate them.

### 1. CULTURAL HORIZONS

Indian History witnesses a **Confluence of Cultures**: the original **Dravidian** Peoples, themselves many Tribes and Clans; then the **Aryan** Migrations from Central Asia. With the Consolidation of the **Caste System** the higher Castes came to control the socio-economic, literary, philosophical and religious Traditions.

Hence today: a **classical and elitist Culture** of the upper Castes, and **several subaltern Cultures** of the Tribal and Dalit Groups. And yet also an overarching **common Indian Ethos**.

> [!important] THE COSMOTHEANDRIC VISION
> India understands Reality as **one, with a triple Dimension: COSMIC, DIVINE and HUMAN**, which cannot be separated.
>
> A holistic and integral View of Life, where there is **no Dichotomy between the sacred and the secular, the spirit and the body**. Life in Harmony with Nature, everything perceived as divine and as one integrated Whole.

| The Four Goals (*Purusharthas*) | The Four Stages (*Ashramas*) |
|---|---|
| *Artha* — Wealth | *Brahmachari* — Student |
| *Kama* — Pleasure | *Grahastha* — Householder |
| *Dharma* — Righteousness | *Vanaprastha* — Forest-Dweller |
| *Moksha* — Final Liberation | *Sanyasi* — Renouncer |

> [!tip] The ancient Educational Ideal, still worth recovering
> Education as ***sadhana***, a sacred Discipline. Rigour. The personal Communication of experiential Knowledge from **Guru** to Student. Harmony and Communion with the whole of Nature.
>
> **In the Indian Tradition, Education is not a Process of gathering Information but a Process of Transformation.** A *Guru* is one who has realized himself or herself, and only such a Person can lead Students to realize the Meaning of their Life.

### 2. PLURALITY OF RELIGIONS

India is the **Cradle of many Religions**.

**Tribal Religions.** The most ancient, prehistoric. **Adivasis** are about **8%** of the Population. Tribal Religions are ***sui generis*** and are **not part of Hindu Religion**, though the fundamentalist Hindu Movement today tries by all means to co-opt the Tribals into the Hindu Fold. Most of the frontier Tribes of the North East accepted Christianity; so did many in the Chotanagpur area.

**Vedic Religion / Hinduism.** Reality is **One**, called **Brahman**, whom the Sages call by different Names. **Brahma** the Creator, **Vishnu** the Preserver, **Shiva** the Destroyer: three Aspects of the One Supreme Reality. **Rama** and **Krishna** are the most important *avatars* of Vishnu.

> [!info] The Indian Conception of History
> For Hinduism, **History is not linear like a flowing River, but like a Pool of Water.** Waves and Ripples appear, but the Pool remains unchanged. From this World of Appearance, ***maya***, and the Chain of Births and Deaths, Humans must be liberated: ***moksha***, attained by different ***margas***: **jnana** (Knowledge), **bhakti** (Devotion), **karma** (selfless Action).

**Popular Hinduism** differs from the philosophical Religion of the Scriptures: daily Sacrifices, Temple Worship, about **forty sacramental Rites (*samskaras*)** from Birth to Death, Pilgrimage, bathing in holy Rivers, Worship at Trees, Rivers and Hills.

> [!tip] The Modern Hindu Renaissance
> Pioneers: **Ram Mohan Roy, Dayanand Saraswati, Ramakrishna Paramahamsa, Vivekananda, Mahatma Gandhi, Aurobindo, Vinoba Bhave.**
> Abolished or reformed: *sati*, Child Marriage, Temple Prostitution, Animal Sacrifice, Polygamy, many Practices of ritual Impurity and Untouchability.
> Factors: Contact with Western Civilization and **Christianity**, English Education, and the Sense of **Social Justice**.

**Jainism and Buddhism** were Reform and Protest Movements **from within** Hinduism, rejecting extreme Ritualism and rigid Casteism, preaching perfect **Equality** of all Humans, ***ahimsa***, and Liberation from all Suffering (*dukkha*) by Self-Enlightenment.

**Zoroastrianism, Judaism, Christianity, Islam** came from outside; **Sikhism** arose through the Reform of the ten Gurus. Today **12% Muslim, 2.5% Christian**.

> [!important] INDIAN SECULARISM IS NOT WESTERN SECULARISM
> Secularism in the West very often meant the **Denial of Religion**.
>
> **Indian Secularism is not the Negation of God, Religion and Faith, but the EQUAL TREATMENT meted out to all Religions on the part of the State.** It is the Acceptance of religious Pluralism and the Freedom to believe, practise and propagate any Religion.

> [!warning] The Threat
> Indian Secularism faces serious Threats from religious Fundamentalism and Communalism, especially the **Hindutva** Ideology, which wants a **"Hindu Rashtra with one homogeneous culture"**, reducing religious Minorities to **second class Citizens**.

### 3. OUR PHILOSOPHICAL HERITAGE

Philosophical Speculation began in India **much before** such Speculation began in Greece and China; the early **Upanishads** already contain it. Two Trends coexist throughout: the Absolute as **impersonal**, and the Absolute as a **personal Deity**.

#### The Six Classical Schools (Darshanas)

| School | Teaching |
|---|---|
| **Nyaya** | The School of **Logic**: clear and logical Thinking as essential for Salvation |
| **Vaisheshika** | Linked to Nyaya; **Dualism of Matter and Soul**; Salvation by fully realizing the Difference |
| **Sankhya** | Dualistic: ***prakriti*** (Matter) and ***purusha*** (Soul); Matter shaped by the three ***gunas***: *sattva, rajas, tamas* |
| **Yoga** | Spiritual, mental and physical **Discipline** and Meditation for Union with the Absolute |
| **Mimamsa** | Originally the School of **Vedic Exegesis**, defending the Vedas |
| **Vedanta** | **The most important**, which in fact replaced all the others |

> [!important] ADVAITA VEDANTA, and SACCIDANANDA
> **Shankara** (ninth century AD) is its great Teacher. Only the Absolute, **Brahman**, is wholly and truly real. The individual Self, ***atman***, **is identical with Brahman**. When this Identity is realized in one's innermost Consciousness, one attains Salvation.
>
> By selfless Action, Piety and Meditation the Person attains real Wisdom (***jnana***), the Layers of Existence are peeled off one by one, Illusion is removed, and one discovers one's real Identity as Brahman, which consists of **Being, Consciousness and Bliss**:
>
> ***Sat + Chit + Ananda = SACCIDANANDA***
>
> Later **Ramanuja, Madhva, Vallabha** qualified this: the Absolute Brahman is a **personal Deity** worthy of Devotion, Praise and Love; and the individual Soul is **distinct** from the Supreme Being, though part of the divine Essence.

The Heritage is **not reducible** to the six Systems. The Philosophies of the **Tribal Religions, Buddhism, Jainism, Islam and Sikhism** must be seriously reckoned with, along with modern Indian Philosophy shaped by contact with the West.

> [!question] The Chapter's own probing Questions. Sit with these.
> India has a Plurality of Cultures, Religions and Philosophies. **Which are to be our Dialogue Partners?** The Culture and Religion of the **higher Castes and dominant Groups**, or of the **marginalized and subaltern Groups**?
>
> Vedic Religion and most classical Philosophies are today **rather Remnants of the Past**. Instead of dialoguing with them, shall we not enter into Dialogue with the **popular Religions and the Thought-Patterns of today**?

### 4. THE APPROACHES OF THE ST. THOMAS CHRISTIANS

Did the earliest Apostolic Church in India contribute to an authentically Indian Theology? **Placid Podipara** characterized this Church as **"Hindu (Indian) in culture, Christian in religion and Syro-Oriental in worship."**

> [!warning] The honest Verdict
> Most Historians agree they **could not contribute much** to a specific Indian Christian Theology, unlike the early Christians of Greece, Rome or Syria. Four Reasons:
> 1. **Isolation** in a far corner of South India
> 2. **Historical Dependence** on the East Syrians for Liturgy, Prayers and Administration
> 3. **Easy Accommodation to the Caste System**, which prevented them from identifying with the Struggles of the oppressed and so from exercising their **prophetic Ministry**
> 4. Probably also a lack of adequate theological Training and local ecclesiastical Leadership

> [!important] Yet TWO Contributions, condemned at the Synod of Diamper (1599), are vindicated today
> **1. Salvation in one's own Religion.** They lived in harmonious and positive Relationship with their Hindu Neighbours, and **believed that each one could be saved in one's own Religion.** In the medieval Period this was a radical theological View. **Today it is widely accepted in the Theology of Religions.**
>
> **2. The Legitimacy of distinct Individual Churches.** They rejected the View that the Western and Latin Form of Christianity was the only true and normative Form:
>
> > "There is one law of St. Thomas and another of St. Peter: the Church founded by the one is distinct and different from the Church by the other; **each is immediately from Christ**" (Mundadan, *Paths of Indian Theology*, 40-42).
>
> **This Ecclesiology is well accepted today.** → [[05 - The Criteria of Christian Theological Reflection]]

### 5. ROBERT DE NOBILI, THE PIONEER MISSIONARY

During the Colonial Period western Missionaries generally held a **very negative Attitude** towards Indian Religions and Cultures, seeking to conquer and replace them. **Robert de Nobili**, the Italian Jesuit, was the Exception.

In the **Madurai Mission** he studied **Tamil and Sanskrit**, penetrated Hindu Religion and Indian Culture, and adopted Hindu Customs, very similar to what the St. Thomas Christians had done. He **behaved like a Brahmin *sanyasi***, wearing the **holy Thread**, the ***kudumi*** and **saffron Clothes**, and entered into Debate with the Brahmins.

> [!important] His Claim
> His was **the first serious Attempt to interpret and reformulate Christianity in Indian Categories and Thought-Patterns.** He wanted to show Indian People that by becoming Christian **they would not lose their Culture, Language and Customs.**
>
> But the Church as a whole **was not ready** for such bold Steps of Inculturation. Facing stiff Opposition from Confreres and from Roman Authorities, de Nobili and his Successors **had to discontinue** this Approach.

### 6. THE INDIAN RENAISSANCE

Both a **Rediscovery of the Heritage of India** and a **social Reform of Hinduism**. Christian Ideals and the Inspiration of some Missionaries contributed to the Reforms undertaken by Hindu Leaders.

- **1828**: **Raja Ram Mohan Roy** founds the **Brahmo Samaj**
- **1881**: **Keshab Chandra Sen** breaks away and founds the **New Dispensation (*Navavidhan*)**, a Hindu-Christian Church centred on Christ, "the one who brings God and man together so that they may be **at one**"
- **1883**: **Pratap Chandra Mozoomdar** writes ***The Oriental Christ***, presenting Christ in the oriental Pattern, having realized that **the Christ presented by the Missionaries in western Categories was not appealing to the Indian Mind**

### 7. THE SPIRIT OF NATIONALISM

New Churches and Associations arose with a nationalistic Spirit: the **Hindu Church of the Lord Jesus** (Nadar Christians of Tirunelveli, **1867**), the Bengal Christian Association (1868), the National Church of Madras, the Calcutta Christo Samaj.

**S.K. Datta, K.T. Paul, J.C. Kumarappa** and **Bishop Azariah** saw the national Resurgence as a ***preparatio evangelica***. According to some, **the Christ of the Western Culture has awakened the Christ of the Indian Culture**, and the Movement for **Swaraj** was a Movement for an Indian Church with its own Freedom and Identity.

### 8. SOME PIONEERS OF INDIAN THEOLOGY

> [!tip] Note who began it
> **The pioneering Work in Indian Theology was first initiated mainly by the PROTESTANT Christians.**

| Pioneer | Contribution |
|---|---|
| **K.M. Banerjee**, *Arian Witness* (1875) | Parallels between the Old Testament and the **Vedas**: Christianity fulfils not only the OT but **also the Indian Scriptures** |
| **Parani Andi**, *Are We Not Hindus?* (1889) | Hinduism and Christianity have **Continuity**, not Opposition |
| **Brahmabandhav Upadhyaya** | Bengali Catholic, nationalist, *sannyasi*, Master of Vedanta. **As the Fathers used Greek Philosophy, we in India must use VEDANTA** to make Faith understandable |
| **Sadhu Sundar Singh** | Born Sikh, trained in **Bhakti**; converted by a Vision of the radiant Jesus. A **Mystic**, insisting on the Precedence of **Heart over Head** |
| **Bishop A.J. Appasamy** | Tamil; followed **Bhakti** rather than Advaita; used *Sat, Chit, Ananda* for the Trinity while stressing **our separate Personalities** in Union with God |
| **P. Chenchaiah** (1880-1958) | Christ as the **New Creation, the New Man**; all should become **"other Christs"** through integral Yoga (after Aurobindo) |
| **V. Chakkarai** (1889-1959) | Began from personal Experience of Christ, explained as ***avatara***; related **Christology to Pneumatology**: the Spirit is the Continuation of the Incarnation |

> [!warning] A theological Caution the book itself raises
> Sundar Singh held that he belonged to the **Body of Christ without belonging to the visible Church**. The book judges this View "indeed, very dangerous and theologically debatable." Of course, **Mystics are said to be exceptional People.**

Later Scholars: **Raimundo Panikkar, Stanley J. Samartha, John B. Chethimattam, D.S. Amalorpavadass.**

### 9. THE CHRISTIAN ASHRAM MOVEMENT

Modelled on Hindu and Buddhist Ashrams. **Protestants were the Pioneers**; Catholics gradually joined.

| Protestant | Catholic |
|---|---|
| S. Jesudason, E. Forrester Paton, P. Chenchaiah, Murray Rogers | **Monchanin, Abhishiktananda, Bede Griffiths, Francis Acharya, Vandana, Sara Grant, Amalorpavadass** |

Ashrams are **Meeting-Points of Hinduism and Christianity**, Centres of a **spiritual-contemplative Approach**. The Layout is designed for total Conversion from within: **Prayer, Study and manual Labour**, integrating the classical Paths of ***nishkama karma, jnana* and *bhakti***. → [[04 - Selected Models of Theology]]

> [!important] The Theology of Dialogue born in the Ashrams
> **First**, Christians must be **rooted in their own Faith**, with a strong Commitment to Christ. **Once that Grounding is there**, one remains open to the Experience of other Religions and dives deep into their Depth.
>
> **Once we really encounter other Faiths, we become more aware of the Uniqueness of our own.** Genuine Dialogue leads to **mutual Enrichment and the Deepening of each Faith.**

## C. THEOLOGIZING IN THE SOCIO-ECONOMIC AND POLITICAL REALITIES

> [!quote] The Parallel that governs Section C
> "In the land of Palestine, marked by inequality and injustice, Jesus proclaimed **the good news** to the poor and the downtrodden. **The predicament and call of Jesus' disciples in India today is almost the same.**"

### 1. THE SOCIO-ECONOMIC SCENARIO

**On the one side**: tremendous industrial and economic Progress. India among the **ten most industrialized Countries** within three Decades. The rich Middle Class grew from **10% to about 30%**.

**On the other side**: the Poor have become poorer and their Number has increased.

> [!warning] The Statistic to quote
> Among **174 Countries, India's Place is 128** from the Point of View of **Social Development** (UN Human Development Report, 1997).

**Causes named**: the **Mixed Economy**, in which capital-intensive heavy Industry is undertaken by Government with the Taxpayers' Money while profit-making consumer Goods remain in private Hands. Though the Caste System is constitutionally abolished, **the System remains practically unchanged**, and there is a **Linkage between Class and Caste Discrimination**. **Liberalization, free Markets and Globalization** widen the Split, and Mechanization and Computerization make the poor and uneducated **unwanted and unemployed**, while over-Consumption exploits natural Resources and destroys the Environment.

### 2. THE POLITICAL SITUATION

India has a **written Constitution** with Executive, Legislature and Judiciary providing **Checks and Balances**. The Preamble declares India a **sovereign, socialist, secular and democratic Republic**.

> [!important] Article 25 of the Constitution
> Guarantees not only the Right to Freedom of Conscience and freely to **profess and practise** Religion, but also **the Right to PROPAGATE one's Religion.**

> [!warning] The Threat named plainly
> **Hindutva** is "a very dangerous Ideology" which claims Legitimacy exclusively for the majority Religion and for a **monolithic Culture**, and calls for a **Hindu Rashtra** where Minorities are caricatured as **"foreigners"**, to be **eliminated or reduced to second class Citizens**.
>
> Also named: religious Fanaticism, the **Ayodhya** Conflict, Attempts to **rewrite the Constitution and Indian History**, Manipulation of Educational Policy and Textbooks; coalition Instability, rampant Corruption, and pro-rich Policies.

### 3. DISCRIMINATION AGAINST WOMEN

> [!warning] The List the book gives. Do not soften it.
> **Female foeticide, infanticide, gender Discrimination in Education, Food and Health Care, Restriction of Freedom of Movement, forced Marriages, Wife Battering, Bride Burning and Dowry Deaths, Sexual Abuse, Rape, Prostitution, and Denial of equal democratic Rights.**

Half the Indian Population is Women. And yet in the **13th Parliament, of 543 Members only 47 were Women**: about **8.5%**.

> [!tip] But where the Chance was given
> Under the **73rd Amendment**, Women received **33% Reservation** in local **Panchayat** Elections, and **proved their Mettle by committed Action**. Many Women Panchayat Presidents became well known for their **courageous Action to meet the basic Needs of the People**.

The Case of **Dalit, Tribal and backward-class Women is worse**. Most are Victims of the technological and computer Revolution and of Globalization, having lost Jobs and Livelihood in Agriculture, Fishing and Cottage Industries.

> [!question] The Chapter's own Challenge
> **"Will the Church and theological fraternity listen to the voice of Indian women and respond to their plight?"**

### 4. THE CRY OF THE DALITS, TRIBALS AND OTHER MARGINALIZED

**Dalits and Tribals together form 22.5%** of the total Population.

> [!important] The Word DALIT
> From the Root ***dal***, meaning **"to break", "to cut", "to oppress"**. It means **"the oppressed People"**, and became popular with the Liberation Movement of **Mahatma Jotirao Phule** (nineteenth century) and **Babasaheb Ambedkar** (twentieth century).
>
> Other Names: untouchables, ***panchamas*** (fifth caste), *chandalas*, Scheduled Castes, ***harijans*** (the Name given by Gandhiji).

As **outcastes** they are thrown out of the residential Areas of the four Castes, treated with Contempt, and **segregated socially, religiously and culturally**. Most are **landless**, agricultural Labourers or doing menial Jobs.

> [!warning] THE DOUBLE DISCRIMINATION. This is examinable.
> Some Dalits embraced Christianity **in search of Liberation**. But they are **doubly discriminated, both by the State and by the Church**.
>
> **The State** denies them the Rights and Privileges given to other Dalits **because they embraced Christianity**.
> **In certain Sections of the Church** they are still striving to get equal Treatment and Justice.

**Scheduled Tribes** are **8%**, in two Categories: **frontier Tribes** of the North-East, most of whom embraced Christianity, and **non-frontier Tribes** elsewhere. Many non-frontier Tribals are today **uprooted from their Habitats** by Industry, Mining, Dams, Deforestation and Migration.

> [!question] The theological Question the book puts
> In the Bible the poor, the oppressed and the marginalized are the **"beloved of God"** and those favoured by Christ. **What is the special Mission of the Church towards them? How should Theology be oriented to their Liberation?**

### 5. CRITIQUE OF A PURELY CULTURAL AND RELIGIOUS APPROACH

> [!important] The Turn of the whole Chapter. Understand this Paragraph.
> The earlier Pioneers of Section B largely followed a **TRANSLATION MODEL**: translating Christian Doctrine and western Theology into Indian cultural, philosophical and religious Categories. And for that they had recourse mostly to **the Religion, Scriptures and Philosophies of the UPPER LAYERS of the Hindu Caste Order.**
>
> **However, the vast Majority of the Indian People belong to the *sudras*, the Dalits and the Tribals.**
>
> The relevant Question is therefore: **whom should Theology address itself to?**

The further Questions follow: Is Theology merely Wisdom, an esoteric Knowledge for an otherworldly Salvation? Has the Gospel anything to do with the socio-economic and political Problems of our People?

> [!important] The Answer
> By all the Evidence of the New Testament, **the Salvation which Christ preached BEGINS HERE**, and it affects, reforms and renews radically the socio-economic and political Structures of human Lives, creating a new human Society based on **Justice, Freedom and Equality**.
>
> And yet: Salvation **transcends** all these, and the Structures themselves **need a firm spiritual Grounding** for their own continuous Renewal.

### 6. CHRIST AND OUR SEARCH FOR NEW HUMANITY

Evangelization is **much more than baptizing People and planting the Church**. It is proclaiming the Love of God manifested in Christ, calling for **Communion and Humanization**.

> [!important] The Formula to remember
> **"Authentic humanization is at the same time divinization."**
>
> **Jesus Christ is the Prototype of the New Humanity and the New Creation.** Christ is seen not merely as the Centre of the Church but as **the Centre of the whole Humankind and of the whole Creation.**

| Theologian | Contribution |
|---|---|
| **Paul D. Devanandan** (1901-1962) | Perhaps the **first** to present this Trend: the Kingdom and the New Creation are **not merely trans-temporal and spiritual** but a new Reality **erupted with Christ**, to be realized **here and now**, though not completely |
| **M.M. Thomas** | Lay Theologian of the **Mar Thoma Church**; the most articulate on the Links among Christ, Salvation, Humanization, the Indian Renaissance and modern Secularity |

This requires **collaboration with People of all Religions and Ideologies**, and therefore Dialogue, and therefore a **redefining of Christian Mission** and a different Style of missionary Praxis.

### 7. INDIAN THEOLOGY OF LIBERATION

> [!tip] A Point of Scholarly Honesty
> Similar theological Developments appeared in Asia and Africa **almost parallel** to South America. **We cannot say that all Liberation Theologies were directly influenced by the South American** (see M. Amaladoss, *Life in Freedom: Liberation Theologies from Asia*, 1997).

Three outstanding Names: **Sebastian Kappen, Samuel Rayan, George M. Soares-Prabhu.** The latter two are Bible Scholars who interpreted the Word of God **from the actual Indian Context**: Poverty, Caste, the Marginalization of Dalits, Tribals and Women, and the basic Religiosity of the Indian People.

> [!important] SEBASTIAN KAPPEN: the unique Indian Contribution
> His Tools: critical Study of **Marxism**, Involvement in the Struggles of the marginalized, Interaction with social Activists, Dialogue with other Faiths and Ideologies.
>
> His Conviction: **all Resources belonging to all Religions, Cultures, Ideologies, Sciences and Technology** are to be harnessed for social Transformation.
>
> **Why an Indian Liberation Theology is unique**: we have a **religiously pluralistic Context**, which calls for Collaboration with People of all Faiths, and the use of **all their Resources, and not merely Christian Resources.**
>
> His Starting Point is a new Indian **Christology or "Jesuology"**, placing Christ and his prophetic Protest **in Continuity with the History of socio-religious Movements of Dissent and Revolt in India**, along the line of the **Buddha and the Bhakti Tradition**, in Opposition to sterile Brahmanic Ritualism.

### 8. DALIT THEOLOGY

Many Dalits embraced Christianity as a **mass Protest Movement** against the Caste System. Christianity provided a **new Identity**. But the tragic thing is that **even after becoming Christians their Situation did not improve much**. Many Churches continued the Caste Discrimination, and they lost the State Privileges their non-Christian Counterparts enjoyed. **Dalit Christians thus lost their Identity and human Dignity and became NON-PERSONS.** → [[04 - Selected Models of Theology]]

> [!important] What Dalit Theology IS
> **"Dalit Theology is the cry of the Dalit Christians by which they try to express their Faith in the midst of their Agonies."** A Dalit Version of Liberation Theology.
>
> They **rejected** the Theology couched in classical Indian philosophical and religious Categories, which are **the Language and Perspective of the upper Castes** by which they maintained Superiority.
>
> Instead they began to discover **their own History and their "little traditions"**, theologizing in their own Language: their **Myths, Stories, Poems, Narratives, Folklore and Rituals**, in which an implicit Theology of Liberation already lies.

> [!quote] The Christological Centre
> **The Crucified Jesus** gives Meaning to their miserable Life; he gives them the Power **both to suffer and to revolt**.
> **The Risen Jesus** gives Hope in the midst of Hopelessness. For Jesus, Death was not the End but the Beginning of a new Life **already on the third Day**, and not at the End of History.
>
> **Suffering and Hope are the Texture of Dalit Theology.**

> [!important] The Biblical Re-reading
> **Israel was an oppressed People, a DALIT Group**; the Old Testament is the Story of this Dalit People and the Saga of their Victory. **God himself came to liberate them.**
>
> The New Testament continues that Story: Jesus, Leader and Prophet of **the little ones of Israel**. The Church at its Origins was a **Jesus Movement**, the Community of the little ones to whom the Mystery of the Kingdom was revealed.
>
> **"Where do we find the God of the Bible today? Is He not with the Dalits? Yes, He is the God of the Dalits."**

### 9. WOMEN'S VOICE IN INDIAN THEOLOGY

Theologies past and present are **by and large the Product of a male-dominated Society**, expressing, often unconsciously, male Perspectives and Domination, with the tragic Consequence of the **Subordination, Exploitation and Oppression of Women**.

> [!important] Two Principles, both required
> **The Principle of Complementarity.** If God created *homo*, the Human Being, as **male and female**, they are constitutive and complementary Parts of being human. **Male and female Perspectives, left to themselves, are incomplete, one-sided and liable to Distortions.** Theology must be penetrated by the **Warmth, Intuitions, and the sharing and caring Nature of Women**.
>
> **The Principle of Equality.** Women are **half the World Population and half the Church Membership**. How unfair to deny them equal Partnership. What a tremendous Loss if the Resources and Talents of half the World Population are simply drained off.

**Vandana Mataji** and **Sara Grant** have made significant Contributions from the **Ashram-Life** Point of View: experience-based, with a contemplative Slant, calling for **a Theology from the Heart**.

> [!warning] The book's own Verdict
> There are a number of theologically trained Women in India, and they have formed an Organization. **But their Voice is very feeble.**
>
> **"Indian theology is badly in need of more substantial contribution from the part of women."**

## D. CONCLUSION: PLURALISM AND CONVERGENCE

> [!important] The Convergence
> The **two major Trends** traced in this Chapter, theologizing from the **cultural, philosophical and religious** Point of View (Section B) and theologizing from the **socio-economic and political** Context (Section C), **have merged together in our times**. All Contexts are related, and **an integrated Approach is slowly emerging.**

**Interreligious Dialogue** continues, but its Attention has turned **from Scriptures, Dogmas and Beliefs to concrete common Problems**: Poverty, Illiteracy, Unemployment, Educational Policy, Women's Problems, Environmental Issues, the Threat of Fundamentalism.

> [!tip] The Shift to name in an exam
> **The Dialogue of LIFE has become more important than the Dialogue of religious Doctrines and Beliefs.**
>
> Yet interreligious **Prayer Meetings and Meditations** remain very relevant, especially where there are communal Conflicts, for they **break new Ground and build mutual Confidence**. Prayer Experience and Grace can be **fresh Meeting-Points**.

### On Inculturation

Some say Inculturation is today **"a spent force."** The book disagrees: it is **still important and very relevant**. The Questions are **how** it is to be done, guided and evaluated.

> [!important] Two Corrections to older Inculturation
> **1.** The traditional Method of **mere Translation and Juxtaposition**, especially in terms of the Culture of the **dominant Groups**, is questioned today. The **"smaller cultures and traditions" of the marginalized must be respected** and interacted with.
>
> **2.** Inculturation must be **a natural Process, not something manipulated or artificially created**. When a Faith is lived by the People in Interaction with the concrete Realities of Life, **it will naturally and spontaneously become inculturated.**

### The Challenge and the Signs of Hope

The Challenge to all the Churches is to **join hands with the Nation** by exercising their **diaconal Ministry of Nation-Building**. The Mission of the Church is **primarily the liberative Mission** in the fuller sense, and **the special Option for the Poor and the marginalized should never be compromised.**

> [!tip] Signs of Hope named by the book
> The **Indian Theological Association**, the Organ of the Catholic Theologians of India.
> The Journals: ***Jeevadhara, Vidyajyoti, The Living Word, Journal of Dharma, Bible Bhashyam***.
> New **Centres, Departments and Chairs** in Christian Theology attached to **Indian secular Universities**, so that Theology grows in constant Encounter with other Disciplines.
