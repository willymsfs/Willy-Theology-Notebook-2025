## A. Glossary of German and Latin terms

| Term | Language | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| ***Realsymbol*** | Ger. | Real symbol — the self-realisation of a being in another, in which the being is genuinely present |
| ***Vertretungssymbol*** | Ger. | Representative / arbitrary sign, assigned by convention; the reality is absent |
| ***Vorgriff auf esse*** | Ger./Lat. | Pre-apprehension of being — the unthematic anticipatory reach toward Being in every judgement |
| ***Geist in Welt*** | Ger. | "Spirit in world" — the human as spiritual yet irreducibly material and historical |
| ***Hörer des Wortes*** | Ger. | "Hearer of the Word" — the human as the one who must listen into history for a possible revelation |
| ***Übernatürliches Existential*** | Ger. | Supernatural existential — the universal, unowed orientation of concrete human existence to God's self-communication |
| ***Selbstmitteilung Gottes*** | Ger. | God's self-communication — God gives Godself, not merely a created gift |
| ***Grundaxiom*** | Ger. | Basic axiom — Rahner's Rule on the identity of the economic and immanent Trinity |
| ***Subsistenzweise*** | Ger. | "Manner of subsisting" — Rahner's proposed gloss on "person" in the Trinity |
| ***Ursakrament*** | Ger. | Primordial sacrament — Christ |
| ***Grundsakrament*** | Ger. | Basic sacrament — the Church |
| ***Anonymer Christ*** | Ger. | Anonymous Christian |
| ***Geheimnis*** | Ger. | Mystery — God as permanently incomprehensible, even in the beatific vision |
| ***Urwort*** | Ger. | Primordial word (heart, light, bread, water, birth, death) |
| ***Grundentscheidung*** | Ger. | Fundamental option — the total self-disposal of a life |
| ***Diasporakirche*** | Ger. | Diaspora Church — the minority Church of the future |
| ***Aktive Selbsttranszendenz*** | Ger. | Active self-transcendence — a creature genuinely surpassing itself by God-given power |
| ***Weltkirche*** | Ger. | World Church — the third epoch of Church history |
| ***Ernstfall*** | Ger. | "The serious case" — Balthasar's term for martyrdom as the test of Christian truth |
| ***Entäußerung*** | Ger. | Hegel's self-externalisation — the shadow behind the necessity objection |
| ***Memoria passionis*** | Lat. | Metz's "dangerous memory" of the suffering of Christ and of history's victims |
| ***Conversio ad phantasma*** | Lat. | The intellect's necessary turning to the sense image (Aquinas, ST I q.84 a.7) |
| ***Reditio completa in seipsum*** | Lat. | The complete return of a being to itself — self-consciousness |
| ***Potentia oboedientialis*** | Lat. | Obediential potency — the creature's capacity to receive what it cannot demand |
| ***Natura pura*** | Lat. | Pure nature — for Rahner a *Restbegriff*, a never-realised theoretical construct |
| ***Restbegriff*** | Ger. | Remainder concept — a boundary notion obtained by subtraction, naming a possibility not a reality |
| ***Gratia increata / creata*** | Lat. | Uncreated grace (God's self) / created grace (the resulting quality in the soul) |
| ***Causa quasi-formalis*** | Lat. | Quasi-formal causality — God as inner determining principle without composition |
| ***Sacramenta significando causant*** | Lat. | "The sacraments cause by signifying" |
| ***Opus operatum*** | Lat. | The sacrament's efficacy from the act performed, independent of the minister's worthiness |
| ***Forma corporis*** | Lat. | The soul as the form of the body (Council of Vienne, 1312) |
| ***Oikonomia / theologia*** | Gk. | God's saving action in history / God as God is in Godself |

## B. Sample exam questions

### Short answer (5 marks each)
1. Define *Realsymbol* and distinguish it from *Vertretungssymbol* with one example of each.
2. What is the *Vorgriff auf esse*? Illustrate.
3. State Rahner's Rule and identify which half is disputed.
4. What is the supernatural existential, and what problem does it solve?
5. Explain "quasi-formal causality" and say why the "quasi" is necessary.
6. Distinguish *Ursakrament* from *Grundsakrament*.
7. What does Rahner mean by an *Urwort*? Give three examples.
8. Distinguish eschatology from apocalyptic in Rahner's usage.
9. State Panikkar's objection to "anonymous Christianity."
10. Name Rahner's three epochs of Church history and their two turning points.

### Essay questions (15–20 marks)
1. **"All beings are by their very nature symbolic, because they necessarily express themselves in order to attain their own nature."** Expound this thesis and assess its adequacy as the foundation of a theology of the sacraments.
2. Trace the ontology of the real symbol through **Trinity, Incarnation, Church and sacrament**. Does the elegance of the scheme purchase its plausibility too cheaply?
3. "Rahner's theology of the symbol makes the sacraments **manifest** grace rather than **confer** it." Evaluate this charge with reference to Trent.
4. Discuss **Rahner's Rule**. Does the "vice versa" compromise the freedom of God?
5. Is the **anonymous Christian** a generous inclusivism or a theological annexation? Argue with reference to Balthasar and Panikkar.
6. **Balthasar vs Rahner:** is the disagreement one of method, of content, or of temperament? Which do you find more adequate, and why?
7. "Rahner solved the nature/grace problem by making grace universal." Assess whether **gratuity** survives the solution.
8. Examine the charge that Rahner's **transcendental subject** is abstract and unhistorical (Metz, Kerr, Lindbeck). How far can Rahner answer it?
9. What use, and what limits, does Rahner's theology have for **theology in India**? Answer with reference to at least two Indian or Asian theologians.
10. "The symbol ontology explains fullness of expression but has no account of the **lying symbol**." Test this claim against Rahner's ecclesiology.
11. Assess Rahner's contribution to **Vatican II** and the accuracy of his claim that the Council inaugurates a "World Church."
12. Is Rahner's theology of the **Cross** adequate? Answer with reference to his theology of death and to Balthasar's critique.
13. **"A lying symbol is not an arbitrary sign but a real symbol turned against its reader."** Expound and defend this claim, and show what follows for the Church's response to scandal.
14. Construct a theology of the **anti-sacrament** from Rahner's axiom that the sacraments cause by signifying. Test it against 1 Cor 11:17–34.
15. "Rahner's ontology of the symbol is built to explain plenitude and therefore cannot explain betrayal." Assess.

### Seminar / viva prompts
- Preach a five-minute **mystagogical homily** on an ordinary experience, following the five-step shape in [[14 - Mystagogy, Ignatian Roots and Spirituality]].
- Construct the Rahnerian argument against **caste segregation at the Eucharist** and identify the point at which it needs a doctrine Rahner never wrote.
- Take one popular devotion in your region and analyse it with the *Realsymbol* / *Vertretungssymbol* distinction.

## C. Bibliography

### Primary — Rahner
- Rahner, Karl. **"The Theology of the Symbol."** In *Theological Investigations* IV, trans. Kevin Smyth, 221–252. London: Darton, Longman & Todd, 1966. **[The core text of this unit.]**
- ———. *Spirit in the World*. Trans. William Dych. New York: Herder & Herder, 1968. (*Geist in Welt*, 1939.)
- ———. *Hearer of the Word*. Trans. Joseph Donceel. New York: Continuum, 1994. (*Hörer des Wortes*, 1941.)
- ———. *Foundations of Christian Faith: An Introduction to the Idea of Christianity*. Trans. William Dych. New York: Seabury, 1978. (*Grundkurs des Glaubens*, 1976.)
- ———. *The Trinity*. Trans. Joseph Donceel. London: Burns & Oates, 1970. (*Der dreifaltige Gott*, 1967.)
- ———. *On the Theology of Death*. New York: Herder & Herder, 1961.
- ———. "Christianity and the Non-Christian Religions." *Theological Investigations* V, 115–134.
- ———. "Concerning the Relationship between Nature and Grace." *Theological Investigations* I, 297–317.
- ———. "The Hermeneutics of Eschatological Assertions." *Theological Investigations* IV, 323–346.
- ———. "Anonymous Christians." *Theological Investigations* VI, 390–398.
- ———. "Towards a Fundamental Theological Interpretation of Vatican II." *Theological Investigations* XX, 77–89.
- ———. *Encounters with Silence*. Trans. James Demske. Westminster, MD: Newman, 1960.
- ———, and Herbert Vorgrimler. *Concise Theological Dictionary*. London: Burns & Oates, 1983.

### Secondary — introductions and studies
- Fields, Stephen M., SJ. *Being as Symbol: On the Origins and Development of Karl Rahner's Metaphysics*. Washington DC: Georgetown University Press, 2000. **[The standard study of the symbol ontology; the best defence.]**
- Vorgrimler, Herbert. *Understanding Karl Rahner: An Introduction to His Life and Thought*. London: SCM, 1986.
- Marmion, Declan, and Mary E. Hines, eds. *The Cambridge Companion to Karl Rahner*. Cambridge: CUP, 2005. **[Best single entry point; chapters on symbol, Trinity, religions, critique.]**
- Kilby, Karen. *Karl Rahner: Theology and Philosophy*. London: Routledge, 2004. **[Argues Rahner's theology can be read without accepting the philosophical foundations — very useful for the critique essays.]**
- ———. *Karl Rahner: A Brief Introduction*. New York: Crossroad, 2007.
- Dych, William V., SJ. *Karl Rahner*. London: Continuum, 1992.
- Endean, Philip, SJ. *Karl Rahner and Ignatian Spirituality*. Oxford: OUP, 2001. **[On the Ignatian core.]**
- Egan, Harvey D., SJ. *Karl Rahner: Mystic of Everyday Life*. New York: Crossroad, 1998.
- Conway, Eamonn. *The Anonymous Christian — A Relativised Christianity? An Evaluation of Hans Urs von Balthasar's Criticisms of Karl Rahner*. Frankfurt: Peter Lang, 1993.

> [!tip] If you read only four things
> 1. Rahner, "The Theology of the Symbol," *TI* IV, 221–252.
> 2. Marmion & Hines, *Cambridge Companion to Karl Rahner* — chapters on symbol, Trinity and critique.
> 3. Kilby, *Karl Rahner: Theology and Philosophy* — for a fair, rigorous critical reading.
> 4. Balthasar, *The Moment of Christian Witness* — short, fierce, and the best way to feel the force of the counter-position.
