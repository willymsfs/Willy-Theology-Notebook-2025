# 18 — Rahner in the Indian Context

> [!info] Prev: [[17 - Critical Reflection II - Theological]] · Next: [[19 - Mind Maps and Diagrams]]

> [!important] Why this note exists
> Rahner is read in Indian seminaries because his theology of universal grace made *Nostra Aetate* thinkable, and because his three-epoch thesis authorises Asian theology to be Asian. He is criticised in Indian seminaries because his method starts from a subject that looks nothing like the subject of Indian reality. Hold both.

---

## 1. What Rahner gives Indian theology

| Rahnerian resource | Indian use |
|---|---|
| **Universal supernatural existential** — grace offered to every human being always | Theological ground for taking the faith of the Hindu, Muslim, Sikh, Jain, Buddhist and tribal neighbour seriously as *graced*, not merely as human striving → [[05 - The Supernatural Existential - Nature and Grace]] |
| **Religions as possibly lawful ways** (Thesis 2) | Permits a positive theology of *sanātana dharma*, *bhakti*, Sufi devotion, tribal religion — not merely a theology of tolerated error → [[11 - Anonymous Christians and the Non-Christian Religions]] |
| **World Church / three epochs** | Direct warrant for genuine inculturation: if the Church could shed circumcision, it can shed Latin-European packaging → [[15 - Rahner and Vatican II - The World Church]] |
| **Ontology of the real symbol** | A metaphysics for taking **ritual, gesture, image and body** with full seriousness — precisely what Indian religious life is made of → [[07 - The Theology of the Symbol - The Core Essay]] |
| **God as absolute mystery (*Geheimnis*)** | Deep resonance with apophatic Indian traditions — *neti neti* ("not this, not this," Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad), the inexpressibility of *Brahman* |
| **Mystagogy** | Fits a culture where the *guru* leads the disciple into experience rather than transmitting propositions → [[14 - Mystagogy, Ignatian Roots and Spirituality]] |

> [!example] The symbol ontology and Indian religious practice
> Consider the deep Indian instinct that the sacred is **not represented but present** — in *darśana* (the mutual seeing between devotee and deity), in *prasāda* (food that has been received back from the deity), in the touch of the elder's feet, in the *rangoli* drawn at the threshold, in *namaskāra* where the joined hands *are* the reverence and not a report of it.
> This is much closer to Rahner's *Realsymbol* than to the modern Western "merely symbolic."
> **Apologetic payoff:** to a student formed by that instinct, the Catholic doctrine of the Eucharist and of sacramental efficacy is not strange but obvious. The estrangement is a modern Western one, and Rahner's essay is written to cure it.
> **Required caution:** the analogy is at the level of a *sensibility about symbol*, not an identity of doctrine. Do not flatten distinct theologies of presence into one another; that would repeat the very error the theology-of-religions critics identify.

## 2. What Indian theologians say against him

### Raimon Panikkar — the reversibility test
Panikkar's celebrated riposte: a Christian would hardly be pleased to be told he is an **"anonymous Hindu."** If the label is unacceptable in reverse, it is not a genuine act of recognition but a classification. Panikkar's own alternative is **cosmotheandric**: reality is irreducibly divine-human-cosmic, and Christ is the mystery at work in all traditions rather than the Christian franchise extended to them (*The Unknown Christ of Hinduism*).
*Assessment:* the reversibility test is decisive against the **term**. It is not decisive against the underlying claim about universal grace, which is now magisterial teaching.

### Michael Amaladoss SJ — beyond inclusivism
Rahner's inclusivism still places Christianity at the centre and reads every other tradition on a Christian map. Amaladoss argues for a theology of **pluralism in principle** — many real ways within one divine plan — and for dialogue in which the other is a **subject** who may correct the Christian, not only an object interpreted by the Christian.
*Assessment:* this is inclusivism's genuine limit. Note also the magisterial constraint: *Dominus Iesus* (2000) presses back hard on pluralism in principle. State the tension honestly rather than resolving it too quickly.

### Aloysius Pieris SJ — religiousness without poverty
Pieris's structural critique: Asia is defined by **two realities — overwhelming religiousness and overwhelming poverty**. European theologies of religion (Rahner's included) address the first and ignore the second. An Asian theology must be **baptised in the Jordan of Asian religion and on the cross of Asian poverty**.
*Assessment:* **the sharpest Asian criticism.** It is Metz's political critique with Asian specificity, and it identifies a genuine absence, not a misunderstanding.

### George M. Soares-Prabhu SJ — the methodological objection
Soares-Prabhu's insistence: theology in India must begin from the **concrete, conflictual, oppressive Indian reality** and from the **Bible read within it**, not from a transcendental analysis of the human subject as such. The starting point of the *Nazareth manifesto* (Lk 4:18–19) is the poor, not the *Vorgriff*. A theology whose first move is the a priori structure of consciousness will always arrive at social sin too late and too abstractly.
*Assessment:* this is a **methodological** disagreement, not a doctrinal one. Rahner and Soares-Prabhu can agree entirely about universal grace and disagree entirely about where theology should start — and where you start determines what you can see.

### The caste question
The most concrete Indian test. Rahner's theology of the **body as real symbol of the person** ought to yield a devastating critique of untouchability: to defile a body is to defile a person; to segregate bodies at the Eucharist is to falsify the Church's own real symbol. Rahner never made this argument — he had no occasion to. **Indian theology can and should make it for him.** This is the most fruitful constructive use of Rahner available to an Indian BTh student.

> [!example] A constructive Rahnerian argument against caste discrimination in the Church
> 1. The body is the person's *Realsymbol*: what is done to the body is done to the person ([[08 - Realsymbol Applied - Trinity, Christ, Church, Sacraments, Body]]).
> 2. The Church is the *Realsymbol* of Christ; the Eucharist is the Church's self-realisation ([[12 - Ecclesiology and Sacramental Theology]]).
> 3. Therefore a Eucharist celebrated with segregated seating, separate cemeteries, or refusal of the common cup is a **falsified symbol** — the Church expressing something other than Christ, and so failing to be what it symbolises.
> 4. This is not a moral supplement to sacramental theology. It is a **sacramental defect** — precisely the argument Paul makes at 1 Cor 11:17–29, where failing to "discern the body" makes the assembly's meal not the Lord's Supper at all.
>
> Note that step 3 requires the theology of the **false symbol** that Rahner never wrote → [[17 - Critical Reflection II - Theological]].
>
> **That doctrine is now supplied**, and the argument is completed to five steps — including the *rule of competing symbols* (when a verbal symbol and a bodily symbol contradict, the bodily one is believed) — in [[24 - The Theology of the False Symbol]] §7.

## 3. Balance sheet for an Indian BTh essay

| Rahner is **usable** for | Rahner is **insufficient** for |
|---|---|
| Grounding interreligious respect theologically, not merely diplomatically | Naming caste, class, patriarchy and dispossession as theological realities |
| Legitimating inculturation as more than aesthetics | Providing the *content* of an Indian theology — that must come from Indian reality and Scripture read within it |
| A metaphysics that honours ritual, body, image and gesture | A theology of the **false** symbol — needed to critique the Church's own practice |
| Preaching that starts from experience (mystagogy) | Preaching that starts from the cry of the poor (Pieris, Soares-Prabhu) |
| Defending Catholic sacramental realism against modern "merely symbolic" reductions | Dialogue in which the other speaks back as a full subject (Panikkar, Amaladoss) |

> [!tip] Exam thesis worth defending
> "Rahner supplies Indian theology with a **grammar** but not a **content**. His ontology of the symbol and his theology of universal grace are among the best tools available for taking Indian religious experience and Indian bodies with theological seriousness. But his transcendental starting point systematically arrives late at the two facts that most define Indian reality — poverty and caste. The right Indian use of Rahner is therefore to take his ontology and start it somewhere else: not from the a priori structure of the subject, but from the crucified body of the poor, which is a real symbol of Christ in a sense Rahner asserted and never developed."
