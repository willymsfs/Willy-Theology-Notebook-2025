# 01 — Introduction and Biblical Foundations

> [!info] Next: [[02 - Historical Development and the Councils]]

## Why the doctrine?
- Not philosophical speculation but the church's attempt to **name the God it had experienced in salvation**: the Father of Jesus, Jesus the Son, the Spirit poured out.
- Method: **lex orandi → lex credendi** — worship (baptism, doxology) first, dogma after.

## Old Testament background
- Bedrock = **strict monotheism**, the **Shema** (Dt 6:4): "The Lord our God, the Lord is one."
- No explicit Trinity — but later read as **adumbrations** ("hints"):
	- Plurals of majesty — "Let **us** make humankind" (Gen 1:26)
	- Theophany — three visitors to Abraham (Gen 18)
- **Personified divine agencies** prepare the way for distinctions in the one God:
	- **Word** (*dabar*) · **Spirit** (*ruach*) · **Wisdom** (*hokmah*)
	- **Prov 8:22–31** — Wisdom as pre-existent "master worker" at creation → key bridge text for a plural-yet-one God.

## New Testament data
- Word "Trinity" **absent**; no formulated doctrine. Instead **functional / narrative trinitarianism**.
- God of Israel = **Father of Jesus**.
- **Jesus** exercises divine prerogatives — forgives sins, shares the divine name, receives worship.
- **Holy Spirit** = distinct, life-giving divine presence (**Spirit Christology**: active at Jesus' conception, baptism, resurrection).
- **Triadic formulas** (the seed-texts):
	- **Mt 28:19** — baptize "in the **name** [singular] of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit"
	- **2 Cor 13:14** — grace of Christ / love of God / fellowship of the Spirit

> [!tip] Exam framing
> The NT gives the **raw data** (Father, Son, Spirit each divine, yet one God); the councils supply the **grammar** to hold it without contradiction. See [[02 - Historical Development and the Councils]].

## Key terms introduced
- **Monotheism**, **Shema**, **triadic formula**, **Spirit Christology**, **adumbration**.
