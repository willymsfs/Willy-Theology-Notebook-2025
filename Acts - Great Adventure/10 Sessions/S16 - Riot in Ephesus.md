# Session 16 — Riot in Ephesus

> **Ministry in Ephesus** · **Passage:** Acts 19 · **Leg:** [[Leg III - To the Ends of the Earth]]

## Slide 1 · Trailhead (5 min)
Two to three years in the capital of Asia: 250,000 people, the Temple of Artemis, and the empire's centre of magic.

## Slide 2 · Story Script (15 min)
Paul finds disciples who have never heard of the Holy Spirit; he baptizes them and the Spirit falls — the fourth Pentecost. Extraordinary miracles flow, even through cloths that touched Paul. Seven freelance exorcists, sons of Sceva, try the Name without the faith — and the demoniac routs them: "Jesus I know, and Paul I know; but who are you?" Magicians burn scrolls worth fifty thousand silver pieces. Then Demetrius the silversmith does the math on lost idol revenue, and for two hours a stadium of twenty-five thousand chants "Great is Artemis of the Ephesians!"

## Slide 3 · Deeper Look (30 min)
- **Ephesus (AD 53-55):** the Hall of Tyrannus; four trade routes; the Temple of Artemis — the largest Greek temple ever built, 127 sixty-foot columns, one of the seven wonders; the magic capital (Augustus burned two thousand magic scrolls in 13 BC) — hence Ephesians' spiritual-warfare language.
- **Ephesians read inside Acts:** 1:3-14 (blessings, adoption, inheritance, sealed by the Spirit; renewal of mind — Rom 12; new creation — 2 Cor 5:17); 2:1-10 (grace through faith; *pistis*/*emunah* = steadfast fidelity, more than belief; the "faith alone" debate — Luther's insertion vs. James; "works" = works of the Law); 4:1-16 (humility, gentleness, patience, unity as conditions of mission); 6:10-18 (the whole armour of God, written from Roman imprisonment to this very magic-saturated city).
- **Sons of Sceva** ("yelping little fox"): God's power cannot be used without God; contrast Rom 15:17-19.
- **Fifty thousand pieces burned:** fifty as the number of repentance (Ps 50/51).
- **Opposition motive check:** Acts 16:16-19 and 19:23-27 — profit. Demetrius fears more than income loss; Wisdom 15:10-13 on the idol-maker's hopeless trade.
- **The fourth Pentecost:** the disciples of John at Ephesus (19:1-7). See [[The Four Pentecosts]].
