# Session 14 — Turning the World Upside Down

> **Converts and Conflicts** · **Passage:** Acts 17 · **Leg:** [[Leg III - To the Ends of the Earth]]

## Slide 1 · Trailhead (5 min)
Three cities, three receptions: riot in Thessalonica, open Bibles in Beroea, philosophers in Athens.

## Slide 2 · Story Script (15 min)
In Thessalonica the accusation is telling: "These men who have turned the world upside down have come here also… saying there is another king, Jesus." In Beroea, the noble-minded test everything against the Scriptures. Then Athens: Paul's spirit is *provoked* by a forest of idols — and among them, an insurance-policy altar: "To an unknown god." Standing on the Areopagus, Paul names the Unknown: the God who made the world, who needs no shrines, who "commands all men everywhere to repent," who has fixed a day of judgment by a man he raised from the dead. Some mock; some delay; Dionysius and Damaris believe.

## Slide 3 · Deeper Look (30 min)
- **Greek thought context:** Gnostic dualism (matter evil, spirit good) vs. Col 2:8-10 — the fullness dwells in Christ bodily.
- **Four ways to know God (CCC 27–38):** creation's design, the human person, reason, revelation. Socratic method — the Socrates/Paul parallel in Athens.
- **Thessalonica:** Paul argues from the OT like the risen Jesus on the Emmaus road (Lk 24); allegorical and moral senses; the Suffering Servant (Is 53); the accusations echo those against Jesus (Lk 23:2).
- **Beroea:** checking claims against Scripture — sometimes cited for *sola scriptura*; the Catholic question: does Scripture interpret itself?
- **Athens:** the *pagus* as forum; the missionary's three needs — know the gospel message, know what the world believes, build the bridge.
- **Vatican II lens:** *Nostra Aetate* 2 — the Church "rejects nothing that is true and holy in these religions," while proclaiming Christ "the way, the truth, and the life."
