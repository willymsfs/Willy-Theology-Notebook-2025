# Session 13 — Council and Second Voyage

> **The Jerusalem Council; Paul's Second Journey** · **Passage:** Acts 15-16 · **Leg:** [[Leg III - To the Ends of the Earth]]

## Slide 1 · Trailhead (5 min)
The Church's first doctrinal crisis: must Gentiles become Jews to become Christians?

## Slide 2 · Story Script (15 min)
The whole Church gathers in Jerusalem. Peter rises: "We believe that we shall be saved through the grace of the Lord Jesus, just as they will." James adds the pastoral application; a letter goes out beginning with words no merely human assembly could write: "It has seemed good to the Holy Spirit and to us." Then the second journey: a night vision — a man of Macedonia pleading, "Come over and help us" — and the gospel crosses into Europe. Lydia the purple-dealer opens her heart and her house; a slave girl is freed of her spirit; and at midnight in a Philippian jail, Paul and Silas sing hymns until an earthquake opens every door — and the jailer's whole household is baptized before dawn.

## Slide 3 · Deeper Look (30 min)
- **The issue:** ritual law for Gentiles; circumcision as covenant sign (Gen 17; Lev 12:3), meaning submission to the whole Law.
- **Peter speaks as head** ("We believe," 15:11); James is pastoral, Peter's judgment final. OT warrant for Gentile inclusion: Jer 12:15; Am 9:11-12; Is 45:21.
- **The decision:** no circumcision; abstain from idol-food (Lev 17:7-9), unchastity (Lev 18), blood (Lev 17:10-14) — temporary measures for Jew–Gentile table unity.
- **"It has seemed good to the Holy Spirit and to us" (15:28):** the Spirit in Church decisions; the keys of the kingdom, binding and loosing (Mt 16:13-19); Peter's office from the prime-minister pattern of Is 22; Spirit + seat of Peter + Magisterium (Jn 16:13). Analogy: Vatican II as a pastoral council (*Gaudium et Spes*).
- **Second journey:** Barnabas and John Mark to Cyprus; Timothy circumcised (pastoral flexibility); the Macedonian vision; Lydia's conversion and household; Philippi's earthquake and the jailer's household baptism.
- **Companion epistle — Philippians:** unity through humility, the Christ-hymn (Phil 2:1-11).
