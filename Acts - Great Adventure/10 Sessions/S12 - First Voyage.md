# Session 12 — First Voyage

> **Paul's First Missionary Journey** · **Passage:** Acts 13-14 · **Leg:** [[Leg III - To the Ends of the Earth]]

## Slide 1 · Trailhead (5 min)
Antioch fasts, prays — and the Spirit says: "Set apart for me Barnabas and Saul." The expedition's third leg begins.

## Slide 2 · Story Script (15 min)
Hands are laid on the missionaries and they sail for Cyprus. A court magician is struck blind — the mirror of Saul's own Damascus blindness. In Pisidian Antioch, Paul stands in the synagogue and preaches the whole sweep of Israel's story ending in a risen Son of David. Jews and Gentiles beg to hear more; jealousy erupts; Paul turns to the Gentiles with Isaiah on his lips: "I have set you to be a light for the Gentiles." In Lystra a lame man leaps at Paul's word — and the crowd tries to sacrifice oxen to "Zeus and Hermes," then stones Paul almost to death. He gets up and walks back into the city.

## Slide 3 · Deeper Look (30 min)
- **Three journeys overview:** (1) establishes churches; (2) letters to the Thessalonians; (3) revisits churches, writes most of the letters.
- **Commissioning:** prayer and fasting before major decisions (Acts 13:1-3; 14:23); the laying on of hands (Nm 8:10).
- **Pattern:** the synagogue first — "to the Jew first and also to the Greek" (Rom 1:16).
- **Elymas blinded (13:11):** a sign of spiritual blindness, paralleling Saul's (9:8). Saul is now "Paul," his Roman name.
- **Pisidian Antioch sermon (13:16-41):** parallels Peter (Acts 2) and Stephen (Acts 7); restoration of scattered Israel — the ten northern tribes mixed among the nations since 722 BC (Rom 9–11); the Davidic promise (2 Sam 7; 1 Sam 8); Is 55:3 and Ps 16:10 fulfilled in Jesus, not David; Ps 2 enthronement; *remez* — the "hint" of the everlasting covenant blessing all nations (Is 55:4-7); Messiah preserved from corruption (Ps 16:10); forgiveness and freedom in Christ (13:38-39; Heb 10:1-4); the warning from Hab 1:5.
- **Peter–Paul parallels:** a lame man healed (3:1-10 // 14:8-13), each healing followed by a "Men, why…?" speech. See [[Luke-Acts Parallels]].
