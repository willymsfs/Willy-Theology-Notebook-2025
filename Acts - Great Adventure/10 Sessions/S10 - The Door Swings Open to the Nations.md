# Session 10 — The Door Swings Open to the Nations

> **St. Peter's "Un-Kosher Picnic"** · **Passage:** Acts 10 · **Leg:** [[Leg II - Crossing Borders]]

## Slide 1 · Trailhead (5 min)
A millennium of separation is about to end on a rooftop in Joppa.

## Slide 2 · Story Script (15 min)
In Caesarea, a Roman centurion prays at the very hour of the evening sacrifice — and an angel tells him his prayers have risen like incense. In Joppa — the port from which Jonah once fled *his* mission to the Gentiles — Peter falls into a trance at noon and sees a sheet lowered by its four corners, full of every unclean animal. "Rise, Peter; kill and eat." Three times. He is still puzzling when Gentile messengers knock. Days later, while Peter is still preaching in Cornelius' house, the Holy Spirit falls on the uncircumcised — Pentecost of the Gentiles.

## Slide 3 · Deeper Look (30 min)
- **Backdrop:** OT holiness as separation; Peter is already lodging with Simon the *tanner* (an unclean trade).
- **Cornelius:** a centurion and God-fearer — interested in Judaism, but not a covenant member (cf. Acts 13:16).
- **Ninth hour = 3 PM,** the evening liturgy (Ex 29:38-39); Gabriel came to Daniel at that hour (Dn 9). Gentile prayers are accepted in place of the evening sacrifice.
- **Jonah–Joppa–Peter:** Jesus calls Peter "Simon Bar-Jona" (Mt 16:17) though his father is John (Jn 1:42) — the reluctant-messenger-to-the-Gentiles motif.
- **The vision:** four corners = the whole earth; kosher (*kashrut*) as the exile-era wall of the table (229 of 341 Mishnah texts concern table fellowship); Jesus lifted the dietary laws (Mk 7:19); clean/unclean stood for Jew/Gentile; lifting the ban = the Gentiles are no longer unclean.
- **New standard of holiness:** Christ's blood brings the Gentiles in (Eph 2:11-16); Abraham's blessing reaches the nations (Gen 12; Gal 3:13-14, 28-29); contact with Christ cleanses instead of contaminating (Mt 8:1-4 — Jesus touches the leper).
- **Tongues fall on the household (10:44-48):** the third of the four Pentecosts. See [[The Four Pentecosts]].
