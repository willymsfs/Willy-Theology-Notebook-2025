# Session 17 — The Road to Jerusalem

> **Paul Heads for Jerusalem** · **Passage:** Acts 20-21 · **Leg:** [[Leg III - To the Ends of the Earth]]

## Slide 1 · Trailhead (5 min)
The third journey closes. Every prophecy on the route says the same thing: chains ahead. Paul goes anyway.

## Slide 2 · Story Script (15 min)
In Troas, "on the first day of the week," the church gathers to break bread; Paul preaches past midnight and young Eutychus, perched in a window, falls asleep — and falls three storeys. Paul embraces him back to life and returns upstairs to the Eucharist. At Miletus, the Ephesian elders weep on Paul's neck as he quotes a saying of Jesus found in no Gospel: "It is more blessed to give than to receive." The prophet Agabus binds his own hands and feet with Paul's belt. In Jerusalem, rumour-fed crowds seize Paul in the Temple, crying the very words once shouted at his Master: "Away with him!"

## Slide 3 · Deeper Look (30 min)
- **Route and purpose:** Jerusalem, then Rome; the collection for the poor (Rom 15:25-27; 2 Cor); Luke rejoins Paul (the "we"-passages); the Feast of Unleavened Bread (Dt 16:16).
- **Sunday theology (20:7):** the Sabbath fulfilled in the day of Resurrection; do not forsake the assembly (Heb 10); the Eucharist (CCC 1343); new creation (CCC 2174); Sunday fulfils the Sabbath (CCC 2175-2176); entering the Sabbath rest (Heb 4:1-11); the Mass as "the Great Exchange"; the message must meet faith in the hearers (Heb 4:2; *pistis/emunah*; *kavana* — focused intention).
- **Eutychus revived** — parallel to Peter and Tabitha, and to Jesus.
- **Miletus farewell (20:17-38):** fierce wolves foretold (1 Tim 1:3-7); the agraphon "more blessed to give" — oral tradition; *ashrei* = "happy/blessed"; postures of prayer — kneeling (20:36; Ps 95:6), raised hands (Ps 141:2), standing (Mk 11:25).
- **Agabus and the sign-acting prophets:** Is 20; Jer 19; Ez 4; Hosea's marriage.
- **Jerusalem (c. AD 58):** a Nazirite vow taken for peace (21:23; cf. 18:18); "to the Jews I became as a Jew" (1 Cor 9:19-23); arrest on the false charge of bringing Gentiles past the Temple barrier — the "dividing wall" Christ has demolished (Eph 2:11-22); "Away with him" (21:36) parallels Jesus' passion.
