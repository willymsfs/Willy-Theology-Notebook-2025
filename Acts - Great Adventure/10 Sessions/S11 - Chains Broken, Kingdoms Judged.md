# Session 11 — Chains Broken, Kingdoms Judged

> **The Church in Antioch** · **Passage:** Acts 11-12 · **Leg:** [[Leg II - Crossing Borders]]

## Slide 1 · Trailhead (5 min)
The shockwave of the un-kosher picnic reaches Jerusalem — and the gospel plants its second capital: Antioch.

## Slide 2 · Story Script (15 min)
Peter faces the circumcision party and simply retells the story — "who was I that I could withstand God?" In Antioch, scattered believers preach to Greeks, Barnabas fetches Saul, and the disciples are first called *Christians*. Then Herod strikes the leadership: James dies by the sword; Peter waits in prison on Passover night, chained between two soldiers — until an angel strikes him awake with Exodus stage-directions: "Get up quickly… put on your sandals." The Church that could not fight prayed instead; the servant girl Rhoda is so overjoyed she leaves Peter standing at the gate. Herod, accepting divine honours, is struck down; "but the word of God grew and multiplied."

## Slide 3 · Deeper Look (30 min)
- **Peter's report accepted (11:1-18);** household salvation (11:14; cf. 18:8; CCC 1655-1657 — the domestic church); Gentile Christians as "a chosen race, a royal priesthood" (1 Pet 2:9-12 // Ex 19:5-6).
- **Antioch:** Barnabas as Jerusalem's delegate; Saul brought in; "Christian" = Latin, "partisan of Christ" (cf. Acts 26:28; 1 Pet 4:16). Agabus foretells famine (AD 40s); relief sent to Judea — solidarity against avarice; mercy as the antidote to greed.
- **Second wave of persecution:** first the laity (Acts 8), now the leaders. Herod Agrippa, grandson of Herod the Great; James the first apostle-martyr (capital punishment: CCC 2267; Rom 13:4). James is not replaced — the Twelve's foundational office is fulfilled.
- **Peter's rescue as new Exodus:** "move quickly," sandals on (Ex 12:11), delivered "out of the hand of Herod" (Ex 3:7-8); the same verb "brought out" (Ex 3:8); Passover timing; the events echo the Resurrection.
- **Guardian angels** (Mt 18:10; Gen 48:16); **intercessory prayer** (CCC 2634-2636); Herod's death as judgment on counterfeit kingship.
