**BIBLICAL FOUNDATIONS II: NEW TESTAMENT**

**Connecting Old and New Testaments**

In our previous session, we saw how the Old Testament established the foundational patterns of God’s people walking together:

- The qahal/edah (assembly) of Israel
- Covenantal gatherings
- Communal discernment
- Prophetic promises of a New Covenant
- The Spirit poured out on all people

**Now we see these patterns FULFILLED in the New Testament.**

**Key Theme**

The New Testament shows us how **Jesus Christ fulfills God’s plan** by:

- Gathering a new People of God
- Forming a community of disciples who walk together
- Establishing the Church that lives synodally
- Pouring out the Holy Spirit on all believers
- Creating the pattern for synodal discernment

**PART 1: JESUS FULFILLS GOD’S PLAN**

**A. Jesus: The New Covenant**

**God’s Plan Achieved in Jesus**

God achieves the New Covenant He promised through the prophets in **Jesus of Nazareth, Messiah and Lord**.

**Jesus’ Life and Message Reveals:**

- God is a communion of love
- God wishes to embrace all humanity in unity
- This happens through grace and mercy

**B. Jesus: The Eternal Son**

**John 1:1** - “In the beginning was the Word” The Word (Logos) existed from all eternity.

**John 1:18** - “No one has ever seen God; it is the only Son, who is nearest to the Father’s heart, who has made him known”

Jesus is the Son of God, destined from eternity to dwell in the Father’s heart.

**John 1:14** - “The Word was made flesh, he lived among us”

**Galatians 4:4** - “When the appointed time came, God sent his Son, born of a woman”

**The Meaning:** Jesus is made human in the fullness of time to bring to fulfillment God’s plan of salvation.

**C. Jesus Never Acts Alone**

**This is crucial for understanding synodality!**

**John 8:29** - “He who sent me is with me, and has not left me to myself, for I always do what pleases him”

**John 6:39** - “Now the will of him who sent me is that I should lose nothing of all that he has given to me”

**John 5:22, 27** - The Father has given all judgment to the Son

**John 14:10** - “Do you not believe that I am in the Father and the Father is in me? The words I say to you I do not speak as from myself: it is the Father, living in me, who is doing this work”

**Key Points:**

- Jesus NEVER acts alone
- He ALWAYS does the Father’s will
- The Father dwells in Him
- The Father carries out His work through the Son
- Jesus was sent into the world for this purpose

**Significance for Synodality:** If Jesus Himself, the Son of God, never acts alone but always in communion with the Father, how much more should the Church act in communion!

**D. The Paschal Mystery: The Climax**

**The Father’s Plan Fulfilled Eschatologically**

The plan reaches its fulfillment in the **Paschal Mystery** - Jesus’ passion, death, and resurrection.

**John 10:17** - “The Father loves me, because I lay down my life in order to take it up again”

**What Happens:**

- Jesus gives His life
- He takes it up again in the resurrection
- He shares this new life with His disciples as sons and daughters, sisters and brothers

**John 3:34** - The outpouring of the Holy Spirit “without reserve”

**E. The Paschal Mystery as New Exodus**

**The New Exodus:**

Just as God led Israel out of Egypt through the Red Sea to freedom, Jesus’ Paschal Mystery is the **new Exodus** that gathers people in unity.

**John 11:52** - Jesus’ death will “gather together in unity (συναγάγῃ εἰς ἕν - synagagē eis hen) the scattered children of God”

**The Unity Jesus Creates:**

- Gathers all who believe in Him
- Conforms them to Himself through Baptism and Eucharist
- Makes them one Body

**F. Jesus’ Prayer for Unity**

**John 17:21** - The High Priestly Prayer

Just before His Passion, Jesus prays to the Father:

“May they all be one. Father, may they be one in us, as you are in me and I am in you, so that the world may believe it was you who sent me.”

**The Model of Unity:**

- As the Father is in Jesus
- As Jesus is in the Father
- So believers are to be one in them

**The Purpose:** “So that the world may believe” - unity is for the sake of mission and witness.

**Significance:** The work of salvation IS this unity. Synodality is not optional - it flows from Jesus’ own prayer and intention.

**PART 2: JESUS FORMING A COMMUNITY OF DISCIPLES**

**A. Jesus the Pilgrim**

**The Walking Jesus:**

**Luke 4:14-15** - Jesus goes through Galilee teaching in synagogues

**Luke 8:1** - “He made his way through towns and villages preaching and proclaiming the Good News of the kingdom of God”

**Luke 9:57** - “As they travelled along they met a man on the road”

**Luke 13:22** - “Through towns and villages he went teaching, making his way to Jerusalem”

**Luke 19:11** - While on the journey to Jerusalem, he tells parables

**Key Point:** Jesus is the **pilgrim** who proclaims the good news of the Kingdom of God. The Christian life is following Jesus on the journey.

**B. Jesus: The Way**

**Luke 20:21** - People acknowledge Jesus teaches “the way of God”

**The Journey to Jerusalem:** **Luke 9:51-19:28** - This entire section of Luke’s Gospel is Jesus’ journey to Jerusalem. It’s not just geographical - it’s theological.

**John 14:6** - Jesus’ Self-Declaration: “I am the Way, the Truth and the Life. No one can come to the Father except through me.”

**Key Points:**

- Jesus IS the Way (not just shows the way)
- He points the way and He IS the way
- He leads to the Father

**John 16:13** - Jesus promises the Holy Spirit “will lead you to the complete truth”

**C. The New Commandment: Walking Together**

**John 15:12-15** - Love One Another

“This is my commandment: love one another, as I have loved you. A man can have no greater love than to lay down his life for his friends. You are my friends, if you do what I command you. I shall not call you servants any more, because a servant does not know his master’s business; I call you friends, because I have made known to you everything I have learnt from my Father.”

**Characteristics of This New Way:**

- Living communion according to Jesus’ standard
- The new commandment of love
- Walking together in history as the People of God
- In a way that fits the gift received

**This is Synodal Life:**

- Friends, not just servants
- Sharing what Jesus has revealed
- Loving as Jesus loved
- Walking together on the journey

**D. The Emmaus Story: Icon of Synodal Church**

**Luke 24:13-35** - The Road to Emmaus

This story gives us a **living icon** of the Church as People of God on a synodal journey.

**The Scene:**

- Two disciples walking (synod = walking together)
- They are sad, confused, disappointed
- Jesus joins them (but they don’t recognize Him)

**Jesus’ Method:**

1. **Accompaniment** - He walks with them (verse 15)
2. **Listening** - “What matters are you discussing?” (verse 17)
3. **More Listening** - He lets them tell their whole story (verses 18-24)
4. **Teaching** - “You foolish men! So slow to believe... starting with Moses and going through all the prophets, he explained to them the passages throughout the scriptures that were about himself” (verses 25-27)
5. **Breaking Bread** - At table, “he took the bread and said the blessing; then he broke it and handed it to them” (verse 30)
6. **Recognition** - “And their eyes were opened and they recognized him” (verse 31)
7. **Mission** - They immediately return to Jerusalem to tell the others (verses 33-35)

**The Synodal Pattern in Emmaus:**

- **Walking together** on the journey
- **The Risen Lord guides** the way
- **Lights up the way** by His Word (Scripture)
- **Feeds** with the Bread of Life (Eucharist)
- **Opens eyes** to recognize Him
- **Sends on mission** to share the good news

This is the complete pattern of synodal Church life!

**E. Jesus Shares His Authority**

**What is Exousia (ἐξουσία)?**

The New Testament uses a specific Greek term for the power Jesus received from the Father: **exousia** = authority.

**John 1:12** - Jesus’ Authority to Make Children of God

“But to all who did accept him he gave power (ἐξουσία - exousia) to become children of God”

**What This Authority Consists In:**

- Imparting grace
- Making people “children of God”
- Given in the power (δύναμις - dynamis) of the Holy Spirit
- Exercised over all creatures

**F. The Apostles Receive This Authority**

**Matthew 28:19-20** - The Great Commission

The risen Lord gives this exousia to the Apostles:

“Go, therefore, make disciples of all the nations; baptize them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit, and teach them to observe all the commands I gave you. And know that I am with you always; yes, to the end of time.”

**What the Apostles Receive:**

- Authority to teach
- Authority to baptize
- Commission to make disciples
- Promise of Jesus’ ongoing presence

**G. ALL the Baptized Share This Authority**

**This is crucial for synodality!**

**By virtue of baptism, EVERY member of the People of God is given a share in this authority.**

**1 John 2:20, 27** - The Anointing of All Believers

“But you have been anointed by the Holy One, and have all received the knowledge... the anointing he gave you teaches you everything”

**John 6:45** - All Are Taught by God

“It is written in the prophets: They will all be taught by God”

**John 16:13** - The Spirit Guides to Complete Truth

“When the Spirit of truth comes he will lead you to the complete truth”

**Key Points:**

- ALL baptized receive the anointing of the Holy Spirit
- ALL are taught by God
- ALL are guided by the Spirit to complete truth
- This is the foundation of the **sensus fidei** (sense of faith)

**Significance for Synodality:** Because ALL the baptized have received:

- The Holy Spirit’s anointing
- Teaching from God
- Guidance to truth

Therefore, ALL can and must participate in the Church’s synodal discernment!

**PART 3: THE EARLY CHRISTIAN COMMUNITY (ACTS 2-4)**

**A. Pentecost: The Birth of the Church**

**Acts 2:1-4** - The Holy Spirit Comes

“When Pentecost day came round, they had all met in one room, when suddenly they heard what sounded like a powerful wind from heaven, the noise of which filled the entire house in which they were sitting; and something appeared to them that seemed like tongues of fire; these separated and came to rest on the head of each of them. They were all filled with the Holy Spirit, and began to speak foreign languages as the Spirit gave them the gift of speech.”

**Key Elements:**

1. **They had all met together** - Unity and gathering
2. **One room** - Community assembled
3. **Holy Spirit came** - God’s initiative
4. **Tongues of fire** - On each of them individually
5. **All filled** - Not just the apostles
6. **Began to speak** - All participated

**B. Peter’s Sermon: Joel’s Prophecy Fulfilled**

**Acts 2:14-21** - Peter Explains What Happened

Peter stands up and explains that what’s happening is the fulfillment of Joel’s prophecy:

**Acts 2:17-18** (quoting Joel 3:1-2):

“In the days to come—it is the Lord who speaks—I will pour out my Spirit on all mankind. Their sons and daughters shall prophesy, your young men shall see visions, your old men shall dream dreams. Even on my slaves, men and women, in those days, I will pour out my Spirit.”

**What This Means:**

- The promised Spirit has come
- Poured out on ALL (not just leaders)
- Sons AND daughters (both genders)
- Young AND old (all ages)
- Slaves (all social classes)
- Result: ALL prophesy, see visions, dream dreams

**For Synodality:** This establishes that the Holy Spirit dwells in ALL the baptized, enabling ALL to participate in the Church’s life and discernment.

**C. The First Christian Community**

**Acts 2:42-47** - Life of the Early Church

“These remained faithful to the teaching of the apostles, to the brotherhood, to the breaking of bread and to the prayers.”

**Four Pillars of Community Life:**

1. **Teaching of the Apostles** - Apostolic doctrine
2. **Brotherhood** (κοινωνία - koinonia) - Communion/fellowship
3. **Breaking of Bread** - Eucharist
4. **Prayers** - Common prayer

**The Community’s Characteristics:**

**Verse 44-45:** “The faithful all lived together and owned everything in common; they sold their goods and possessions and shared out the proceeds among themselves according to what each one needed.”

**Verse 46:** “They went as a body to the Temple every day but met in their houses for the breaking of bread; they shared their food gladly and generously.”

**Verse 47:** “They praised God and were looked up to by everyone. Day by day the Lord added to their community those destined to be saved.”

**Key Features:**

- Lived together in unity
- Shared possessions
- Cared for those in need
- Worshipped together (Temple and homes)
- Breaking of bread in homes
- Joyful and generous
- Praised God
- Attractive witness - people joined daily

**D. The Community of Prayer**

**Acts 1:14** - Gathered with Mary

“All these joined in continuous prayer, together with several women, including Mary the mother of Jesus, and with his brothers.”

**Who Was Present:**

- The Apostles
- Several women (named explicitly!)
- Mary, the mother of Jesus
- Jesus’ brothers (relatives)

**Key Point:** Women are specifically mentioned as part of the praying community from the very beginning.

**E. Communal Decision-Making: Choosing Matthias**

**Acts 1:15-26** - Replacing Judas

**The Process:**

**Verse 15:** “One day Peter stood up to speak to the brothers—there were about a hundred and twenty persons in the congregation”

**Note:** The whole congregation is present (about 120 people)

**Verse 21-22:** Peter explains the criteria for replacing Judas - must be someone who has been with them from John’s baptism through the Ascension.

**Verse 23:** “Having nominated two candidates, Joseph Barsabbas and Matthias”

**Verse 24-25:** They prayed: “Lord, you can read everyone’s heart; show us therefore which of these two you have chosen”

**Verse 26:** “They then drew lots for them, and as the lot fell to Matthias, he was listed as one of the twelve apostles”

**The Synodal Pattern:**

- Community gathers
- Leader (Peter) articulates the need
- Criteria are established
- Community nominates candidates
- They pray for Holy Spirit’s guidance
- Decision is made with prayer and lots
- Community accepts the decision

**F. Community Faces Challenges Together**

**Acts 4:23-31** - Prayer After Peter and John’s Release

When Peter and John were released from the Sanhedrin:

**Verse 24:** “As soon as they were released they went to the community and told them everything the chief priests and elders had said to them. When they heard it they lifted up their voice to God all together”

**Key Points:**

- They returned to the community (not acting alone)
- Told everything to the community
- Prayed together “all together” (ὁμοθυμαδόν - homothymadon)

**Their Prayer (verses 24-30):**

- Acknowledged God as Creator
- Recalled Scripture (Psalm 2)
- Described their situation
- Asked for boldness to speak
- Asked for signs and wonders

**Verse 31:** “As they prayed, the house where they were assembled rocked; they were all filled with the Holy Spirit and began to proclaim the word of God boldly.”

**Result:**

- The place shook (sign of God’s presence)
- ALL filled with the Holy Spirit (not just leaders)
- ALL began to proclaim boldly

**G. Handling Internal Conflicts**

**Acts 6:1-6** - The Appointment of the Seven

**The Problem (verse 1):** “About this time, when the number of disciples was increasing, the Hellenists made a complaint against the Hebrews: in the daily distribution their own widows were being overlooked.”

**The Solution:**

**Verse 2:** “So the Twelve called a full meeting of the disciples”

**Note:** They don’t decide alone - they call the whole community together.

**Verse 2-4:** The Twelve explain:

- “It would not be right for us to neglect the word of God so as to give out food”
- “You, brothers, must select from among yourselves seven men of good reputation, filled with the Spirit and with wisdom; we will hand over this duty to them”
- “This will leave us free to devote ourselves to prayer and to the service of the word”

**Verse 5:** “The whole assembly approved of this proposal and elected...”

**Key Point:** “The whole assembly” makes the decision to approve and elect!

**Verse 6:** “They presented these to the apostles, who prayed and laid their hands on them”

**The Synodal Pattern:**

- Problem arises in community
- Leaders call the community together
- Leaders propose a solution
- Whole assembly approves
- Community elects/selects
- Apostles confirm through prayer and laying on of hands

**Roles Are Distinct:**

- Community discerns and chooses
- Apostles confirm and ordain
- But both participate!

**PART 4: THE COUNCIL OF JERUSALEM (ACTS 15) - THE SYNODAL MODEL**

**Introduction: Why This is So Important**

**The Tradition of the Church has called this “the Apostolic Council of Jerusalem.”**

**From the Document:** “Across the centuries, this event has been interpreted as the **paradigm for Synods** celebrated by the Church.”

This means Acts 15 is the **MODEL** for how the Church does synodal discernment!

**A. The Issue at Stake**

**The Question:** Must Gentile (non-Jewish) converts to Christianity be circumcised and follow the whole Mosaic Law, or is faith in Jesus Christ sufficient?

**Why This Matters:**

- This is a decisive moment for the early Church
- The answer will determine if Christianity remains a Jewish sect or becomes universal
- It’s controversial - strong opinions on both sides

**Acts 15:1** - The Problem Stated

“Then some men came down from Judaea and taught the brothers, ‘Unless you have yourselves circumcised in the tradition of Moses you cannot be saved.’”

**Acts 15:2** - The Conflict

“This led to disagreement, and after Paul and Barnabas had had a long argument with these men it was arranged that Paul and Barnabas and others of the church should go up to Jerusalem and discuss the problem with the apostles and elders.”

**B. The Journey and Consultation**

**Acts 15:3-4** - Reporting to Communities

“All the members of the church saw them off, and as they passed through Phoenicia and Samaria they told how the pagans had been converted, and this news was received with the greatest satisfaction by the brothers. When they arrived in Jerusalem they were welcomed by the church and by the apostles and elders, and gave an account of all that God had done with them.”

**Key Points:**

- The whole church sends them
- They consult churches along the way
- Those churches rejoice at the news
- In Jerusalem, welcomed by church, apostles, AND elders
- They report what GOD has done (not just their own work)

**C. The Assembly Gathers**

**Acts 15:4** - Who Gathers?

“They were welcomed by the church and by the apostles and elders”

**Three Groups:**

1. The church (ἡ ἐκκλησία - the whole community)
2. The apostles
3. The elders

**Acts 15:6** - The Meeting Begins

“The apostles and elders met to look into the matter”

**D. Open Discussion and Debate**

**Acts 15:7a** - “After the discussion had gone on a long time...”

The Greek word used is **ἐκζήτησις (ekzētēsis)** - meaning thorough discussion, debate, controversy.

**Key Point:** There was a **lively and open discussion**. Different views were expressed. People debated. This wasn’t a rubber-stamp meeting!

**E. Peter’s Authoritative Witness**

**Acts 15:7b-11** - Peter Speaks

Peter stands up and gives his testimony:

**His Argument:**

1. **God has already decided** (verse 7): “You know that in the early days God made his choice among you: the pagans were to learn the Good News from me and so become believers”
2. **God gave the Holy Spirit** (verse 8): “In fact God, who can read everyone’s heart, showed his approval of them by giving the Holy Spirit to them just as he had to us”
3. **God made no distinction** (verse 9): “God made no distinction between them and us, since he purified their hearts by faith”
4. **Don’t test God** (verse 10): “It would only provoke God’s anger now, surely, if you imposed on the disciples the very burden that neither we nor our ancestors were strong enough to support?”
5. **Saved by grace** (verse 11): “Remember, we believe that we are saved in the same way as they are: through the grace of the Lord Jesus”

**Significance:** Peter, as leader, gives authoritative witness based on:

- His own experience (Cornelius - Acts 10)
- What God has done
- Theological understanding of salvation by grace

**F. The Assembly Listens**

**Acts 15:12** - “This silenced the entire assembly”

After Peter speaks, everyone becomes quiet and listens.

**Then:** “and they listened to Barnabas and Paul describing all the signs and wonders God had worked through them among the pagans”

**Key Points:**

- The whole assembly (πᾶν τὸ πλῆθος - pan to plēthos) is present throughout
- They listen to Peter’s authoritative teaching
- They listen to Paul and Barnabas’s testimony
- They’re hearing what GOD has done

**G. James Interprets and Decides**

**Acts 15:13-18** - James Speaks

After everyone has spoken, James (leader of the Jerusalem church) speaks:

**His Method:**

1. **Acknowledges Peter’s testimony** (verse 14): “Symeon has described how God first arranged to enlist a people for his name out of the pagans”
2. **Interprets with Scripture** (verses 15-18): “This is entirely in harmony with the words of the prophets”

**He quotes Amos 9:11-12:** “After that I will return and rebuild the fallen House of David; I will rebuild it from its ruins and restore it. Then the rest of mankind, all the pagans who are consecrated to my name, will look for the Lord, says the Lord who made this known so long ago.”

**His Theological Vision:**

- This is God’s plan from the prophets
- God intended to call Gentiles as His people
- Scripture confirms what’s happening
- God is including “a people out of the pagans” (ἐξ ἐθνῶν λαὸν - ex ethnōn laon)

**H. James’s Decision and Practical Rules**

**Acts 15:19-21** - The Decision

**Verse 19-20:** “I rule, then, that instead of making things more difficult for pagans who turn to God, we send them a letter telling them merely to abstain from anything polluted by idols, from fornication, from the meat of strangled animals and from blood.”

**His Reasoning (verse 21):** “For Moses has always had his preachers in every town, and is read aloud in the synagogues every sabbath”

**What James Does:**

- Makes a decision (as leader of Jerusalem church)
- Provides practical rules (minimum requirements)
- Shows pastoral sensitivity (doesn’t burden Gentiles)
- Shows wisdom (considers Jewish believers too)
- Grounds decision in Scripture and tradition

**I. The Letter is Written**

**Acts 15:22-29** - The Decision Letter

**Verse 22:** “Then the apostles and elders decided to choose delegates...”

**Note:** Apostles AND elders together decide.

**The Letter (verses 23-29):**

**Who Sends It:** “The apostles and elders, your brothers, send greetings to the brothers of pagan birth in Antioch, Syria and Cilicia”

**The Key Statement (verse 28):** **“It has been decided by the Holy Spirit and by ourselves...”**

This is crucial! The decision is:

1. By the Holy Spirit (primary)
2. By ourselves (secondary - human agents)

**The Decision:** “...not to saddle you with any burden beyond these essentials: you are to abstain from food sacrificed to idols, from blood, from the meat of strangled animals and from fornication. Avoid these, and you will do what is right.”

**J. The Decision is Received**

**Acts 15:30-31** - In Antioch

“The party left and went down to Antioch, where they summoned the whole community and delivered the letter. The community read it and were delighted with the encouragement it gave them.”

**Key Points:**

- Whole community summoned
- Letter read to all
- Community receives it gladly
- They’re delighted with the decision

**K. The Complete Synodal Pattern in Acts 15**

**Let’s analyze the complete process:**

**1. THE PROBLEM ARISES (verses 1-2)**

- Serious controversy in the community
- Paul and Barnabas have “long argument” with those insisting on circumcision
- Community decides to consult Jerusalem

**2. CONSULTATION EN ROUTE (verse 3)**

- They pass through other churches
- Share what God is doing
- Other communities rejoice

**3. WELCOME IN JERUSALEM (verse 4)**

- Church, apostles, and elders welcome them
- Paul and Barnabas report what God has done

**4. THE ASSEMBLY GATHERS (verses 4, 6, 12)**

- The whole church is present (verse 4: ἡ ἐκκλησία)
- Apostles and elders meet to examine (verse 6)
- The whole assembly present throughout (verse 12: πᾶν τὸ πλῆθος)

**5. OPEN DEBATE (verse 7a)**

- Lively and open discussion (ἐκζήτησις)
- Different opinions expressed
- Time given for thorough discussion

**6. AUTHORITATIVE WITNESS (verses 7b-11)**

- Peter speaks with authority
- Based on experience (Cornelius)
- Theological reasoning (salvation by grace)
- Appeal to what God has done

**7. LISTENING TO TESTIMONY (verse 12)**

- Assembly becomes quiet
- Listens to Barnabas and Paul
- Hears about signs and wonders
- Focus on what God has done

**8. INTERPRETATION WITH SCRIPTURE (verses 13-18)**

- James interprets events
- Uses Scripture (Amos 9:11-12)
- Shows this is God’s plan all along
- Theological framework provided

**9. DECISION BY THE LEADER (verses 19-21)**

- James formulates the decision
- Gives practical rules
- Shows pastoral wisdom
- Balances different concerns

**10. CHOOSING DELEGATES (verse 22)**

- Community involved in choosing representatives
- Apostles and elders decide together (ἔδοξε τοῖς ἀποστόλοις καὶ τοῖς πρεσβυτέροις)
- Note: “with the whole church” (σὺν ὅλῃ τῇ ἐκκλησίᾳ)

**11. THE SPIRIT’S GUIDANCE (verse 28)**

- “It has been decided by the Holy Spirit and by ourselves”
- Recognizes divine guidance
- Human decision under Spirit’s direction

**12. RECEPTION (verses 22, 30-31)**

- Assembly in Jerusalem adopts it (verse 22)
- Assembly in Antioch receives it gladly (verses 30-31)
- Decision accepted by communities

**L. Who Participates and How?**

**Everyone plays an active part, but with varied roles and contributions:**

**1. THE WHOLE CHURCH (πᾶν τὸ πλῆθος)**

- Present throughout (verse 12)
- Involved in final decision (verse 22: σὺν ὅλῃ τῇ ἐκκλησίᾳ)
- Receives and adopts the decision

**2. THE APOSTLES**

- Peter gives authoritative witness
- Share in the decision-making
- Send the letter

**3. THE ELDERS**

- Consulted from the beginning (verse 2)
- Share in examining the matter (verse 6)
- Share in the decision (verse 22)

**4. JAMES**

- Guides the church in Jerusalem
- Provides interpretation with Scripture
- Formulates the decision (verse 19: “I rule”)
- Exercises leadership authority

**5. PAUL AND BARNABAS**

- Bring the question
- Give testimony of what God has done
- Represent the Gentile mission

**M. The Role of the Holy Spirit**

**Key Principle:**

The decision is taken by James, who guides the Church in Jerusalem, **by virtue of the action of the Holy Spirit**.

**Acts 15:28** - “It has been decided by the Holy Spirit and by ourselves”

**What This Means:**

- The Holy Spirit guides the Church along its path
- The Spirit guarantees fidelity to the Gospel of Jesus
- Human decisions are made under divine guidance
- The Church discerns what the Spirit is saying

**How the Spirit Worked:**

- Through Peter’s experience and testimony
- Through signs and wonders among Gentiles
- Through Scripture (Amos)
- Through communal discernment
- Leading to consensus and unanimity

**N. The Movement Toward Consensus**

**Acts 15:25** - “We have unanimously decided...”

The Greek word is **ὁμοθυμαδόν (homothymadon)** - with one mind, unanimously, in agreement.

**The Process:**

- Initially divergent opinions
- Open and thorough discussion
- Listening to the Holy Spirit
- Listening to testimony of what God has done
- Each giving their judgment
- Moving toward consensus
- Arriving at unanimity

**This consensus is:**

- The fruit of communal discernment
- Guided by the Holy Spirit
- In service of the evangelizing mission of the Church

**O. The Ordered Structure**

**Acts 15 Demonstrates:**

The way the People of God moves forward is:

- Orderly
- Well thought out
- Each person has a specific position and role

**Compare to Paul’s Teaching:**

**1 Corinthians 12:12-17** - The Body of Christ

- Many members, one body
- Each member necessary
- Different functions

**Romans 12:4-5** - One Body, Many Members “Just as each our bodies has several parts and each part has a separate function, so all of us, in union with Christ, form one body, and as parts of it we belong to each other”

**Ephesians 4:4** - One Body, One Spirit “There is one Body, one Spirit”

**P. Summary of the Synodal Model from Acts 15**

**THE ACTS 15 PATTERN FOR SYNODALITY:**

1. **Serious issue** arises that affects the mission
2. **Local church** recognizes need for broader consultation
3. **Journey** with consultation along the way
4. **Whole community** gathers (church, apostles, elders)
5. **Open and thorough debate** with different views expressed
6. **Listening** to multiple testimonies:

- Leaders’ authoritative witness
- Missionaries’ experience
- What God has done

8. **Interpretation with Scripture** and tradition
9. **Discernment** of the Spirit’s guidance
10. **Decision** by those with authority (but not alone)
11. **Consensus** emerges under the Spirit
12. **Letter** communicates decision
13. **Reception** by the communities
14. **Joy** at the resolution 14
15. **Mission** continues with clarity

**PART 5: PAUL’S TEACHING ON THE CHURCH AS BODY**

**A. The Eucharistic Synaxis as Model**

**Paul’s Vision:**

Paul, in light of the **Eucharistic synaxis** (assembly for Eucharist), develops the image of the Church as the **Body of Christ**.

**Purpose:** To explain both:

- The unity of the organism
- The diversity of its members

**B. All Members Are Necessary**

**1 Corinthians 12:12-17** - Every Part Needed

“Just as a human body, though it is made up of many parts, is a single unit because all these parts, though many, make one body, so it is with Christ... If the foot were to say, ‘I am not a hand and so I do not belong to the body,’ would that mean that it stopped being part of the body?... If your whole body was just one eye, how would you hear anything?”

**The Point:** In the Church, just as in the human body:

- All members are necessary
- Each has a specific role
- Unity doesn’t mean uniformity
- Diversity is essential

**C. Baptism Creates Equal Dignity**

**Galatians 3:28** - Unity in Christ

“There are no more distinctions between Jew and Greek, slave and free, male and female, but all of you are one in Christ Jesus”

**1 Corinthians 12:13** - One Body by Baptism

“In the one Spirit we were all baptized, Jews as well as Greeks, slaves as well as citizens, and one Spirit was given to us all to drink”

**Key Points:**

- ALL have the same dignity by virtue of Baptism
- Social, ethnic, gender distinctions don’t determine value
- ALL baptized into one Body
- ALL drink of one Spirit

**D. Each Contributes According to Their Gift**

**Ephesians 4:7** - Different Gifts

“Each one of us has been given his own share of grace, given as Christ allotted it... to each one of us is given his grace, according to the measure of Christ’s gift”

**Ephesians 4:11-13** - Variety of Ministries

“And to some, his gift was that they should be apostles; to some, prophets; to some, evangelists; to some, pastors and teachers; so that the saints together make a unity in the work of service, building up the body of Christ”

**The Purpose:**

- Building up the body of Christ
- Until we reach unity in faith
- Mature manhood
- The fullness of Christ

**E. All Are Equally Responsible**

**Key Teaching from the Document:**

“All are equally responsible for the life and mission of the community and all are called to work in accordance with the law of mutual solidarity in respect of their specific ministries and charisms”

**Why?** “Inasmuch as every one of them finds his or her energy in the one Lord”

**1 Corinthians 15:45** - Christ, the Life-Giving Spirit

Christ is the source of life for all members.

**F. Summary: Paul’s Ecclesiology**

**Paul teaches:**

1. Church is Christ’s Body - organic unity
2. Many members - essential diversity
3. Baptism creates equal dignity - no superiority based on ethnicity, social status, or gender
4. Each has specific gifts - from the Holy Spirit
5. All contribute to the mission - according to their gift
6. All equally responsible - for life and mission
7. Mutual solidarity required - respect for different ministries and charisms
8. One source of energy - the Lord Jesus Christ

**This is the theological foundation for synodality:**

- All participate (equal dignity)
- Each contributes uniquely (diverse gifts)
- Unity in diversity (one body, many members)
- Common mission (building up the Body)

**PART 6: THE ESCHATOLOGICAL VISION**

**A. The Journey’s End**

**The End-Point:**

The journey of the People of God is toward the **new Jerusalem**, enveloped by the radiant splendor of God’s glory.

**B. The Heavenly Liturgy**

**Apocalypse 5:6, 9, 11, 13** - The Lamb and the Assembly

**The Vision:**

John sees:

- **The Lamb** “standing that seemed to have been sacrificed”
- **People ransomed** “of every race, language, people and nation”
- **Made into a kingdom and priests** “for God, to rule the world”
- **Angels** “ten thousand times ten thousand and thousands upon thousands”
- **All creatures** in heaven, on earth, under the earth, in the sea

**They all sing:** “To the One who is sitting on the throne and to the Lamb, be all praise, honor, glory and power, for ever and ever”

**Significance:**

- Universal gathering - all peoples, languages, nations
- Heavenly liturgy - worship together
- Priests and kingdom - all share in Christ’s priesthood
- Creation included - all creation united

**C. God’s Dwelling with Humanity**

**Apocalypse 21:3** - The Promise Fulfilled

“Then I heard a loud voice call from the throne, ‘You see this city? Here God lives among men. He will make his home among them; they shall be his people, and he will be their God; his name is God-with-them.’”

**The Deepest Meaning:**

This promise holds the **deepest meaning of God’s saving plan**:

- God dwelling among humanity
- Making His home with them
- They will be His people
- He will be their God
- **God-with-them** (Emmanuel)

**For Synodality:**

The synodal journey is not just an organizational technique. It’s the way the People of God walks through history toward this eschatological fulfillment:

- God dwelling with us
- Perfect communion
- Unity of all peoples
- Heavenly liturgy
- The Kingdom fully realized

**SUMMARY AND SYNTHESIS**

**The New Testament Pattern for Synodality**

**1. JESUS FULFILLS GOD’S PLAN**

- Never acts alone - always with the Father
- Paschal Mystery = new Exodus gathering people in unity
- Forms a community of disciples
- Shares His authority with them

**2. JESUS IS THE WAY**

- Pilgrims following Jesus on the journey
- Emmaus as icon of synodal Church
- Walking together, guided by Word and Eucharist

**3. ALL BAPTIZED SHARE IN CHRIST’S AUTHORITY**

- Anointed by the Holy Spirit (1 John 2:20, 27)
- Taught by God (John 6:45)
- Guided to complete truth (John 16:13)
- Foundation of sensus fidei

**4. PENTECOST: SPIRIT ON ALL**

- Joel’s prophecy fulfilled
- Spirit poured on sons, daughters, old, young, slaves
- All prophesy - all participate
- Not just leaders but whole community

**5. EARLY COMMUNITY PATTERN (ACTS 2-4)**

- Teaching, fellowship, breaking bread, prayers
- Shared life and possessions
- Communal decision-making (choosing Matthias)
- Praying together
- All filled with Spirit

**6. ACTS 15: THE PARADIGM**

- Serious issue arises
- Whole community gathers
- Open and thorough debate
- Listening to testimonies
- Authoritative teaching
- Scriptural interpretation
- Decision by leaders under the Spirit
- “By the Holy Spirit and by ourselves”
- Consensus and unanimity
- Reception by communities

**7. CHURCH AS BODY OF CHRIST**

- One body, many members
- All necessary and valued
- Equal dignity through baptism
- Different gifts from the Spirit
- All contribute to mission
- Mutual solidarity required

**8. ESCHATOLOGICAL GOAL**

- Journey toward New Jerusalem
- Universal gathering of all peoples
- Heavenly liturgy
- God dwelling with humanity
- Perfect communion fulfilled

**What the New Testament Teaches About Synodality**

**THEOLOGICAL FOUNDATIONS:**

1. **Trinitarian** - Jesus never acts alone; always in communion with Father and Spirit
2. **Christological** - Jesus is the Way we follow together
3. **Pneumatological** - Holy Spirit guides all believers, not just leaders
4. **Ecclesiological** - Church is Body of Christ with many members
5. **Sacramental** - Baptism creates equal dignity; Eucharist creates communion
6. **Eschatological** - Journey toward final unity in God’s Kingdom

**PRACTICAL PATTERNS:**

1. **Everyone participates** - whole community involved
2. **Roles are distinct** - leaders have specific authority but don’t act alone
3. **Open discussion** - different views expressed and debated
4. **Multiple testimonies** - listening to various witnesses
5. **Scriptural grounding** - decisions interpreted with Scripture
6. **Spiritual discernment** - seeking what the Spirit is saying
7. **Movement to consensus** - from divergent views to unanimity
8. **Communal reception** - decisions accepted by communities
9. **Joy in resolution** - unity brings gladness
10. **Mission continues** - decisions serve evangelization

**CONNECTING TO SYNODALITY TODAY**

**From Biblical Foundations to Contemporary Practice**

**The New Testament gives us:**

1. **The Theological Why** - Jesus’ communion with Father; Spirit on all believers

3. **The Ecclesial Who** - All baptized (equal dignity, different roles)
4. **The Spiritual Dynamic** - “By the Holy Spirit and by ourselves”

**These biblical foundations underlie:**

- Vatican II’s ecclesiology of communion
- Pope Francis’s vision of synodal Church
- Diocesan synods and pastoral councils
- The Synod of Bishops
- All synodal structures and processes

**The Church today is called to:**

- Recover these biblical patterns
- Implement them faithfully
- Adapt them to contemporary contexts
- Always maintaining the essential elements

**CONCLUSION**

The New Testament establishes clearly that synodality is not a modern invention or organizational preference. It is:

**ROOTED IN:**

- The Trinity (Jesus’ communion with Father)
- Christology (Jesus as the Way)
- Pneumatology (Spirit poured on all)
- Ecclesiology (Church as Body of Christ)

**MODELED IN:**

- Jesus forming a community of disciples
- Pentecost and the early community
- The Council of Jerusalem (Acts 15)
- Paul’s teaching on the Body

**DIRECTED TOWARD:**

- Mission and evangelization
- Unity as witness
- Building up the Body
- The eschatological Kingdom

**The transition from Old Testament to New Testament shows:**

- Continuity: God still gathers a people to walk together
- Fulfillment: In Christ and by the Spirit
- Universality: Now including all peoples
- Interiority: Spirit and Law written on hearts
- Participation: All members empowered by the Spirit

**As we move forward in this course**, we will see how the Church through history has tried to live out these biblical foundations, sometimes faithfully, sometimes less so, and how Vatican II and Pope Francis call us to recover this essential dimension of the Church’s life.
