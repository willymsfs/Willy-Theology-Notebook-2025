This principle by **Cyprian of Carthage**, a 3rd-century bishop and martyr, is considered a foundational pillar of **synodality**. It describes a way of running the Church that balances leadership with community involvement.

In simple English, here is what these three parts mean:

1. The Threefold Rule of Decision-Making

Cyprian argued that for a decision to be truly “of the Church,” it must involve three specific groups:

• **“Nothing without the bishop” (_nihil sine episcopo_):** The bishop is the successor to the Apostles and the “overseer” of his flock. He holds the final responsibility and the “deciding vote” in matters of governance.

• **“Nothing without the council of presbyters and deacons” (_nihil sine consilio vestro_):** The bishop does not act as a lone dictator. He must work with his “senate” or council of priests (presbyters) and deacons, who are his closest collaborators.

• **“Nothing without the consent of the people” (_et sine consensu plebis_):** The “People of God” have an “instinct of faith” (_sensus fidei_) that helps the Church discern the truth. Cyprian believed that the everyday members of the Church must be listened to and their agreement sought before major actions are taken.

2. The Unity of Bishops

Cyprian also stated that **“the episcopate is one, of which each member has an undivided share”**. This means that even though there are thousands of bishops in different cities, they do not lead separate, independent “churches.” Instead, they all share in **one single office**. If you have a piece of a diamond, you have the same substance as the whole diamond; similarly, each bishop exercises the full authority of the Apostles, but they must do so in **hierarchical communion** with one another.

A Simple Example

Imagine a local church needs to decide how to help the poor during a city-wide crisis.

• **The Bishop** doesn't just make a decree from his office.

• First, he meets with his **priests and deacons** to look at the budget and the theology of the mission.

• Then, he consults the **lay faithful** to hear their “sense” of what the community actually needs.

• Finally, the Bishop makes the decision, knowing he has the **consent** of his people and the **counsel** of his clergy. This ensures the decision isn't just his own idea, but the “walking together” of the whole community.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

**Analogy for Understanding:** Think of the Church like a **large ship**. The **Bishop** is the Captain who has the final authority to steer. The **Presbyters and Deacons** are the Officers who provide the technical expertise and advice to the Captain. The **People** are the Crew and Passengers whose life and destination depend on the voyage. For the ship to reach its port safely, the Captain must lead, the Officers must advise, and the Crew must be in agreement with the direction. If the Captain ignores the Officers or the Crew, the ship risks mutiny or going off course; but without the Captain, the ship has no direction at all. All three are necessary for the journey.
