Introduction to Synodality

1.	Why the Church speaks of synodality today?

Synodality belongs to the very nature of the Church (International Theological Commission). Pope Francis names this moment a kairós – a decisive time, not an optional reform.

Key assertion Synodality is not a method added to the Church. It is the way the Church already exists, now rediscovered. This prevents reducing it to a mere strategy, policy, or pastoral experiment.

Etymology of “Synod”
Greek roots:
•	συν (syn) = with, together.
•	ὁδός (hodos) = path, road, way, journey.
Literal meaning: walking together on the way.
Theological weight (ITC):
•	Not symbolic decoration.
•	The Church is a pilgrim people journeying through history toward the Kingdom
•	Jesus is “the Way” (Jn 14:6).
•	Early Christians called “followers of the Way” (Acts 9:2; 19:9).
•	Church = people moving together in Christ, not parallel individual paths.

Meaning of Synodality
Synodality = modus vivendi et operandi → the way the Church lives, discerns, and acts as a community guided by the Holy Spirit. Not merely an event, but a permanent way of being.

Patristic Insight – St John Chrysostom
St John Chrysostom offers a crucial definition for this course: the Church is a name standing for 'walking together' (sýnodos).
He describes the Church as an assembly convoked to give God thanks, similar to a choir—a harmonic reality (σύστημα sýstema) where relations are ordered and converge in a common mind (homónoia).

Synod vs. Council

Aspect	Details
Theological convergence	Early Church: Greek sýnodos = Latin synodus or concilium. Concilium adds Hebrew qahal – assembly convoked by the Lord. Both express People of God “called together” as an assembly.
Vatican II usage	“Council” and “synod” used synonymously for the conciliar session.
Current Canon Law (1983)	Council: usually Ecumenical, Plenary, or Provincial – often legislative. Synod: usually Synod of Bishops or Diocesan Synod – more consultative/pastoral.


From “Synod” to “Synodality”
•	“Synod” = specific event (a meeting).
•	“Synodality” = neologism (new word) describing a constitutive dimension of the Church → Church is synodal by nature → Modus vivendi et operandi (the way of living and operating) of the entire People of God.

Three Levels of Synodality
1.	The Style Ordinary way the Church lives and works:
o	Listening to the Word.
o	Celebrating the Eucharist.
o	Functioning as communion where all share responsibility.
2.	The Structures Institutions that enable listening:
o	Synod of Bishops.
o	Diocesan synods.
o	Pastoral councils.
3.	The Events Specific gatherings convoked by competent authority to discern the path forward.

Link to Communion and Mission Synodality gives substance to the Church as “Communion”. Transforms communion from static concept into dynamic action: all members journey together and actively participate in evangelising mission.

Analogy: The Choir (St John Chrysostom)
•	A Synod (event) = a specific concert or rehearsal where the choir gathers.
•	Synodality = the underlying musicality, mutual listening, and harmonic reality that makes the choir a unified whole rather than isolated singers.
Without synodality (listening and harmonising), the synod (concert) becomes impossible.
