Old Testament roots of synodality:
 1: Start with the very beginning – creation itself
God creates the human person (man and woman) in His own image and likeness. From the start, humans are social beings – we are made for relationship, for communion. Our first calling is to cooperate with God, to care for creation together, and to move forward toward the goal He sets (Genesis 1:26-28).
Synodality begins here: the idea that we are not meant to walk alone but together with others and with God.
 2: Sin breaks the harmony, but God refuses to abandon the plan
Sin enters and tears apart the beautiful network of relationships that showed truth, goodness, and beauty in creation. Human hearts become blinded to their true calling.
Yet God, rich in mercy, does not give up. Again and again He confirms and renews His covenant in order to gather everything that has been scattered and to set it back on the path of unity. He heals human freedom and invites us to accept the gift of union with Him and with one another in creation, our common home.
Examples of these renewing covenants: Genesis 9:8-17 (after the flood), Genesis 15 and 17 (with Abraham), Exodus 19-24 (at Sinai), 2 Samuel 7:11 (with David).
The key point: God’s entire saving plan is about gathering a scattered people and putting them back on a shared path.
 3: God convokes an assembly – the qahal
To carry out this plan, God does not deal with isolated individuals. He calls together a people. He convokes Abraham and his descendants (Genesis 12:1-3; 17:1-5; 22:16-18).
This gathered people is called in Hebrew qahal or ‘edah (often translated into Greek as ekklēsia – the same word later used for “church”). The great moment of sealing this assembly is the Sinai covenant (Exodus 24:6-8; 34:20ff).
Freed from slavery in Egypt, Israel now becomes a people worthy to speak directly with God. On the exodus journey they gather around Him, celebrate His worship, and live according to His law, recognising that they belong to Him alone (Deuteronomy 5:1-22; Joshua 8; Nehemiah 8:1-18).
Synodality shows itself here as “assembly” – a people called together by God to walk with Him.
 4: The assembly has order and includes everyone
In the desert God commands a census of the tribes and gives each its proper place (Numbers 1-2). At the very centre stands the Lord Himself – the only true guide and shepherd.
He makes Himself present through the ministry of Moses (Numbers 12; 15-16; Joshua 8:30-35). Moses is helped in a subordinate and collegial way by judges (Exodus 18:25-26), elders (Numbers 11:16-17, 24-30), and Levites (Numbers 1:50-51).
Crucially, the assembly is not just adult men. It includes women, children, and even foreigners living among them (Joshua 8:33, 35).
Every time God renews the covenant, He calls the whole people as His partner (Deuteronomy 27-28; Joshua 24; 2 Kings 23; Nehemiah 8).
So synodality in the Old Testament is inclusive and ordered: everyone has a place, and the people together listen, decide, and commit themselves.
 5: The prophets keep calling the people to faithful walking together
The prophets constantly remind Israel to journey through history’s difficulties while staying faithful to the covenant. They summon the people to turn their hearts back to God and to live justice toward neighbours – especially the poor, the oppressed, and strangers – as the visible sign of God’s mercy (Jeremiah 37:21; 38:1).
To make this faithful journey possible, God promises interior renewal: a new heart and a new spirit (Ezekiel 11:19-20), a new exodus (Jeremiah 37-38), and above all a new covenant written not on stone tablets but on human hearts (Jeremiah 31:31-34).
This renewed covenant will reach universal horizons: the Servant of the Lord will gather the nations (Isaiah 53), and the Spirit will be poured out on every member of the people (Joel 3:1-4).
The prophetic voice therefore insists that true synodal walking requires continual listening, heartfelt conversion, and concrete justice.
Putting it all together
The Old Testament picture of synodality is this: God calls a people to walk together with Him through history, gathers them regularly in assembly to listen to His Word and renew the covenant, gives them ordered roles so everyone belongs, and – through the prophets – keeps calling them to interior conversion so they can stay faithful on the shared path.
All of this prepares the ground for what will be fully revealed in Jesus Christ: a People of God journeying together in communion, guided by the Spirit.
 
BIBLICAL FOUNDATIONS I: OLD TESTAMENT
Why Study Old Testament Foundations?
The synodal life of the Church is not a modern invention or organizational technique. It is rooted deeply in God’s plan of salvation revealed in Scripture. The Old Testament shows us the original pattern of how God calls His people to walk together, to gather in assembly, and to discern His will communally.
Key Theme
The Old Testament reveals that from the beginning, God’s plan involves gathering a people who journey together, listen to His voice, and respond in communion.

PART 1: THE PEOPLE OF GOD ON A JOURNEY 
A. God’s Original Plan: Created for Communion
From the Book of Genesis:
The Old Testament shows that God created the human person - man and woman - in His image and likeness as a social being. This is foundational for understanding synodality.
Genesis 1:26-28 - The Creation Account
God said: “Let us make man in our image, in the likeness of ourselves, and let them be masters of the fish of the sea, the birds of heaven, the cattle, all the wild beasts and all the reptiles that crawl upon the earth.”
Key Points:
•	Human beings are created to work WITH God
•	We are created as social beings (not isolated individuals)
•	We are called to move forward in the sign of communion
•	We are to care for creation and direct it towards its goal
•	This is the pattern: cooperation, not isolation
B. Sin Disrupts the Journey Together
The Reality of Sin:
From the beginning, sin plagues God’s plan. Sin tears apart the network of ordered relationships that expressed:
•	Truth
•	Goodness
•	Beauty of creation
Effect of Sin: Sin blinds human hearts to their calling to walk together in communion.
But God’s Response:
God, rich in mercy, does not abandon His plan. He confirms and renews His covenant to bring all that has been scattered back to the path of unity.
God’s Actions:
•	Healing human freedom
•	Directing humanity to welcome the gift of union with God
•	Restoring unity with our brothers and sisters
•	Renewing creation, our common home
Biblical Examples of God’s Renewed Covenant:
1.	Genesis 9:8-17 - Covenant with Noah after the flood
2.	Genesis 15 - Covenant with Abraham
3.	Genesis 17 - Confirmation of Abraham’s covenant
4.	Exodus 19-24 - Sinai covenant with Israel
5.	2 Samuel 7:11 - Covenant with David
C. The Calling of Abraham: Beginning of the Journey
Genesis 12:1-3 - God’s Call to Abraham
“Leave your country, your family and your father’s house, for the land I will show you. I will make you a great nation; I will bless you and make your name so famous that it will be used as a blessing.”
Genesis 17:1-5 - God Makes Abraham Father of Nations
God changes Abram’s name to Abraham, meaning “father of a multitude of nations.”
Genesis 22:16-18 - God’s Promise After the Test
“I swear by my own self... I will shower blessings on you, I will make your descendants as many as the stars of heaven and the grains of sand on the seashore.”
Significance:
God convokes Abraham and his descendants. This is the beginning of God assembling a people who will walk together through history toward a promised future.
D. The Assembly - QAHAL and EDAH
Two Hebrew Words for Assembly:
קָהָל (QAHAL) - Often translated as ἐκκλησία (ekklesia) in Greek
•	Means: assembly, congregation, gathering
•	Used for the people gathered together
עֵדָה (EDAH) - Also means assembly or congregation
•	Emphasizes the people as a community with common testimony
Key Point: These assemblies are convoked by God Himself. The people don’t gather on their own initiative - they are called together by the Lord.
E. The Sinai Covenant: The Assembly Ratified
Exodus 24:6-8 - The Blood of the Covenant
“Moses took half the blood and put it in basins, and the other half he sprinkled on the altar. Then he took the Book of the Covenant and read it to the listening people, and they said, ‘All that the Lord has said we will do and we will obey.’ Then Moses took the blood and sprinkled it over the people and said, ‘This is the blood of the covenant that the Lord has made with you, containing all these rules.’”
Exodus 34:20ff - The Covenant Renewed
After the golden calf incident, God renews the covenant with the people.
Significance of the Sinai Covenant:
The assembly (qahal/edah) ratified at Sinai makes the People freed from slavery:
•	Important and worthy to speak to God
•	A people who gather around their God
•	A community that celebrates His worship (cult)
•	A people who live by His law
•	A people who recognize they belong to Him alone
F. The First Form of Synodal Vocation
Key Statement from the Document:
“Qahal/edah is the first form in which the People of God’s synodal vocation is disclosed.”
What This Means:
The Old Testament assembly is the original pattern of:
•	God calling people together
•	People gathering to listen to God
•	Community responding together
•	Journeying as one people
This is synodality in its earliest form!
G. The Orderly Organization of the Assembly
Numbers 1-2 - The Census and Organization
God orders Moses to take a census of the tribes of Israel, giving each tribe its place in the camp and on the march.
Key Elements:
At the Center: The Lord Himself, who becomes present through:
•	The Ark of the Covenant
•	The Tabernacle (Tent of Meeting)
Primary Leader: Moses, through whom God guides the people
Associated Leaders (in subordinate and ‘collegial’ way):
1.	The Judges - Exodus 18:25-26 Moses appointed capable men as leaders of thousands, hundreds, fifties, and tens to judge the people in ordinary matters.
2.	The Elders - Numbers 11:16-17, 24-30 The Lord told Moses: “Gather for me seventy of the elders of Israel... I will take some of the spirit which is on you and put it on them. Then they will share the burden of the people with you.”
3.	The Levites - Numbers 1:50-51 The tribe of Levi was set apart for service of the Tabernacle and leading worship.
Significance:
This shows that even in the Old Testament, leadership was shared and ordered. Moses didn’t act alone but with the elders, judges, and Levites. This is an early form of collegiality.
H. Who Participates in the Assembly?
Not Just Men!
Exodus 24:7-8 - Men ratify the covenant
But also:
Joshua 8:33, 35 - The Whole Assembly
“All Israel, aliens and citizens alike, with their elders, scribes and judges... half of them in front of Mount Gerizim and half in front of Mount Ebal... Joshua then read out every word of the Law—the blessing and the curse—according to all that is written in the Book of the Law. Of all that Moses had commanded, there was not one word that Joshua failed to read to the whole assembly of Israel, including the women and children, and the strangers resident with them.”
Key Point: The assembly of the People of God consists of:
•	Men
•	Women
•	Children
•	Even foreigners (aliens/strangers)
Everyone is included when God renews His covenant!
I. Regular Renewal of the Covenant
The assembly is convoked regularly when the Lord renews His covenant:
1.	Deuteronomy 27-28 - Blessings and curses proclaimed to all
2.	Joshua 24 - Joshua gathers all tribes at Shechem for covenant renewal
3.	2 Kings 23 - King Josiah’s reform and covenant renewal
4.	Nehemiah 8 - Ezra reads the Law to the returned exiles
Pattern:
•	People gather as assembly
•	Word of God is proclaimed
•	People respond together
•	Covenant is renewed
•	They commit to walk together in obedience
This gathering, listening, responding together is the synodal pattern established in the Old Testament.

PART 2: COVENANTAL ASSEMBLIES AND COMMUNAL DISCERNMENT 
A. The Nature of Israel’s Assemblies
Key Characteristics:
1.	Convoked by God - Not human initiative but divine summons
2.	Centered on God’s Presence - The Lord is in the midst
3.	Purpose: Covenant Renewal - Remembering and recommitting
4.	Involves Everyone - All members of the community
5.	Requires Response - Listening leads to decision and action
B. The Desert Journey: Learning to Walk Together
Numbers 12 - The Challenge to Moses’ Authority
When Miriam and Aaron challenge Moses, God vindicates Moses’ unique role while also showing the need for shared leadership.
Numbers 15-16 - Rebellion and Its Consequences
The rebellion of Korah, Dathan, and Abiram shows what happens when people reject the ordered structure God has established.
Numbers 11:16-17, 24-30 - The Seventy Elders
The Story: Moses was overwhelmed by the burden of leading the people alone. God commanded him to gather seventy elders.
God’s Action: “I will take some of the spirit which is on you and put it on them. Then they will share the burden of the people with you, and you will not have to bear it alone.”
What Happened: When the Spirit rested on the seventy elders, they prophesied. Two men (Eldad and Medad) who weren’t at the meeting also prophesied in the camp.
Joshua’s Reaction: “My lord Moses, stop them!”
Moses’ Response: “Are you jealous on my account? If only the whole people of the Lord were prophets, and the Lord gave his Spirit to them all!”
Key Lessons:
1.	Shared Leadership - The burden is not meant to be carried alone
2.	Spirit-Given Authority - Leadership comes from God’s Spirit
3.	Generosity in Leadership - Moses wishes ALL could share in prophecy
4.	Communal Discernment - The elders help bear the burden together
This is an early model of synodal leadership!
C. Joshua and the Assembly
Joshua 8:30-35 - Covenant Ceremony at Mount Ebal
After entering the Promised Land, Joshua gathers ALL the people:
•	Citizens and foreigners
•	Men, women, and children
•	With the elders, scribes, and judges
He reads EVERY word of the Law to them. This is communal listening and renewed commitment.
Joshua 24 - The Great Assembly at Shechem
The Pattern:
1.	Joshua recounts salvation history (verses 1-13)
2.	Calls for decision: “Choose today whom you wish to serve” (verse 15)
3.	People respond: “We will serve the Lord” (verse 16-18)
4.	Joshua challenges them: “You cannot serve the Lord” (verse 19)
5.	People insist: “We will serve the Lord our God” (verse 24)
6.	Covenant is made with all the people (verse 25)
Significance:
This shows communal discernment:
•	Recalling God’s saving acts
•	Presenting the choice clearly
•	People deliberating together
•	Making a common commitment
•	Sealing the decision in covenant
This is not one person deciding for others, but the whole assembly discerning and choosing together.
D. Later Covenant Renewals
2 Kings 23:1-3 - Josiah’s Reform (7th century BC)
The Process:
1.	King summoned all the elders
2.	He went up to the Temple with all the people of Judah, all the inhabitants of Jerusalem, priests, prophets, and the whole populace
3.	The Book of the Covenant (found in the Temple) was read aloud to them
4.	The king stood by the pillar and in the presence of the Lord made a covenant
5.	All the people gave their allegiance to the covenant
Key Point: Even the king doesn’t act alone. The whole people participate in the covenant renewal.
Nehemiah 8:1-18 - Assembly After the Exile
The Setting: The people have returned from Babylonian exile and rebuilt Jerusalem’s walls.
The Assembly:
•	All the people gathered as one
•	Men, women, and all who could understand
•	From daybreak until noon
•	Ezra the scribe read from the Book of the Law
•	The people listened attentively
The Response (verses 5-6): “Ezra blessed the Lord, the great God, and all the people raised their hands and answered, ‘Amen! Amen!’ Then they bowed down and worshiped the Lord with their faces to the ground.”
The Explanation (verse 8): “They read from the book of the Law of God, translating and giving the sense, so that the people understood what was read.”
The Celebration (verses 9-12): After understanding God’s Word, the people celebrated with great joy because they understood the words that had been explained to them.
Significance:
This shows the full synodal pattern:
•	Assembly of all the people
•	Reading of God’s Word
•	Explanation so all can understand
•	Communal response
•	Shared joy and celebration
•	Common commitment to live by God’s Word

PART 3: PROPHETIC LISTENING AND OBEDIENCE 
A. The Role of the Prophets
The Prophets’ Mission:
The prophets teach the People of God the need to journey through the hardships of history in faithfulness to the covenant.
What the Prophets Do:
•	Call people to conversion
•	Demand justice in relationships
•	Speak for those without voice
•	Interpret the signs of the times
•	Point to God’s future plans
This is relevant to synodality because it shows that listening must include:
•	Prophetic voices
•	Critique when necessary
•	Call to conversion
•	Attention to justice and the poor
B. Conversion of Hearts
The Prophets’ Call:
The prophets invite the people to:
1.	Conversion of hearts to God - Return to covenant faithfulness
2.	Justice in relationships with neighbors - Especially toward: 
o	The poorest
o	The oppressed
o	Foreigners
Why This Matters:
Justice toward neighbours, especially the poor and foreigners, is a tangible witness to God’s mercy.
Key Prophetic Texts:
Jeremiah 37:21 - Care for the imprisoned Jeremiah himself experienced being imprisoned and appealed for justice.
Jeremiah 38:1 - Speaking truth even when dangerous The prophet continues to proclaim God’s word despite opposition.
C. God’s Promise: A New Heart and Spirit
The Problem: Human hearts are hard and unable to keep the covenant faithfully.
God’s Solution: He promises to give a new heart and spirit.
Ezekiel 11:19-20 - The New Heart
“I will give them a single heart and I will put a new spirit in them; I will remove the heart of stone from their bodies and give them a heart of flesh, so that they will keep my laws and respect my observances and put them into practice. Then they shall be my people and I will be their God.”
Key Elements:
•	Single heart - Unity of purpose
•	New spirit - From God Himself
•	Heart of flesh - Soft, responsive (not stone)
•	Result - Ability to keep God’s laws
•	Goal - True covenant relationship
Significance for Synodality:
True synodal walking together requires:
•	Hearts transformed by God
•	Spirit-given ability to listen and obey
•	Unity that comes from God’s gift
•	Responsiveness to God’s Word
D. The Promise of a New Exodus
Jeremiah 37-38 - God Will Act Again
Despite the people’s failures and the exile to Babylon, God promises to act again. He will lead a new Exodus.
The Pattern:
•	As God led the people out of Egypt
•	Through the wilderness
•	To the Promised Land
So God will:
•	Lead them out of exile
•	Through difficulties
•	To a renewed relationship with Him
E. The New Covenant
Jeremiah 31:31-34 - The New Covenant Prophecy
This is one of the most important prophetic texts:
“See, the days are coming—it is the Lord who speaks—when I will make a new covenant with the House of Israel and the House of Judah, but not a covenant like the one I made with their ancestors on the day I took them by the hand to bring them out of the land of Egypt. They broke that covenant of mine, so I had to show them who was master. It is the Lord who speaks.
No, this is the covenant I will make with the House of Israel when those days arrive—it is the Lord who speaks. Deep within them I will plant my Law, writing it on their hearts. Then I will be their God and they shall be my people.
There will be no further need for neighbour to try to teach neighbour, or brother to say to brother, ‘Learn to know the Lord!’ No, they will all know me, the least no less than the greatest—it is the Lord who speaks—since I will forgive their iniquity and never call their sin to mind.”
Key Characteristics of the New Covenant:
1.	Internalized - Written on hearts, not stone tablets
2.	Universal Knowledge - All will know God, from least to greatest
3.	No Need for Teachers - Direct relationship with God
4.	Forgiveness - God will forgive and forget their sins
5.	True Relationship - “I will be their God and they shall be my people”
Significance:
This promise looks forward to:
•	Pentecost and the gift of the Holy Spirit
•	The Law written on hearts by the Spirit
•	All members of God’s people having direct access to God
•	The sensus fidei - the sense of faith in all believers
This is the foundation for understanding how ALL the baptized can participate in the Church’s synodal life!
F. Universal Horizons
Isaiah 53 - The Servant of the Lord
The prophets promise that the covenant will not be limited to Israel alone. The Servant of the Lord will gather together the nations.
The Vision:
•	God’s salvation extends to all peoples
•	The covenant becomes universal
•	All nations will be included
G. The Outpouring of the Spirit
Joel 3:1-4 (or Joel 2:28-32 in some Bibles) - Spirit Poured Out on All
This is the climactic promise that sets the stage for Pentecost:
“After this I will pour out my spirit on all mankind. Your sons and daughters shall prophesy, your old men shall dream dreams, and your young men see visions. Even on the slaves, men and women, will I pour out my spirit in those days.”
Revolutionary Elements:
1.	All mankind - Not just Israel
2.	Sons AND daughters - Women included
3.	Old AND young - All ages
4.	Even slaves - Social status doesn’t matter
5.	Men AND women - Both genders
The Result: All will prophesy, dream dreams, see visions
Significance for Synodality:
This promise underlies the Catholic understanding that:
•	ALL the baptized receive the Holy Spirit
•	ALL have gifts to contribute
•	ALL can hear God’s voice
•	ALL participate in the prophetic office of Christ
•	The sensus fidei belongs to ALL the faithful
This is why synodality involves the WHOLE People of God, not just the hierarchy!

PART 4: SUMMARY AND SYNTHESIS (5 )
The Old Testament Pattern for Synodality
1. GOD INITIATES
•	God calls and gathers a people
•	The assembly is convoked by the Lord
•	This is not human initiative but divine summons
2. PEOPLE JOURNEY TOGETHER
•	From Abraham’s call forward
•	Through Exodus and wilderness
•	Into the Promised Land
•	Through exile and return
•	Always as a people, not isolated individuals
3. ORDERED STRUCTURE
•	Primary leader (Moses, Joshua, etc.)
•	Elders, judges, priests, Levites
•	But INCLUDING all the people
•	Men, women, children, foreigners
4. REGULAR ASSEMBLIES
•	Covenant ratification (Sinai)
•	Covenant renewals (Shechem, under Josiah, with Nehemiah)
•	All the people gather
•	God’s Word is proclaimed
•	People respond together
5. COMMUNAL DISCERNMENT
•	Joshua 24: “Choose today whom you wish to serve”
•	People deliberate and decide together
•	Elders bear the burden together with Moses
•	Prophets challenge and guide
6. INCLUSIVE PARTICIPATION
•	Men and women
•	Young and old
•	Citizens and foreigners
•	Rich and poor
•	All are included in the assembly
7. LISTENING AND OBEDIENCE
•	Prophets call to faithful listening
•	Conversion of heart required
•	Justice toward neighbours, especially the poor
•	Walking together in God’s ways
8. GOD’S PROMISES FOR THE FUTURE
•	New heart and spirit (Ezekiel)
•	New covenant written on hearts (Jeremiah)
•	Spirit poured out on ALL (Joel)
•	All will know God, from least to greatest
•	Universal salvation including all nations
The Foundation for Christian Synodality
What the Old Testament Establishes:
1.	God’s People Walk Together - Synodality is not a modern invention but rooted in God’s original plan
2.	Assembly is Essential - Qahal/Edah as the first form of synodal vocation
3.	Everyone Participates - Not just leaders, but all members of the community
4.	Ordered Leadership - Leaders serve the community in hearing God’s voice
5.	Prophetic Dimension - Need for those who speak truth and call to justice
6.	Covenant Renewal - Regular gathering to hear God’s Word and respond
7.	Promise of the Spirit - Joel’s prophecy points to Pentecost and the age of the Church
The Transition to the New Testament
The Old Testament patterns and promises find their fulfillment in:
•	Jesus Christ, the new Moses who leads the new Exodus
•	The Church, the new People of God
•	Pentecost, when the Spirit is poured out on all
•	The New Covenant written on hearts
•	The Council of Jerusalem (Acts 15) as the first Christian synodal event

CONCLUSION
The Old Testament establishes the fundamental patterns that the Church will adopt and fulfill in Christ:
•	God gathers a people to walk together
•	All members participate in the assembly
•	Leadership is ordered but shared
•	Regular gathering for covenant renewal
•	Communal discernment and decision
•	Prophetic voices call to justice
•	God promises interior transformation by His Spirit
•	The Spirit will be given to ALL
These patterns are not abolished in the New Testament but fulfilled and perfected in Christ and His Church, as we will see in our next session on the New Testament foundations of synodality.
