The five golden threads

  • Thread - Jesus continues his work — Acts is volume two of one work: what Jesus "began to do and teach" (Acts 1:1) he continues to do through his Spirit-filled Church.
  • Thread - New Exodus, End of Exile — The signs and wonders of Acts renew the signs of the Exodus: the Church is leading a new exodus out of the bondage of sin.
  • Thread - The New Israel without borders — The kingdom is planted in the heart, not bounded by land: the New Israel has no ethnic or geographic frontier.
  • Thread - Suffering as seed — Persecution does not stop the gospel; it scatters it - the blood of the martyrs is the seed of the Church.
  • Thread - The Church we know today — The Church we know today - apostolic authority, sacraments, Magisterium and the office of Peter - is visible in seed form throughout Acts.

Session 1 — Reading the Map

Introduction to Acts · Passage: Introduction · Leg: Leg I - Base Camp Jerusalem

Slide 1 · Trailhead (5 min)

Acts completes the three-part Great Adventure series (Bible Timeline → Matthew → Acts). A thirty-year span after the Resurrection, in which the Church "turned the world upside down" (Acts 17:6).

Slide 2 · Story Script (15 min)

Begin with Luke the physician at his desk, writing to one man — Theophilus, "lover of God" — telling him volume two of a story: what Jesus began to do (the Gospel) and what he continues to do through his Spirit-filled Church. Invite the class: every lover of God is Theophilus; this letter is addressed to you.

Slide 3 · Deeper Look (30 min)

  • Author: Luke, physician, sometime companion of Paul. Date: likely before or in the early AD 60s — no mention of Nero's persecution (AD 65), Paul's martyrdom, or the fall of the Temple (AD 70).
  • Audience: Gentile Christians and potential converts; Luke omits Hebrew-Aramaic presuppositions.
  • Structure from Acts 1:8: Jerusalem (1:1–8:3) → Judea & Samaria (8:4–12:25) → ends of the earth (13:1–28:31). Peter dominates chapters 1–12, Paul chapters 13–28.
  • Intertextual key — the Luke–Acts parallels: Jesus prays at his baptism / the disciples pray awaiting Spirit-baptism (Lk 3:21 // Acts 1:14); anointing by the Spirit (Lk 3:21-22 // Acts 2:1-4); healing of a paralytic (Lk 5:17-26 // Acts 3:1-10; 20:9-12); four trials of Jesus // four trials of Paul (Lk 22–23 // Acts 23–26); dying words of Jesus // of Stephen (Lk 23:34, 46 // Acts 7:59-60). See Luke-Acts Parallels.

Session 2 — Prelude to Power

Ascension, waiting, Matthias · Passage: Acts 1 · Leg: Leg I - Base Camp Jerusalem

Slide 1 · Trailhead (5 min)

Between Resurrection and Pentecost: forty days of instruction, then waiting in the upper room.

Slide 2 · Story Script (15 min)

The disciples ask the wrong-shaped question — "Lord, will you at this time restore the kingdom to Israel?" — dreaming of thrones and armies. Jesus answers with a mission instead of a map: "You shall receive power… you shall be my witnesses." Then the cloud takes him, and 120 people go upstairs to wait and pray with Mary.

Slide 3 · Deeper Look (30 min)

  • Kingdom expectation: reuniting the twelve tribes under a Davidic king — read against Daniel 2 (Nebuchadnezzar's five kingdoms, the fifth everlasting) and Daniel 7:13-14 (the Son of Man coming with clouds — Jesus' most-used title).
  • Ascension clouds = messianic presence language (the pillar of cloud, Ex 13). Jesus is enthroned in heaven; power flows from a restored King.
  • Mary intercedes — the Queen Mother pattern (Gebirah, 1 Kgs 2). Peter speaks as steward (al ha-bayit, "over the house" — cf. Mt 18; Is 22).
  • The 120: the number needed to found a new community. Judas' office must be filled (Pss 69; 109) — the New Israel needs twelve pillars.
  • Casting lots: not chance but discernment (Lev 16:7-10; Prov 16:33; 1 Chr 24:31 — priestly duties in the Temple). Jesus is the new Temple, the apostles the new priests. Luke opens both his books with lots (Zechariah, Lk 1).

Session 3 — The Fire Falls

The Power of Pentecost · Passage: Acts 2 · Leg: Leg I - Base Camp Jerusalem

Slide 1 · Trailhead (5 min)

Pentecost = "fifty days": the Feast of Weeks, fifty days after Passover (Lev 23; Dt 16:16) — a harvest feast that became the feast of the giving of the Law at Sinai.

Slide 2 · Story Script (15 min)

Jerusalem is packed with pilgrims from fifteen nations. Suddenly: a sound like driving wind, tongues as of fire, and Galilean fishermen preaching in every mother-tongue in the crowd. Babel runs backwards. Peter — the man who denied Jesus to a servant girl — stands and preaches to thousands. By evening, three thousand are baptized.

Slide 3 · Deeper Look (30 min)

  • Acts 2 parallels Sinai (Ex 19): fire, theophany, covenant, and the new law of the Spirit (Rom 8:2).
  • Wind and fire in the OT: Ex 3:1-3; 13:21-22; 19:16-21; Ps 104:3-4.
  • Reversal of Babel (Gen 11:1-9): the division of tongues undone in the Church "which speaks all tongues" (Ad Gentes 4).
  • Tongues mark four new beginnings: Jews (Acts 2), Samaritans (Acts 8), Gentiles (Acts 10), disciples of John at Ephesus (Acts 19). See The Four Pentecosts.
  • Peter's sermon: Joel 2:28-32 fulfilled; "last days" = the end of the Old Covenant; proofs that Jesus is Messiah (2:22-36).
  • Response: repent and be baptized. Infant baptism from the 2nd century — the new circumcision of the heart (1 Pet 3:21); "crooked generation" (Dt 32:5; Phil 2:15).
  • The four pillars of the community (2:42): the apostles' teaching, fellowship, the breaking of bread (Eucharist), and the prayers.

Session 4 — First Steps, First Signs

The Miraculous Power of Jesus' Name · Passage: Acts 3 · Leg: Leg I - Base Camp Jerusalem

Slide 1 · Trailhead (5 min)

After Pentecost's three thousand, the Jerusalem church models Church life (Catechesi Tradendae 10). Now the first recorded miracle.

Slide 2 · Story Script (15 min)

Three o'clock — the hour of the evening sacrifice. A man lame from birth sits where he has always sat, at the Beautiful Gate, hand out. Peter has no silver, no gold — only a Name. "In the name of Jesus Christ of Nazareth, walk." And the man doesn't just walk; he leaps into the Temple, and the whole crowd knows him.

Slide 3 · Deeper Look (30 min)

  • Luke's double witness pattern: divine sign (3:1-11) then apostolic testimony (3:12–4:23); cf. Heb 2:3-4; 1 Cor 2:3-5; Rom 15:18-19.
  • Ninth hour = 3 PM, the hour of the evening liturgy (Ex 29:38-42; Nm 28:1-8; Dn 9:21) — foundation of the Liturgy of the Hours (CCC 1174–1178).
  • "Beautiful Gate": likely Josephus' Corinthian-bronze gate on the east side; the poor sat at the gate. The Temple was Israel's centre — Jesus now is.
  • The lame leaping = messianic sign (Is 35:6; Mal 4:2).
  • Theology of the Name: a name expresses essence; YHWH the unpronounceable tetragrammaton, spoken once yearly by the high priest; calling on the Name invokes presence (Ex 20:24; Dt 12:5; Ps 91:14). Divine name formulas: Yahweh-jireh (Gen 22:14), -rapha (Ex 15:26), -nissi (Ex 17:15), -shalom (Jgs 6:24), -ra'ah (Ps 23), -tsidkenu (Jer 23:6), -shammah (Ez 48:35).
  • Yeshua = "God saves" (Mt 1:21; Lk 1); Simeon sees "the salvation of God" (Lk 2); salvation comes to Zacchaeus' house (Lk 19:9).
  • Peter's sermon in Solomon's Portico: Jesus as God's "Servant" — Is 42:1-4; 52:13–53:12; the call to repentance; "to you first" (Acts 3:26).

Session 5 — First Resistance

The Church Acts with Boldness · Passage: Acts 4:1-31 · Leg: Leg I - Base Camp Jerusalem

Slide 1 · Trailhead (5 min)

The healing draws a crowd — and the authorities. First arrest, first trial, first test of nerve.

Slide 2 · Story Script (15 min)

Peter and John spend the night in custody. In the morning they face the same court that condemned Jesus. The judges look at two "uneducated, common men" and are astonished — then they recognize the source: "they had been with Jesus." Ordered to silence, the apostles answer: "We cannot but speak of what we have seen and heard." Released, the Church doesn't hide; it prays Psalm 2 — and the room shakes.

Slide 3 · Deeper Look (30 min)

  • Confrontation between the old leadership and the body of Christ; the emerging Church does the work of Christ.
  • "Cornerstone" (Acts 4:11): Is 28:14-16; Jesus applies it in Lk 20:9-19; developed in Eph 2:19-22; 1 Pet 2:4-9.
  • Salvation in three tenses: we have been saved (Rom 8:24), are being saved (1 Pet 3:21), will be saved if we persevere (Mt 10:22; Mk 13:13) — cooperation with grace.
  • New Israel and the new Temple: the Sanhedrin no longer rules reconstituted Israel; the apostles who speak in Jesus' name do (Lk 20:9-19; 22:29-30; Acts 1:15-26).
  • Prayer of the Church: Psalm 2 in full — the nations' plot against the LORD's Anointed thwarted; the believers apply it prophetically to themselves; they ask for boldness, and the place is shaken like a second Pentecost. When light and darkness collide, darkness gives way.

Session 6 — Danger Inside the Camp

Problems Within, Persecutions Without · Passage: Acts 4:32-5:42 · Leg: Leg I - Base Camp Jerusalem

Slide 1 · Trailhead (5 min)

The camp's greatest dangers are twofold: deception within, persecution without. Barnabas versus Ananias and Sapphira.

Slide 2 · Story Script (15 min)

Barnabas lays a field's price at the apostles' feet — freely, wholly. Ananias and Sapphira stage the same gesture with a hidden discount, lying not to men but to the Holy Spirit. Both fall dead, and "great fear came upon the whole church." Meanwhile Peter's mere shadow heals the sick in the streets, an angel opens prison doors by night, and beaten apostles walk out of the council rejoicing to be counted worthy of dishonour for the Name.

Slide 3 · Deeper Look (30 min)

  • One heart and soul: land-and-blessing theology (Gen 12:1-3; 15) transposed — Eretz Israel is no longer the point; the New Covenant blessing is in the heart; the Kingdom is not limited by geography (Dt 30:1-10; Dn 9's seventy weeks leading to Jesus, who ends the exile of sin).
  • Ananias and Sapphira: Satan fills their hearts; parallel to Achan's sin (Josh 7, herem); there is no private sin; lying to the Spirit = lying to a divine Person (Constantinople, AD 381).
  • Signs and wonders: Exodus signs renewed in the Church's new exodus (Acts 2:19; 4:30; 6:8; 14:3; 15:12); sacrament vs. sacramental; God uses even shadows (5:15); the communion of saints.
  • Angels free the apostles (Heb 13:2). Gamaliel's counsel — if this is of God, you cannot overthrow it (cf. CCC 269); the "changing of the guard" in Israel's leadership.
  • Civil disobedience in charity and justice (CCC 450, 2242); the blood of the martyrs as the seed of the Church.

Session 7 — The First Martyr's Torch

The First Christian Martyr · Passage: Acts 6-7 · Leg: Leg I - Base Camp Jerusalem

Slide 1 · Trailhead (5 min)

From Jerusalem toward Judea and Samaria; persecution scatters the seed. Enter the seven, and Stephen.

Slide 2 · Story Script (15 min)

Greek-speaking widows are being overlooked at the daily distribution. The apostles refuse to abandon the Word or the widows — they ordain seven Spirit-filled men to serve. One of them, Stephen, face shining like an angel's, retells Israel's whole story before the council: a people forever resisting the deliverers God sends. When he says he sees the Son of Man standing at God's right hand, they drag him outside the city. He dies like his Master — "Lord, do not hold this sin against them" — while a young man named Saul guards the cloaks.

Slide 3 · Deeper Look (30 min)

  • Hellenists (Greek-speaking, Septuagint) vs. Hebrews (Aramaic-speaking, Palestine); care of widows central to Judaism (Dt 26:12; Jas 1:27); the seven have Greek names — God's welcome of the marginalized wing.
  • Diaconate: prayer and the laying on of hands = consecration and commissioning (Nm 8:10; 1 Tim 3:8-13; 4:14; Phil 1:1). Vatican II restored the permanent diaconate.
  • Stephen–Jesus parallels: irresistible wisdom, false witnesses on the Temple, the blasphemy charge (Lev 24:16), mob violence, the vision of the Son of Man, the dying prayer for enemies (Mt 26:57-68 // Acts 6; Lk 23:34, 46 // Acts 7:59-60).
  • Stephen's speech: salvation history from Abraham to the present; two themes — Israel's rebellion, and God appearing in Gentile lands; critique of fixation on land and Temple; Moses' life in three forties; the future prophet (Dt 18:15); the golden calf (Ex 32); wilderness apostasy (Ez 20:7-8); exile foretold (Am 5:25-27); "Heaven is my throne" (Is 66:1-2); God's two concessions — a king (1 Sam 8) and a temple; Jesus is the new King and the new Temple; the law given by angels (CCC 332).
  • Stephen sees the beatific vision — "Son of Man" from Dn 7:13-14: our hope under persecution.

Session 8 — Over the Wall

Philip in Samaria; the Ethiopian Eunuch · Passage: Acts 8 · Leg: Leg II - Crossing Borders

Slide 1 · Trailhead (5 min)

Persecution scatters the Church — and the scattering becomes sowing. First frontier: Samaria.

Slide 2 · Story Script (15 min)

Philip the deacon walks into the one region every self-respecting Jew walks around. Centuries of hatred — Assyria, intermarriage, rival worship — and Philip preaches Christ there, and Samaria rejoices. A celebrity magician tries to buy the Spirit and is scorched by Peter's rebuke. Then an angel sends Philip to an empty desert road, where a chariot rolls by carrying the treasurer of Ethiopia, reading Isaiah 53 aloud and understanding nothing — until Philip climbs in.

Slide 3 · Deeper Look (30 min)

  • Samaria's backstory: the Divided Kingdom (930 BC), the northern prophets (Elijah, Elisha, Amos, Hosea), Hosea's unfaithful-bride message, the Assyrian conquest of 722 BC, intermarriage, mutual hatred. Jesus already crossed this line (Jn 4; Lk 17) — Philip follows his Master.
  • Restoration sign: Hosea 2:11's "end of mirth" reversed — "there was much joy in that city" (Acts 8:8).
  • Simon the Magician: tradition's "father of heretics" (Justin Martyr); proto-Gnosticism (Platonic dualism, sōma sēma esti — "the body is a tomb"; secret knowledge). "Simony" is named after him.
  • Confirmation: Peter and John lay hands to complete what baptism began (Heb 6:1-2); the bishop as ordinary minister.
  • The Ethiopian eunuch: a God-fearer excluded by Dt 23:1, reading Isaiah's Suffering Servant (Is 52–53) and needing a guide — perspicuity vs. the Magisterium's role. Is 56 promises eunuchs a place in the coming kingdom. Philip baptizes him; the Spirit carries Philip off like Elijah (1 Kgs 18; 2 Kgs 2). Tradition: the eunuch evangelized Ethiopia.

Session 18 — The Prisoner Speaks

Paul Makes His Defense · Passage: Acts 22:1-25:12 · Leg: Leg III - To the Ends of the Earth

Slide 1 · Trailhead (5 min)

What looks like failure — near-lynching, years of custody — becomes a preaching tour before ever-higher courts.

Slide 2 · Story Script (15 min)

From the barracks steps, Paul silences a murderous crowd — in Hebrew. He tells his story: the zealous Pharisee, the Damascus light, the commission to the Gentiles. At the word "Gentiles" the riot reignites. Before the Sanhedrin he sets Pharisee against Sadducee with one sentence about the resurrection. A murder plot by forty sworn fasters is foiled by a nephew's tip and a midnight cavalry escort to Caesarea. Governor Felix trembles at Paul's talk of justice and judgment — and keeps him two years hoping for a bribe. Festus inherits the case, and Paul plays the citizen's card: "I appeal to Caesar."

Slide 3 · Deeper Look (30 min)

  • Five apologiai across Acts 22–26, before audiences of rising rank: the crowd, the Sanhedrin, Felix, Festus, Agrippa (Session 19).
  • The four trials of Paul parallel the four trials of Jesus: Sanhedrin, Felix, Festus, Agrippa // Sanhedrin, Pilate, Herod, Pilate — the Session One chart. See Luke-Acts Parallels.
  • Defence strategy develops with the audience: to the Jews, a brother's testimony of Jewish credentials and divine commission; to the council, the resurrection as the real issue; to Felix, the continuity of "the Way" with the faith of the fathers and the hope of resurrection; to Festus, innocence and the appeal to Caesar — the hinge that turns the story toward Rome.
  • Roman citizenship as providential instrument; the suffering foretold at his conversion (9:16) now unfolding.

Session 19 — Before Kings and Through the Storm

Final Defense and Witness in Rome · Passage: Acts 25:13-28:31 · Leg: Leg III - To the Ends of the Earth

Slide 1 · Trailhead (5 min)

The fifth and finest apologia — then shipwreck, serpent, and the capital of the world.

Slide 2 · Story Script (15 min)

In full pageantry, King Agrippa and Bernice take their seats, and the prisoner in chains tells his story one last time — so compellingly that Festus shouts "Paul, you are mad!" and Agrippa deflects: "In a short time you would persuade me to be a Christian!" The verdict everyone reaches: "This man could have been set free if he had not appealed to Caesar" — the exact echo of Pilate on Jesus. Then the sea voyage: fourteen days of storm, an angel by night, 276 lives saved on Paul's word, bread broken on a doomed deck. Malta: a viper strikes and fails. Rome: two years under guard, "preaching the kingdom of God… quite openly and unhindered." Luke ends mid-adventure — because the adventure hasn't ended.

Slide 3 · Deeper Look (30 min)

  • Before Agrippa: Paul the Pharisee on trial for "the hope of the restoration of Israel" — the twelve tribes; the North exiled 722 BC; restoration possible only through the Resurrection and the ingathering of the Gentiles (Jer 31:1, 9-10, 31-33; Ez 36:22-25; 37:16-22). Paul's theology of restoration (Rom 11) — go to the Gentiles, where the ten tribes are, to stir Israel to jealousy.
  • "Rise and stand" (26:16) — prophetic commissioning language (Ez 2:1-2); "nothing but what Moses and the prophets said": the Messiah must suffer (Ps 89:38-51; Is 49:4; 53:3).
  • "Kick against the goads" (26:14): the ox injuring itself by resisting the master — Paul had long resisted the Spirit's prodding.
  • Agrippa's "this man could have been released" (26:32) parallels Pilate (Lk 23:16, 22), closing the Jesus–Paul trial parallels.
  • Malta (28): three months; signs of God's mark and protection (the viper, the healings).
  • Rome (28:11-31): encouragements on arrival; the Jews approached first (28:17-25); the Is 6 indictment of unbelief; the open ending — the gospel "unhindered" (akōlytōs, the book's last word): the reader must finish the story.

Thread — Jesus continues his work

The idea, in one sentence

Acts is volume two of one work: what Jesus "began to do and teach" (Acts 1:1) he continues to do through his Spirit-filled Church.

Explanation

Thread — New Exodus, End of Exile

The idea, in one sentence

The signs and wonders of Acts renew the signs of the Exodus: the Church is leading a new exodus out of the bondage of sin.

Thread — Suffering as seed

The idea, in one sentence

Persecution does not stop the gospel; it scatters it - the blood of the martyrs is the seed of the Church.

Beaten apostles leave the council rejoicing to be counted worthy of dishonour for the Name (Acts 5). Thread — The Church we know today

The idea, in one sentence

The Church we know today - apostolic authority, sacraments, Magisterium and the office of Peter - is visible in seed form throughout Acts.

Explanation

The apostolic office must be filled (Acts 1); the four pillars of Acts 2:42 are teaching, fellowship, breaking of bread and prayers; the seven are ordained by prayer and the laying on of hands (Acts 6); Peter and John lay hands on the Samaritans to complete what baptism began (Acts 8); the Jerusalem Council writes "it has seemed good to the Holy Spirit and to us" (15:28) with Peter's judgment final - the prototype of every ecumenical council; the church at Troas breaks bread on the first day of the week (20:7).

Thread — The New Israel without borders

The idea, in one sentence

The kingdom is planted in the heart, not bounded by land: the New Israel has no ethnic or geographic frontier.

Explanation

Land-and-blessing theology (Gen 12:1-3; 15) is transposed - Eretz Israel is no longer the point (Dt 30:1-10). Stephen exposes the idolizing of land and Temple; Jesus is the new King and the new Temple (Acts 7). The wall falls in stages: Samaritans (Acts 8), the eunuch excluded by Dt 23:1 and promised a place by Is 56, the Gentiles of Cornelius' house (Acts 10), and the Council's ruling that Gentiles need not be circumcised (Acts 15). Paul's restoration theology gathers the scattered ten tribes among the nations (Rom 9-11; Jer 31; Ez 36-37).

Standard Session Architecture

Each session runs as one day on the trail. Same six blocks every time — the repetition is the pedagogy.

Slide Block Time What happens
1 Trailhead 5 min Recap the map; locate today's reading on the three-leg chart
2 Story Script 15 min Narrative retelling of the passage as adventure — written to be told aloud
3 Deeper Look 30 min Exegesis: Greek terms, OT connections, Luke–Acts parallels
4 Greek / Hebrew terms — The session's word list
5 Guideposts 10 min CCC, Church Fathers, Vatican II and magisterial documents
6 Campfire 10 min Application questions + a written "Dear Lord…" prayer
— Provisions — Reading assignment for the next session

Roughly 70 minutes of content in a 75–80 minute class.

Why this shape

  • Story before exegesis — students meet the text as narrative before analysis.
  • Guideposts anchor the class in the Church's reading, not private interpretation.
  • Campfire converts study into prayer, which is where the material is meant to land.
