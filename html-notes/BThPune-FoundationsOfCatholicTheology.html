New Testament Background and World

Part of <a href="#000-bth-pune-foundations-of-catholic-theolog">000 - BTh Pune - Foundations of Catholic Theology (MOC)</a>

I. General introduction to the NT

  • Name "New Testament": from Irenaeus (late 2nd c.), used by Origen to distinguish Christian from Jewish scriptures; based on 2 Cor 3:6. "New" = fulfilment of God's promises to Israel, not abolition of the "Old."
  • 27 books: Gospels (Mt, Mk, Lk, Jn), Acts, Pauline Epistles, Catholic Epistles, Revelation. Unity: one God, one Jewish-Christian tradition, one story (creation → new creation).
  • Four dimensions of NT theology: historical (life, death, resurrection of Jesus), literary (genres), theological (faith seeking to understand God's saving acts), canonical (normative collection).

II. Geography of Palestine

  • Fertile Crescent: well-watered arc from the Persian Gulf up the Tigris–Euphrates, across Syria/Palestine, down to the Nile Delta. Palestine = land-bridge between Asia and Africa → trade route + battleground.
  • Five longitudinal zones (W→E):
  • Coastal Plain — flat, fertile, few harbours (Israelites poor seafarers).
  • Central Mountain Range — Galilee (N), Samaria (centre), Judah/Judea (S); most biblical history here.
  • Rift/Jordan Valley — deepest depression on earth; Sea of Galilee, Jordan River, Dead Sea (1,300 ft below sea level, 26–33% salt, no outlet).
  • Trans-Jordanian Plateau — Decapolis, Perea, Ammon, Moab, Edom.
  • Desert — Syro-Arabian desert; Negeb (around Beersheba).

III. Political world of the NT

  • Greek era (332–63 BCE): Alexander → Hellenization; Ptolemies (Egypt) vs Seleucids (Syria); Antiochus IV Epiphanes desecrates the Temple → Maccabean Revolt → Hasmonean Dynasty (142–63 BCE).
  • Roman era (63 BCE→): Pompey takes Jerusalem 63 BCE; Augustus and the Pax Romana; Pontius Pilate (26–36 CE), procurator who condemned Jesus.
  • Herodian dynasty: Herod the Great (37–4 BCE, Idumean, rebuilt the Temple, massacre of the innocents); sons — Archelaus (Judea, deposed), Herod Antipas (Galilee/Perea, executed the Baptist, mocked Jesus), Philip (built Caesarea Philippi); Herod Agrippa I (killed James, imprisoned Peter, Acts 12).
  • Sanhedrin: supreme council of chief priests, scribes, elders under the High Priest.

IV. Jewish world and its languages

  • Cosmopolitan, multilingual (trilingual titulus on the Cross: Hebrew, Latin, Greek).
  • Aramaic — mother tongue of most Jews incl. Jesus (post-exile); OT paraphrased as Targums.
  • Greek — lingua franca; "Galilee of the Gentiles," marketplace, non-Jews.
  • Hebrew — near-dead for ordinary people; sacred language of Scripture, rabbis, synagogue.
  • Latin — Roman officials, soldiers, law; few loanwords (centurion, denarius).
  • Sects/groups: Pharisees, Sadducees, Essenes, Zealots, Scribes, Herodians, Samaritans.

V. Philosophical schools (Greco-Roman)

  • Platonism — dualist; material world a shadow; free the soul from the body.
  • Cynics — behaviour over theory; frugality, nature, virtue; pioneered the diatribe.
  • Epicureans — sensation as truth; materialist atoms; gods indifferent; death final.
  • Stoicism — universe as one organism energized by divine reason/Logos; live by nature; Paul at Athens (Acts 17:18).
  • Sophism — rhetoric for a fee; relativism; "man is the measure of all things" (Protagoras).
  • Gnosticism — 2nd c.; matter evil, soul an alien spark; secret gnosis; denies the Incarnation and Jesus' physical death.

Indian Christian Theology and Inculturation

Part of <a href="#000-bth-pune-foundations-of-catholic-theolog">000 - BTh Pune - Foundations of Catholic Theology (MOC)</a>

1. Pioneers and reformers

  • Thomas Stephens (1549–1619) — Jesuit, "Father of Inculturation in India"; wrote the Khristapurana, a biblical epic in Marathi/Konkani in the Vaishnavite literary tradition. Substituted Indian terms: puja (sacrifice), jnana-snana "bath of wisdom" (baptism), adipurushache karma (original sin), Vaikunthnatha (Jesus). Avoided Veda/avatara.
  • Robert de Nobili (1577–1656) — Italian Jesuit, Madurai 1606; lived as a Hindu sannyasi (ochre robe, vegetarian, Sanskrit + Tamil). Accommodation method: kept Indian social customs (kudumi tuft, sandal paste, sacred thread) as cultural not idolatrous. Called the Gospel Satya Veda (True Veda).

2. 19th-century inculturation and Hindu reformers

  • Raja Ram Mohan Roy (1774–1833) — founder of Brahmo Samaj; drawn to the Sermon on the Mount but rejected Christ's deity and the Trinity.
  • K.C. Sen (1838–1884) — the "Asiatic Christ" (Jesus fully oriental, belongs to India); Divine Humanity; presented the Trinity through Saccidananda.

3. Abhishiktananda, Bede Griffiths, and "Christian Advaita"

  • Swami Abhishiktananda (Henri Le Saux) — French Benedictine, co-founded Saccidananda Ashram; synthesis in the "cave of the heart" (hrdguhayam); the Trinitarian mystery as the culmination of the Advaitic experience.
  • Bede Griffiths — English Benedictine, led Saccidananda Ashram; Christian doctrine stands between Sankara's monism (jnana) and Ramanuja's dualistic devotion (bhakti).
  • Christian Advaita: non-dual but distinct — a communion of love (koinonia) where distinctiveness remains; identity of communion, not absolute identity of being. Against the total absorption implied by Aham Brahmasmi.

4. Harmony of biblical experience with Vedic concepts

  • Trinity as Saccidananda (Sat-Cit-Ananda): Father = Sat (Being/Source), Son/Logos = Cit (Wisdom/Consciousness), Spirit = Ananda (Bliss).
  • Johannine "I AM" (Ego Eimi) read with Upanishadic Aham Brahmasmi — Jesus's self-awakening and unity with the Father.
  • Logos / Word harmonized with Om / Adisabda (primordial sound of true existence).

5. The Jordan, the Tiber and the Ganges (Raimon Panikkar)

Three rivers = three phases of Christian self-consciousness: - Jordan → Exclusivism: Jewish origins, water baptism, historical faith; salvation within boundaries. - Tiber → Inclusivism: European Christendom, institution, superior "fulfilment" of other religions. - Ganges → Pluralism: post-colonial, dialogical; religiousness as a universal human dimension; confident pluralism without conquest.

6. Hindu avatara vs Christian Incarnation

  • Worldview: avatara = cyclic, repeated descents across yugas to restore dharma; Incarnation = once-for-all, linear, eschatological.
  • Nature: avataras take bodies of "pure matter" (suddha-sattva), free of karma and real suffering; Christ takes a truly limited human nature (pain, hunger, death).
  • Crucified Avatar / Prema-Avatar: Indian Christian theology adopts and modifies avatara — Jesus' uniqueness in kenosis and redemptive suffering love on the Cross, vs the triumphant wicked-destroying avataras.

7. Theological perspectives on inculturation

  • More than adaptation: transforming a culture from within by the Gospel; rooted in the Incarnation — the Word takes flesh in local cultures without colonial imposition.
  • Marga integration: Christology mapped onto the paths to moksha — jnana (knowledge), bhakti (devotion), karma (action) — culminating in Jesus's Prema Marga (path of love).
  • Subaltern focus: inculturation in India joins liberation — poverty and caste, a "contrast community" uplifting Dalits and Tribals. See 08 - Contextual and Liberation Theologies.

Christology and Indian Christology

Part of <a href="#000-bth-pune-foundations-of-catholic-theolog">000 - BTh Pune - Foundations of Catholic Theology (MOC)</a>

1. Christ as the fullness of revelation (JP II / Dei Verbum)

  • The most intimate truth about God and salvation shines forth in Christ — mediator and fullness of all revelation.
  • The words/events of Jesus are limited human realities, but their source is the divine Person of the incarnate Word → definitive, complete revelation.
  • Uniqueness: Christ the "lodestar" of humanity; the Church necessary for salvation — not merely one complementary way among religions.
  • Grace outside the Church: sincere seekers can reach eternal life under grace, but are in an "unsatisfactory situation" compared to those with the fullness of the means of salvation.

2. Significance of Jesus in the Indian pluralistic context

  • India = deep religious pluralism; Indian Christology asks where Jesus is on the Indian road, not only who he is dogmatically.
  • Significance discovered through interreligious relationships and experience.
  • The marginalized Jesus: identifies with the suffering of Dalits, Tribals, women → empowerment for liberation.
  • Uniqueness claims = symbolic statements of faith, never framed in opposition to other religions.

3. "One Jesus and Many Expressions"

NT has no single exhaustive title — manifold expressions per community need: - Mark — the Suffering Messiah (Son of Man who must suffer). - Matthew — the New Moses (fulfilment, authoritative teacher of the new law). - Luke — the Prophet (universal mission, option for the poor). - John — the Revealer of the Father (intimate Father–Son love, believers share divine life).

4. Indian Christology — the four margas

  • Jnana Marga → Advaita Christology: ontological unity with the Divine; Jesus as Wisdom, the Awakened One, Jivanmukta, Yogi, the Cit of Saccidananda; goal = koinonia, not absorption.
  • Bhakti Marga → Avatar Christology: loving surrender to a personal God; Jesus as Grace, personal Saviour, Guru-Mauli, Antaryamin (indwelling Lord), the Crucified Avatar (historical, permanent, kenotic — unlike effortless Hindu avatars).
  • Karma Marga → Abba Christology: prophetic action, justice; Jesus as the Kingdom, the Prophet, Suffering Servant (Vedanamurti Sevak), the Dalit, the Supreme Satyagrahi.
  • Prema Marga → integrating path: love (agape) as the confluence of intellect, devotion, action; Jesus as Incarnate Love (Prema Avatar), The Way (Marga) itself.

5. Soares-Prabhu's portraits of Jesus

  • Jesus and the Poor: responds to poverty out of the religious experience of God as liberative power; identifies with the poor (wandering, begging, outcasts); effective concern via the Love Commandment; the Kingdom as a contrast community with no rich or poor.
  • Jesus the Listener: the "sacred art of listening" — attentive to Abba in prayer and to humanity; compassionate listening (Zacchaeus, the blind man, the Samaritan woman) affirms dignity, breaks barriers.
  • Spirituality of Solidarity: rooted in the Abba experience → all are siblings; solidarity with the powerless + confrontation with the powerful → the Cross as the natural outcome.
  • Table Fellowship: dining with tax collectors/sinners = revolutionary; new holiness of mercy not ritual purity; "acted parable" of the Kingdom; direct challenge to caste — caste discrimination incompatible with the Eucharist. See 04 - The Sacraments, George Soares-Prabhu.

The Sacraments

Part of <a href="#000-bth-pune-foundations-of-catholic-theolog">000 - BTh Pune - Foundations of Catholic Theology (MOC)</a>

1. Reconciliation (Penance) — Sacrament of Healing

  • Bible: OT shub (return to God) — David (2 Sam 12:13), Ps 51; NT — healing the paralytic (Mk 2:1–12); institution "Receive the Holy Spirit… whose sins you forgive…" (Jn 20:22–23; Mt 16:19; 18:18).
  • Theology: Christ the Ursakrament of the Father's mercy; priest in persona Christi (Judge, Healer, Father, Teacher). Three models: therapeutic, juridical, relational.
  • Matter = acts of the penitent: contrition, confession, satisfaction. Form = absolution ("…I absolve you from your sins…").
  • Effects: forgiveness of sins, reconciliation with God and Church, remission of punishment, peace of conscience, spiritual strength.
  • Indian: sin ~ Hindu shadripus (six enemies) / Buddhist tanha; stress on communitarian/structural sin (caste, exploitation, bonded labour); reconciliation builds Kingdom communities across caste/class.

2. Anointing of the Sick — Sacrament of Healing

  • Bible: Twelve anoint the sick with oil (Mk 6:7–13); James 5:14–16.
  • Theology: Church as healing community, Christ the Divine Physician; part of sacramentum exeuntium with Penance + Viaticum.
  • Matter = Oil of the Sick (Oleum Infirmorum) on forehead and hands. Form = "Through this holy anointing…"
  • Effects: strength, peace, courage; forgiveness of sins (if unable to confess); union with Christ's Passion; sometimes physical healing.
  • Indian: sickness as disruption of cosmic order (Rta) / karma; integral (body + spirit) healing; communal celebration against fatalism.

3. Baptism — Sacrament of Initiation ("gateway", "identity card")

  • Bible: Jesus' baptism (Mt 3:13–17); Great Commission (Mt 28:18–20); born of water and Spirit (Jn 3:1–8); dying/rising with Christ (Rom 6:1–11).
  • Theology: incorporation into the Paschal Mystery; regeneration; indelible character; equality of all (Gal 3:28); Body of Christ.
  • Matter = water (immersion/pouring ×3). Form = "I baptize you in the Name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit."
  • Effects: cleansing of original + personal sin, adoptive sonship, indelible mark, incorporation into the Church.
  • Indian: Jnana Snana (bath of enlightenment) / Snana Samskar; parallels Ganges purification; challenges caste — all equal in Christ.

4. Confirmation — Sacrament of Intensification

  • Bible: promise of the Paraclete (Jn 14–16); Pentecost (Acts 2:1–4); laying on of hands (Acts 8:14–17; 19:1–7).
  • Theology: perfects baptismal grace; seven gifts of the Spirit; mature witness, "soldier of Christ"; indelible character.
  • Matter = anointing with Sacred Chrism on the forehead with laying on of the hand. Form = "Be sealed with the Gift of the Holy Spirit."
  • Effects: outpouring of the Spirit, increase of the seven gifts, strength to proclaim the faith prophetically.
  • Indian: for subaltern/tribal communities, the Spirit = liberating power against subjugation; prophetic witness.

5. Eucharist — "source and summit"

  • Bible: institution (Lk 22:7–20; Mt 26; Mk 14; 1 Cor 11:23–26); Bread of Life (Jn 6); Emmaus (Lk 24:13–35).
  • Theology: sacrifice, memorial (anamnesis), communitarian meal; Transubstantiation; the Spirit effects the change through the Epiclesis.
  • Matter = wheat bread + grape wine. Form = words of Institution.
  • Effects: communion with Christ and Church, nourishment, cleansing of venial sins, mission to serve the poor.
  • Indian Christian understanding — Khrist-Preet-Samskar (Sacrament of Love), integrating the three margas:
  • Jnana Marga (Khrist-Pragatya-Samskar) — Real Presence + Word; "Christian Advaita" = loving communion, not absorption.
  • Bhakti Marga (Khrist-Prasad-Samskar) — Eucharist as Prasad; communion with the Antaryami (indwelling Lord).
  • Karma Marga (Khrist-Yagna-Samskar) — Christ's yagna → Nishkama Karma (selfless service), Lokasamgraha (welfare of all); share bread, fight injustice.
  • Socio-cultural: Jesus' table fellowship shattered purity laws → prophetic critique of caste; one table for Dalits and all. See 03 - Christology and Indian Christology.

Priesthood

Part of <a href="#000-bth-pune-foundations-of-catholic-theolog">000 - BTh Pune - Foundations of Catholic Theology (MOC)</a>

1. Biblical foundation

  • OT: evolution from "natural priesthood" (patriarchs, kings — Noah, Abraham, David) to "professional" Levitical/Aaronic priesthood (cult, sacrifice, Law). Whole people a "kingdom of priests" (Ex 19:6).
  • Melchizedek (Gen 14; Ps 110): priest-king, no genealogy → prefigures Christ's eternal, non-Levitical priesthood.
  • Jesus: not of Levi; never called himself/disciples "priest"; ministry prophetic + Kingdom-centred; replaced sterile cultic priesthood with love, compassion, kenotic service.
  • Hebrews: Jesus the Eternal High Priest "in the order of Melchizedek" — offers himself once for all in the heavenly sanctuary; superior New Covenant.

2. Priesthood in Church history

  • Early Church: avoided cultic terms (hiereus/sacerdos); used episkopoi, presbyteroi, diakonoi; leadership pastoral, not primarily cultic.
  • 3rd c.–Middle Ages: after the Temple's fall + Christianized empire, "sacred–secular divide"; ministers called sacerdos on the Levitical model; heavy hierarchicalism (Pseudo-Dionysius) — active clerics vs passive laity; priest as "sacred person" defined by power to consecrate.
  • Reformation: Luther/Calvin — "priesthood of all believers," ministry of the Word.
  • Trent (1545–63): defensive — Holy Orders as ascending ladder of power, indelible character, focus on Eucharistic sacrifice.
  • Vatican II: from pyramid to People of God; reclaims the common priesthood of the faithful — common and ministerial differ "in essence and not only in degree," mutually ordered.

3. Theology of ordained ministry

  • Holy Orders: minister = the Bishop alone; matter = laying on of hands; form = consecratory prayer.
  • Indelible character (sphragis): permanent seal configuring to Christ; not lost through sin or laicization.
  • In persona Christi capitis: sacramental representation through which the Risen Christ leads, teaches, sanctifies.
  • Threefold function: teach (Prophet/Word), sanctify (Priest/Sacrament), shepherd (King/Pastor).

4. Spirituality of priesthood

  • Kenotic + Shepherd spirituality (Paschal Mystery; the Good Shepherd).
  • Servant of the Word — listen/contemplate, be God's "microphone," denounce injustice.
  • Servant of the Sacraments — Eucharist source and summit; become "the body given and blood shed."
  • Servant of the World — immersed in the human condition, with "the last, the lost, the least."

5. Special questions

  • Celibacy: charism and gift; eschatological sign + pastoral availability; needs affective maturity and prayer.
  • Married priests: Eastern Catholic Churches ordain married men; Latin Church rare exceptions (Pastoral Provision).
  • Ordination of women: Ordinatio Sacerdotalis (JP II) — Church has no authority to ordain women (Christ chose only men as the Twelve; constant tradition; in persona Christi).

6. Pastores Dabo Vobis (JP II, 1992) — formation

Title from Jer 3:15 ("I will give you shepherds"). Four pillars (SHIP): 1. Spiritual — the axis; union with God, lectio divina, Eucharist, Penance, pastoral charity. 2. Human — the foundation; "bridge not an obstacle"; psychological balance, affective maturity, friendship. 3. Intellectual — philosophy + theology (fides quaerens intellectum) for the new evangelization. 4. Pastoral — the goal; the "compassion" of Christ; practical shepherding of souls. - Stages: Propaedeutic → Discipleship (philosophy) → Configuration (theology) → Pastoral. - Ongoing formation: permanent, all ages — against burnout, routine activism.

Holy Spirit (Pneumatology)

Part of <a href="#000-bth-pune-foundations-of-catholic-theolog">000 - BTh Pune - Foundations of Catholic Theology (MOC)</a>

1. Who the Holy Spirit is

  • A distinct divine Person (intellect, emotion, will) — not an impersonal force; God's indwelling presence.
  • The "GIFT" par excellence (Acts 2:38), given gratuitously.
  • The bond of Trinitarian love between Father and Son.

2. In the Old Testament

  • Ruach = breath/wind/spirit — YHWH's active force in creation, revelation, redemption.
  • Extension of God's presence (not yet a distinct person): order from chaos, life, empowering prophets and kings.
  • Foreshadows the Spirit-filled Messiah.

3. In the New Testament

  • The age of the Spirit: Pentecost outpouring (fulfils Joel), transcends cultures; eyewitnesses → courageous ministers.
  • Indwelling: believers as "temples of the Holy Spirit" (1 Cor 6:19–20); union with the Risen Christ.
  • Charisms (charismata): prophecy, healing, tongues — for building up the Church, not status.
  • Wellspring of unity: breaks barriers, one language of love.

4. In the Gospel of John (the Paraclete)

  • Descends like a dove at the baptism — sign that Jesus is the Son of God.
  • Born of water and the Spirit (Jn 3:5–8) — regeneration.
  • Paraclete (Helper/Advocate, Jn 14–16): teaches and guides into all truth; glorifies Christ; convicts the world of sin, righteousness, judgment.
  • Johannine Pentecost: "handed over the Spirit" (Jn 19:30); breathes on the disciples for forgiveness (Jn 20:22–23).

5. Indian contextual pneumatology

  • Saccidananda: Father = Sat, Son = Cit, Spirit = Ananda (Bliss).
  • Spirit as OM (Abhishiktananda): the tremor at the threshold of sound whispering "Abba" in the heart.
  • Hindu resonances (FABC 81): Atman (inner Self), Prana (breath, ~ ruach/pneuma), Antaryamin (inner controller), Sakti (divine energy).
  • Asian pneumatology "from below": the Spirit in pluralism, popular religiosity, liberation/ecological/women's movements — the "Power of the Powerless."

6. Titles, symbols, terms

  • Titles: Paraclete/Helper/Comforter/Advocate; Spirit of Truth; the Gift.
  • Symbols: wind/breath, fire, dove, water, oil/anointing, seal, hand & finger.
  • Terms: Epiclesis (invocation transforming the Eucharistic gifts); charismata (gifts for the Church).

Ecclesiology and Ecclesial Documents

Part of <a href="#000-bth-pune-foundations-of-catholic-theolog">000 - BTh Pune - Foundations of Catholic Theology (MOC)</a>

1. Nature of the Church

  • Ekklesia = Hebrew Qahal Yahweh — the gathered community; free, united, self-governing.
  • Mystery: founded in the Trinity — from the Father, inaugurated by Christ, animated by the Spirit.
  • Sacrament (Vatican II): "efficacious sign of grace" — sign and instrument of communion with God and unity of humankind.

2. Models of the Church (Avery Dulles)

  • Institution — visible structures, hierarchy, societas perfecta; critiqued as over-clerical/juridical.
  • Community / Mystical Communion — fellowship, equality, Mystical Body, Spirit-interconnected.
  • Herald — kerygmatic; gathered by the Word, proclaims the Gospel.
  • Servant — suffering-servant body; renounces power, serves the world, keeps Kingdom hope alive.

3. People of God & Communion

  • People of God (Lumen Gentium): replaces static societas perfecta with a pilgrim People; equal dignity of laity, religious, clergy; universal call to holiness.
  • Communion (communio): the central idea of Vatican II (1985 Synod); the Universal Church as a communion of local churches; levels — hierarchical, among the baptized, ecumenical, inter-religious.

4. Vatican II — 16 documents (1962–65)

Four Constitutions: 1. Lumen Gentium (Church) — the "Magna Carta"; Mystery, Sacrament, People of God, collegiality. 2. Dei Verbum (Divine Revelation) — Scripture, Tradition, Magisterium. 3. Gaudium et Spes (Church in the Modern World) — human dignity, social justice, solidarity. 4. Sacrosanctum Concilium (Sacred Liturgy) — active participation, vernacular.

Major Decrees/Declarations: Unitatis Redintegratio (ecumenism), Ad Gentes (mission), Presbyterorum Ordinis (priests), Christus Dominus (bishops), Apostolicam Actuositatem (laity); Nostra Aetate (non-Christian religions), Dignitatis Humanae (religious freedom), Gravissimum Educationis (education).

5. Papal document collections

  • John Paul II: universal presence of the Spirit, "seeds of the Word." Redemptor Hominis, Dominum et Vivificantem, Redemptoris Missio, Centesimus Annus, Sollicitudo Rei Socialis, Evangelium Vitae, Veritatis Splendor, Fides et Ratio, Ut Unum Sint; Theology of the Body.
  • Benedict XVI: faith and reason, love, hope. Deus Caritas Est, Spe Salvi, Caritas in Veritate, Verbum Domini, Sacramentum Caritatis, Summorum Pontificum; Jesus of Nazareth trilogy.
  • Francis: "a poor Church for the poor," integral ecology, synodality. Evangelii Gaudium, Laudato Si', Fratelli Tutti, Amoris Laetitia, Christus Vivit, Querida Amazonia.

Contextual and Liberation Theologies

Part of <a href="#000-bth-pune-foundations-of-catholic-theolog">000 - BTh Pune - Foundations of Catholic Theology (MOC)</a>

1. Dalit theology & mission to Dalits

  • Concern: caste oppression, purity/pollution ideology, double discrimination (state + Church), landlessness, globalization.
  • Method: "Dalitheothics" — faith critique of the profit market via Jesus' kenotic love; political/economic/socio-religious empowerment; "option for justice"; reclaiming resistance symbols (the Dalit drum).
  • Thinkers: L. Stanislaus, George Soares-Prabhu (table fellowship vs caste), Samuel Rayan.

2. Subaltern theology

  • Concern: the "silences and absences" of history — struggles of peasants, tribals, marginalized.
  • Method: "history from below" — subalterns as active subjects, not passive victims. Critiqued for neglecting caste and gender.
  • Thinkers: Subaltern Studies Collective (Ranajit Guha, Chakraborty, Hardiman, Chatterjee, Spivak); Gramsci.

3. Feminist theology

  • Concern: dismantling patriarchy, androcentrism, misogyny; equality and dignity of women; oppressive readings and silenced voices.
  • Method: "feminist consciousness"; Schüssler Fiorenza's four principles — suspicion, remembrance, proclamation, creative visualization; "Review, Recover, Reconstruct."
  • Thinkers: Cady Stanton, Maguire, Schüssler Fiorenza, Elizabeth Johnson; Asian — Katoppo, Chung Hyun Kyung, Kwok Pui-Lan.

4. Eco-theology

  • Concern: destruction of the oikos by throwaway culture, greed; ecological degradation hits the poor ("ecological refugees").
  • Method: Integral Ecology — "the cry of the earth and the cry of the poor"; charity, environmental justice, mercy, solidarity; "ecological conversion"; world as sacrament, cosmotheandric family, humans as stewards/co-creators.
  • Thinkers: Pope Francis (Laudato Si'), Leonardo Boff, Rachel Carson, Teilhard de Chardin, Vandana Shiva, Sundar Lal Bahuguna.

5. Social Teaching of the Church

  • Concern: integral development, human dignity and rights; social/political/economic/ecological crises rooted in sin.
  • Sources: Scripture, Tradition, Church experience, social sciences.
  • Five principles: Common Good · Universal Destination of Goods · Subsidiarity · Participation · Solidarity.
  • Documents/Popes: Rerum Novarum (Leo XIII), Mater et Magistra (John XXIII), Sollicitudo Rei Socialis / Centesimus Annus (JP II), Laudato Si' / Fratelli Tutti (Francis).

6. Asia and Justice (Asian liberation theology)

  • Concern: critiques faith/society dualism and other-worldliness enabling exploitation; struggle for equality, freedom, bread; justice as constitutive of faith and central to the Kingdom.
  • Method: from relief/welfare to structural change; social analysis; liturgy/Eucharist tied to human-rights struggles.
  • Thinker: Samuel Rayan (praxis-oriented, grassroots).

7. Religious pluralism / Christians amidst other communities

  • Concern: tension between salvation through Christ alone and God's universal salvific will; countering fundamentalism, exclusivism, communal violence.
  • Method: paradigm shift Ecclesiocentrism → Christocentrism → Theocentrism; Fourfold Dialogue (action, religious experience, theological exchange, social); "seeds of the Word."
  • Thinkers: Felix Wilfred, Stanley Samartha (theocentric Christology), Raimon Panikkar (cosmotheandric; Jordan–Tiber–Ganges → 02 - Indian Christian Theology and Inculturation).

Christian Spirituality and Mary

Part of <a href="#000-bth-pune-foundations-of-catholic-theolog">000 - BTh Pune - Foundations of Catholic Theology (MOC)</a>

I. Nature of Christian spirituality

  • Spirituality = conscious life-integration through self-transcendence toward ultimate value.
  • Christian: the ultimate value is the triune God in Christ, animated by the Spirit.
  • Marks: life of grace and faith · Christocentric & Trinitarian · communitarian & just · divinizing (theosis).

II. Schools and traditions

  • Martyrdom (Apostolic Fathers) — martyrdom as the ultimate imitation of Christ ("pure bread of Christ").
  • Desert (Abbas/Ammas) — askesis, spiritual combat, the cell, apatheia (purity of heart).
  • Cistercian (Bernard) — simplicity; love from fear to the "kiss of the mouth."
  • Franciscan — kenosis, poverty, joy and thanksgiving.
  • Carmelite (Teresa of Avila) — the "Interior Castle"; degrees of prayer.
  • Salesian (Francis de Sales) — heart-centred, relational; universal call to holiness, gentleness, humility.

III. Spiritual theology (discipline)

  • Definition: from revelation + religious experience, defines the supernatural life and directs growth to perfection.
  • Method: deductive + inductive (avoids empty psychologizing / detached intellectualism).
  • Three ways: Purgative (beginners — purification), Illuminative (proficient — fervour, discernment), Unitive (perfect — charity, God's presence).
  • Rahner's critique: stages too formal → growth as increasing "existential depth" within one's concrete "Situation."

IV. Mary

  • Spirituality of Mary — her own journey: Servant, fiat, hearing and keeping the Word.
  • Marian spirituality — praying like Mary; prototype of receptivity. Balthasar: "spirituality of spiritualities."
  • Stages of Marian formation: Initiation (Annunciation — consent), Formation (Nazareth — silence, humility), Transformation (Crucifixion — compassion, surrender), Mission (Pentecost — missionary discipleship).
  • Psycho-spiritual: Mary as archetype of the sacred feminine / Great Mother; virtues as maturity — humility→ego-transcendence, obedience→will-integration, hope→resilience, charity→emotional maturity.

V. Fourth Gospel in the light of Indian culture

  • Word: as Sat/Cit (Chandogya Upanishad parallel); "Word became flesh" ~ Avatara (Crucified/Kenotic); pre-incarnate Word ~ Om/Adisabda.
  • Three margas united in Prema Marga: Jnana ("I and the Father are one" — Guru, Cit), Karma (foot-washing, Nishkama Karma, Satyagrahi), Bhakti ("abide in me" — Antaryamin).
  • Indian realities: Guru-Shishya discipleship; signs meeting poverty/sickness; "I AM" backed by action ~ Satyagraha; union sayings ~ Advaita, with Johannine dualisms (light/darkness).

Church History and the MSFS

Part of <a href="#000-bth-pune-foundations-of-catholic-theolog">000 - BTh Pune - Foundations of Catholic Theology (MOC)</a>

I. Indian Church history

St. Thomas Christians

  • 52–72 CE: St. Thomas lands at Kodungaloor (52), seven-and-a-half churches, martyred at Mylapore (72).
  • 345 CE: Thomas of Cana + 400 Syrian Christians → East Syrian (Chaldean) link.
  • Coonan Cross Oath (3 Jan 1653): protest against Latinization; split into Pazhakuttukar (Syro-Malabar Catholics) and Puthenkuttukar (Malankara/Jacobite).

Portuguese / Padroado

  • 1498 Vasco da Gama; 1510 Albuquerque takes Goa.
  • Padroado = royal patronage (Portuguese kings nominate to Church offices) → "Goan Schism" after Rome's Propaganda Fide (1622).
  • Paravas: mass baptism (~20,000, 1535); St. Francis Xavier arrives 1542.

Synod of Diamper (1599)

  • 20–26 June 1599 under Archbishop Menezes; St. Thomas Christians forced to cut ties with Babylon, condemn Nestorianism, accept papal supremacy, surrender Syriac books. Francis Ros SJ → Latin bishop of Angamali.

Modern period

  • 19th–20th c.: Indian Christian theology (Brahmabandhav Upadhyay "Hindu-Christian"; A.J. Appasamy, Vengal Chakkarai — bhakti).
  • Recent: Dalit, Tribal, Feminist theologies. See 08 - Contextual and Liberation Theologies.

II. Medieval highlights

  • 476 fall of Western Rome → bishops take civic roles.
  • 800 Leo III crowns Charlemagne → "Christendom."
  • 880–1046 Iron Age of the Papacy (corruption, feudalism).
  • 1073–85 Gregorian Reform — Dictatus Papae (1075), against lay investiture, simony, concubinage.
  • 1378–1417 Western Schism → resolved by the Council of Constance (Martin V).
  • 12th–13th c. Mendicants — Franciscans, Dominicans (poverty, preaching, cities).

III. Fr. Mermier and the MSFS

Before the Indian foundation

  • Pierre-Marie Mermier b. 28 Aug 1790; ordained 1813.
  • 1821 parish missions with Abbé Favre (vs indifference, Jansenism) in Savoie.
  • 1834 community at La Roche-sur-Foron; 1837 Annecy house "La Feuillette"; 24 Oct 1838 Bishop Rey establishes the Missionaries of St. Francis de Sales (MSFS).
  • 1838 co-founds Daughters of the Cross (with Claudine Echernier).
  • 1843 Decretum Laudis from the Holy See.

After the Indian foundation

  • Steered the mission from France: 26 priests + 5 brothers to India, 1845–1862; personal correspondence, united "Salesian family."
  • Stroke June 1858; led until death 1862.

IV. MSFS pioneers to India

  • 1845: Propaganda Fide assigns the Vicariate of Visakhapatnam to the MSFS.
  • Embark May/June 1845 on Courrier de l'Inde; Pondicherry 8 Sept 1845; Yanam/Visakhapatnam Feb 1846.
  • Six pioneers: Fr. Jacques Martin (architect, 1st Rector, d. 1846), Fr. Jean-Marie Tissot (45 yrs; 1st Bishop of Visakhapatnam 1886), Fr. Jean Thevenet (youngest; churches in Aurangabad, Kamptee), Fr. Joseph Lavorel, Bro. Pierre Carton, Bro. Sulpice Fontanel.
  • Fr. Sébastien Neyret (arr. 1847) — Vicar Apostolic 1849, first bishop from the congregation.

Old Testament Foundations — Prophets, Wisdom, Anthropology

Part of <a href="#000-bth-pune-foundations-of-catholic-theolog">000 - BTh Pune - Foundations of Catholic Theology (MOC)</a>

1. The Prophets (Nevi'im)

Nature of prophecy

  • Navi' = called by God to communicate a divine perspective on contemporary life/politics — not a fortune-teller.
  • Rose with the monarchy as checks-and-balances against kings vs the Sinai covenant.
  • Messengers of the "Divine Council": "Thus says YHWH." True vs false tested by fulfilment.

Books

  • Former Prophets: Joshua, Judges, 1–2 Samuel, 1–2 Kings (Deuteronomistic History).
  • Major: Isaiah, Jeremiah, Ezekiel (Daniel = Major in the Christian canon; Writings/apocalyptic in the Hebrew).
  • Minor (Book of the Twelve): Hosea, Joel, Amos, Obadiah, Jonah, Micah, Nahum, Habakkuk, Zephaniah, Haggai, Zechariah, Malachi.

Message

  • God controls history; prosperity/disaster reflects covenant faithfulness.
  • Fierce critique of complacent institutional religion ignoring social justice (Amos: selling the righteous for silver).
  • Cycle: sin → punishment → repentance → deliverance; hope of a New Covenant written on the heart (Jeremiah, Ezekiel).

2. Wisdom Literature

  • Nature: empirical — human observation/experience discerning the order God embedded in creation (vs direct revelation).
  • Books: Proverbs, Job, Ecclesiastes, Song of Songs, Wisdom Psalms; apocryphal — Wisdom of Solomon, Sirach.
  • Themes:
  • "The fear of YHWH is the beginning of wisdom."
  • Retribution theology (Proverbs): right behaviour → blessing.
  • Theodicy / innocent suffering (Job): assaults strict retribution; God's wisdom beyond comprehension.
  • Search for meaning (Ecclesiastes): hevel (vanity); enjoy simple gifts, fear the Lord.
  • Lady Wisdom (Sophia/Hokmah): personified, pre-existent, God's craftsman — vs Mistress Folly.

3. Theological anthropology

  • Imago Dei (Gen 1): humans as God's representatives, stewards of creation; capacity for communion.
  • Body-person unity: nepes ("living being," not a trapped soul), basar (flesh = whole person); no strict Greek dualism; creation is good.
  • The Fall: sin as ontological rupture — alienation from God, others, nature; essence = grasping autonomy to define good/evil.

Key terms

Navi' · Theodicy · Hokmah/Sophia · Vaticinia ex eventu (prophecy after the event) · Deuteronomistic History · nepes · basar · hevel.
