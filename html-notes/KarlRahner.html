Karl Rahner and the Ontological Reality of the Symbol — Course Home

Tract: Fundamental / dogmatic theology through one thinker — Karl Rahner SJ (1904–1984), the most influential Catholic theologian of the twentieth century, read through the key that unlocks his whole system: the ontology of the symbol.

The thesis in one line

"Being is of itself symbolic, because it necessarily 'expresses' itself." — The Theology of the Symbol, TI IV, 224. Everything that exists comes to itself only by going out of itself into another. God does this eternally (Trinity), historically (Incarnation), sacramentally (Church), and personally (body, heart, word).

Why this matters for a BTh student

Rahner is not one topic among many. He supplies the grammar used by Lumen Gentium, Dei Verbum, Gaudium et Spes and Nostra Aetate, by most post-conciliar sacramental theology, and by the standard formula "the Church is the sacrament of Christ." Learn Rahner and half of contemporary Catholic dogmatics becomes readable.

Golden thread

Self-expression is not a loss of self but the way to self. Read that sentence into God (the Father utters the Word), into Christ (the Logos utters himself as a human being), into the Church (Christ utters himself in history), into the sacraments (the Church utters itself in seven decisive acts), and into you (you utter yourself in your body, your words, your heart). One ontology; the whole of dogmatics falls into place behind it.

The examiner's trap

Do not say "for Rahner the sacraments are only symbols." That is precisely the position he wrote the essay to destroy. For Rahner a real symbol is more real, not less — it is the reality's own way of being present.

Citation habit

Cite the symbol essay as: Karl Rahner, "The Theology of the Symbol," in Theological Investigations IV, trans. Kevin Smyth (London: Darton, Longman & Todd, 1966), 221–252. Give the page for every quotation — examiners notice.

Character of his theology — five marks

  1. Pastoral origin. Almost every essay begins from a real question — may we concelebrate? is devotion to the Sacred Heart superstitious? can my Hindu neighbour be saved?
  2. The essay, not the system. He writes Untersuchungen (investigations), provisional and open. Do not expect a closed architecture.
  3. Mystagogical intent. The goal is not information but leading the reader into the experience of grace already present.
  4. Radical continuity with tradition. He almost never rejects a dogma; he re-reads it from the inside so it becomes livable.
  5. Notoriously difficult prose. Long German sentences, coined compounds. This is a genuine criticism, not just a joke → 16 - Critical Reflection I - Philosophical.

Rahner on his own project

His stated aim was to show that Christian dogma, far from being a burden imposed from outside, articulates what the human being already is: a creature whose very existence is a question to which God is the answer.

02 — Philosophical Foundations I: Spirit in the World

Prev: <a href="#01-life-context-and-works">01 - Life, Context and Works</a> · Next: <a href="#03-philosophical-foundations-ii-hearer-of-th">03 - Philosophical Foundations II - Hearer of the Word</a>

The question of the book

Geist in Welt (1939) is a commentary on one article of Aquinas: ST I, q.84, a.7 — "Can the intellect understand anything without turning to phantasms?" Aquinas answers no. Rahner asks what that answer implies about the human being as such.

The paradox in the title: the human is spirit (open to being as such, capable of the infinite) yet always in world (bound to sense, matter, history). Neither half may be dropped.

Step 1 — Conversio ad phantasma (the turn to the image)

  • The human intellect never grasps a pure essence directly. It must turn to the sense image (phantasma).
  • You cannot think "tree" without some imagined tree; you cannot think "justice" without a remembered scene of injustice.
  • Consequence: human knowing is incarnate. There is no angelic short cut. Knowledge of God will therefore always be mediated through the world — never a bare intuition of the divine.

Illustration

A student can define dharma only by summoning instances — the elder who kept his word, the story from the Mahābhārata, the teacher who refused a bribe. The concept lives on the back of the image. Strip away every image and the concept goes silent.

Step 2 — Excessus / the Vorgriff auf esse

If knowing is always tied to the finite image, how do we know that any given thing is finite? To recognise a limit you must already, somehow, be beyond it.

  • Vorgriff auf esse = the pre-apprehension / anticipatory reach toward Being as such that accompanies every act of judgement.
  • It is not a concept, not an object, never directly seen. It is the unthematic horizon within which objects appear.
  • In every judgement ("this is a tree, and only a tree") the mind silently measures the object against the whole of being.

The horizon illustration (use this in exams)

You never look at the horizon; you look at the field, the tree, the road. Yet nothing could appear as a bounded thing without the horizon behind it. The Vorgriff is the horizon of the mind. God is not an object in the field of vision — God is the light and reach that makes the field visible.

Second illustration — the frame

A photograph shows a courtyard. The frame is never in the picture, but without it there is no picture. Rahner: absolute Being is the frame of every finite act of knowing.

Step 3 — From Vorgriff to God

Rahner's move (inherited from Joseph Maréchal SJ, Le point de départ de la métaphysique, cahier V):

  1. Every act of knowing reaches beyond the object toward unlimited being.
  2. This reaching is not an empty projection — a dynamism cannot be intelligibly oriented toward nothing.
  3. Therefore the term of the Vorgriff is absolute, infinite Being = God, affirmed implicitly in every act of knowledge.

Result: the human being is a transcendent creature by structure. Even the atheist affirms God performatively in the very act of denying God, because the denial is a judgement, and judgement runs on the Vorgriff.

This is exactly where critics attack

Does a dynamism toward the infinite prove an actual infinite term? Kant would say no — this is the ontological move smuggled back in. See 16 - Critical Reflection I - Philosophical.

Key term: emanatio and self-presence

Rahner reads Aquinas's account of knowledge as the being's return to itself (reditio completa in seipsum). A stone cannot return to itself; a plant partially; a human completely — self-consciousness. But — and this is decisive for the symbol essay — the return to self happens only by going out into the other (the world, the image, the body). There is no self-possession without self-expression.

The sentence that becomes the symbol ontology

A being comes to itself precisely by emptying itself out into what is other than itself. Twenty years later this becomes: "Being is of itself symbolic, because it necessarily 'expresses' itself" (TI IV, 224). → 07 - The Theology of the Symbol - The Core Essay

03 — Philosophical Foundations II: Hearer of the Word

Prev: <a href="#02-philosophical-foundations-i-spirit-in-the">02 - Philosophical Foundations I - Spirit in the World</a> · Next: <a href="#04-the-transcendental-method-in-theology">04 - The Transcendental Method in Theology</a>

The question of the book

Hörer des Wortes (1941) is Rahner's philosophy of religion. If Geist in Welt asked what is the human being who knows?, this asks: what must the human being be, if God should choose to speak in history — and if that word is to be hearable?

Note the modesty of the claim. Philosophy cannot prove that God has spoken. It can only show that the human being is structurally the one who must listen.

The three theses

  1. The human being is spirit — constituted by the Vorgriff, standing before Being as such, therefore before God as absolute mystery.
  2. The human being is finite and historical — spirit in world. Any divine word must therefore come in history, in matter, in words, in a body — never as a private mystical bypass of the world.
  3. The human being must therefore attend to history as the place where a possible revelation would occur. The human is homo audiens — "the one who listens into history for a possible word of God."

The formula

The human being is "the obediential potency for the Word of God" — potentia oboedientialis pro Verbo Dei.

Potentia oboedientialis (obediential potency)

Scholastic use Rahner's transformation
Meaning A merely passive, negative non-repugnance: the creature does not resist God's action A positive, dynamic openness; the creature is made for this, though it cannot claim it
Danger avoided — Extrinsicism: grace as an arbitrary superstructure bolted on from outside
Danger risked — Making grace owed to nature; see the corrective in 05 - The Supernatural Existential - Nature and Grace

Rahner walks a razor's edge here, and he knows it: - If openness to God is only negative, revelation becomes extrinsic, an interruption with no purchase on human life. Result: a religion nobody can experience as their own. - If openness is positive in the sense of a demand, grace ceases to be free gift. Result: naturalism. - His solution: the openness is real and constitutive, but the fulfilment remains utterly gratuitous — an emptiness that cannot fill itself and has no claim on being filled.

Illustration — the unaddressed letterbox

A letterbox is genuinely, structurally for letters. Yet no letterbox can produce a letter, and no amount of waiting obliges anyone to write. The human being is a letterbox shaped for a word that may never be spoken — and has been spoken.

Illustration from Indian experience

A village builds a mandapa for a guest who has not yet come, because the tradition says a guest may come. The structure is real, ordered, expectant — and the coming remains pure gift. Atithi devo bhava ("the guest is God") describes a readiness, not a claim.

Why revelation must be historical and verbal

Because the human is spirit in world: - A revelation that bypassed body, language, community and history would be unhearable by a being whose knowing runs through the phantasma (02 - Philosophical Foundations I - Spirit in the World). - Hence: incarnation, scripture, sacrament, Church are not concessions to human weakness. They are the only possible form a word of God could take for beings like us.

Exam line

"For Rahner, the Incarnation is not God stooping to a lower medium; it is God taking the only medium in which a being that is spirit-in-world can hear anything at all."

Where this leads

Two roads lead out of this note: - Toward method: how do you actually do theology this way → 04 - The Transcendental Method in Theology - Toward grace: the openness is not merely natural; it has already been graced → 05 - The Supernatural Existential - Nature and Grace

Critical note (preview)

Karl Barth's objection in principle: any "philosophy of the possible hearer" builds a praeambula fidei that lets human reason set the terms on which God may speak. Barth's Nein! to Emil Brunner's natural theology applies here with full force. Rahner's reply: he is not deriving revelation from anthropology, only showing that revelation, if given, is not alien to the hearer. See 16 - Critical Reflection I - Philosophical.

04 — The Transcendental Method in Theology

Prev: <a href="#03-philosophical-foundations-ii-hearer-of-th">03 - Philosophical Foundations II - Hearer of the Word</a> · Next: <a href="#05-the-supernatural-existential-nature-and-g">05 - The Supernatural Existential - Nature and Grace</a>

What "transcendental" does not mean

Not "mystical", not "otherworldly", not "transcendent". Transcendental is a technical Kantian term:

A transcendental enquiry asks not what do we know? but what must be true of the knower for such knowledge to be possible at all?

It is a search for conditions of possibility (Bedingungen der Möglichkeit).

The classroom illustration

Ordinary question: "What is written on the blackboard?" Transcendental question: "What must light, eye and mind be, such that anything can be read at all?" Rahner does the second kind of question about faith: not only "what does the Church teach about grace?" but "what must the human being be, such that a doctrine of grace could mean anything to them?"

The two poles: transcendental and categorical

Transcendental Categorical (kategorial)
What it is The a priori structure of the subject; unthematic, always operative The concrete, historical, objectified, nameable
In revelation God's universal self-offer in every human's depth Sinai, the prophets, Jesus of Nazareth, Scripture, dogma
In grace The supernatural existential Baptism, the Eucharist, an act of charity
In knowing The Vorgriff This tree, this proposition
Risk if isolated Vague religiosity with no content Extrinsicism: revelation as arbitrary information

Rahner's constant claim: the two poles are never separable. There is no transcendental revelation without categorical mediation, and no categorical revelation that is not the coming-to-word of what the transcendental already is.

Exam sentence

"Transcendental revelation is the always-and-everywhere of God's self-offer; categorical revelation is its here-and-now historical objectification. Rahner's whole theology is an argument that neither survives without the other."

The method in four moves

  1. Start from a lived human experience — loving without return, standing by a promise, facing death, forgiving.
  2. Ask the transcendental question — what must be true of the human being for this experience to be possible?
  3. Show that the answer opens onto absolute mystery — the experience only makes sense if the human is oriented to an infinite, self-giving horizon.
  4. Identify this horizon with the God of Christian revelation, and show that the dogma in question is the precise articulation of what step 3 uncovered.

Worked example — the dogma of grace

  1. Experience: a nurse sits with a dying stranger through the night, with nothing to gain.
  2. Transcendental question: what makes such unconditional self-gift possible?
  3. Answer: only a being already carried by an unconditional love that it did not generate.
  4. Dogma: this is what the Church calls gratia, and specifically uncreated grace — God's own self-communication → 06 - God's Self-Communication and Quasi-Formal Causality.

Worked example — the Trinity

  1. Experience: we are addressed by God in Jesus (Word) and inwardly enabled to respond (Spirit).
  2. Transcendental question: what must God be for this double structure of salvation to be a genuine giving of God, not of a created substitute?
  3. Answer: God must be self-utterance and self-giving in Godself.
  4. Dogma: immanent Trinity → 09 - Rahner's Rule - The Grundaxiom of the Trinity.

The "turn to the subject" (anthropologische Wende)

Rahner's programmatic claim: dogmatic theology today must be theological anthropology, and such a turn is not a reduction of God to the human but the recognition that every statement about God is made by and for a human hearer, and therefore already says something about the human.

Rahner's formula

Christology is "self-transcending anthropology," and anthropology is "deficient Christology" — the human being is the question to which Jesus Christ is the unsurpassable answer.

The most contested sentence in Rahner

Balthasar, Ratzinger and Metz all read the "turn to the subject" as the point where theology risks becoming anthropology in a louder voice. Know both the claim and the objection → 16 - Critical Reflection I - Philosophical, 17 - Critical Reflection II - Theological.

05 — The Supernatural Existential: Nature and Grace

Prev: <a href="#04-the-transcendental-method-in-theology">04 - The Transcendental Method in Theology</a> · Next: <a href="#06-god-s-self-communication-and-quasi-formal">06 - God&#x27;s Self-Communication and Quasi-Formal Causality</a>

The problem Rahner inherited

The classic Catholic difficulty, sharpened in the 1940s–50s nouvelle théologie controversy:

  • Baroque scholastic position (natura pura): human nature is a complete, self-sufficient whole with its own natural end (natural beatitude). Grace is added from outside as a wholly gratuitous superstructure.
  • Guarantees: the gratuity of grace. Grace is never owed.
  • Cost: extrinsicism — grace becomes irrelevant to actual human experience. You could live your whole life and never notice it. Faith becomes an external burden.
  • Henri de Lubac (Surnaturel, 1946): there is only one concrete human nature, created with a natural desire for the vision of God (desiderium naturale videndi Deum). No "pure nature" ever existed.
  • Guarantees: grace touches the heart of human longing.
  • Cost: if nature demands the vision of God, is God still free to refuse it? Humani Generis (1950) warned against exactly this.

The dilemma in one line

How can grace be both utterly gratuitous (never owed) and utterly relevant (the fulfilment of what I actually am)?

Rahner's solution: the übernatürliches Existential

Definition. The supernatural existential is a permanent, universal, gratuitously given modification of the concrete human situation, by which every human being — always and everywhere, before any decision, baptism or knowledge — is really oriented to the God who offers Godself in self-communication.

Unpack the two words: - Existential (from Heidegger's Existenzial): not an accidental property one might lack, but a constitutive structure of concrete human existence. Like historicity or embodiment: you cannot step outside it. - Supernatural: it is not owed to nature. It is a free gift of God, given with the actual order of creation because God willed the universal salvific will (1 Tim 2:4).

The move that solves the dilemma: the gift is given universally but remains unowed. It is gratuitous not because it is rare but because it is free. God's freedom is protected not by making grace scarce, but by locating its gratuity in the divine will rather than in its distribution.

The oxygen illustration

You have never once decided to breathe air; the atmosphere was there before you, and every breath you take presupposes it. You are so surrounded by it that you notice it only when you climb a mountain. Yet no lung earns an atmosphere. The supernatural existential is the atmosphere of grace: universal, unnoticed, presupposed by every spiritual act — and given, not owed.

The adoption illustration

A child adopted at birth grows up inside a love it never chose, never earned, and cannot remember beginning. That love is not an extra layer on top of the child's "real" life; it is the medium in which the child's life is actually lived. And it remains pure gift.

"Pure nature" survives — as a Restbegriff

Rahner does not abolish natura pura. He keeps it as a remainder concept (Restbegriff): a theoretical construct obtained by subtracting the supernatural existential in thought, in order to say precisely what grace adds. It names a possibility, never a reality. No human being has ever existed in pure nature.

Exam distinction

De Lubac: one nature, with an intrinsic natural desire for God (risks compromising gratuity). Rahner: one concrete order, in which nature is always already modified by an unowed supernatural existential (gratuity preserved in the divine will). Baroque scholasticism: two orders, extrinsically related (gratuity preserved, relevance lost).

Critical reflection (fuller treatment in notes 16–17)

Objection 1 — Has gratuity really been saved? If the existential is given with creation to everyone, universally and necessarily as this order stands, has it not become de facto a property of nature? Critics (some neo-Thomists; more recently Lawrence Feingold, The Natural Desire to See God) argue Rahner keeps the word "gratuitous" while emptying it. Rahner's reply: gratuity is a matter of the ground of the gift (God's free will), not of its rarity. A gift given to everyone is not thereby earned by anyone.

Objection 2 — Is grace still experienced as grace? If grace is unthematic and universal, what distinguishes it phenomenologically from ordinary human generosity? Postliberal critics (Lindbeck) press this: Rahner posits a universal experience that no one can identify without the Christian vocabulary that supposedly only expresses it.

Objection 3 — Does the sacramental order lose its necessity? If grace is already offered to all, why baptism? Rahner: because grace by its nature seeks historical, bodily, ecclesial embodiment — the same logic as the symbol ontology. What is anonymous seeks to become explicit; what is offered seeks to become event → 07 - The Theology of the Symbol - The Core Essay.

Objection 4 — The abstraction critique (Metz). The "existential" is described in terms of individual interiority. What of structures, oppression, the concrete history of suffering? Rahner accepted this correction late in life ("mystical-political") → 17 - Critical Reflection II - Theological.

06 — God's Self-Communication and Quasi-Formal Causality

Prev: <a href="#05-the-supernatural-existential-nature-and-g">05 - The Supernatural Existential - Nature and Grace</a> · Next: <a href="#07-the-theology-of-the-symbol-the-core-essay">07 - The Theology of the Symbol - The Core Essay</a>

The governing category: Selbstmitteilung Gottes

God's self-communication is Rahner's master-concept for grace, Trinity, Incarnation and glory alike.

The claim

In grace God does not merely give something; God gives Godself. The gift and the Giver are identical.

This is not a novelty but a retrieval. The scholastic tradition had drifted toward treating grace primarily as created grace (gratia creata) — a supernatural quality infused into the soul, a created effect of God's action. Rahner reverses the priority:

Created grace Uncreated grace
What it is A created supernatural quality in the soul God's own self, the indwelling Trinity
Scholastic priority Primary; uncreated grace follows as a consequence Secondary; it is the effect and disposition produced by God's self-gift
Rahner's order Consequence Primary reality of grace

Rahner's thesis: created grace is the created consequence of uncreated grace — the change in the creature that necessarily follows when God gives Godself. Not the other way round.

Scriptural warrant: Jn 14:23 ("we will come to them and make our home with them"); Rom 5:5 ("God's love has been poured into our hearts through the Holy Spirit who has been given to us"); 2 Pet 1:4 ("participants of the divine nature").

The causal problem

If God gives Godself, how does God relate to the creature? Standard scholastic causality offers:

  • Efficient causality — God as the cause producing an effect distinct from himself. But then the gift is a creature, not God. Too little.
  • Formal causality — the form actually constituting the being of the thing (as the soul is the form of the body). But then God would become an intrinsic constituent of the creature. Pantheism. Too much.

The solution: quasi-formal causality

Definition. In grace God communicates Godself to the creature in the manner of a formal cause — as an inner, constitutive, determining principle of the graced person's being — yet without ceasing to be free, transcendent and unchanged, and without being absorbed into the creature as a component part. Hence quasi.

Unpack the "quasi": 1. Real formal determination: the graced person is genuinely, intrinsically transformed; God is not merely acting on them from outside. 2. No ontological composition: God does not become a part of a larger whole. The creature does not become divine in essence. 3. Freedom preserved: unlike a natural form, which informs by necessity, God informs by free personal self-donation, and can be freely refused.

The illustration of the beloved

When someone loves you, they do not merely send gifts (efficient causality); nor do they dissolve into you (formal causality strictly speaking). They give themselves, and you become genuinely a different person — a beloved person — from the inside, while remaining wholly yourself and while they remain wholly other. That is the analogy Rahner is reaching for.

The light illustration

Air becomes luminous when light is present. The light really determines the air from within: the air is now lit air. Yet the light is not a part of the air, does not become air, and can withdraw. Aquinas used lumen; Rahner uses the same instinct with a personalist grammar.

Precision matters here

Do not say "God becomes the form of the soul." Say: "God communicates Godself after the manner of a formal cause, without the composition that formal causality would ordinarily entail." That word manner is the whole doctrine.

The three modalities of one self-communication

Rahner's elegance is that a single act of divine self-gift takes three forms:

  • Grace = the self-communication offered and freely acceptable or refusable.
  • Incarnation = the self-communication given to one human reality so radically and irrevocably that it becomes the self-utterance of God → 10 - Christology - Self-Communication and Evolution.
  • Glory = the same self-communication no longer mediated by faith.

Therefore: grace and Incarnation are not two different divine acts but one act in different modalities. This is why Rahner can say Christology is "self-transcending anthropology."

God as absolute mystery (Geheimnis)

The self-communicating God remains incomprehensible — and Rahner insists incomprehensibility is not a defect to be overcome in heaven. Even the beatific vision is the vision of the incomprehensible God as incomprehensible: the mystery is not solved, it is embraced.

Quote

Mystery for Rahner is not "the not-yet-known" but the permanent, positive horizon of all knowing. God is mystery not because we have too little light but because we stand in too much.

This links straight back to the Vorgriff (02 - Philosophical Foundations I - Spirit in the World) and forward to mystagogy (14 - Mystagogy, Ignatian Roots and Spirituality).

Critical reflection

  • Is "quasi-formal causality" coherent, or a verbal fix? Critics (neo-Thomists; analytically minded readers) argue that quasi does all the work and is never cashed out. Either God formally determines the creature — with the metaphysical consequences — or God does not. Reply: all analogical language about God works this way; "quasi" flags a genuine analogy of proper proportionality, not an evasion. Compare "God is a person" — no less strained.
  • Does the priority of uncreated grace endanger the creature's integrity? If the primary reality of grace is God, is the creature's real change still real? Reply: created grace is retained precisely to secure that. Rahner subordinates it; he does not delete it.
  • Trent's concern: Trent taught that justification involves a real inherent transformation, against a merely imputed righteousness. Rahner's scheme keeps inherent transformation, but as consequence. Whether that satisfies Trent's intent is a live disputed question → 17 - Critical Reflection II - Theological.

  • Why is efficient causality inadequate for grace? Why is strict formal causality dangerous?
  • Explain "quasi" in "quasi-formal causality" with one illustration.

07 — The Theology of the Symbol: Zur Theologie des Symbols

Prev: <a href="#06-god-s-self-communication-and-quasi-formal">06 - God&#x27;s Self-Communication and Quasi-Formal Causality</a> · Next: <a href="#08-realsymbol-applied-trinity-christ-church-">08 - Realsymbol Applied - Trinity, Christ, Church, Sacraments, Body</a>

Reference

Karl Rahner, "The Theology of the Symbol," Theological Investigations IV (London: DLT, 1966), 221–252. German: "Zur Theologie des Symbols," Schriften zur Theologie IV (1960), first published 1959 in a collection on devotion to the Sacred Heart.

1. The occasion — a small question with a huge answer

Rahner was asked to justify devotion to the Sacred Heart of Jesus. The pious objection was blunt: is it not crude, even superstitious, to venerate a bodily organ? Is the Heart "just a symbol"?

Rahner's response is characteristic: the problem is not the devotion but the impoverished notion of symbol presupposed by the objection. Theology, he observes, is saturated with symbol — sacraments, the Church as sign, the Incarnation, sacred images, the Body of Christ — yet has no worked-out ontology of symbol. So he writes one.

The programmatic complaint

Theology cannot go on using the word "symbol" in a dozen incompatible senses while never asking what a symbol is in the order of being.

Method note: this is transcendental method applied to a devotional practice — start from what Christians actually do, ask its conditions of possibility, and end with an ontology → 04 - The Transcendental Method in Theology.

2. The decisive distinction

Realsymbol (real symbol / symbolic reality) Vertretungssymbol (representative sign, arbitrary symbol)
Relation to what it signifies The symbolised constitutes the symbol as its own self-realisation; it is genuinely present in it Extrinsic, conventional, assigned by agreement
Could it be otherwise? No — it is this reality's own way of existing outwardly Yes — any other sign could be substituted
Presence The reality is there, in and through the symbol The reality is absent, merely pointed to
Examples A smile as symbol of joy; a body as symbol of a person; the Word as symbol of the Father Traffic light; national flag; the letters d-o-g; a mathematical sign

The error the whole essay exists to kill

"Only a symbol." In ordinary speech "symbol" means less real than the thing. Rahner reverses it: a real symbol is the maximal presence of a reality outside itself. Where the modern says "merely symbolic," Rahner says "therefore truly present."

Smile and joy

A smile is not a coded message about joy, agreed by convention, which could equally have been a raised eyebrow. Joy realises itself in the smile; the joy is there, in the face. Remove the bodily expression entirely and the joy has not been hidden — it has been prevented from fully being.

Word and speaker

When someone says "I forgive you," the forgiveness is not somewhere else, being reported. The forgiveness happens in the word. That is a real symbol. Contrast: the word "chair" for a chair — pure convention, an entirely arbitrary Vertretungssymbol.

Contrast pair for exams

Handshake (real symbol of a reconciliation actually occurring) vs the referee's yellow card (pure convention; could have been blue). A mother's embrace (real symbol of love realising itself) vs a wedding invitation card (a sign about an event elsewhere).

3. The two theses of the essay

Thesis 1 — the ontological thesis

TI IV, 224

"All beings are by their very nature symbolic, because they necessarily 'express' themselves in order to attain their own nature."

Read this in three steps:

  1. Being is plural in itself. Rahner starts from the scholastic axiom omne ens est unum (every being is one), but argues that unity is not the bare absence of plurality. In every finite being there is an inner plurality (matter and form, essence and existence, act and potency). Even in God there is a plurality — the divine processions — and there it is not a defect but the highest perfection.
  2. This plurality is a self-plurality. A being "others" itself: it produces from itself an other which is nevertheless itself. Rahner calls this the being's expression (Ausdruck), its Selbstvollzug — self-realisation.
  3. The being attains itself in what it expresses. The expression is not an optional overflow after the being is already complete. It is how the being comes to itself. There is no self-possession without self-expression.

Where this comes from

Directly out of Geist in Welt: the human comes to self-presence (reditio in seipsum) only by going out into the world through the phantasma (02 - Philosophical Foundations I - Spirit in the World). Rahner now universalises that structure into a general ontology.

The obvious objection, answered in advance

"Doesn't this make God incomplete before self-expression?" Rahner's answer: in God, the self-utterance is eternal and necessary and internal, and it constitutes God's perfect self-possession. God is not first solitary and then expressive. God is the eternal act of self-utterance. Plurality-in-unity is thus the highest mode of being, of which finite symbolic structure is a distant participation. (Whether this fully escapes the charge of a Hegelian necessity is the great critical question → 17 - Critical Reflection II - Theological.)

Thesis 2 — the definition of symbol

TI IV, 234 (substance)

"The symbol strictly speaking (symbolic reality) is the self-realisation of a being in the other, which is constitutive of its essence."

And the fuller formula near the end of the essay:

TI IV, 251

The symbol is the reality, constituted by the thing symbolised as an inner moment of itself, which reveals and proclaims the thing symbolised, and is itself full of the thing symbolised, being its concrete form of existence.

Break this definition into its four claims — memorise these four:

# Claim Meaning
1 Constituted by the symbolised The symbol is produced by what it symbolises, not chosen by an observer
2 As an inner moment of itself The symbol belongs to the symbolised's own being; it is not external to it
3 Reveals and proclaims It has genuine epistemic function — you really know the reality through it
4 Is full of the symbolised: its concrete form of existence The reality is not elsewhere; the symbol is where the reality is

4. Two corollaries Rahner draws out

(a) Symbol and knowledge. Because a being expresses itself in its symbol, the symbol is the place where it is known. This is not second-best knowledge. To know a person through their face, words and deeds is not a poor substitute for some direct intuition of their soul — there is no such intuition available to spirit-in-world.

(b) Symbol and causality. If a reality constitutes its symbol as its own self-realisation, then the symbol causes by signifying. The scholastic axiom sacramenta significando causant ("the sacraments cause by signifying") ceases to be a puzzle and becomes an instance of a general ontological law → 12 - Ecclesiology and Sacramental Theology.

5. The five applications (summarised here, developed in note 08)

Notice the descending chain: Father → Word → flesh → Church → sacrament → this water, this bread, this word of absolution. One single logic runs the whole length of it. That is the elegance of Rahner and also the point at which critics say the system is too smooth.

6. And so: the Sacred Heart

Rahner returns to the original question. The heart is what he calls an Urwort — a primordial word, one of those words (heart, light, water, bread, word, birth, death) which are not technical labels but original expressions of a total human reality.

  • Because the body is the real symbol of the person (not a container for a soul), the body's centre can genuinely symbolise the person's centre.
  • The heart names the innermost core of the person as loving and self-giving.
  • The pierced heart of Christ (Jn 19:34) is therefore the real symbol of the total self-gift of the incarnate Word — and, note, the very theme of Rahner's 1936 theology dissertation E latere Christi.

Conclusion: devotion to the Sacred Heart is not a sentimental attachment to an organ. It is the adoration of the whole self-giving Christ precisely where he expresses himself.

The exam paragraph

"Rahner's essay begins with a devotional embarrassment and ends with an ontology. Its thesis — that being is symbolic because it must express itself to attain itself — allows him to say that a real symbol is not less real than what it signifies but is that reality's own mode of presence. This single principle then unifies Trinity, Incarnation, Church, sacrament and Christian devotion under one law of being."

7. First critical soundings (developed fully in 16–17)

  1. Is the ontology Christian or Idealist? "Being must express itself to attain itself" reads very like Hegel's Entäußerung (self-externalisation). If self-expression is necessary to being, is creation still free? Rahner distinguishes the necessary inner-Trinitarian utterance from the free utterance ad extra — but critics ask whether the distinction is stable given that he grounds both in one law of being.
  2. Does the analogy stretch too far? The essay moves from smiles to the Trinity in a few pages. Is "symbol" being used univocally across creaturely and divine being? Rahner would invoke analogy; the reader must check that he actually deploys it.
  3. Does symbolic causality preserve sacramental efficacy? If grace is already universally offered (05 - The Supernatural Existential - Nature and Grace), does the sacrament confer or merely manifest? Colman O'Neill OP and other Thomists press hard here → 17 - Critical Reflection II - Theological.
  4. Where is sin? An ontology of self-expression handles fullness well. It has more difficulty with the symbol that lies — the body that conceals, the sacrament received unworthily, the Church that misrepresents Christ. Rahner touches this but does not develop it.

08 — Realsymbol Applied: The Five Applications

Prev: <a href="#07-the-theology-of-the-symbol-the-core-essay">07 - The Theology of the Symbol - The Core Essay</a> · Next: <a href="#09-rahner-s-rule-the-grundaxiom-of-the-trini">09 - Rahner&#x27;s Rule - The Grundaxiom of the Trinity</a>

This note takes the ontology of note 07 and walks it down the whole chain of Christian doctrine. One principle, five applications.

Application 1 — The Trinity: the Logos as Realsymbol of the Father

The claim. The Father, in perfectly knowing and possessing himself, utters himself. That utterance is not a thought about the Father; it is the Father's own reality, wholly received in another mode of subsisting. The Word is the real symbol of the Father.

Scriptural anchoring Rahner leans on: - Jn 1:1 — "the Word was with God and the Word was God" - Col 1:15 — "the image (eikōn) of the invisible God" - Heb 1:3 — "the reflection of God's glory and the exact imprint (charaktēr) of God's very being"

The decisive point

The Father does not first exist in solitary self-possession and then speak. The Father possesses himself precisely in uttering the Word. Self-expression is constitutive of self-possession — Thesis 1 of the symbol essay, now read into the immanent Trinity.

Why this matters: it means the symbol structure is not a creaturely limitation projected onto God. It is the reverse — creaturely symbolism is a distant participation in the Trinitarian structure of being itself. God is the archetypal Realsymbol.

Guard against modalism

Rahner is not saying the Word is an "expression" in the sense of an appearance or mode of the one God. The Word is a distinct hypostasis, consubstantial. But note that Rahner does prefer "distinct manners of subsisting" (distinkte Subsistenzweisen) to "three persons" for modern ears — which earns him a charge of near-modalism from Moltmann and others → 17 - Critical Reflection II - Theological.

Application 2 — Christology: the humanity of Jesus as Realsymbol of the Logos

The claim. The human nature of Jesus is not an instrument the Logos picks up, nor a costume worn, nor a screen behind which God hides. It is what comes to be when the Logos utters himself outwards into the non-divine.

Rahner, TI IV

The humanity of Christ is the self-disclosure of the Logos itself, so that when God, expressing himself, exteriorises himself, that very thing appears which we call the humanity of the Logos.

Two enormous consequences:

  1. Against every hidden-God Christology. We do not see the humanity and infer the divinity. In seeing the man Jesus we see the self-utterance of God — "whoever has seen me has seen the Father" (Jn 14:9) becomes an ontological statement, not a pious hyperbole.
  2. Human nature is defined Christologically. If human nature is what comes to be when God expresses himself, then to be human is precisely to be the possible self-expression of God. Hence Rahner's famous formula: Christology is self-transcending anthropology; anthropology is deficient Christology → 10 - Christology - Self-Communication and Evolution.

Illustration — the singer and the song

A song is not an instrument the singer uses; it is the singer's own self going out into audible form. You do not hear the song and then deduce a singer; the singer is there, in the song. Push the analogy no further than Rahner does — the song does not have a human will and a human soul. The illustration carries the presence, not the hypostatic union.

Limit of the analogy

Any illustration of the hypostatic union risks Monophysitism (the humanity swallowed by divinity) or Nestorianism (two subjects side by side). Rahner's symbolic reading preserves Chalcedon by insisting the humanity is fully real and fully other precisely because it is the Logos's own self-utterance. The more united to God, the more genuinely itself — his axiom of radical dependence and genuine autonomy increasing in direct, not inverse, proportion.

Application 3 — The Church: Realsymbol of the risen Christ

The claim. The Church is the abiding presence of the incarnate Word in space and time — Christ's own self-realisation in history after the Ascension.

Terms: - Ursakrament / Grundsakrament — primordial or basic sacrament. Strictly: Christ is the Ursakrament; the Church is the Grundsakrament, the basic sacrament, deriving from him. (Usage varies among authors — Otto Semmelroth, Edward Schillebeeckx and Rahner all use these terms with slight differences. Say which sense you mean.) - The Church is therefore not an organisation founded by Christ and now running on its own; it is Christ's symbolic reality.

Reception: this is the language Lumen Gentium 1 adopts — the Church as "sacrament, that is, sign and instrument of communion with God and of unity among the whole human race" → 15 - Rahner and Vatican II - The World Church.

Then what about the sinful Church?

A real symbol can be a deficient symbol. Rahner grants that the Church can obscure what it exists to express. This is why he speaks of the Church as ecclesia semper reformanda and of the "sinful Church," and it is the point at which the symbol ontology needs supplementing — an unresolved edge in his system.

Application 4 — The Sacraments: Realsymbole of the Church's own act

The claim. In the seven sacraments the Church realises its own essence in relation to an individual at a decisive moment of that person's life. The sacrament is the Church's Realsymbol.

The classic axiom becomes intelligible: sacramenta significando causant — the sacraments cause by signifying. On the ordinary understanding this is nearly paradoxical: how can a sign cause? On Rahner's ontology it is a straightforward instance of the general law: a reality's self-expression is the mode in which that reality is present and operative.

Traditional framing Rahner's re-framing
Sacrament = instrumental efficient cause; grace "flows" through the sign as through a channel Sacrament = the symbolic self-realisation of the Church, in which the grace always-already offered becomes historically, bodily, ecclesially tangible and eschatologically definitive for this person
Risk: a "grace-machine" picture, grace as a quantity dispensed Risk: the sacrament looks declaratory rather than conferring — the main objection against him

Illustration — the wedding vow

"I take you as my wife" does not report a marriage occurring elsewhere; it is where the marriage happens. The words do not describe reality, they effect it, precisely by signifying it. Every sacrament works on this grammar. (Compare J. L. Austin's performative utterance — a useful modern parallel, though Rahner does not use it.)

Illustration — the letter of pardon

A prisoner has already been pardoned by the ruler's decision (the grace always-already offered). The document read aloud in the prison yard does not create the ruler's mercy — but it is the moment when that mercy becomes public, dated, tangible, irrevocable, and this prisoner's own. Rahner's sacrament sits here. Whether that is enough for ex opere operato conferral is precisely the disputed point → 12 - Ecclesiology and Sacramental Theology.

Application 5 — The body as Realsymbol of the person (and so: the Sacred Heart)

The claim. The body is not a container, vehicle or prison of the soul. On Aristotelian-Thomist hylomorphism (the soul as forma corporis — dogmatically affirmed at the Council of Vienne, 1312), the body is the soul's own self-realisation in space and matter. Therefore: the body is the real symbol of the person.

Consequences that matter pastorally: - A caress, a blow, a tear, a shared meal are not "external signs of inner states." They are where the person actually is and acts. - Therefore: the resurrection of the body is not a curious appendix to salvation. If the body is the person's real symbol, then a disembodied salvation would be an unfinished salvation → 13 - Theological Anthropology, Death and Eschatology. - Therefore: sacraments must be bodily. Water, oil, bread, touch, word. - Therefore: bodily sin and bodily reverence are serious. What is done to the body is done to the person.

And so the Urwort "heart". Heart, light, bread, water, word, birth, death — these are Urworte, primordial words which name a total human reality rather than labelling a part. "Heart" names the person's innermost core as loving. Hence:

The pierced heart of Christ (Jn 19:34; cf. Zech 12:10) is the real symbol of the total self-gift of the incarnate Word. Devotion to the Sacred Heart adores the whole Christ at the point where he expresses himself most completely — the self-emptying love of the crucified.

09 — Rahner's Rule: The Grundaxiom

Prev: <a href="#08-realsymbol-applied-trinity-christ-church-">08 - Realsymbol Applied - Trinity, Christ, Church, Sacraments, Body</a> · Next: <a href="#10-christology-self-communication-and-evolut">10 - Christology - Self-Communication and Evolution</a>

The diagnosis that provoked it

Rahner opens Der dreifaltige Gott (1967; ET The Trinity, 1970) with a devastating observation:

Rahner's complaint

Christians are, in their practical life, "almost mere 'monotheists'." Should the doctrine of the Trinity be dropped as false, most religious literature could remain virtually unchanged.

Two causes he identifies: 1. The order of the treatises. Western manuals treat De Deo Uno first (the one God, from reason and metaphysics) and De Deo Trino second (the Trinity, from revelation). The Trinity becomes an appendix to a doctrine of God already complete without it. 2. The Augustinian-Thomistic axiom that all divine action ad extra is common to the three persons (opera Trinitatis ad extra sunt indivisa). Taken strictly, this means no divine person is present to us as that person — only the one God is. Then the Trinity has no purchase on salvation history, hence none on prayer or life.

The axiom itself

Rahner's Rule (*Grundaxiom*), *The Trinity*, 22

"The 'economic' Trinity is the 'immanent' Trinity, and the 'immanent' Trinity is the 'economic' Trinity."

Terms: - Economic Trinity (oikonomia, the divine "housekeeping"): God as revealed and acting in salvation history — the Father who sends, the Son incarnate, the Spirit poured out. - Immanent Trinity (theologia): God as God is eternally in Godself, apart from any relation to creation.

Two halves, very different weights:

Half Content Status
(a) Economic → immanent What God shows us in the economy is truly God, not a mask or a created substitute Almost universally accepted. Otherwise revelation reveals nothing and we are agnostic about God
(b) Immanent → economic ("and vice versa") The immanent Trinity is nothing other than what is given in the economy Hotly disputed. Does this collapse God into history and compromise divine freedom?

Why the symbol ontology makes this axiom inevitable

This is the link the examiner is looking for. Rahner's Rule is not a separate doctrine — it is the Trinity read through 07 - The Theology of the Symbol - The Core Essay:

  1. The Father eternally utters himself in the Word (Word = Realsymbol of the Father).
  2. The Incarnation is that same Word uttering himself outwards into the non-divine.
  3. A real symbol contains what it symbolises. Therefore the Word made flesh really is the eternal Word's self-utterance, not a created stand-in.
  4. Therefore what happens in the economy is the eternal Trinitarian life taking historical form. Economic = immanent.

Exam-winning link sentence

"Rahner's Rule is the Trinitarian application of his ontology of the real symbol: because a real symbol is the reality's own self-realisation in the other, God's self-utterance in history is God's eternal self-utterance, and not a duplicate of it."

What the axiom achieves

Achievement Explanation
Trinity becomes soteriologically relevant The Trinity is no longer speculation about God's inner arithmetic; it is the structure of our salvation
The missions are proper, not appropriated Only the Son becomes incarnate; the incarnation is not interchangeable among the persons. This corrects the over-strict reading of opera ad extra indivisa
Grace is Trinitarian God's self-communication has a threefold structure: source (Father), utterance (Son), acceptance-in-us (Spirit) → 06 - God's Self-Communication and Quasi-Formal Causality
Prayer and doctrine reconnect You can now say why we pray to the Father, through the Son, in the Spirit
Method reversed Start from the economy — from Jesus and the Spirit — not from a metaphysical De Deo Uno

Illustration

Suppose a friend spends thirty years showing you kindness. If you say "yes, but who she really is behind all that remains unknown," you have made the friendship worthless. Rahner's first half says: what she has shown you is who she is. The disputed second half says: there is nothing more to her than what the showing gives — and that is where critics say the analogy breaks down for God, because God's infinity always exceeds any economy.

Rahner's own terminological caution

Rahner is uneasy with "three persons." In modern usage "person" implies a distinct centre of consciousness and freedom — which, said three times of God, tends toward tritheism. He proposes instead:

"Three distinct manners of subsisting" (drei distinkte Subsistenzweisen) — one divine essence subsisting in three really distinct modes.

He insists there is one divine consciousness and one divine will, possessed in three distinct ways.

The modalism charge

Jürgen Moltmann (The Trinity and the Kingdom) reads this as Idealist modalism: one absolute subject in three modes, closer to Sabellius than to the Cappadocians. Rahner's defence: Subsistenzweise names a real distinction in God (unlike modalism's merely apparent one); he retains "person" as the dogmatically binding term and offers his phrase as an explanatory gloss, not a replacement. Know both sides.

The critical debate on the "vice versa"

The objection (Walter Kasper, Paul Molnar, Bruce Marshall, Yves Congar in part): If the immanent Trinity is nothing but the economic Trinity, then: - God's eternal being seems to require the economy — creation and incarnation become necessary to God being God. - Divine freedom and gratuity are lost. Grace ceases to be gift and becomes divine self-completion. - Take it further (as Moltmann, Eberhard Jüngel and Robert Jenson do) and you get a God who is constituted by history — which some regard as a legitimate development and others as the dissolution of classical theism.

Paul Molnar's Divine Freedom and the Doctrine of the Immanent Trinity is the standard book-length statement of this worry.

Rahner's defence: - He says is, not is exhausted by. Identity of reality, not identity of mode: the same Trinity is given in the economy under the conditions of history and freedom. - The economy is freely willed; the immanent processions are necessary. He preserves the distinction within the identity. - He never denies God's incomprehensibility; God given in the economy remains absolute mystery → 06 - God's Self-Communication and Quasi-Formal Causality.

A fair assessment for your exam answer:

Half (a) is now near-universal Catholic teaching and is presupposed by Dei Verbum. Half (b) is best read as an epistemological claim (we have no access to an immanent Trinity behind the economic one) rather than an ontological claim (there is nothing more to God than the economy). Read ontologically it endangers divine freedom; read epistemologically it is both defensible and fruitful. Rahner's own texts do not settle which he meant — and that ambiguity is itself a legitimate criticism.

Reception

  • Adopted almost universally in post-conciliar Trinitarian theology as a starting point (Kasper, LaCugna, Johnson, Moltmann, Jüngel, Boff).
  • Catherine Mowry LaCugna (God for Us) radicalises it: the immanent/economic distinction should be dropped altogether, since theologia is only accessible as oikonomia. Most critics think she goes beyond Rahner.
  • Used in the Catechism of the Catholic Church substance if not in his words (CCC 236 on oikonomia and theologia mutually illuminating).

10 — Christology: Self-Communication, Evolution, and the Searching Heart

Prev: <a href="#09-rahner-s-rule-the-grundaxiom-of-the-trini">09 - Rahner&#x27;s Rule - The Grundaxiom of the Trinity</a> · Next: <a href="#11-anonymous-christians-and-the-non-christia">11 - Anonymous Christians and the Non-Christian Religions</a>

The problem with textbook Christology

Rahner's complaint about the manuals: Chalcedon is treated as a terminus rather than a beginning. "Two natures in one person" is repeated as a formula while the underlying picture remains mythological — God disguised as a man, a divine subject wearing a human costume, the humanity a mere instrument.

Rahner's principle about Chalcedon

A dogmatic formula is not only an end but a beginning: every genuine formula opens onto the mystery it protects, and must be thought further, not merely repeated.

1. The hypostatic union as the supreme case of symbolic causality

From 08 - Realsymbol Applied - Trinity, Christ, Church, Sacraments, Body: the humanity of Jesus is the Realsymbol of the Logos. Not a tool, not a veil, not a puppet — the Logos's own self-utterance into the non-divine.

Rahner's axiom of proportionality (learn this; it does enormous work):

Nearness to God and creaturely autonomy vary in **direct**, not inverse, proportion.

The closer a creature is to God, the more genuinely itself, free and autonomous it is. God is not a competitor to creaturely freedom.

Applied to Christ: Jesus is not less human because he is God's self-utterance; he is the most fully human being who has ever existed. His human freedom, his human knowledge, his human obedience, his human fear in Gethsemane are all fully real. This gives Rahner a strong defence of Chalcedon's vere homo against every latent Monophysitism in popular piety.

Practical test

Ask a congregation: "Did Jesus really not know the day or the hour (Mk 13:32)?" Latent Monophysitism will say "he pretended not to know." Rahner's Christology permits the plain answer: his human consciousness genuinely did not know, while he possessed an unthematic, immediate self-presence to the Logos at the ground of his being. (Rahner develops this as the distinction between Christ's basic self-consciousness and his thematic, historically developing knowledge.)

2. Incarnation as the unique modality of self-communication

Recall the three modalities from 06 - God's Self-Communication and Quasi-Formal Causality:

Modality Character
Grace Self-communication offered, acceptable or refusable
Incarnation Self-communication given to one human reality absolutely and irrevocably, and accepted absolutely from the human side
Glory Self-communication unveiled

Therefore the Incarnation is not a foreign body in the order of grace. It is that order's unsurpassable and eschatologically victorious instance — the point at which the offer becomes irrevocable because it has been definitively accepted.

Corollary that shocked many: grace and Incarnation are not two divine acts but one, in different modes. This is why Rahner can move so freely between anthropology and Christology — and why critics say the Incarnation loses its scandalous particularity → 17 - Critical Reflection II - Theological.

3. Evolutionary Christology (Christologie innerhalb evolutiver Weltanschauung)

Rahner asks: can the Incarnation be thought within a world understood as evolving?

The argument in five steps: 1. Matter and spirit are not two unrelated realms. Spirit is not foreign to matter; the human being is spirit-in-world (02 - Philosophical Foundations I - Spirit in the World). 2. Evolution shows genuine novelty: matter → life → consciousness. Each stage is more than a rearrangement of the previous. 3. Such becoming-more is active self-transcendence (aktive Selbsttranszendenz): the creature genuinely transcends itself, yet only by a power given by God, who is the dynamism within the process rather than an occasional intervener from outside. 4. Hominisation: in the human being, the cosmos becomes conscious of itself and stands open before absolute mystery. 5. The Incarnation is the goal-point of this self-transcendence: the moment when the cosmos, in one of its members, accepts God's self-communication absolutely. Christ is where the world's openness reaches its unsurpassable term.

Rahner and Teilhard de Chardin

There is genuine convergence (cosmic Christology, the world's ascent toward Christ as Omega). But Rahner's argument is transcendental and metaphysical, not scientific-cosmological, and he is more careful than Teilhard to keep the Incarnation free: the world's openness does not produce Christ; it is the receptivity that God's free self-gift fulfils. Distinguish the two in an exam.

The objection to state

If Christ is the "goal" of cosmic self-transcendence, does the Incarnation become necessary — the natural completion of evolution rather than free grace? Rahner's reply: the world's openness is an empty capacity, and its fulfilment remains wholly gratuitous; a summit is not caused by the slope that leads up to it. Whether that fully answers the objection is a fair essay question.

4. Transcendental and searching Christology

Transcendental Christology. By transcendental method Rahner derives the a priori idea of an "absolute saviour" (absoluter Heilbringer) — the one in whom God's self-communication to history becomes irreversible and historically manifest. Philosophy cannot say that such a one exists; it can say what would count as one. Then a posteriori, the Christian says: this is Jesus of Nazareth.

Guard the order

The transcendental step does not deduce Jesus. It shows that the Gospel is not an arbitrary intrusion into human history. Confuse the order and you have made Christ a corollary of anthropology — which is exactly what Balthasar accused Rahner of doing.

Searching Christology (suchende Christologie). Rahner names three ordinary human acts in which people are already implicitly reaching for the absolute saviour:

Act What it implicitly affirms
Absolute love of neighbour That the other is worth an unconditional gift — which is intelligible only if the human is loved unconditionally by God
Readiness for death Accepting one's own end in hope means entrusting oneself to a mystery that receives it
Hope in the future Refusing to treat history as absurd means expecting an unsurpassable fulfilment

Pastoral illustration

A woman who has spent forty years caring for a disabled child, without recognition and without expectation, is enacting an absolute love. Rahner's claim is not that she is "secretly Christian" in her opinions. It is that the structure of her act only makes sense if the absolute self-gift she practises is grounded in reality — and Christians name that ground Jesus Christ. This is the doorway to 11 - Anonymous Christians and the Non-Christian Religions.

5. Cross and resurrection

Rahner is often accused of a thin theology of the Cross. His actual position:

  • The Cross is the historical embodiment of the definitive acceptance of God's self-communication by a human freedom — obedience unto death is where the absolute "yes" is uttered from within history.
  • The Resurrection is not an added miracle proving the Cross; it is the manifestation of the Cross's validity — the definitive acceptance shown to have been accepted by God.
  • Rahner is reserved about satisfaction theories (Anselm's Cur Deus Homo): he denies that the Cross causes God to become gracious. God's saving will is prior; the Cross is its real symbol in history — again the symbol ontology.

Is that enough?

Balthasar's charge: Rahner's Cross is an epistemological event (it manifests) rather than a soteriological one (it achieves); the descent into hell, the Ernstfall, the sheer horror, are underweighted. This is a strong criticism and you should engage it, not dodge it → 17 - Critical Reflection II - Theological.

11 — Anonymous Christians and the Non-Christian Religions

Prev: <a href="#10-christology-self-communication-and-evolut">10 - Christology - Self-Communication and Evolution</a> · Next: <a href="#12-ecclesiology-and-sacramental-theology">12 - Ecclesiology and Sacramental Theology</a>

Key text

"Christianity and the Non-Christian Religions," Theological Investigations V, 115–134. See also "Anonymous Christians," TI VI; "Observations on the Problem of the 'Anonymous Christian'," TI XIV.

The problem to be solved

Two Catholic convictions were in tension by the mid-twentieth century:

  1. Universal salvific will — "God desires everyone to be saved and to come to the knowledge of the truth" (1 Tim 2:4).
  2. Necessity of Christ and the Church — "no other name" (Acts 4:12); extra Ecclesiam nulla salus.

The traditional escape routes (invincible ignorance, baptism of desire, implicit faith) were individual, exceptional and grudging. They said nothing about the religions as such, and nothing about the fact that most human beings who have ever lived were never in a position to hear the Gospel.

Rahner's question is therefore not "can a good Hindu be saved?" but the far sharper one: how are we to think theologically about the vast majority of humanity, and about the religions that actually shape their lives?

The four theses

Thesis 1 — Christianity is the absolute religion

Christianity understands itself as the religion intended for all, founded on God's unsurpassable self-communication in Christ. It cannot relativise this without ceasing to be itself. But: this claim becomes existentially binding on a particular person or culture only from the moment it genuinely, historically confronts them. Before that moment, it is not yet their question.

Thesis 2 — A non-Christian religion may be a lawful religion

Until the Gospel genuinely enters a person's historical situation, their own religion may be, for them, a legitimate means of salvation, containing not only natural elements and human error but genuine supernatural elements of grace.

The reasoning: - Grace is universally offered (05 - The Supernatural Existential - Nature and Grace). - The human being is social and historical (03 - Philosophical Foundations II - Hearer of the Word) — grace never arrives as private interior lightning; it takes social, ritual, communal, historical form. - Therefore, if grace is genuinely offered to all, it must be capable of taking form within the religions people actually have.

This is the genuinely radical step

Rahner moves the discussion from individuals ("perhaps this good pagan is saved despite his religion") to religions ("this religion may be a positive means of salvation, not merely an obstacle overcome"). This is what Nostra Aetate 2 later expresses when it says the Church "rejects nothing of what is true and holy in these religions."

Note the limits Rahner himself sets

He does not say all religions are equally valid, nor that everything in them is grace. Error, sin and depravation are real in every religion — including, he insists, in the empirical Christian religion. "Lawful" is a relative and provisional judgement, not a blanket endorsement.

Thesis 3 — The non-Christian may be an anonymous Christian

A person who accepts, in the depths of their freedom, the grace of God — often in accepting their own existence, their duty, their neighbour, their death — has accepted the reality that Christians name Christ, even without the name.

The definition to memorise

An anonymous Christian is one who, without explicit knowledge of Christ or membership in the Church, has in fact accepted God's self-communication in their free acceptance of their own existence, and is therefore justified by the grace of Christ.

What the term does and does not claim: - It is a theological judgement made by Christians about the possibility of salvation, not a sociological label to be pinned on people. - It is not a claim that Hindus, Muslims or Buddhists are "really Christians without knowing it" in the sense of secretly holding Christian beliefs. - Rahner says explicitly that one should not go up to a Buddhist and inform him he is an anonymous Christian. The term belongs to Christian self-understanding, in the same way that "prevenient grace" describes God's action rather than a person's opinion.

Thesis 4 — The Church is the vanguard, not the boundary

The visible Church is not the sum total of the saved but the historically tangible vanguard (Vortrupp), the explicit expression and sacramental sign of a grace that is at work far beyond its visible borders.

Therefore mission is not made superfluous, but redefined:

Mission is not Mission is
Rescuing souls from certain damnation Bringing to explicit, communal, sacramental expression what grace is already doing
Importing grace from outside Giving grace its proper historical, ecclesial body — the symbol ontology again
A numbers game Service of the fullness, clarity, and eschatological certainty of salvation

Rahner's own analogy

Explicit faith stands to anonymous faith as waking self-knowledge stands to implicit self-possession. The second is real; the first is fuller, freer, and more secure. Nobody says "then why wake up?"

Illustration for the Indian classroom

Example

A Hindu woman in a Kolkata basti spends her life caring for tuberculosis patients nobody else will touch. She has never heard the Gospel preached in a way she could receive. Rahner's four theses in order: 1. Christianity is still the absolute religion — this is not relativised. 2. Her Vaishnava piety, with its bhakti and its ethic of seva, may be for her a lawful way in which grace has taken social and ritual form. 3. In her unconditional self-gift she has accepted the God whom Christians confess in Christ — she is an anonymous Christian. 4. The Church exists to name aloud, sacramentally and communally, the mystery she is already living — not to inform her that she was Christian all along.

And immediately the critique: she might reasonably reply that being categorised in a Christian scheme, however generously, is a form of theological annexation. Hold both halves → 18 - Rahner in the Indian Context.

The criticisms — know all five

  1. It patronises. To call the other an "anonymous Christian" is to name them in my terms. Hans Küng: an "elegant conquest"; Raimon Panikkar: the Christian would hardly be pleased to be told he is an "anonymous Hindu." Asian and African theologians press this hardest. Rahner's reply: the term is intra-Christian, a statement of hope about God's universal will, never an address to the other. He accepted that the word was unfortunate and offered "anonymous Christianity" as a description of a state of affairs, not a title for persons. Assessment: the reply is partly successful. The logical structure survives; the rhetoric does not. Many now keep Rahner's substance while abandoning his phrase.

  2. It empties mission and martyrdom of seriousness. Hans Urs von Balthasar, Cordula oder der Ernstfall (The Moment of Christian Witness, 1966): if everyone is anonymously saved, the Ernstfall — the test case, the martyr's blood — becomes a mere illustration of a general truth. Christianity loses its edge. Rahner's reply: the martyr is precisely the one in whom the anonymous becomes fully explicit and costly; universality of the offer does not diminish the cost of the response. Assessment: Balthasar's charge is rhetorically devastating and theologically contestable. Rahner never denied the Cross; but the system does make it harder to say why the Gospel is urgent.

  3. It flattens the religions. The pluralist objection (John Hick, God and the Universe of Faiths): the theory grants "honorary status" while keeping Christianity at the centre — inclusivism as a soft imperialism. Meanwhile the exclusivist objection is the mirror image: it concedes too much. Assessment: being attacked from both sides is evidence that inclusivism occupies a real and defensible middle position, not that it is incoherent.

  4. It works with an abstract "religion". Postliberal objection (George Lindbeck, The Nature of Doctrine): Rahner assumes a common core religious experience which the various religions then express. But religions are cultural-linguistic systems which form rather than express experience. There is no pre-linguistic experience available for anonymous acceptance. Assessment: the strongest philosophical objection in the file. Rahner's transcendental experience is by definition unthematic, so Lindbeck's demand for identifiable content may miss the target — but the burden of proof genuinely shifts to Rahner.

  5. It individualises. The acceptance described is a solitary transcendental act. Where are structures, oppression, community, history? (Metz, liberation theologians.) → 17 - Critical Reflection II - Theological

Magisterial reception

  • Lumen Gentium 16 — those who through no fault of their own do not know the Gospel but sincerely seek God and follow conscience "may attain eternal salvation."
  • Gaudium et Spes 22 — the Holy Spirit offers to all, "in a manner known to God," the possibility of being associated with the Paschal Mystery.
  • Nostra Aetate 2 — the Church "rejects nothing of what is true and holy in these religions."
  • Ad Gentes 7 and Redemptoris Missio (John Paul II, 1990) — reaffirm the necessity and urgency of explicit mission; Redemptoris Missio 10 affirms universal grace while insisting the Church remains the ordinary means.
  • Dominus Iesus (CDF, 2000) — cautions against theories that make the religions as such parallel ways of salvation. This bears directly on Thesis 2 and is the strongest magisterial pressure on Rahner's position. Note carefully: it does not condemn Rahner by name, and it upholds universal grace — but it narrows "lawful religion."

Balanced exam judgement

"Rahner's achievement was to give theological substance to the universal salvific will without surrendering the uniqueness of Christ. His inclusivism supplied much of the grammar of Nostra Aetate. The vulnerable point is not the doctrine of universal grace, which is now common Catholic teaching, but the specific claim that non-Christian religions are as religions lawful means of salvation, and the unfortunate term 'anonymous Christian', which achieves generosity at the cost of granting the other no voice in their own description."

12 — Ecclesiology and Sacramental Theology

Prev: <a href="#11-anonymous-christians-and-the-non-christia">11 - Anonymous Christians and the Non-Christian Religions</a> · Next: <a href="#13-theological-anthropology-death-and-eschat">13 - Theological Anthropology, Death and Eschatology</a>

1. The Church as Grundsakrament

The claim. The Church is the abiding real symbol of the risen Christ in history: the sign in which he continues to utter himself, and therefore the sign in which he is genuinely present and effective.

Terminology — get this right:

Term Standard usage Referent
Ursakrament (primordial sacrament) Christ himself The Incarnate Word is the original sacrament of God
Grundsakrament (basic/fundamental sacrament) The Church Derived from Christ; the sacrament of the sacraments
The seven sacraments Acts of the Church The Church's self-realisation for the individual

Usage warning

Otto Semmelroth (Die Kirche als Ursakrament, 1953), Edward Schillebeeckx (Christ the Sacrament of the Encounter with God, 1960) and Rahner all use these terms with slight variations; some authors call the Church the Ursakrament. State your usage explicitly in an essay and you cannot be marked wrong.

Magisterial fruit: Lumen Gentium 1 — the Church is "in Christ like a sacrament, that is, a sign and instrument both of a very closely knit union with God and of the unity of the whole human race." LG 9, 48; Sacrosanctum Concilium 5, 26; Gaudium et Spes 45 repeat the pattern. This is arguably the single most consequential piece of Rahner-influenced language in the conciliar documents → 15 - Rahner and Vatican II - The World Church.

2. Sacramental causality re-thought

The classic axiom

Sacramenta significando causant — "the sacraments cause by signifying" (Aquinas, ST III q.60–62; Trent, DS 1606, 1608).

The classic difficulty

On a picture of instrumental efficient causality, the sign and the causing are two things loosely tied together: God attaches an effect to a rite. This invites the "grace-machine" caricature — grace as a substance dispensed through a channel, and the sign as almost incidental.

Rahner's re-framing

On the ontology of the real symbol, signifying and causing are one act:

A real symbol is the self-realisation of a reality in the other. The Church, realising itself in this act for this person, is Christ's grace becoming present here. It causes because it signifies, and it signifies because it is that reality's own way of being present.

Instrumentalist picture Rahner's symbolic picture
Sign and cause Externally joined Identical in one act
Grace A created quality conveyed God's self-communication made tangible → 06 - God's Self-Communication and Quasi-Formal Causality
Where grace begins At the rite Always already offered (05 - The Supernatural Existential - Nature and Grace); the rite makes it historical, ecclesial, definitive
Ex opere operato means God guarantees the transfer God guarantees the Church's self-realisation in this act, independent of the minister's worth

Illustration — the wedding vow (performative)

"I take you as my wife" does not report a marriage happening elsewhere. It is where the marriage happens. The saying is the doing. Every sacrament runs on this grammar.

Illustration — the amnesty proclaimed

A ruler has already decreed pardon for all prisoners (grace universally offered). The herald reading the decree in the prison yard does not manufacture the ruler's mercy — but at that moment the mercy becomes public, dated, addressed to this prisoner, and irrevocable in the order of history. Rahner's sacrament stands here.

The standing objection

Colman O'Neill OP and other Thomists: if grace is universally offered before the sacrament, and the sacrament makes it tangible, has the sacrament actually conferred anything, or only declared it? Trent taught that the sacraments contain and confer the grace they signify (DS 1606). A merely manifestative account would fall short.

Rahner's reply: - The sacrament genuinely effects something new: grace previously offered and (perhaps) accepted unthematically now becomes historically embodied, ecclesially owned, eschatologically certain. That is not nothing — on his ontology it is the difference between a reality half-realised and a reality fully expressed. - On his own principles, what is not expressed is not fully real. So the sacrament does not merely announce a completed fact; it completes it.

Assessment for your essay: the reply is strong given the symbol ontology and weak if one does not grant that ontology. This is a genuine watershed: your judgement on Rahner's sacramental theology depends almost entirely on whether you accept Thesis 1 of the symbol essay.

3. Opus operatum and the Wort-Sakrament connection

Rahner extends the symbol logic to the word: - The Church's preaching is also a form of self-realisation. He speaks of an "exhibitive word" (exhibitives Wort) — a word which does not merely inform but effects what it says. - This gives a Catholic account of the Protestant emphasis on the Word: the sacrament is the word at its highest density (Wort in seiner höchsten Dichte), not an alternative to it. Ecumenically fruitful.

4. Ecclesiology of the diaspora

Rahner's sociological realism about the Church's future in the West:

  • The era of a culturally Christian society is over. The Church will be a Diasporakirche — a minority in a pluralist world, held together by personal conviction rather than social inheritance.
  • The "little flock" (kleine Herde): not a defeat but a return to the Church's normal condition.
  • Consequence: faith must become personally appropriated and experienced, or it will not survive at all → 14 - Mystagogy, Ignatian Roots and Spirituality.
  • Practical proposals, often controversial in his day: married clergy as a possibility to be discussed, wider lay ministry, "basic communities," the ordination of viri probati for communities deprived of the Eucharist, structural decentralisation after the "world Church" turn.

The best-known sentence in Rahner

"The devout Christian of the future will either be a 'mystic', one who has 'experienced' something, or he will cease to be anything at all." — "Christian Living Formerly and Today," TI VII, 15.

5. Church, sin, and the limits of the symbol model

The hardest question for Rahner's ecclesiology: how can the real symbol of Christ persecute, abuse, or betray?

His answer: the Church is a sündige Kirche — a sinful Church, not merely a Church of sinners. As a real symbol it can be a deficient and even distorting expression of what it symbolises, without ceasing to be its symbol. The indefectibility promised is of the whole in the end, not of every act.

Critical judgement

This is honest but under-developed. The symbol ontology is built to explain fullness of expression; it explains failure of expression only by negation. A theology of the lying symbol — the body that conceals, the Church that misrepresents — is the largest unfilled gap in Rahner's system, and is worth saying so in an essay. For the constructive completion — counter-witness, the anti-sacrament, and why scandal in the Church is ontologically worse than the same act elsewhere — see 24 - The Theology of the False Symbol.

13 — Theological Anthropology, Death and Eschatology

Prev: <a href="#12-ecclesiology-and-sacramental-theology">12 - Ecclesiology and Sacramental Theology</a> · Next: <a href="#14-mystagogy-ignatian-roots-and-spirituality">14 - Mystagogy, Ignatian Roots and Spirituality</a>

1. The human being defined

Rahner's compressed definition, which gathers the whole of notes 02–06:

The human being is

the event of God's free, forgiving, absolute self-communication — the finite spirit whose very being is an unlimited openness (Vorgriff) to absolute mystery, who is spirit only in and through matter and history, and who is always already addressed by God's offer of Godself.

Four consequences:

Statement Where it comes from
The human is transcendence — a question that cannot answer itself 02 - Philosophical Foundations I - Spirit in the World
The human is historical and bodily — no escape into pure interiority 03 - Philosophical Foundations II - Hearer of the Word
The human is always already graced 05 - The Supernatural Existential - Nature and Grace
The human is defined Christologically — Christ shows what humanity is 10 - Christology - Self-Communication and Evolution

The formula to memorise

"Anthropology is deficient Christology; Christology is self-transcending anthropology." Not: theology reduced to anthropology. Rather: whoever asks what a human being is has already begun to ask about Christ.

2. Body and soul: the symbol ontology applied to the person

  • Following the Council of Vienne (1312), the soul is forma corporis — the form of the body, not a pilot in a ship.
  • Therefore the body is the real symbol of the soul (08 - Realsymbol Applied - Trinity, Christ, Church, Sacraments, Body).
  • Therefore human freedom is enacted bodily and historically, or not at all.

Pastoral payoff: the widespread popular dualism — "the body is just a shell; what matters is the soul" — is not Catholic doctrine. Rahner gives students the metaphysics to say why.

3. Freedom and the Grundentscheidung

  • Freedom, for Rahner, is not primarily the capacity to choose between this and that (Wahlfreiheit). It is the capacity to dispose of oneself as a whole before God — Freiheit as self-determination toward one's final destiny.
  • Hence a fundamental option (Grundentscheidung): the total orientation of a life, realised through, but not identical with, individual acts.
  • A single act may express or contradict the fundamental option; it is not automatically identical with it.

The controversy

This is developed further by Josef Fuchs and others, and criticised in Veritatis Splendor (John Paul II, 1993, §§65–70) where the theory is judged, in some forms, to detach the fundamental option from concrete acts and so to weaken the reality of mortal sin. Note carefully: Veritatis Splendor does not condemn the notion of a fundamental orientation as such; it rejects a separation between that orientation and particular free choices. Rahner's own version keeps the two connected precisely by his symbol ontology — the fundamental option must express itself in acts to be real at all.

4. Death: On the Theology of Death (1958)

Death as act and as passion (the central thesis).

Aspect Meaning
Passion (Leiden) Death happens to us: violent, unchosen, the wage of sin, the visible face of finitude
Act (Tat) Death is also our final, total self-disposal — the moment when a whole life is gathered into one definitive act of freedom

Both must be held. Death as pure passion makes salvation something that happens to a corpse; death as pure act romanticises an experience most people meet in helplessness.

The pancosmic thesis. At death the soul does not become acosmic (worldless, an isolated spirit awaiting reassembly). It becomes pancosmic: it enters into a deeper, open relation to the material universe as a whole rather than to one body.

What the pancosmic thesis is for

  1. To preserve hylomorphism: a soul is essentially related to matter, so a wholly worldless soul is incoherent.
  2. To make sense of the communion of saints and the intercession of the dead as a real relation to the world.
  3. To give a framework for the "already/not yet" of the resurrection.

Status: speculative, and Rahner presents it as such. It has been criticised as unverifiable and as a metaphysical patch. Say so.

Death and Christ. Christ's death is the supreme instance: the total passion (he is killed) which is also the total act (he hands himself over). "Into your hands I commend my spirit" (Lk 23:46) is the definitive human self-disposal. Our deaths become salvific by participation in his → 10 - Christology - Self-Communication and Evolution.

5. The hermeneutics of eschatological assertions

Key text

"The Hermeneutics of Eschatological Assertions," Theological Investigations IV, 323–346.

The rule:

Important

Eschatological statements are not advance reportage of future events. They are the transposition into the future of what the Christian already experiences of salvation in Christ now. Eschatology is anthropology and Christology conjugated in the future tense.

The distinction that follows:

Eschatology Apocalyptic
Extrapolates from the present experience of grace in Christ toward its fulfilment Claims direct knowledge of future events, projecting a script backwards from heaven
Legitimate; necessary; controlled by Christology Illegitimate as theology; a genre to be interpreted, not a source of information
"Because Christ is risen, our future is..." "In the year X, the following will occur..."

Worked applications: - Heaven = the final and unveiled state of God's self-communication now given in grace — not a place with furniture. - Hell = the real possibility of a definitive self-closure against that self-communication. Rahner holds it must be preached as a real possibility for me, never asserted as a fact about anyone. He is not a universalist; he insists we may and must hope for the salvation of all while affirming that damnation is genuinely possible. - Purgatory = the process of the integration of the whole person into the definitive act of self-disposal; not a punishment-tariff. - Resurrection of the body = required by the symbol ontology: since the body is the person's real symbol, salvation of the person entails salvation of the body → 08 - Realsymbol Applied - Trinity, Christ, Church, Sacraments, Body. - Parousia = the manifestation of what is already true in Christ, not the arrival of a new fact.

Illustration — the seed and the letter

To say "this acorn will become an oak" is not a report from the future; it is a statement about what the acorn now is, projected forward. To say "on the third Tuesday of next March a tree will be felled by lightning" is a different kind of claim entirely. Eschatology speaks the first way; apocalyptic pretends to speak the second.

The objection

If eschatology is only the future tense of present experience, do we still have a genuine promise of God about the future, or only a description of ourselves? Critics (Moltmann, Theology of Hope; Metz) argue Rahner's rule domesticates biblical hope, removing its power to protest against present injustice. This is the same abstraction critique as elsewhere → 17 - Critical Reflection II - Theological.

14 — Mystagogy, Ignatian Roots and Spirituality

Prev: <a href="#13-theological-anthropology-death-and-eschat">13 - Theological Anthropology, Death and Eschatology</a> · Next: <a href="#15-rahner-and-vatican-ii-the-world-church">15 - Rahner and Vatican II - The World Church</a>

The claim of this note

Rahner's technical apparatus — Vorgriff, existential, symbol, quasi-formal causality — exists to serve a spiritual conviction he received from the Spiritual Exercises: that God gives Godself immediately to the ordinary person in the ordinary world. Miss this and you have misread him as a speculative philosopher.

1. The Ignatian core

What Rahner took from Ignatius of Loyola:

Ignatian element Rahnerian transposition
The immediate experience of God in the Exercises (Annotation 15: let the Creator deal directly with the creature) Transcendental experience of God as the unthematic ground of every act
"Finding God in all things" The world as graced; the secular is not godless → 05 - The Supernatural Existential - Nature and Grace
Discernment of spirits; the "consolation without preceding cause" The point where God's self-communication touches the subject non-objectively
Election — the fundamental decision about one's life Grundentscheidung / fundamental option → 13 - Theological Anthropology, Death and Eschatology
Suscipe: "Take, Lord, receive… give me only your love and your grace" Grace as God's self, not a created quantity → 06 - God's Self-Communication and Quasi-Formal Causality
Contemplation to attain love: God labouring in creatures Active self-transcendence; evolutionary Christology → 10 - Christology - Self-Communication and Evolution

Rahner's own testimony

Asked what most influenced his theology, he pointed not to Heidegger or Maréchal but to Ignatius: the conviction that God really can be encountered directly, and that theology exists to remove the obstacles to that encounter.

Late text worth knowing: Ignatius of Loyola Speaks to a Modern Jesuit (in Ignatius of Loyola, 1978) — Rahner writes in Ignatius's voice, and says more plainly than anywhere else what he thinks theology is for.

2. Mystagogy

Definition. Mystagogy (Gk. mystagōgia, "leading into the mystery") is the patristic term for the post-baptismal catechesis that unfolded the meaning of the rites already undergone. Rahner generalises it into the basic form of all theology and preaching.

The mystagogical principle

The task is not to inform people about a God they have never met, but to help them recognise, name and surrender to the mystery already at work in their own lives.

Method: point to human experiences which cannot be accounted for within a closed, finite horizon:

Experience What it opens onto
Loving where there is nothing to gain Unconditional love as the ground of the person
Forgiving what cannot be undone A mercy not generated by the forgiver
Standing by a duty when no one will ever know An absolute claim not derived from society
Radical hope in the face of death A future not produced by the one who hopes
The silence after prayer that is not empty God as mystery rather than as object
Joy that outruns its occasion Grace exceeding its finite trigger

Mystagogical homily shape (usable in the pulpit)

  1. Name the experience concretely: the sleepless night in the hospital corridor.
  2. Refuse the reductive explanation: this is not merely biology, duty, or habit.
  3. Show the horizon: what you did there only makes sense if the human being is held by something unconditional.
  4. Name it: this is what the Church calls grace; this is the God whose face we see in Christ crucified.
  5. Send back: go and live it again, now knowing whose it is.

3. "The Christian of the future will be a mystic"

*TI* VII, 15

"The devout Christian of the future will either be a 'mystic', one who has 'experienced' something, or he will cease to be anything at all."

Read it precisely — three things it does not mean: 1. Not extraordinary phenomena — visions, levitation, locutions. Rahner is explicitly uninterested in these. 2. Not elitism — this is a claim about ordinary Christians, not a spiritual aristocracy. 3. Not anti-institutionalism — the mystic still needs the Church, the sacraments and the community; mystagogy leads into the liturgy, not away from it.

What it does mean: in a diaspora situation (12 - Ecclesiology and Sacramental Theology) faith can no longer be carried by social inheritance. It must rest on a personally appropriated experience of God. This is a sociological diagnosis with a spiritual prescription.

4. The mysticism of everyday life

Rahner's essays on ordinary things — "On Truthfulness," "The Consolation of Time," and the meditations in Encounters with Silence (1938) and The Need and the Blessing of Prayer — press the same point.

"Experiences of the Spirit" — Rahner's own list

Being silent though one could have defended oneself; forgiving with no thanks and no visible response; obeying not because it is convenient but because one must; giving with no acknowledgement; being utterly lonely and, in that loneliness, not despairing; hoping against every calculation. In these, he says, grace is happening, whether or not it is called by name.

Why this matters for the whole system: it is the pastoral cash value of the supernatural existential and of anonymous Christianity. If grace is universal, it must be experienceable in the ordinary — otherwise the doctrine is empty.

5. Prayer

  • Rahner's first published book was a book of prayers (Worte ins Schweigen, 1938) — not a system.
  • His theology of prayer follows the symbol ontology: prayer is the human being expressing himself before God, and thereby becoming himself. Silent prayer is not the absence of expression but expression that has grown beyond words.
  • On unanswered prayer: God's response is God's self, not the granting of the object. The "silence" of God is the mode in which the incomprehensible mystery is present → 06 - God's Self-Communication and Quasi-Formal Causality.

6. Critical reflection

  • Strength: almost uniquely among twentieth-century systematicians, Rahner's speculative and spiritual writings are continuous. The prayers and the metaphysics say the same thing in two registers. This is a real theological achievement and it answers the caricature of Rahner as a dry transcendentalist.
  • Weakness 1 — the interior turn. The experiences he names are largely those of a solitary conscience: silence, loneliness, unrewarded fidelity. Where is the experience of grace in collective struggle, in a workers' movement, in a community resisting injustice? This is Metz's charge, and it lands.
  • Weakness 2 — verifiability. If grace is unthematic, how would anyone know whether they had experienced it or merely experienced strong feeling? Rahner's answer — that transcendental experience is by definition not objectifiable — is consistent but unfalsifiable, and critics say it protects itself too well.
  • Weakness 3 — class and culture. The "mysticism of everyday life" presupposes a certain leisure of the reflective self. A bonded labourer's day may not afford the interiority Rahner's method assumes. This is where Indian theologians press hardest → 18 - Rahner in the Indian Context.

15 — Rahner and Vatican II: The World Church

Prev: <a href="#14-mystagogy-ignatian-roots-and-spirituality">14 - Mystagogy, Ignatian Roots and Spirituality</a> · Next: <a href="#16-critical-reflection-i-philosophical">16 - Critical Reflection I - Philosophical</a>

Companion unit

For the Council itself — what it was, how it ran, and what each document says — see the Vatican II unit. Directly relevant here: 03 - How the Council Ran (the periti and how they exercised influence without a vote), 09 - The Turning Points (the rejection of De fontibus, and the reordering of Lumen Gentium), and 05 - Lumen Gentium - The Church.

1. His actual role

  • Peritus (expert) appointed by John XXIII, serving principally as adviser to Cardinal Franz König of Vienna; also consulted by the German-speaking bishops as a bloc.
  • Present at the crucial rejection of the preparatory schemas in the first session (1962), especially De fontibus revelationis, which he had criticised in a widely circulated memorandum.
  • Not the author of any document. Influence, not authorship — say this precisely in an exam. The conciliar texts are compromises; Rahner supplied concepts and arguments that shaped them.

The irony worth mentioning

In 1962 Rome had placed his writings under pre-censorship. Within months he was a council peritus advising a cardinal. The reversal marks the change of theological climate that the Council itself was.

2. Where his fingerprints are

The single most important shift

Revelation as self-communication rather than as a deposit of propositions. Dei Verbum 2 states that God reveals himself and the mystery of his will, and that this happens in "deeds and words having an inner unity." That last phrase is the symbol ontology in conciliar dress: word and deed are not two channels but one self-expression.

3. The "World Church" thesis

Key text

"Towards a Fundamental Theological Interpretation of Vatican II" (1979), Theological Investigations XX, 77–89.

The thesis: Vatican II is the beginning of the third epoch of Church history — the moment when Christianity begins actually (not merely theoretically) to be a world Church (Weltkirche).

Epoch Duration Character Turning point
I. Jewish Christianity c. 30 – c. 50 The Church within Judaism; the Gospel in Jewish categories The Council of Jerusalem (Acts 15): Gentiles need not become Jews
II. Hellenistic and European Christianity c. 50 – 1962 The Gospel in Greek philosophical and Roman legal form; Europe exports a European Church Vatican II
III. World Church 1962 – The Gospel takes flesh in many cultures on their own terms, not as European Christianity in translation —

Rahner's crucial claim: the transition from epoch I to II involved a genuine, painful, theological discontinuity — circumcision, kashrut, the whole Torah-observant shape of discipleship was set aside. The transition to epoch III must be equally radical. It cannot be satisfied by translating Latin texts into Tamil or by adding local music to a Roman rite.

Evidence he cites at the Council itself: the vernacular liturgy (Sacrosanctum Concilium), the genuinely worldwide episcopate present in the aula, the declaration on religious liberty (Dignitatis Humanae), the openness to other religions (Nostra Aetate), the address to the whole world rather than to Catholics only (Gaudium et Spes).

What epoch III actually demands

If the Jerusalem Council could set aside circumcision — the very sign of the covenant — then a genuine world Church must be willing to ask which of its European inheritances are Gospel and which are Hellenistic-Roman packaging: canonical structures, philosophical vocabulary, liturgical forms, clerical culture, the shape of ministry. Rahner does not answer these questions; he insists they are legitimate and unavoidable.

Contested

Critics from both directions: conservatives say the thesis relativises the doctrinal achievements of the patristic and scholastic tradition, treating Nicaea's homoousios as culturally contingent packaging; contextual and liberation theologians say Rahner announced the world Church from a chair in Münster and never handed over the microphone — the theory of inculturation remained European. Both criticisms have force → 18 - Rahner in the Indian Context.

4. Rahner on the Council's reception

  • He judged the Council a beginning, not a conclusion, and grew impatient with restorationist readings in the 1970s.
  • His late writings press for structural consequences: real episcopal collegiality, decentralisation, subsidiarity, wider ministries, a Church willing to be a creative minority rather than a defensive fortress → 12 - Ecclesiology and Sacramental Theology.
  • His last public act (1984) was a letter defending Gustavo Gutiérrez to Cardinal Juan Landázuri Ricketts of Lima against Roman moves toward censure — evidence that he had absorbed the political critique of his own student Metz.

16 — Critical Reflection I: Philosophical Critiques

Prev: <a href="#15-rahner-and-vatican-ii-the-world-church">15 - Rahner and Vatican II - The World Church</a> · Next: <a href="#17-critical-reflection-ii-theological">17 - Critical Reflection II - Theological</a>

How to use this note

For each critique: (a) who says it, (b) the argument, (c) Rahner's reply or the best available Rahnerian reply, (d) a verdict. Examiners reward a defended verdict far more than a list of names.

Critique 1 — "This is not Thomism" (Gilson, Fabro, Van Steenberghen)

(a) Who. Étienne Gilson and the historical-Thomist school; Cornelio Fabro, La svolta antropologica di Karl Rahner (1974, "Rahner's anthropological turn"); Fernand Van Steenberghen.

(b) The argument. - Rahner reads Aquinas through Maréchal's Kantian lens, and Maréchal through Heidegger. The result is a modern transcendental subject dressed in Thomist vocabulary. - For Aquinas, the intellect is receptive: it abstracts from what is given. For Rahner, the intellect is productive: its own a priori dynamism (Vorgriff) constitutes the horizon within which objects appear. That is Kant's Copernican revolution, not Aquinas's realism. - Fabro presses further: Rahner's "anthropological turn" inverts the order of theology. Aquinas begins from God (Prima Pars); Rahner begins from the human subject and reaches God as the term of the subject's dynamism. This is not development but reversal.

(c) Reply. - Rahner does not claim to be doing historical exegesis of Aquinas. Geist in Welt is an avowedly systematic retrieval — asking what Aquinas's doctrine of the conversio ad phantasma implies once the modern question of the subject has been raised and cannot be un-raised. - The receptive/productive contrast is overdrawn: Aquinas's intellectus agens is already an a priori active principle. Rahner claims to be radicalising it, not replacing it. - Beginning from the subject is a pedagogical and apologetic order (ordo inveniendi), not an ontological one (ordo essendi). God remains first in being.

(d) Verdict. The historical criticism is largely correct and largely beside the point. Rahner is not offering a commentary; he is offering a philosophy. The question is not "is this Aquinas?" but "is it true?" — and on that Fabro's deeper charge (the inversion of theological order) still needs answering, and merges into Balthasar's → 17 - Critical Reflection II - Theological.

Critique 2 — The Vorgriff does not prove God (the Kantian objection)

(b) The argument. - Rahner infers from a dynamism of the intellect toward the unlimited to an actually existing infinite term. But Kant's Transcendental Dialectic is precisely a demonstration that reason's unavoidable drive toward the unconditioned licenses no inference to a real unconditioned object. The idea of the infinite is a regulative ideal, not a proof of an infinite being. - Alternative explanations are available: the "openness" may be a formal feature of negation (the mind's ability to say "not this") rather than a positive orientation to Being; or, deflationarily, a byproduct of linguistic recursion. - The move looks like a disguised ontological argument: from the structure of thought to the existence of its object.

(c) Reply. - The Vorgriff is not an inference from a concept to an existent; it is a claim that the actual functioning of judgement presupposes a real horizon. A horizon that was merely nothing could not function as a measure by which the finite is recognised as finite. - Rahner's is a retorsion argument: to deny the Vorgriff one must make a judgement, and judgement operates by it. Denial is performatively self-defeating.

(d) Verdict. The retorsion argument has genuine force against a thoroughgoing denial, but the critic can grant the transcendental horizon while denying it is God — it might be the totality of the possible, or the merely formal notion of being. Rahner's identification of the horizon with the God of Christian revelation is a theological step, not a philosophical proof. Rahner sometimes acknowledges this; his readers frequently do not.

Critique 3 — Onto-theology (Heidegger, Marion)

(a) Who. Heidegger's charge that Western metaphysics conflates Being with a highest being; developed theologically by Jean-Luc Marion, Dieu sans l'être (God Without Being, 1982).

(b) The argument. - If God is the term of the Vorgriff auf esse, then God is thought within the horizon of being — the horizon has become the condition under which God may appear. God is measured by our categories. - Marion: God must be thought as gift and love before being; the icon (which gives itself and cannot be mastered) rather than the idol (which reflects the gaze back and stops at what the seer can hold). Rahner's horizon is arguably an idol of the concept.

(c) Reply. - Rahner's God is absolute mystery (Geheimnis) — explicitly incomprehensible, never an object, never grasped, and remaining incomprehensible even in the beatific vision (06 - God's Self-Communication and Quasi-Formal Causality). This is precisely a refusal of onto-theology. - The Vorgriff terminates in a horizon that cannot be objectified. That is much closer to Marion's "saturated phenomenon" than Marion allows.

(d) Verdict. Rahner is a weaker case of onto-theology than Marion suggests, because his doctrine of incomprehensibility does the very work Marion demands. But the vocabulary of esse keeps the charge alive rhetorically. A good essay notes that the two are closer than the polemic implies.

Critique 4 — The abstract, unhistorical subject (Metz)

(a) Who. Johann Baptist Metz — Rahner's own doctoral student and closest friendly critic. Faith in History and Society (1977); Theology of the World (1968).

(b) The argument. - The transcendental subject is timeless, classless, genderless, bodiless — an idealist construct. Real human beings are situated in histories of oppression and privilege. - Transcendental theology cannot generate a critique of society. It reconciles the subject with a mystery already present, rather than mobilising resistance against present suffering. - Metz proposes instead: memoria passionis, mortis et resurrectionis Jesu Christi — the "dangerous memory" of the suffering of Jesus and of history's victims, which interrupts rather than integrates. Theology must be narrative, practical and political, not transcendental. - The sharpest form: transcendental theology cannot say why the suffering of the dead matters, because it has no category for unredeemed history.

(c) Reply. - Rahner accepted much of this. He affirmed a "mystical-political" understanding of Christianity, insisted transcendentality is always mediated categorically and historically (04 - The Transcendental Method in Theology), and defended Gutiérrez publicly in 1984. - He denied that his method entailed political quietism: the same self-communication that grounds prayer grounds the imperative of love of neighbour, which is unavoidably social.

(d) Verdict. The strongest critique in this note. Rahner's concession is genuine but comes as a supplement, not a reconstruction. The system's centre of gravity remains the individual subject before mystery. Metz's point is not that Rahner is wrong but that he is insufficient — and that judgement has been broadly accepted, including in India → 18 - Rahner in the Indian Context.

Critique 5 — The linguistic turn (Kerr, Wittgenstein)

(a) Who. Fergus Kerr OP, Theology after Wittgenstein (1986); also Immortal Longings (1997).

(b) The argument. - Rahner's anthropology posits a self prior to language and community — a subject that has a pre-linguistic transcendental experience which language then expresses. - Wittgenstein's private-language argument denies this. There is no meaning, and no self-relation, prior to public linguistic practice. The self is constituted in language and community, not before them. - Kerr detects a residual Cartesianism: an inner theatre of consciousness, the "mentalist" picture, dressed in Thomist clothes.

(c) Reply. - Rahner's subject is never a disembodied subject: he insists relentlessly on spirit in world, on the conversio ad phantasma, on the body as real symbol. This is anti-Cartesian at the root (02 - Philosophical Foundations I - Spirit in the World). - Transcendental experience is not a private inner episode. It is the unthematic structure of ordinary, public, embodied acts — of loving, forgiving, hoping. It has no content that could be a "private object."

(d) Verdict. Kerr's diagnosis is partly deflected, because Rahner's spirit-in-world doctrine anticipates it. But Kerr's deeper point stands: Rahner's rhetoric of interiority, depth and the "innermost core" invites the mentalist reading, and his followers often took it. A theology of the symbol ought to have been more resistant to interiorism than Rahner's actually was.

Critique 6 — Experiential-expressivism (Lindbeck)

(a) Who. George Lindbeck, The Nature of Doctrine (1984), which names Rahner (with Schleiermacher and Tillich) as the paradigm of the experiential-expressive model of religion.

(b) The argument. - Lindbeck's typology: cognitive-propositional (doctrines as truth-claims), experiential-expressive (doctrines as symbolic expressions of a prior universal inner experience), cultural-linguistic (religions as language-games that form experience). - On the middle model, which he attributes to Rahner: all religions express one core experience differently — which makes doctrine secondary, and makes "anonymous Christianity" almost trivially true, since everyone has the core experience. - Lindbeck's counter: there is no religion-neutral experience. Learning a religion is learning a language; the language shapes what can be experienced at all. A Buddhist's nirvāṇa and a Christian's grace are not two expressions of one thing.

(c) Reply. - Lindbeck's classification is a caricature. Rahner never says doctrine is merely expression: his whole symbol ontology says expression is constitutive and that the categorical is necessary, not decorative (07 - The Theology of the Symbol - The Core Essay). - More sharply: Rahner's position anticipates the cultural-linguistic model. The claim that grace must take social, ritual, historical form (11 - Anonymous Christians and the Non-Christian Religions, Thesis 2) is precisely a claim that religions form the way grace is lived. - Lindbeck's model has its own difficulty: if religions are incommensurable language-games, interreligious understanding and God's universal salvific will both become hard to state.

(d) Verdict. Lindbeck's typology misplaces Rahner, and the symbol ontology is Rahner's best defence — a real symbol is not the expression of a prior reality but the reality's own self-realisation. Still, the challenge to identify the transcendental experience without borrowing Christian vocabulary remains unmet, and may be unmeetable in principle.

Critique 7 — Style and unfalsifiability

(b) The argument. - The prose is famously impenetrable: chains of subordinate clauses, coined compounds, qualifications on qualifications. For a theology whose stated aim is mystagogy, this is not a trivial defect — it is a performative contradiction. - Systematically: several central terms (quasi-formal causality, unthematic experience, anonymous Christianity) are constructed so that no possible observation counts against them. A theory immune to counter-evidence buys its safety at the price of content.

(c) Reply. Transcendental claims are by nature not empirically falsifiable — they state conditions of possibility, and are tested by retorsion (can you coherently deny them while doing what you are doing?), not by observation. On style: Rahner wrote prayers, hymns and homilies in an entirely different register, so the difficulty is a feature of the genre, not of the man.

(d) Verdict. The style criticism is fair and worth stating plainly. The unfalsifiability criticism is partly answered by the nature of transcendental argument, but it correctly identifies why Rahner's system is so hard to argue with — and why arguments about him often become arguments about the method rather than about the claims.

Critique 1 — Balthasar: the anthropological reduction

(a) Who. Hans Urs von Balthasar — fellow Jesuit (until 1950), fellow student of Erich Przywara, and Rahner's great counter-pole. Cordula oder der Ernstfall (1966; ET The Moment of Christian Witness); The Theology of Karl Barth; Love Alone is Credible; the Herrlichkeit trilogy.

(b) The argument. 1. Method. Rahner begins from the human subject's a priori structure. Revelation is then received into a framework it did not create, and is measured by categories established in advance. Balthasar's alternative: begin from the form (Gestalt) of revelation, which is self-authenticating in its beauty, as a masterpiece imposes itself and teaches the viewer how to look. 2. The blunting of the Ernstfall. If everyone is already anonymously graced, martyrdom — the "serious case," the moment of witness unto blood — becomes a vivid illustration of a truth that would have held anyway. Cordula, the legendary companion of Ursula who at first fled and then returned to die, is Balthasar's emblem: the point where Christianity is either everything or nothing. 3. The Cross. Rahner's Cross manifests the definitive acceptance of God's self-gift. Balthasar's Cross achieves something — the Son bears godforsakenness, descends into hell, enters the abyss of the Father's absence. Rahner's soteriology, on this reading, has no room for Holy Saturday. 4. Aesthetics vs. transcendence. Beauty, glory, form and drama are near-absent from Rahner; for Balthasar they are the very mode in which God is known.

(c) Reply. - Rahner's a priori is formal and empty: it specifies that revelation must be receivable, not what it will contain. He explicitly denies deducing Christ (10 - Christology - Self-Communication and Evolution). - Universality of the offer does not lessen the cost of an explicit response. The martyr is where the anonymous becomes fully explicit and fully costly. - Rahner's reticence about satisfaction theories is a considered position, not an omission: he denies that the Cross changes God's disposition, holding that God's saving will is prior and the Cross is its real symbol in history.

(d) Verdict. The most rhetorically powerful critique, and partly unfair. Balthasar's charge that Rahner deduces Christ from anthropology is not sustainable from Rahner's texts. But (2) and (3) land: Rahner's system genuinely finds it hard to articulate the urgency and scandal of the Gospel, and his theology of the Cross is thin. The mature judgement — now widely shared — is that the two are complementary correctives: Rahner secures the universality of grace, Balthasar the particularity of the form. A theology with only one of them is defective.

Critique 2 — Ratzinger: the loss of the concrete

(a) Who. Joseph Ratzinger / Benedict XVI, Principles of Catholic Theology; Introduction to Christianity; interviews from the 1980s onward.

(b) The argument. - Transcendental method abstracts from the concrete, historical, liturgical and biblical form of revelation. It substitutes a philosophy of the subject for the memory of Israel and the Church. - Anonymous Christianity relativises the historical event of Jesus of Nazareth in favour of a universal structure. - Post-conciliar Rahnerianism (as distinct from Rahner) fed a theology impatient with tradition, cut loose from the liturgy and the Fathers.

(c) Reply. - Rahner insists relentlessly that transcendental revelation is never available apart from categorical mediation — Scripture, dogma, sacrament, Church (04 - The Transcendental Method in Theology). - His symbol ontology makes the concrete necessary, not optional: what is not expressed is not fully real (07 - The Theology of the Symbol - The Core Essay).

(d) Verdict. The criticism of Rahner's followers is more accurate than the criticism of Rahner. But Ratzinger identifies a real structural risk: a method whose starting point is the subject will, in weaker hands, treat the particular as illustration. Note also that Ratzinger's own Introduction to Christianity is closer to Rahner on universal grace than the later polemics suggest.

Critique 3 — Trinity: modalism and the collapse of divine freedom

(a) Who. Jürgen Moltmann (The Trinity and the Kingdom); Walter Kasper (The God of Jesus Christ); Paul Molnar (Divine Freedom and the Doctrine of the Immanent Trinity); Bruce Marshall.

(b) The argument. 1. Modalism. "Three distinct manners of subsisting" (Subsistenzweisen) with one divine consciousness and one divine will is, Moltmann charges, Idealist modalism — one absolute subject in three modes. Genuine interpersonal relations (the Son prays to the Father) become unintelligible. 2. The "vice versa." If the immanent Trinity simply is the economic Trinity, God's inner life appears to require the economy. Creation and Incarnation become necessary to God's self-constitution, and grace ceases to be gift. 3. Molnar's precise worry: the axiom, taken ontologically, removes the ground on which one could say God would still be triune love had there been no world.

(c) Reply. - Subsistenzweise names a real distinction (unlike modalism's apparent one), and Rahner retains "person" as the binding dogmatic term, offering his phrase as explanation, not replacement. - On (2): Rahner says the immanent Trinity is the economic, not that it is exhausted by it. The processions are necessary; the economy is free. Identity of reality, not of mode.

(d) Verdict. On modalism, Rahner is defensible but exposed — one consciousness and one will is the classical Latin position, so Moltmann's charge would convict Augustine and Aquinas too; the real dispute is West vs. East, not Rahner vs. orthodoxy. On the "vice versa," the ambiguity is real and unresolved in Rahner's own texts. Best position for an essay: read the axiom epistemologically (we have no access to an immanent Trinity behind the economy) rather than ontologically (there is nothing more to God than the economy). → 09 - Rahner's Rule - The Grundaxiom of the Trinity

Critique 4 — Sacraments: manifestation or conferral?

(a) Who. Colman O'Neill OP, Meeting Christ in the Sacraments; Thomist sacramental theologians generally.

(b) The argument. - Trent: the sacraments contain and confer the grace they signify (DS 1606). This is real efficient instrumental causality. - On Rahner's account grace is universally offered before the sacrament (05 - The Supernatural Existential - Nature and Grace); the sacrament makes it tangible, ecclesial, definitive. But "making tangible" is manifestation, not conferral. - Consequence: ex opere operato is weakened, and the sacraments become the Church's celebration of a grace already possessed rather than the moment of its giving.

(c) Reply. - On Rahner's ontology, what is not expressed is not fully real. Historical, bodily, ecclesial embodiment is therefore not a decoration on an already-complete grace; it completes it. The sacrament effects a genuinely new state of affairs. - Ex opere operato is fully preserved and even strengthened: the guarantee attaches to the Church's self-realisation, which does not depend on the minister's holiness.

(d) Verdict. The reply is strong if you grant Thesis 1 of the symbol essay and weak if you do not. This is the clearest case in the whole file where a criticism of Rahner is really a criticism of his ontology. Say that in an essay and you will be marking the actual fault line.

Critique 5 — Grace: has gratuity survived?

(a) Who. Neo-Thomists; more recently Lawrence Feingold, The Natural Desire to See God according to St Thomas Aquinas and His Interpreters; Steven Long.

(b) The argument. A gift given to absolutely everyone, always, as a constitutive feature of concrete existence, is functionally indistinguishable from a property of nature. Rahner keeps the word "supernatural" while emptying its content. Humani Generis (1950) warned against exactly this.

(c) Reply. Gratuity is located in the ground of the gift (God's free will) and not in its rarity. A gift given to all is not thereby owed to any. And the Restbegriff of pure nature is retained precisely to mark the difference (05 - The Supernatural Existential - Nature and Grace).

(d) Verdict. Rahner's reply is coherent. But the critic can restate the worry as an epistemic one: if the existential is universal and unthematic, no experience could distinguish a world with it from a world without it — so the doctrine does no work beyond marking a conceptual boundary. That version is harder to answer.

Critique 6 — Feminist critiques

(a) Who. Anne Carr (Transforming Grace — a sympathetic Rahnerian feminist); Elizabeth Johnson (She Who Is — uses Rahner critically); Mary Ann Hinsdale; Susan Ross on sacramentality.

(b) The argument. - The "transcendental subject" is presented as universal but is in practice the generic male European subject; women's specific experience — of embodiment, of exclusion from orders and from theological speech — vanishes into the abstraction. - Rahner's magnificent theology of the body as real symbol is never brought to bear on sexual difference, on women's bodies, or on the Church's treatment of them. - His ecclesiology never seriously interrogates the exclusion of women from ordained ministry — a striking silence for a theologian so willing to question structures.

(c) Reply / resources within Rahner. - The symbol ontology is a gift to feminist theology: if the body is the person's real symbol, then bodily particularity is theologically weighty and the abuse of bodies is a theological outrage. Johnson and Ross use exactly this. - His doctrine of God as absolute incomprehensible mystery undercuts every literalised masculine God-image; Johnson builds She Who Is partly on this Rahnerian ground.

(d) Verdict. Correct as a critique of omission, not of doctrine. The most productive feminist verdict is Carr's: Rahner supplies tools he did not use. That is a real criticism and also a real endorsement.

Critique 7 — Liberation and political critiques

(a) Who. Metz (see 16 - Critical Reflection I - Philosophical); Gustavo Gutiérrez; Jon Sobrino; Ignacio Ellacuría; in Asia, Aloysius Pieris.

(b) The argument. - Sin is treated primarily as the individual's self-closure; structural sin — caste, class, colonial dispossession — is under-theorised. - The "preferential option for the poor" cannot be derived from a transcendental analysis of the subject; it comes from the concrete history of Israel, the ministry of Jesus, and the cry of the oppressed. - Pieris sharpens it for Asia: Rahner's theology is a theology of religiousness without a theology of poverty. Asia's reality is both, and the second is where the Gospel bites.

(c) Reply. Rahner endorsed political theology in his later work, spoke of the unity of love of God and love of neighbour as an identity rather than a parallel, and defended Gutiérrez to Rome in 1984.

(d) Verdict. Fair. The concession is real but architectural change never followed. → 18 - Rahner in the Indian Context

Critique 8 — The symbol ontology itself (the criticism that matters most for this unit)

(b) The arguments, in order of seriousness.

  1. The Hegelian shadow. "Being must express itself in order to attain its own nature" resembles Hegel's Entäußerung — Spirit externalising itself to become itself. If self-expression is necessary to being as such, and creation is God's self-expression, is creation free? Does God need a world? Rahner's distinction: the inner-Trinitarian utterance is necessary and complete; the utterance ad extra is free — the Father's utterance of the Word is not lacking anything that the world supplies. Assessment: the distinction is correct as far as it goes, but Rahner grounds both in one law of being, and the law is stated without qualification. He needed a more careful account of analogy at exactly this point. This is the most serious philosophical objection to the essay.

  2. Univocity worries. The essay moves in a few pages from smiles to the immanent Trinity. Is "symbol" being used analogically or univocally? Rahner would say analogically; the text does not always show its working. In an exam, note that he owes the reader an explicit analogia entis at this juncture.

  3. No theology of the false symbol. The ontology explains fullness of expression superbly and failure only privatively. But the body can conceal as well as reveal; the Church can misrepresent Christ; the sacrament can be received in bad faith. A hypocrite's smile is a real bodily expression of something other than joy. Rahner has no developed account of the lying symbol, and this is arguably the largest structural gap in his system → 12 - Ecclesiology and Sacramental Theology. Constructive reply: the missing doctrine can be built from Rahner's own principles — falsity is possible only for free spirit, the liar fails to attain himself, and symbolic causality run in reverse yields the anti-sacrament. See 24 - The Theology of the False Symbol.

  4. Where is the Cross in the symbol ontology? Self-expression sounds like plenitude overflowing. But the supreme Christian symbol is a broken body. Balthasar's point resurfaces here in its sharpest ontological form: does a metaphysics of expression have room for kenosis — for expression as self-emptying rather than self-attainment? A Rahnerian reply worth developing: the pierced heart is the symbol essay's own culminating case, and self-expression there is self-emptying — the two coincide precisely in the crucified. That reply is available but Rahner does not develop it. It is developed in 24 - The Theology of the False Symbol §9, where it also turns out to require an amendment to Thesis 1: for a being whose nature is self-gift, expressing itself and expending itself are one act.

  5. Defence worth citing. Stephen M. Fields SJ, Being as Symbol: On the Origins and Development of Karl Rahner's Metaphysics (Georgetown, 2000), argues the symbol ontology has genuine roots in Aquinas and Bonaventure rather than in German Idealism, and that the necessity objection rests on a misreading. Cite Fields when defending Rahner.

(d) Verdict. Objections 1 and 3 are serious and only partly answerable. Objection 4 points to a development Rahner should have made and did not. The ontology is fertile, elegant and genuinely illuminating — and it is stated with less metaphysical care than a claim of that scope requires.

Why this note exists

Rahner is read in Indian seminaries because his theology of universal grace made Nostra Aetate thinkable, and because his three-epoch thesis authorises Asian theology to be Asian. He is criticised in Indian seminaries because his method starts from a subject that looks nothing like the subject of Indian reality. Hold both.

1. What Rahner gives Indian theology

Rahnerian resource Indian use
Universal supernatural existential — grace offered to every human being always Theological ground for taking the faith of the Hindu, Muslim, Sikh, Jain, Buddhist and tribal neighbour seriously as graced, not merely as human striving → 05 - The Supernatural Existential - Nature and Grace
Religions as possibly lawful ways (Thesis 2) Permits a positive theology of sanātana dharma, bhakti, Sufi devotion, tribal religion — not merely a theology of tolerated error → 11 - Anonymous Christians and the Non-Christian Religions

The symbol ontology and Indian religious practice

Consider the deep Indian instinct that the sacred is not represented but present — in darśana (the mutual seeing between devotee and deity), in prasāda (food that has been received back from the deity), in the touch of the elder's feet, in the rangoli drawn at the threshold, in namaskāra where the joined hands are the reverence and not a report of it. This is much closer to Rahner's Realsymbol than to the modern Western "merely symbolic." Apologetic payoff: to a student formed by that instinct, the Catholic doctrine of the Eucharist and of sacramental efficacy is not strange but obvious. The estrangement is a modern Western one, and Rahner's essay is written to cure it. Required caution: the analogy is at the level of a sensibility about symbol, not an identity of doctrine. Do not flatten distinct theologies of presence into one another; that would repeat the very error the theology-of-religions critics identify.

2. What Indian theologians say against him

Raimon Panikkar — the reversibility test

Panikkar's celebrated riposte: a Christian would hardly be pleased to be told he is an "anonymous Hindu." If the label is unacceptable in reverse, it is not a genuine act of recognition but a classification. Panikkar's own alternative is cosmotheandric: reality is irreducibly divine-human-cosmic, and Christ is the mystery at work in all traditions rather than the Christian franchise extended to them (The Unknown Christ of Hinduism). Assessment: the reversibility test is decisive against the term. It is not decisive against the underlying claim about universal grace, which is now magisterial teaching.

Michael Amaladoss SJ — beyond inclusivism

Rahner's inclusivism still places Christianity at the centre and reads every other tradition on a Christian map. Amaladoss argues for a theology of pluralism in principle — many real ways within one divine plan — and for dialogue in which the other is a subject who may correct the Christian, not only an object interpreted by the Christian. Assessment: this is inclusivism's genuine limit. Note also the magisterial constraint: Dominus Iesus (2000) presses back hard on pluralism in principle. State the tension honestly rather than resolving it too quickly.

Aloysius Pieris SJ — religiousness without poverty

Pieris's structural critique: Asia is defined by two realities — overwhelming religiousness and overwhelming poverty. European theologies of religion (Rahner's included) address the first and ignore the second. An Asian theology must be baptised in the Jordan of Asian religion and on the cross of Asian poverty. Assessment: the sharpest Asian criticism. It is Metz's political critique with Asian specificity, and it identifies a genuine absence, not a misunderstanding.

George M. Soares-Prabhu SJ — the methodological objection

Soares-Prabhu's insistence: theology in India must begin from the concrete, conflictual, oppressive Indian reality and from the Bible read within it, not from a transcendental analysis of the human subject as such. The starting point of the Nazareth manifesto (Lk 4:18–19) is the poor, not the Vorgriff. A theology whose first move is the a priori structure of consciousness will always arrive at social sin too late and too abstractly. Assessment: this is a methodological disagreement, not a doctrinal one. Rahner and Soares-Prabhu can agree entirely about universal grace and disagree entirely about where theology should start — and where you start determines what you can see.

The caste question

The most concrete Indian test. Rahner's theology of the body as real symbol of the person ought to yield a devastating critique of untouchability: to defile a body is to defile a person; to segregate bodies at the Eucharist is to falsify the Church's own real symbol. Rahner never made this argument — he had no occasion to. Indian theology can and should make it for him. This is the most fruitful constructive use of Rahner available to an Indian BTh student.

A constructive Rahnerian argument against caste discrimination in the Church

  1. The body is the person's Realsymbol: what is done to the body is done to the person (08 - Realsymbol Applied - Trinity, Christ, Church, Sacraments, Body).
  2. The Church is the Realsymbol of Christ; the Eucharist is the Church's self-realisation (12 - Ecclesiology and Sacramental Theology).
  3. Therefore a Eucharist celebrated with segregated seating, separate cemeteries, or refusal of the common cup is a falsified symbol — the Church expressing something other than Christ, and so failing to be what it symbolises.
  4. This is not a moral supplement to sacramental theology. It is a sacramental defect — precisely the argument Paul makes at 1 Cor 11:17–29, where failing to "discern the body" makes the assembly's meal not the Lord's Supper at all.

Note that step 3 requires the theology of the false symbol that Rahner never wrote → 17 - Critical Reflection II - Theological.

That doctrine is now supplied, and the argument is completed to five steps — including the rule of competing symbols (when a verbal symbol and a bodily symbol contradict, the bodily one is believed) — in 24 - The Theology of the False Symbol §7.

Exam thesis worth defending

"Rahner supplies Indian theology with a grammar but not a content. His ontology of the symbol and his theology of universal grace are among the best tools available for taking Indian religious experience and Indian bodies with theological seriousness. But his transcendental starting point systematically arrives late at the two facts that most define Indian reality — poverty and caste. The right Indian use of Rahner is therefore to take his ontology and start it somewhere else: not from the a priori structure of the subject, but from the crucified body of the poor, which is a real symbol of Christ in a sense Rahner asserted and never developed."

A — Core symbol theology

Rahner's Thesis 1 on symbol :: All beings are by their very nature symbolic, because they necessarily express themselves in order to attain their own nature.

Rahner's Thesis 2 on symbol :: The symbol strictly speaking is the self-realisation of a being in the other, which is constitutive of its essence.

Realsymbol :: A real symbol — the reality's own self-realisation in another, in which the reality is genuinely present; it could not be otherwise.

Vertretungssymbol :: An arbitrary or representative sign, assigned by convention, in which the reality is absent; it could have been any other sign.

Two examples of a Realsymbol :: A smile as the realisation of joy; the humanity of Jesus as the self-utterance of the Logos.

Two examples of a Vertretungssymbol :: A traffic light; the letters d-o-g standing for a dog.

The four elements of Rahner's full definition of symbol (TI IV, 251) :: (1) constituted by the symbolised, (2) as an inner moment of itself, (3) revealing and proclaiming it, (4) being full of it as its concrete form of existence.

Why is plurality in God a perfection, not a defect? :: Because in God self-expression (the utterance of the Word) is the very mode of perfect self-possession — God is not first solitary and then expressive.

The occasion of "The Theology of the Symbol" :: A request to justify devotion to the Sacred Heart against the charge that a bodily organ is "only a symbol."

Urwort :: A primordial word (heart, light, bread, water, birth, death) which names a total human reality rather than labelling a part.

B — Philosophical foundations

Vorgriff auf esse :: The unthematic pre-apprehension of Being as such which accompanies every judgement and makes recognition of the finite as finite possible.

Conversio ad phantasma :: Aquinas's doctrine (ST I q.84 a.7) that the human intellect knows only by turning to the sense image; for Rahner it proves that human knowing is irreducibly incarnate.

The horizon illustration :: You never look at the horizon, yet nothing appears as a bounded object without it — so God is the unobjectifiable horizon of all knowing, not an object within it.

Potentia oboedientialis in Rahner :: Not merely a passive non-repugnance but a positive, constitutive openness to a possible divine word — while the fulfilment remains wholly unowed.

The three theses of Hörer des Wortes :: (1) The human is spirit; (2) the human is finite and historical; (3) therefore the human must attend to history for a possible word of God.

"Transcendental" (technical sense) :: Concerning the a priori conditions of possibility of experience or knowledge — not "mystical" or "otherworldly."

Transcendental vs categorical revelation :: Transcendental = God's universal unthematic self-offer in the depths of every person; categorical = its concrete historical objectification (Sinai, Jesus, Scripture, dogma). Neither exists without the other.

C — Grace

Übernatürliches Existential :: The supernatural existential — a permanent, universal, gratuitously given modification of concrete human existence orienting every person to God's self-communication.

How does the supernatural existential solve the nature/grace dilemma? :: Grace is universal (so relevant, not extrinsic) yet unowed (so gratuitous) — gratuity lies in the freedom of the giver, not in the rarity of the gift.

Restbegriff :: A "remainder concept" — Rahner's status for natura pura: a theoretical construct obtained by subtracting grace in thought, naming a possibility that never existed in reality.

Selbstmitteilung Gottes :: God's self-communication — in grace God gives not merely something but Godself; gift and Giver are identical.

Created vs uncreated grace, and Rahner's ordering :: Uncreated grace = God's own self, the indwelling Trinity; created grace = the resulting supernatural quality in the soul. Rahner makes uncreated grace primary and created grace its consequence.

Quasi-formal causality :: God communicates Godself as an inner constitutive principle of the graced person (like a formal cause) without becoming a component part of the creature and without ceasing to be free and transcendent.

Why not efficient causality for grace? :: Because an efficient cause produces an effect distinct from itself — the gift would then be a creature, not God.

Why not strict formal causality? :: Because God would become an intrinsic constituent of the creature — pantheism.

The three modalities of God's one self-communication :: Grace (offered to all), Incarnation (given absolutely to one), Glory (unveiled).

D — Trinity and Christology

Rahner's Rule (Grundaxiom) :: "The economic Trinity is the immanent Trinity, and the immanent Trinity is the economic Trinity."

Which half of Rahner's Rule is disputed, and why? :: The "vice versa" (immanent → economic) — critics argue it makes creation necessary to God and destroys divine freedom.

Subsistenzweise :: "Distinct manner of subsisting" — Rahner's preferred alternative to "person," since modern "person" implies a separate centre of consciousness and would suggest tritheism.

Rahner's diagnosis of Western Trinitarian theology :: Christians are practically "mere monotheists"; if the Trinity were dropped, most religious literature would remain unchanged.

Rahner's axiom on nearness to God and creaturely autonomy :: They vary in direct, not inverse, proportion — the closer to God, the more genuinely itself and free the creature is.

The humanity of Jesus, in one phrase :: The Realsymbol of the Logos — what comes to be when God utters himself into the non-divine.

Aktive Selbsttranszendenz :: Active self-transcendence — a creature genuinely surpassing itself by a power given by God; the engine of Rahner's evolutionary Christology.

The three acts of "searching Christology" :: Absolute love of neighbour; readiness for death; hope in the future.

Christology and anthropology, Rahner's formula :: "Christology is self-transcending anthropology; anthropology is deficient Christology."

E — Church, sacraments, last things

Ursakrament vs Grundsakrament :: Ursakrament = Christ, the primordial sacrament of God; Grundsakrament = the Church, the basic sacrament derived from him. (Usage varies between authors — state yours.)

Sacramenta significando causant, on Rahner's account :: Signifying and causing are one act: the sacrament is the Church's self-realisation, and a real symbol is the reality's own mode of presence.

The main objection to Rahner's sacramental theology :: If grace is already universally offered, the sacrament seems to manifest rather than confer — falling short of Trent's "contain and confer" (DS 1606). Chief critic: Colman O'Neill OP.

Rahner's reply on sacramental conferral :: On his ontology what is not expressed is not fully real; the sacrament therefore genuinely completes grace by making it historical, ecclesial and definitive.

Diasporakirche :: The Church of the diaspora — a minority Church in a pluralist world, held by personal conviction rather than social inheritance.

The "mystic of the future" quotation :: "The devout Christian of the future will either be a 'mystic', one who has 'experienced' something, or he will cease to be anything at all." (TI VII, 15)

Death as act and passion :: Death is both something that happens to us (passion, the wage of sin) and our final total self-disposal in freedom (act). Both must be held.

The pancosmic thesis :: At death the soul becomes not acosmic but pancosmic — entering a deeper open relation to the material universe as a whole rather than to one body. Speculative; Rahner presents it as such.

The rule for eschatological assertions :: They are not advance reportage of future events but the transposition of present Christian experience of salvation into the future; eschatology is Christology in the future tense.

Eschatology vs apocalyptic :: Eschatology extrapolates from present grace in Christ; apocalyptic claims direct knowledge of future events read backwards from heaven.

F — Theology of religions

The four theses on non-Christian religions :: (1) Christianity is the absolute religion; (2) a non-Christian religion may be a lawful religion containing supernatural grace until the Gospel genuinely confronts it; (3) the non-Christian may be an anonymous Christian; (4) the Church is the vanguard, the explicit expression of a grace at work beyond its borders.

Definition of "anonymous Christian" :: One who, without explicit knowledge of Christ, has accepted God's self-communication in freely accepting their own existence, and is therefore justified by the grace of Christ.

Two things "anonymous Christian" does NOT mean :: (1) That the person secretly holds Christian beliefs; (2) that it is a label to be used in addressing the other — Rahner said one should not tell a Buddhist he is one.

Why does Rahner argue from human sociality to the lawfulness of religions? :: Because grace never arrives as private interior lightning; a being that is spirit-in-world receives grace only in social, ritual, historical form — so grace must be able to take form within actual religions.

Panikkar's reversibility objection :: A Christian would not be pleased to be called an "anonymous Hindu" — so the term classifies rather than recognises.

Balthasar's Cordula objection :: If everyone is anonymously graced, martyrdom — the Ernstfall — becomes a mere illustration; Christianity loses its edge.

Lindbeck's objection :: Rahner is "experiential-expressive" — he posits a universal core experience which religions merely express, whereas religions are cultural-linguistic systems that form experience.

Rahner's answer to "then why mission?" :: Mission gives grace its proper historical, communal, sacramental body and brings it to explicit, certain, fuller expression — waking is fuller than sleeping, though both are real life.

G — Critique and context

Fabro's charge :: La svolta antropologica — Rahner's "anthropological turn" inverts the theological order, starting from the subject rather than from God, and is not genuine Thomism.

Marion's charge :: Onto-theology — God thought within the horizon of being becomes an idol; God must be thought as gift and love before being.

Rahner's best answer to Marion :: His doctrine of God as absolute incomprehensible mystery, never objectifiable, remaining incomprehensible even in the beatific vision.

Metz's charge :: The transcendental subject is abstract and unhistorical; theology must be narrative, practical and political, grounded in the memoria passionis — the dangerous memory of suffering.

Kerr's charge :: Rahner retains a pre-linguistic, quasi-Cartesian self; after Wittgenstein there is no self-relation prior to public language and community.

Molnar's charge :: The "vice versa" of Rahner's Rule endangers divine freedom — one could no longer say God would be triune love had there been no world.

The three unanswered problems in the symbol ontology :: (1) the Hegelian shadow — is self-expression necessary, and is creation then free? (2) no theology of the false or lying symbol; (3) expression as plenitude sits awkwardly with the Cross as kenosis.

Pieris's "two realities" of Asia :: Overwhelming religiousness and overwhelming poverty — European theologies of religion address the first and ignore the second.

Soares-Prabhu's methodological objection :: Indian theology must begin from the concrete oppressive Indian reality and the Bible read within it, not from the transcendental structure of the subject.

Rahner's three epochs of Church history :: (1) Jewish Christianity, ending at the Council of Jerusalem; (2) Hellenistic-European Christianity, ending at Vatican II; (3) the World Church, beginning 1962.

Four conciliar texts Rahner influenced :: Lumen Gentium 1 and 16 (Church as sacrament; salvation of those who have not heard); Dei Verbum 2 (revelation as self-communication, one source); Gaudium et Spes 22; Nostra Aetate 2.

Rahner's role at Vatican II, precisely stated :: Peritus advising Cardinal Franz König — influence on concepts and arguments, not authorship of any document.

H — The false symbol (constructive extension, note 24)

Why is a false symbol NOT a Vertretungssymbol? :: Because it deceives precisely because it is a real symbol — Judas's kiss betrays only because a kiss really is the self-realisation of affection.

The five modes of symbolic failure :: Deficient (finitude, not culpable) · concealing (may be truthful) · lying · idol · counter-symbol / anti-sacrament. The last three are culpable.

Why can only free spirit produce a false symbol? :: Because a stone's expression is automatic; only a being that freely disposes of itself can withhold, veil or falsify its own self-expression. Falsity is the price of self-possession.

What happens to the liar, on Rahner's own law? :: Since a being attains itself by expressing itself, one who expresses himself falsely fails to attain himself — the lie is self-mutilation before it is deception.

The parasitism thesis :: The false symbol borrows its power from the true; counterfeit presupposes currency. Falsity is therefore never original, never final, and always in principle exposable.

Marion's idol vs icon, used inside Rahner :: Icon = the gaze passes through toward the invisible; idol = the gaze stops and the symbol goes opaque. An idol is a symbol that has ceased to be transitive — e.g. clericalism.

The anti-sacrament :: Significando causant run in reverse — a rite that signifies division causes division, by the same law. Stated by Paul at 1 Cor 11:17–34: "it is not the Lord's supper that you eat."

The rule of competing symbols :: When a verbal symbol and a bodily symbol contradict, the bodily one is believed — because the body is the person's real symbol and human knowing runs through the phantasma.

Why is scandal in the Church ontologically worse than the same act elsewhere? :: Because a real symbol is the reality's own mode of presence, so what is done in the symbol is predicated of what it symbolises. "A few bad individuals" presupposes the Vertretungssymbol view of the Church.

The five criteria for discerning a false symbol :: Fruit (Mt 7:16) · coherence (word vs body) · cost (Balthasar's Ernstfall) · direction (icon or idol) · victim (who is excluded? — they read it accurately).

Why is the Cross not a false symbol, though it looks like one? :: Because there is no gap between expression and expressed: God's power is self-emptying love. The test of a true symbol is not resemblance but the coincidence of expression and reality.

How does the Cross close the kenosis gap? :: At the Cross self-expression and self-emptying are the same act — for a being whose nature is self-gift, expressing itself and expending itself coincide. This amends Thesis 1's acquisitive-sounding "in order to attain."

Four scriptural loci for the false symbol :: Judas's kiss (Lk 22:48) · the fig tree in leaf without fruit (Mk 11:12–25) · whitewashed tombs (Mt 23:27) · "it is not the Lord's supper" (1 Cor 11:20).

The obvious post-Rahnerian dialogue partner on symbol and its corruption :: Louis-Marie Chauvet, Symbol and Sacrament (1987).

THE MAN

Karl Rahner SJ, 1904–1984. German Jesuit. Freiburg (Heidegger's seminars) → Innsbruck → Munich → Münster. Geist in Welt rejected 1936, published 1939. Pre-censored by Rome 1962; peritus at Vatican II months later. 23 volumes of Theological Investigations. Died 30 March 1984, weeks after defending Gutiérrez.

THE ONE IDEA

A being attains itself by expressing itself in another. Self-expression is not loss of self but the road to self. God does this eternally (Word), historically (flesh), ecclesially (Church), sacramentally (sacraments), personally (body, heart).

THE CORE TEXT

"The Theology of the Symbol," TI IV, 221–252 (1959). Occasion: justifying devotion to the Sacred Heart. - Thesis 1: "All beings are by their very nature symbolic, because they necessarily express themselves in order to attain their own nature." - Thesis 2: "The symbol strictly speaking is the self-realisation of a being in the other, which is constitutive of its essence." - Realsymbol (reality present; could not be otherwise) vs Vertretungssymbol (conventional; reality absent). - Never say "only a symbol." A real symbol is the maximal presence of a reality outside itself.

THE FIVE APPLICATIONS

# Symbol of Symbolised
1 The Word / Logos → the Father
2 The humanity of Jesus → the Logos
3 The Church (Grundsakrament) → the risen Christ
4 The seven sacraments → the Church's own essence
5 The body, the heart → the person

PHILOSOPHY IN FOUR TERMS

  • Conversio ad phantasma — no human knowing without the sense image → knowing is incarnate.
  • Vorgriff auf esse — unthematic reach toward Being in every judgement; God as horizon, never object.
  • Potentia oboedientialis — positive constitutive openness to a possible word of God; fulfilment unowed.
  • Transcendental method — ask not what do we know but what must the knower be.

GRACE IN FOUR TERMS

  • Supernatural existential — universal + unowed modification of concrete existence orienting all to God. Solves nature/grace: gratuity lies in the freedom of the giver, not the rarity of the gift.
  • Natura pura survives only as a Restbegriff (remainder concept).
  • Self-communication — God gives Godself; uncreated grace primary, created grace its consequence.
  • Quasi-formal causality — God determines the graced person from within as a formal cause, without composition, without pantheism, freely.
  • Three modalities of one self-communication: grace (all) · Incarnation (one, irrevocably) · glory (unveiled).

TRINITY

Rahner's Rule: "The economic Trinity is the immanent Trinity, and the immanent Trinity is the economic Trinity." (The Trinity, 22) - Half (a) economic→immanent: accepted. - Half (b) "vice versa": disputed — endangers divine freedom (Molnar, Kasper). - Prefers "three distinct manners of subsisting" to "three persons"; one consciousness, one will → modalism charge (Moltmann). - Diagnosis: Christians are practically "mere monotheists."

CHRISTOLOGY

  • Humanity of Jesus = Realsymbol of the Logos → against every "God in disguise" picture.
  • Direct proportion axiom: nearness to God and creaturely autonomy vary directly → Jesus is the most free and most human.
  • Evolutionary Christology: active self-transcendence → hominisation → Incarnation as the world's unsurpassable openness fulfilled.
  • Transcendental Christology (a priori idea of an absolute saviour) then a posteriori identified with Jesus. Does not deduce Christ.
  • Searching Christology: absolute love of neighbour · readiness for death · hope in the future.

RELIGIONS — THE FOUR THESES

  1. Christianity is the absolute religion.
  2. A non-Christian religion may be lawful, containing supernatural grace, until the Gospel genuinely confronts it.
  3. The non-Christian may be an anonymous Christian.
  4. The Church is the vanguard, not the boundary; mission makes explicit, it does not import grace. → Fruit: LG 16, GS 22, NA 2. Pressure: Dominus Iesus (2000) on Thesis 2.

CHURCH, SACRAMENT, LAST THINGS

  • Ursakrament = Christ · Grundsakrament = Church · sacraments = the Church's self-realisation for the individual.
  • Significando causant: signifying and causing are one act.
  • Diaspora Church, "little flock" → hence: "The Christian of the future will be a mystic… or will cease to be anything at all."
  • Death = passion + definitive act; soul becomes pancosmic, not acosmic.
  • Eschatology = present grace in the future tense, not advance reportage. Distinguish from apocalyptic.

VATICAN II

Peritus to Cardinal König. Influence, not authorship. LG 1 & 16, DV 2, GS 22, NA 2, SC. Three epochs: Jewish Christianity (→ Council of Jerusalem) · Hellenistic-European (→ Vatican II) · World Church (1962–).

B. Sample exam questions

Short answer (5 marks each)

  1. Define Realsymbol and distinguish it from Vertretungssymbol with one example of each.
  2. What is the Vorgriff auf esse? Illustrate.
  3. State Rahner's Rule and identify which half is disputed.
  4. What is the supernatural existential, and what problem does it solve?
  5. Explain "quasi-formal causality" and say why the "quasi" is necessary.
  6. Distinguish Ursakrament from Grundsakrament.
  7. What does Rahner mean by an Urwort? Give three examples.
  8. Distinguish eschatology from apocalyptic in Rahner's usage.
  9. State Panikkar's objection to "anonymous Christianity."
  10. Name Rahner's three epochs of Church history and their two turning points.

Essay questions (15–20 marks)

  1. "All beings are by their very nature symbolic, because they necessarily express themselves in order to attain their own nature." Expound this thesis and assess its adequacy as the foundation of a theology of the sacraments.
  2. Trace the ontology of the real symbol through Trinity, Incarnation, Church and sacrament. Does the elegance of the scheme purchase its plausibility too cheaply?
  3. "Rahner's theology of the symbol makes the sacraments manifest grace rather than confer it." Evaluate this charge with reference to Trent.
  4. Discuss Rahner's Rule. Does the "vice versa" compromise the freedom of God?
  5. Is the anonymous Christian a generous inclusivism or a theological annexation? Argue with reference to Balthasar and Panikkar.
  6. Balthasar vs Rahner: is the disagreement one of method, of content, or of temperament? Which do you find more adequate, and why?
  7. "Rahner solved the nature/grace problem by making grace universal." Assess whether gratuity survives the solution.
  8. Examine the charge that Rahner's transcendental subject is abstract and unhistorical (Metz, Kerr, Lindbeck). How far can Rahner answer it?
  9. What use, and what limits, does Rahner's theology have for theology in India? Answer with reference to at least two Indian or Asian theologians.
  10. "The symbol ontology explains fullness of expression but has no account of the lying symbol." Test this claim against Rahner's ecclesiology.
  11. Assess Rahner's contribution to Vatican II and the accuracy of his claim that the Council inaugurates a "World Church."
  12. Is Rahner's theology of the Cross adequate? Answer with reference to his theology of death and to Balthasar's critique.
  13. "A lying symbol is not an arbitrary sign but a real symbol turned against its reader." Expound and defend this claim, and show what follows for the Church's response to scandal.
  14. Construct a theology of the anti-sacrament from Rahner's axiom that the sacraments cause by signifying. Test it against 1 Cor 11:17–34.
  15. "Rahner's ontology of the symbol is built to explain plenitude and therefore cannot explain betrayal." Assess.

Seminar / viva prompts

  • Preach a five-minute mystagogical homily on an ordinary experience, following the five-step shape in 14 - Mystagogy, Ignatian Roots and Spirituality.
  • Construct the Rahnerian argument against caste segregation at the Eucharist and identify the point at which it needs a doctrine Rahner never wrote.
  • Take one popular devotion in your region and analyse it with the Realsymbol / Vertretungssymbol distinction.

The finding worth an essay

The false symbol is now the most connected concept in the unit — ahead of the symbol ontology itself, and ahead of Rahner.

It entered at 12 edges and rose to 15 once Chauvet was checked in. That is a structural result, not a compliment to note 24. A doctrine that connects to fifteen existing concepts was never an optional extra: the graph was load-bearing around a hole. Ecclesiology, sacramental theology, the caste argument, the Cross, Balthasar's critique and Chauvet's whole project were all reaching for a doctrine that was not there.

Rahner's system therefore has four pillars, not three: method, symbol ontology, grace — and, implicitly, an account of how symbolisation fails, which he never wrote down.

Caveat worth keeping honest: part of this centrality is an artefact of where the recent writing went. Two of the last three edits were about falsity, so falsity accumulated edges. The claim survives anyway — but state it as "the unit now turns on this" rather than "Rahner's corpus does."

24 — The Theology of the False Symbol

Prev: <a href="#23-knowledge-graph">23 - Knowledge Graph</a> · Presupposes <a href="#07-the-theology-of-the-symbol-the-core-essay">07 - The Theology of the Symbol - The Core Essay</a> and <a href="#08-realsymbol-applied-trinity-christ-church-">08 - Realsymbol Applied - Trinity, Christ, Church, Sacraments, Body</a>

Status of this note — read first

Rahner did not write this. Notes 01–18 expound him; this note extends him. Throughout, the register is marked: - [R] = Rahner's own stated position - [R→] = follows from Rahner's principles though he did not draw it - [+] = constructive proposal, mine to defend, not his to blame

In an exam, keep these distinct. An examiner will reward a clearly flagged constructive extension and penalise the same material presented as exegesis.

1. The gap, stated precisely

Rahner's Thesis 1: "All beings are by their very nature symbolic, because they necessarily 'express' themselves in order to attain their own nature." [R]

The ontology is built to explain plenitude: the Father utters the Word, the Logos utters flesh, Christ utters the Church, the Church utters sacraments, the soul utters the body. Expression is fullness overflowing.

But the actual religious world is full of expressions that do not tell the truth about what they express:

Case The trouble
The hypocrite's smile Genuine bodily expression — of something other than joy
Judas's kiss The sign of love performing betrayal
A Eucharist at a segregated table The rite says "one body"; the seating says "two"
A Church that abuses the vulnerable The real symbol of Christ acting against Christ
A sacrament received in bad faith The sign performed, the reality refused
Devotion that stops at the image Symbol that has ceased to point beyond itself

Rahner touches this only privatively: the Church is ecclesia peccatrix, a real symbol that can be a deficient expression of what it symbolises [R] → 12 - Ecclesiology and Sacramental Theology. That is honest but thin. Deficiency is not the same as falsity, and a metaphysics that cannot distinguish them cannot say what is wrong with Judas.

The question of this note

What is the ontological status of a symbol that misrepresents what it symbolises?

2. First move: the false symbol is not a Vertretungssymbol

The tempting shortcut is to say: a false symbol is merely an arbitrary sign misapplied. This is wrong, and getting it wrong wrecks everything after it. [+]

The load-bearing claim

A lying symbol deceives precisely because it is a real symbol, not an arbitrary one. Judas's kiss betrays because a kiss really is the self-realisation of affection. If a kiss were a mere convention like a traffic light, the betrayal would carry no weight — one does not feel betrayed by a mislabelled road sign.

So: falsity is a mode of real symbolisation, not its absence. The false symbol borrows the whole ontological weight of the Realsymbol and turns it against the one who reads it. This is why the deepest wounds are inflicted through the truest signs — the parent's embrace, the priest's blessing, the friend's word.

[+] Corollary — the parasitism thesis. The false symbol is ontologically parasitic. Counterfeit currency circulates only because real currency exists; forgery presupposes signature; a lie presupposes a background of truth-telling. There can be no world in which most symbols are false, for then none would function and none could deceive. Falsity is therefore always secondary, derivative, unoriginal — Augustine's privatio boni transposed into semiotics: a privatio veritatis symbolicae.

3. A typology — five modes of symbolic failure

Not all failure is falsity. Distinguish, or you will call reticence a lie and a lie a limitation. [+]

# Mode Definition Culpable? Example
1 Deficient symbol Genuine expression, inadequate to what it expresses No — this is finitude itself Any human word about God; the Church always short of Christ
2 Concealing symbol Expression that veils while revealing Not necessarily The composed face at a funeral; the disciplina arcani; the messianic secret
3 Lying symbol Expression asserting what is not the case Yes Judas's kiss; the whitewashed tomb; Ananias's gift
4 Idol Symbol that arrests the gaze instead of transmitting it Usually Clericalism; institutional self-preservation; devotion that stops at the image
5 Counter-symbol / anti-sacrament Symbol that effects the contrary of what it signifies Yes, gravely Eucharist at a segregated table

On (1) — deficiency is not failure

Every finite symbol is deficient. [R] God remains incomprehensible even in the beatific vision → 06 - God's Self-Communication and Quasi-Formal Causality. To call finitude "failure" would make creation itself a lie. Deficiency is the condition of creaturely symbolisation, not a defect in it.

On (2) — concealment can be truthful

[R→] Rahner's own doctrine of God as absolute mystery requires this: revelation conceals in revealing. God's self-communication does not abolish incomprehensibility; it gives it. So a symbol that veils is not thereby false — it may be the only truthful way to express something that would be falsified by full display.

Test for the difference: concealment serves the reality; lying serves the expresser against the reality. Reticence protects what it does not show; hypocrisy substitutes for what it does not have.

On (4) — borrowing Marion against Rahner's critic

[+] Jean-Luc Marion's idol/icon distinction (God Without Being) is usually deployed against Rahner → 16 - Critical Reflection I - Philosophical. It is better used inside him: - Icon — the gaze passes through toward the invisible; the symbol effaces itself in serving what it expresses. - Idol — the gaze stops; the symbol reflects the viewer back to himself and goes opaque.

On Rahner's ontology the idol is a symbol that has ceased to be transitive — it no longer realises another in itself but only itself. This gives a precise account of clericalism: a ministry instituted to express Christ that now expresses the minister.

On (5) — the anti-sacrament

This is the sharpest category and the real payoff of the note. See §6.

4. How is falsity possible at all? The ontological account

If being expresses itself in order to attain itself, how can expression go wrong? Four steps. [+], built on [R]

Step 1 — Falsity requires freedom. In God, self-utterance is necessary, eternal and perfect: the Word is the adequate expression of the Father, and there is no gap [R] → 08 - Realsymbol Applied - Trinity, Christ, Church, Sacraments, Body. In creatures below spirit, expression is automatic: a stone cannot misrepresent itself. Only where self-expression is free — where a being disposes of itself (Grundentscheidung, → 13 - Theological Anthropology, Death and Eschatology) — can it be withheld, distorted, or falsified.

Therefore: **the possibility of the false symbol is the price of spirit.**

Lying is not a lower capacity than truth-telling; it is a higher one than mere deficiency, because it presupposes self-possession. Only someone who can express himself truly can express himself falsely. This is why hypocrisy is a specifically human and a specifically religious sin — cf. Aquinas, ST II-II q.111 a.2, that hypocrisy is a species of lying.

Step 2 — The liar damages himself first. On Rahner's law, a being attains itself by expressing itself. It follows immediately that one who expresses himself falsely fails to attain himself. [R→]

The result worth stating in one line

The lie is self-mutilation before it is deception. Whatever it does to the hearer, it first prevents the speaker from becoming who he is.

This grounds the intrinsic evil of lying without appeal to consequences — an argument Augustine (De mendacio) and Aquinas (ST II-II q.110) reach by other routes. Rahner's ontology gets there in one step, and this is arguably the strongest confirmation that the ontology is worth having.

Step 3 — Nothing escapes self-expression, including falsity. The lying symbol still expresses — it expresses the liar's disintegration. Judas's kiss is a false symbol of love and a true symbol of Judas. So the false symbol does not defeat Thesis 1; it confirms it in a privative mode. [R→]

Pastoral consequence

This is why hypocrisy eventually becomes legible. A person cannot indefinitely express what he is not, because he is expressing himself the whole time in everything else. The lie has to be maintained; the truth maintains itself. Institutions work the same way, which is why cover-ups fail late but reliably.

Step 4 — Falsity is parasitic, therefore defeasible. Because the false symbol borrows its power from the true (§2), it can never be foundational and can always in principle be exposed by the reality it borrows from. [+] There is no symmetry between truth and falsehood in Rahner's ontology, and therefore no dualism: the counterfeit presupposes the coin.

What the dossier shows

Biblically, the false symbol is not a marginal case but a central prophetic concern, and the prophets' verdict is consistently harsher on false worship than on no worship. A theology of symbol with no account of this cannot preach Amos or Matthew 23.

6. Application I — the Church as counter-witness

The problem. If the Church is the Realsymbol of the risen Christ [R], what happens when it acts against him?

[R→] Three theses:

(a) The Church does not cease to be Christ's symbol when it sins. Otherwise sin would have power to unmake the historical prolongation of the Incarnation. Compare the doctrine of the indelible character in orders and baptism: the minister who betrays remains ordained. The symbol persists under falsification — which is exactly what makes falsification possible and grave.

(b) It becomes a counter-witness (Gegenzeugnis): it reveals Christ and obscures him in the same act. Not two alternating states, but one act with two symbolic vectors. The Church that preaches mercy while protecting an abuser expresses both, simultaneously, and the observer will believe the one enacted in bodies.

(c) Therefore scandal in the Church is ontologically worse than the same act elsewhere.

The consequence Rahner's ontology forces

Because a real symbol is the reality's own mode of presence, what is done in the symbol is predicated of what it symbolises. An assault committed by a bank employee defames a bank. An assault committed by a priest, in a Church that is Christ's real symbol, is read — accurately, on Rahner's own principles — as saying something about Christ.

This is why the abuse crisis is a theological catastrophe and not merely a moral or administrative one, and why "a few bad individuals" is not merely a poor defence but an ontologically incoherent one. It presupposes exactly the Vertretungssymbol view of the Church that the whole essay was written to destroy.

(d) The remedy is not disowning the symbol but re-expression. On Rahner's own law, the Church attains itself by expressing itself. Repentance is therefore not an interruption of the Church's symbolic function but its exercise: public, bodily, costly contrition is itself a real symbol, and one that expresses Christ truly. [R→] Concealment of sin is symbolic self-mutilation; confession of sin is symbolic self-attainment.

7. Application II — the sacramental lie

Rahner reinterprets sacramenta significando causant as symbolic causality: signifying and causing are one act [R] → 12 - Ecclesiology and Sacramental Theology.

[+] Run the axiom in reverse and you get a doctrine of the anti-sacrament.

If a sacrament causes by signifying, then a rite that signifies division will cause division — by the same law, with the same efficacy.

This is not a rhetorical flourish. It is Paul's actual argument at 1 Cor 11: the Corinthian assembly performs the rite correctly and the meal "is not the Lord's supper," and the assembly meets "not for the better but for the worse." The rite retains its causal structure and its content has been inverted.

Three levels of sacramental falsity:

Level Who falsifies Protected by Result
Minister The unworthy celebrant Ex opere operato — the sacrament's objectivity does not rest on his holiness [R] Sacrament valid; the minister lies about himself, not about the sacrament
Recipient The one who receives in bad faith Not protected 1 Cor 11:29 — receives "judgement"; the sacramentum is present, the res refused
Assembly The community whose enacted arrangements contradict the rite Nothing protects this The counter-symbol: two contradictory real symbols in one act

The rule of competing symbols **[+]**

When a verbal symbol and a bodily symbol contradict each other, the bodily one is believed. This follows directly from Rahner's own doctrine that the body is the person's real symbol and that human knowing runs through the phantasma → 02 - Philosophical Foundations I - Spirit in the World, 08 - Realsymbol Applied - Trinity, Christ, Church, Sacraments, Body. Words are the thinner symbol; arrangements of bodies are the thicker one; and the thicker, repeated weekly, wins.

The caste case, now completed

Note 18 built an argument against caste segregation at the Eucharist and stopped at step 3 for want of this doctrine → 18 - Rahner in the Indian Context. It can now be finished:

  1. The body is the person's Realsymbol: what is done to bodies is done to persons. [R]
  2. The Church is Christ's Realsymbol; the Eucharist is the Church's self-realisation. [R]
  3. A Eucharist celebrated with segregated seating, separate cemeteries, separate cups or a divided procession enacts two contradictory real symbols in one act — "one body" verbally, "two bodies" bodily. [+]
  4. By the rule of competing symbols, the bodily one is what is actually communicated and therefore, by significando causant, what is actually caused. The rite produces division while saying unity. [+]
  5. Therefore this is not a moral supplement to sacramental theology but a sacramental defect in the act itself — which is precisely Paul's charge at 1 Cor 11:29, that failing to discern the body makes the assembly's meal not the Lord's Supper.

The everyday version

A house draws a rangoli at its threshold — the real symbol of welcome, the household expressing its own hospitality — and then turns away the guest who comes to it. Or: a public garlanding of Ambedkar's statue in a village that maintains the two-tumbler system at the tea shop. In each case the symbol is not weakened but inverted: it now expresses, with full symbolic force, the falsity of the one who performs it.

8. Five criteria for discerning a false symbol

[+] A workable test, since the ontology is useless pastorally unless it can be applied.

# Criterion Question Source
1 Fruit Does what it produces match what it claims? Mt 7:16; the fig tree
2 Coherence Do the verbal symbol and the bodily symbol agree? The rule of competing symbols, §7
3 Cost Does the symbol cost the expresser anything? A costless symbol of self-gift is suspect Balthasar's Ernstfall → 17 - Critical Reflection II - Theological
4 Direction Does it point beyond itself, or arrest attention on itself? Marion's icon/idol
5 Victim Who is excluded by this symbol? Ask them — they read it accurately Metz's memoria passionis; Soares-Prabhu

Why criterion 5 matters most

It is the point where the critiques of notes 16–18 stop being objections to Rahner's ontology and become operations within it. Metz, Pieris and Soares-Prabhu argue that the transcendental subject arrives too late at suffering. Criterion 5 answers structurally: the excluded are the privileged readers of symbols, because a false symbol is transparent from underneath and opaque from above. The man at the head of the procession cannot see that the procession is divided; the man at its tail can see nothing else.

This is a genuine integration, not a truce — and it is the single most useful thing in this note.

9. The Cross — the limit case that resolves everything

The Cross looks like a false symbol, and by every ordinary criterion it fails:

  • Omnipotence expressed as total helplessness.
  • Kingship expressed by a placard nailed up in mockery (Jn 19:19).
  • The Son of God expressed as one apparently abandoned by God (Mk 15:34).

[+] Why it is nevertheless the truest symbol.

There is no gap between expression and expressed at Golgotha. The apparent contradiction is the content: God's power is self-emptying love, so a symbol showing omnipotence as helplessness is not a lie about God but the one adequate statement of him. Pilate's title, intended as a Vertretungssymbol of contempt, turns out to be objectively true and is written in three languages so that all may read it.

Two payoffs at once

(a) The criterion of falsity is refined. A symbol is false not when it looks unlike what it expresses but when it is unlike it. Judas's kiss looks like love and is not; the Cross looks like defeat and is love. Appearance is not the test — coincidence of expression and reality is.

(b) The kenosis gap closes. Note 17 charged that a metaphysics of self-expression as self-attainment has no room for the Cross as self-emptying → 17 - Critical Reflection II - Theological. The answer is now available and Rahner never gave it: at the Cross, self-expression and self-emptying are the same act. The Son attains himself precisely in pouring himself out (Phil 2:6–11), because what he is, is self-gift. Far from being an exception to Thesis 1, the Cross is its supreme instance — and the point at which the thesis has to be read Christologically rather than merely metaphysically.

The honest addition

If this reply works, it also corrects Thesis 1 as stated. Rahner wrote that a being expresses itself "in order to attain its own nature," which sounds acquisitive — expression as a means to self-possession. The Cross requires the stronger form: for a being whose nature is self-gift, expressing itself and expending itself are one and the same. That is not a gloss on Rahner; it is an amendment, and it is the amendment Balthasar was asking for.

10a. Chauvet checked — where this note stands corrected

Sourcing caveat, stated first

This section was compiled from the publisher's description, reviews, and a chapter-by-chapter reading summary — not from the book open in front of me. The "three temptations" below are a summariser's rendering, not Chauvet's verbatim wording, and no page references are given because none have been verified. Before teaching this section, read Symbol and Sacrament Part II (symbolic exchange) and the chapter on Christian identity, and replace the paraphrases with real citations.

Bibliographic detail, verified. Louis-Marie Chauvet, Symbole et sacrement: une relecture sacramentelle de l'existence chrétienne (Paris: Cerf, 1987); ET Symbol and Sacrament: A Sacramental Reinterpretation of Christian Existence, trans. Madeleine M. Beaumont and Patrick Madigan SJ (Collegeville: Liturgical Press, 1995).

What checks out

  • The onto-theology critique is the book's actual project, not an incidental theme. Chauvet argues that scholastic sacramental theology adopted the language of causality because of its onto-theological metaphysics, not because causality was theologically apt — grace, being (in his phrase) a non-object, a non-value, resists instrumental and productionist categories.
  • "Symbolic exchange and mediation" is right: the programme replaces sign, cause, instrument with symbol, linguistic act, mediation.
  • He does have a doctrine of sacramental corruption, and this note's claim on that point survives — see below.

Correction 1 — Chauvet's target is Aquinas, not Rahner

This note's earlier wording implied Chauvet critiques Rahner. He does not, at least not centrally. The critique of the "productionist scheme" is aimed at Thomas Aquinas and classical scholasticism; the opening argument tracks shifts within Aquinas's own thought between the Sentences commentary and the Summa. Rahner is not the antagonist.

The accurate statement is succession, not attack: reviewers place Chauvet as the first radically different European sacramental theology since the transformation of neo-scholasticism by Rahner and Schillebeeckx. He displaces the causality framework that Rahner was still working to reinterpret from inside — which is a real difference, but a difference of generation and strategy, not a polemic.

How to say it in an essay

"Rahner reinterprets sacramental causality as symbolic causality; Chauvet proposes to abandon the register of causality altogether in favour of symbolic exchange. Chauvet is not refuting Rahner but declining the terrain on which Rahner fought."

Correction 2 — Chauvet's typology of corruption runs on a different axis from mine

He identifies three temptations, each a way of "killing the presence of the absence" of the Risen One — of converting him into a dead body or an available object:

Chauvet's temptation What it does
Closed systems of religious knowledge Doctrine that masters the mystery instead of serving it
Sacramental magic The rite as a technique that secures grace as a product
Moralism divorced from God's otherness Ethics that no longer receives, only performs

The distinction that matters

Chauvet's axis is grasping; mine is misrepresenting. His corrupt symbol is the symbol held too tightly — reified, objectified, made available, turned from gift into possession. My lying symbol is the symbol that says what is not the case — Judas's kiss, the divided assembly saying "one body."

These are genuinely different failures, and neither typology contains the other. His "sacramental magic" overlaps my idol (§3.4) almost exactly. But he has no equivalent of the lying symbol or the counter-symbol, and I have no equivalent of his first and third temptations. The honest verdict: this note's §3 typology is complementary to Chauvet, not derived from him and not superseded by him.

Correction 3 — his ethics-as-verification is a stronger ally than I claimed

Chauvet structures Christian identity as a triad — Scripture / Sacrament / Ethics, corresponding to cognition–recognition–praxis — and holds that abstracted from one another the three lose their meaning. Crucially, ethics functions as the return-gift that verifies the reception of the divine gift: sacraments are valueless if they are not the living memory of the crucified God and if they do not lead worshippers to become in their practice what they have received.

That is an independent route to criterion 1 (fruit) and criterion 5 (victim) in §8, and it substantially reinforces the anti-sacrament argument of §7: on Chauvet's own terms, a liturgy that does not issue in the practice it signifies has failed as liturgy, not merely as morals. Cite him here — it is the strongest external support this note has.

The real tension — and it touches §2

Chauvet's governing formula is "the presence of the absence." The liturgical assembly is the sacramental representation of the absence of the Risen One; Christ is symbolically present precisely as bodily absent, and the temptation is always to fill that absence with an available object.

Rahner's Realsymbol runs the other way: the symbol is the reality's own mode of maximal presence.

This bears directly on §2's load-bearing claim that a false symbol deceives because it is a real symbol. On Rahner's account, that works: the symbol carries genuine presence and can therefore be turned against its reader. On Chauvet's account, one would have to say instead that the false symbol deceives by converting an absence into a possession — the lie is not misrepresented presence but refused absence.

Open question, worth an essay

Are these two accounts of symbolic falsity reconcilable, or does a Chauvetian reading dissolve §2? A defensible answer: they name the two directions in which symbolisation fails — Rahner's ontology exposes the symbol that says what is not, Chauvet's exposes the symbol that holds what cannot be held. If so, the five-mode typology of §3 needs a sixth column for possessive falsity, and this note is incomplete as it stands.

11. Summary — the seven claims of this note

  1. The false symbol is not an arbitrary sign; it deceives because it is a real symbol. [+]
  2. Five modes must be distinguished: deficient, concealing, lying, idol, counter-symbol. Only the last three are culpable. [+]
  3. Falsity is possible only for free spirit — it is the price of self-possession. [+]
  4. The liar fails to attain himself: the lie is self-mutilation before it is deception. [R→]
  5. Falsity is parasitic on truth and therefore never original, never final, and always in principle exposable. [+]
  6. Run significando causant in reverse and you get the anti-sacrament: a rite signifying division causes division. This completes the caste argument of note 18. [+]
  7. The Cross shows that the test of a true symbol is not resemblance but the coincidence of expression and reality — and in closing that question it also closes the kenosis gap of note 17. [+]
  8. Open and unfinished: Chauvet's "presence of the absence" names a second direction of symbolic falsity — the symbol held too tightly rather than the symbol that misrepresents. §3's typology does not yet cover it. → §10a [+]

The thesis sentence if you write this as an essay

"Rahner's ontology of the symbol has no doctrine of the lying symbol, and the omission is not accidental: an ontology in which being expresses itself in order to attain itself is built to explain plenitude. Yet the resources for the missing doctrine are his own. Because self-expression in spirit is free, it can be falsified; because a being attains itself by expressing itself, the falsifier fails to attain himself; and because symbolic causality is real, a rite that signifies division causes it. The completed doctrine does not overturn Rahner — it makes his ecclesiology able to say what is wrong with a Church that betrays, and his sacramental theology able to say what is wrong with a Eucharist that divides."
