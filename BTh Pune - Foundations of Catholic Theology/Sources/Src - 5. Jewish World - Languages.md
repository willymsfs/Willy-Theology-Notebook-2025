# Src — 5. Jewish World of the NT — Languages

> [!info] Source note · theme: [[01 - New Testament Background and World]]

**Topic**: First-century Palestine was multilingual — the trilingual titulus on the Cross (Hebrew, Latin, Greek — Jn 19:20).

- **Latin** — official language of the Roman conquerors (governors, soldiers). Few Jews fluent; only loanwords (centurion, legion, denarius, praetorium, colony). Jesus' trial before Pilate needed an interpreter.
- **Greek** — *lingua franca*; strong in "Galilee of the Gentiles"; economic necessity in the marketplace; rooted in 2nd-c. BC Seleucid Hellenization (Maccabean reaction only delayed it); most educated upper-class Jews knew some.
- **Aramaic** — mother tongue of the majority (adopted in the 6th-c. exile), related to Hebrew.
- **Hebrew** — sacred language of scribes/rabbis; near "dead" for ordinary people; synagogue read Hebrew + **Targum** (Aramaic paraphrase).

**Languages of Jesus**: Aramaic (mother tongue — preached to the people), Hebrew (debating scribes), Greek (non-Jews — the centurion, the Syro-Phoenician woman).
