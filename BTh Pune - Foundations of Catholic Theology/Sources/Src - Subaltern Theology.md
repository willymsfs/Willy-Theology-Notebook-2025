# Src — Subaltern Theology

> [!info] Source note · theme: [[08 - Contextual and Liberation Theologies]]

**Topic**: New ways of thinking about marginality, power, and resistance in India; connecting faith to social suffering (Jesus' table fellowship with the marginalized). Applications: digital poverty, tribal revolts, Bar Dance Girls, silencing of Dalit/female voices.

## Definition & context
- Connects faith to concrete Indian realities.
- Arrived **"a little late"** — theology adopted it as Subaltern Studies was declining. **Onoda metaphor** (soldier hiding 29 years).

## Subaltern Studies
- Term: British military ("junior officer") → **Gramsci** (*Prison Notebooks*) for oppressed classes lacking voice.
- **Collective** (1980s) — 12 volumes 1982–2005.
- Rejects elite history: **Colonial** (Indians passive) + **Nationalist** (only elites credited); asserts an autonomous subaltern political space of resistance.

## Method — "history from below"
- Subalterns as **subjects of history**, "makers of their own destiny."
- **Silences** (Trouillot): constructed at four stages — sources, archives, narratives, standard history.

## Thinkers
- **Collective**: Ranajit Guha, Dipesh Chakraborty, David Hardiman, Partha Chatterjee, Gyanendra Pandey, Gayatri Spivak, Ajay Skaria.
- Influences: Gramsci, Marx, E.P. Thompson, Hobsbawm, James C. Scott.
- Others: Trouillot (silences), Robert Park ("marginal man"), Laura Mulvey ("male gaze").

## Critiques
1. Rigid elite/subaltern dichotomy. 2. Agency only in revolts (misses everyday resistance). 3. **Absence of women** (Spivak). 4. **Neglect of caste** (Marxist class-centrism).
