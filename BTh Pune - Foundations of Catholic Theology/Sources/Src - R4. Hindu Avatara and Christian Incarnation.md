# Src — R4. Hindu Avatara and Christian Incarnation

> [!info] Source note (reading, Noel Sheth SJ) · themes: [[03 - Christology and Indian Christology]] · [[02 - Indian Christian Theology and Inculturation]]

**Thesis**: *avatara* and Incarnation stem from contrasting worldviews (cyclic vs linear) but differences are nuanced; comparison aids dialogue and self-understanding.

## Development
- **Avatara** ("descent"): *Bhagavad-gita* — Krsna descends to protect the good, destroy the wicked, restore *dharma*. Later Vaisnavism: bodies of "pure matter" (*suddha-sattva*). Types: *purna*, *amsa*, *antaryamin*, *arca*.
- **Incarnation**: Word made flesh (John); **Chalcedon (451)** — hypostatic union, two natures in one Person; fully divine + human, can suffer, sinless.

## Similarities
Immanence + transcendence; active involvement in the world; vulnerability to misuse (Aryan/Brahmin dominance / colonization).

## Differences (cyclic vs linear)
- **Frequency vs uniqueness**: many avataras across *yugas* vs once-for-all (nuance: Second Coming; Aquinas — multiple natures possible).
- **Perfect vs imperfect**: avatara suffering only apparent (for *bhakti*) vs Christ truly suffers (the Cross).
- **Ahistorical vs historical**: avataras real but not time-bound vs Christ real + historical (body-soul unity).
- **Partial vs full**: partial avataras vs full Incarnation.
- **Scope/non-violence**: avataras limited purpose, often violent vs Christ universal salvation, non-violence, redeems matter/history.

## Complementarity
- Christians learn: *vatsalya* (parental devotion to the Child); cosmic sense/ecology.
- Hindus learn: historical commitment; redemptive self-suffering (Gandhi).
