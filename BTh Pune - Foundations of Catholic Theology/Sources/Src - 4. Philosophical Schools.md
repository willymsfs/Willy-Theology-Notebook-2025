# Src — 4. Philosophical Schools (Greco-Roman World)

> [!info] Source note · theme: [[01 - New Testament Background and World]]

- **Platonism** — Plato (427–347 BCE); Neo-Platonism (Plotinus 205–270). This world = "insubstantial shadows"; escape matter to return home; dualist (body/soul). Declined by NT times but shaped the Fathers.
- **Cynics** — Antisthenes (disciple of Socrates, 300s BCE); behaviour over theory, frugality, return to nature; virtue the goal; no interest in god; the **diatribe**.
- **Epicureans** — Epicurus (342–270 BCE); "sensation" as the standard of truth; free from superstition — atoms determine events, gods uninvolved, death final; secret friendship meetings. **Paul met them (Acts 17:18).**
- **Stoicism** — Athens (the *stoa*); Epictetus, Seneca, Marcus Aurelius; universe as one organism energized by the ***Logos***; live by natural law → tranquillity; passions pathological; sparks of the Logos in all → "citizens of the world"; providence/astrology. Influenced Pauline virtue lists; **Paul debated them (Acts 17:18)**.
- **Sophism** — no single system; **Protagoras** — "man is the measure of all things"; itinerant rhetoric teachers for a fee; relativism, skepticism → "sophism" = fallacious argument.
- **Gnosticism** — roots in Iran/Egypt, mainly 2nd c. AD; from *gnosis* (knowledge); non-historical revelation; matter evil, soul alien; denied the reality of the Incarnation and Jesus' death; counter-church. Parallels with John's "know" (Jn 17:3) though later; Paul warns of a false "way of knowledge."
