# Src — Religious Pluralism in the Hindu Perspective

> [!info] Source note (reading, Rosario Rocha SJ) · themes: [[02 - Indian Christian Theology and Inculturation]] · [[08 - Contextual and Liberation Theologies]]

**Thesis**: pluralism is **inherent** to Hinduism (texts, philosophy, history) — an ethos of assimilation and tolerance, now challenged by Hindutva. Method: phenomenological ("art of empathy").

## Roots & scripture
- **Socio-historical**: *Laws of Manu* 'we'/'they'; Aryan–native inculturation (rise of Shiva).
- **Vedas**: "henotheism" (Max Muller); *ekam sad vipra bahudha vadanti* ("Truth is one, sages call it by various names").
- **Upanishads**: *Atman* = *Brahman*; plurality of *margas* (jnana/karma/bhakti).
- **Bhagavadgita**: synthesis; *nirguna* vs *saguna* Brahman.

## Philosophy & dissent
- Six *darsanas*; tolerance of radical dissent (*Lokayata*/*Carvaka*).
- **Assimilation not destruction**: Buddha absorbed as an avatar of Vishnu.

## Contemporary
- **Radhakrishnan** (unified spiritual oneness); **Gandhi** (all religions true but imperfect; *Ahimsa*, secular India).
- **Hindutva** threat — minorities as threat, contradicts the pluralistic ethos.
- Hindu pluralism prompted the **secular Constitution**.
