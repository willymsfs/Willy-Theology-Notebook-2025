# Src — Inculturation: Some Theological Perspectives

> [!info] Source note (reading, Michael Paul Gallagher) · theme: [[02 - Indian Christian Theology and Inculturation]]

**Thesis**: inculturation = a two-way **"double conversion"** transforming culture from within; rooted in Creation, Incarnation, Redemption, Pentecost, Trinity.

## Context
- Deep roots (Paul at Athens, four Gospels, Jewish→Gentile shift).
- End of colonialism → take local cultures seriously; overcome the bias of Western Christianity as "normal"; **double enrichment**.

## Definition
- Tied to **evangelization**; transformation **from within** (Paul VI); gospel "received, felt, celebrated, lived within the deep language of a local culture." Santo Domingo 1992: discernment → inculturation → evangelized culture.

## Four theological foundations
- **Creation** (culture as response to God, "fellow-workers"); **Incarnation** (gospel takes flesh; self-emptying); **Redemption** (confront "structures of sin," purification via the Cross); **Pentecost** (unity in diversity; the Spirit already at work — Church receives too).

## Trajectory (Peter Phan)
1. Translate/adapt categories. 2. Mutual (superficial) borrowing. 3. **Deepest: self-government, self-support, self-propagation, self-theologizing** (Rahner: faith must find a new form).

## Conclusion
- **Trinity**: cultures communitarian → goal is to mirror the "communion-love" of the triune God.
