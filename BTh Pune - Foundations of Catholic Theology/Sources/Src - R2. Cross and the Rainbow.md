# Src — R2. The Cross and the Rainbow

> [!info] Source note (reading, Stanley J. Samartha) · themes: [[03 - Christology and Indian Christology]] · [[08 - Contextual and Liberation Theologies]]

**Full title**: "The Cross and the Rainbow: Christ in a Multireligious Culture."

**Thesis**: abandon exclusive claims about Christ; adopt a **theocentric (Mystery-centred) Christology** — "relational distinctiveness" honouring Christian faith while recognizing other paths.

## Problem with exclusivism
- Exclusive claims block harmony/cooperation; institutional hesitation (*Nostra Aetate*, WCC) to treat others as "fellow pilgrims"; evangelicals use dialogue instrumentally.

## Roots of Indian pluralism
- **One and the Many**: Truth (*Sat*) is One, sages call it by many names; Mysterious Centre (*Satyasya Satyam*).
- ***Adhikara*** (aptitude): people born into religions by *karma*/*adhikara* → superiority claims unwarranted.
- Culture of accommodation (right to "propagate").

## Theocentric Christology
- Critique of "Christomonism" (Jesus as tribal god); Bible theocentric (Kingdom of God; God "all in all"); covenant = **rainbow** for all humanity; **relational distinctiveness** not normative exclusivism.

## Plurality of salvation
- Trinity and *sat-cit-ananda* both culturally-conditioned symbols; different diagnoses (*sin* / *avidya* / *dukkha*) → different salvations (*moksha*, *nirvana*).
- Word as *Sabda* recovered by hearing.

## Art as bridge
- Aesthetics over apologetics; "unbaptized koinonia" (non-Christian artists engaging Jesus); **Christ belongs to Christ, not exclusively to institutional Christianity.**
