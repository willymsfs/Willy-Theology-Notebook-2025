# Src — Prophets: A Critical Study of Ezekiel 34:1–10

> [!info] Source note · theme: [[11 - Old Testament Foundations (Prophets, Wisdom, Anthropology)]]
> Note: this is a focused exegesis of **Ezekiel 34:1–10**, not a general survey of the prophets.

## Main topic — the "failed shepherds"
- Ezek 34:1–10 = prophetic indictment of Israel's exilic leadership (kings, priests, nobles).
- **Shepherd metaphor**: leaders should feed/protect/guide — but they feed themselves and abandon the flock.
- Accusation: self-interest over pastoral/covenantal responsibility.

## Exegesis / scholarship
- **Systemic failure** (ICC): structural exploitation, economic oppression → exile.
- **Socio-political** (Anchor Bible): ruling elites' poor alliances weakened Judah.
- **Covenantal** (Julie Galambush): shepherding a relational duty, not power.
- **Trauma/healing** (Michael Chan): the flock traumatized; message = critique + therapeutic promise.
- **Divine intervention**: not internal reform — God replaces the predatory shepherds, becomes true shepherd-king.

## Prophecy today
- Speaking truth to power; facing resistance; balancing critique with hope.
- Pastoral implication: **servant leadership** (transparency, accountability, strengthening the weak) — mirror for Parish Councils.
