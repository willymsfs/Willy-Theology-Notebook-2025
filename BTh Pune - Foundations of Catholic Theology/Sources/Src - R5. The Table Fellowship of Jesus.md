# Src — R5. The Table Fellowship of Jesus

> [!info] Source note (reading) · themes: [[03 - Christology and Indian Christology]] · [[08 - Contextual and Liberation Theologies]] · [[George Soares-Prabhu]]

**Full title**: "The Table Fellowship of Jesus: Its Significance for Dalit Christians in India Today."

**Thesis**: Jesus' table fellowship dismantled purity-based religion; community on God's unconditional mercy, not "cleanness" → invalidates caste. Caste at the Eucharist = sacrilege.

## Gospel evidence
- All Synoptic strands: Jesus habitually dined with outcasts → elite hostility. Justified as physician/shepherd; outcasts' readiness to repent vs self-assured "righteous."

## Jewish background
- Meals = "sharing life," fellowship before God; festive meals anticipate liberated Israel.
- Pharisees/Essenes: **holiness = separation**; strict purity hierarchies (analogous to caste) — tax collectors/"sinners" permanently polluted, excluded.

## Theological meaning
- **New experience of God** — *Abba*, mercy crossing purity boundaries.
- **New holiness** — from "holiness code" (be separated) to **"mercy code"** (be compassionate); religion = interhuman compassion.
- **New community** — nothing outside defiles (Mk 7:15); no status hierarchy, only service/love.

## For Dalit Christians today
- Caste incompatible with Christianity; **"Christian Dalit" an oxymoron** — a failure/unrepentant sin of the Indian Church.
- **Eucharistic sacrilege**: caste segregation at the Eucharist parodies the sacrament (cf. Paul rebuking Corinth, 1 Cor 11).
