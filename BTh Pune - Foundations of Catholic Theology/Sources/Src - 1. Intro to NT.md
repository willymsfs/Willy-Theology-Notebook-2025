# Src — 1. Introduction to the New Testament

> [!info] Source note · theme: [[01 - New Testament Background and World]]

**Topic**: Introduction to the NT course (Naveen Rebello SVD) — forming pastors as reliable interpreters of the Word. Hook: the Ethiopian eunuch (Acts 8:26–40) — interpreters need guidance across cultural/linguistic barriers.

## Course structure
- **Part I — World of the NT**: Palestine geography; Greek period (333–63 BC) + Roman period (63 BC–70 AD); Greco-Roman philosophy (Platonism, Cynics, Epicureanism, Stoicism, Sophism, Gnosticism); Jewish sects (Pharisees, Sadducees, Essenes, Zealots, Scribes, *Am-Ha-Arez*, Herodians, Samaritans), institutions (Temple, Synagogue, Sanhedrin, Priesthood).
- **Part II — Text of the NT**: name, content, origins, manuscripts, canon; Synoptics, John, Acts, Pauline, Hebrews, Catholic Epistles, Revelation.
- **Part III — Biblical Hermeneutics**: Textual, Source, Form, Historical, Literary, Redaction, Canonical, Rhetorical, Reader-response criticism — plus **Dhvani** (Indian method).

## Key concepts
- **Three approaches**: phenomenological (descriptive), theological (sacred/inspired), critical (historical/literary) — theological + critical are complementary.
- **Study steps**: Introduction, Exegesis, Theology; the **"4 I's"** — Information, Interpretation, Insights/Inspirations, Integration.
- **OT vs NT**: "Old" = already existing; "New" = definitive/unsurpassed, not "better" or superseding. Unity: one God, one Jewish-Christian tradition, one story (creation → new creation).
- **Literal sense** (Pius XII, *Divino Afflante Spiritu* §35): go back in spirit to ancient times via history, archaeology, ethnology.

## Definitions
- **Scripture** (Latin *scriptus*, written) — authoritative sacred writings.
- **Bible** (Greek *ta Biblia*, "books") — a library; canons: Jewish 24, Protestant 66, Catholic 73.
- **Testament/Covenant** (*berith* / *diatheke* / *testamentum*) — formal agreement with mutual obligations; Old = Sinai/Moses, New = Calvary/Jesus.
- **"Hebrew Bible"** critiqued — excludes Aramaic, LXX, Deutero-canon; can deny the OT as Christian Scripture.

## Names/dates
- Greek period 333–63 BC (Alexander, Ptolemy, Seleucid); Roman 63 BC–70 AD (Herod the Great 37–4 BC, Augustus 27 BC–14 AD, Pilate, Nero).
- "New Testament" term: Irenaeus, Clement of Alexandria (†215), Origen (†254); Jerome's Vulgate (*testamentum*).
- One-author-of-both-testaments reaffirmed: Council of Toledo (5th c.), Leo IX (1053), Innocent III (1208), Lyons II (1274).
- Reference authors: Raymond Brown, M. Eugene Boring, Mark Allan Powell, Bruce Metzger, Delbert Burkett.
