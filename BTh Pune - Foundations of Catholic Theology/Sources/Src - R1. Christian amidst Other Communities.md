# Src — R1. Christians Amidst Other Communities

> [!info] Source note (reading, Felix Wilfred) · themes: [[08 - Contextual and Liberation Theologies]] · [[02 - Indian Christian Theology and Inculturation]]

**Thesis**: the Christian community's flourishing is measured by the **quality of its relationships** with others; the *indirect* influence of Jesus/Gospel has been more impactful than direct preaching; build bridges, not apologetics.

## Indirect influence of Jesus
- **Gandhi** (Sermon on the Mount → *satyagraha*, cross as voluntary suffering); **Ramakrishna/Vivekananda** (mystical experience of Jesus; service); **Jotirao Phule** (equality → emancipation of shudras/women); **Ram Mohan Roy** (Gospel → reform, abolition of *satee*).
- Mediation of modernity for dalits/tribals (education, self-confidence); missionary language studies (Caldwell — Dravidian) → pluralism.

## Five future trajectories
1. **Unfinished agenda** — side with dalit/tribal movements ("substantive nationalism").
2. **Shudra → servanthood** — foster universal servanthood beyond the caste "collective shudra."
3. **People as ends, not means** — *caro cardo salutis*; serve the marginalized as ends, not for conversion.
4. **Active civil society** — shift from vertical (State/minority rights) to **horizontal relationships**.
5. **Pilgrims on the ethical path** — join secular movements; stop "aggravating contradictions" (elite schools vs marginalized).

## Conclusion
In Asia **identity = a network of relationships**; Christianity's Indian-ness depends on joint ethical options and mutual learning.
