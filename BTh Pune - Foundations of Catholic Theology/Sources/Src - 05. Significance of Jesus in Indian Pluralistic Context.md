# Src — 05. Significance of Jesus in the Indian Pluralistic Context

> [!info] Source note (reading, ITA 1998) · themes: [[03 - Christology and Indian Christology]] · [[08 - Contextual and Liberation Theologies]]

**Thesis**: To stay relevant in pluralistic India, Jesus must be "discovered afresh" through Indian lived experience → new images (Guru, Avatar, Yogi, Dalit) fostering dialogue, solidarity, liberation.

## Historical Jesus & Reign of God
- Context of Roman + religious domination; inaugurates the **reign of God** (love, liberation, option for the poor). Exclusive NT claims balanced with inclusive practice; other traditions have salvific roles (Vatican II).

## Christology across cultures
- Jewish (historical fulfilment) · Hellenistic (*Logos*, deification) · Syrian (sacraments, deification) · Latin (divine law/order, debt of guilt).

## Indian context
- Pluralism + caste/tribe/gender stratification; political instability; globalization/WTO as new colonization; **Hindutva** and communal clashes.

## New images of Jesus
Guru · Avatar · **Adipurusha** (primordial human) · Yogi · **the Dalit Jesus** (identified with the broken/dehumanized); tribals + women find liberation.

## Approach to pluralism
- God's Spirit in other religions; Jesus = "definitive, though non-exhaustive symbol of God-experience"; uniqueness as **symbolic statements** of faith, not against others.

## Serving Church
- Continue Jesus' Reign of God; prioritize the marginalized; democratic culture, "Church of the laity"; autonomous local Indian Church.
